## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of providing palliative cognitive and behavioral support in
subjects with Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder due to Prion
Disease (PrD-NCD) by co-administering (a) a histamine H3 receptor
antagonist or inverse agonist and (b) a muscarinic M1/M4 receptor
agonist, in particular the combination of pitolisant and xanomeline
(optionally with a peripheral antimuscarinic such as trospium to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects). The invention
addresses cognitive symptoms, daytime alertness, and quality-of-life
during the rapidly progressive course of prion disease; it does not
assert disease-modification of the underlying prion proteinopathy.

## Background of the invention

Prion Disease Neurocognitive Disorder is the neurocognitive
manifestation of a group of rare, uniformly fatal, transmissible
neurodegenerative diseases caused by conformational conversion of
the cellular prion protein (PrP^C) to a pathological isoform
(PrP^Sc). The clinical syndromes include sporadic Creutzfeldt–Jakob
disease (sCJD; the most common form), familial CJD, iatrogenic CJD,
variant CJD (vCJD), Gerstmann–Sträussler–Scheinker syndrome (GSS),
fatal familial insomnia (FFI), and kuru. PrD-NCD is recognized in the
*Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition,
Text Revision* (DSM-5-TR) as Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder due
to Prion Disease, and in the International Classification of Diseases,
11th Revision (ICD-11) at code 6D85 (Major) and the corresponding mild
form. The clinical syndrome is characterized by rapidly progressive
cognitive decline, often accompanied by myoclonus, ataxia, visual or
cerebellar disturbance, pyramidal or extrapyramidal signs, and
behavioral changes including apathy, depression, and (in advanced
stages) akinetic mutism.

The estimated annual incidence of sCJD is approximately 1 to 2 cases
per million population per year worldwide, with median survival from
clinical onset of approximately 6 months for sCJD and somewhat longer
courses for the inherited and atypical forms. Approximately 85 % of
human prion disease is sporadic, 10–15 % is genetic, and a small
fraction is acquired (iatrogenic or variant). Despite extensive
investigational effort, no disease-modifying therapy is approved by
the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for any prion
disease in any age group, and the mainstay of clinical management is
supportive care with symptomatic palliation. Symptomatic palliation
typically includes clonazepam or levetiracetam for myoclonus,
selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors or mirtazapine for affective
symptoms, low-dose atypical antipsychotics for agitation, and
non-pharmacologic supports for daily function.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses is the
absence of a defensible, mechanism-based palliative regimen for the
cognitive and arousal symptoms that dominate the clinical course of
PrD-NCD. Cholinergic deficits in cortex and hippocampus are
documented in prion disease at autopsy and contribute to the rapid
loss of cognition and arousal. Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors
(donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine), although approved for
Alzheimer's-type major NCD, have shown limited and inconsistent
benefit in prion disease in case series, and their tolerability in
the rapidly declining prion population is poor.

The present invention recognizes that two non-overlapping cholinergic
mechanisms can be combined to support arousal and cognition during
the prion disease course without relying on acetylcholinesterase
inhibition. First, the histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic
auto- and heteroreceptor that, when constitutively active, suppresses
the release of histamine from histaminergic neurons and the release
of acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine from cortical,
striatal, and hippocampal neurons. Selective inverse agonism at H3
by pitolisant (Wakix®, FDA-approved for excessive daytime sleepiness
in adults with narcolepsy) elevates extracellular histamine and
supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine,
without the abuse liability of stimulant scheduling and without the
peripheral cholinergic burden of an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor.
The H3 axis therefore addresses the *vigilance and arousal substrate*
of the cognitive impairment of PrD-NCD.

Second, the muscarinic M1 receptor is a Gαq-coupled postsynaptic
receptor concentrated in cerebral cortex and hippocampus that
mediates a substantial portion of cholinergic facilitation of
cognition; the M4 receptor is a Gαi-coupled receptor concentrated
in striatum that contributes to the antipsychotic and
agitation-attenuating effect of selective muscarinic agonism.
Xanomeline is an M1/M4 preferring muscarinic agonist developed in
the 1990s, more recently formulated with the peripherally
restricted antimuscarinic trospium as the FDA-approved
xanomeline–trospium combination (Cobenfy®) for the treatment of
schizophrenia in adults. The xanomeline axis therefore addresses
the *cortical–hippocampal cholinergic substrate* of the cognitive
impairment of PrD-NCD, in a manner that is mechanistically distinct
from acetylcholinesterase inhibition and that obviates the need for
endogenous acetylcholine availability.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance- and
arousal-enabled cognition (an H3 antagonist axis) and post-synaptic
muscarinic facilitation of cortical–hippocampal cognition (an M1/M4
agonist axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for
palliative cognitive support in PrD-NCD. The combination is
expected to produce a greater, and more sustained, palliative
cognitive and arousal benefit than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose, by acting on two non-overlapping CNS
axes that converge on the same cortical–limbic cognitive circuit.
The invention does not assert disease-modification of the underlying
prion proteinopathy.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, (b) xanomeline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, and (c) optionally a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic
agent (for example, trospium chloride) in an amount effective to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects, together with one
or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of providing
palliative cognitive and arousal support in a subject with Major or
Mild Neurocognitive Disorder due to Prion Disease, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of
pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline,
optionally further with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic.
Co-administration may be simultaneous, in a unitary dosage form or in
separate dosage forms, or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising (a)
pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) xanomeline (optionally co-formulated with trospium) in a second
unitary dosage form, and (c) written instructions for the palliative
treatment of PrD-NCD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about 4.5 mg
to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily. The invention
contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about 50 mg to about
250 mg, administered orally as the free base or as the tartrate salt,
in two divided doses; when xanomeline is co-administered with
trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is about 40 mg to about
60 mg administered orally in two divided doses, matching the marketed
xanomeline-tartrate / trospium-chloride product (Cobenfy®). The mass
ratio of pitolisant to xanomeline (calculated on the free-base
equivalents) ranges broadly from about 1:200 to about 1:5, and is
preferably from about 1:50 to about 1:10. The dose-equivalent molar
relationship and the timing relationship are the inventive features,
not the absolute mass ratio.

The technical effect of the combination is (a) an attenuation, in
subjects with PrD-NCD, of the rate of decline on validated cognitive
instruments (Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) or Montreal
Cognitive Assessment (MoCA); Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR)
sum-of-boxes); (b) an extension of the period of preserved
independence as measured by Activities of Daily Living
(ADCS-ADL) and Modified Rankin Scale (mRS); and (c) a reduction in
caregiver burden (Zarit Burden Interview), each greater than would
be obtained with either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose. The combination is opioid-free, stimulant-free, and has no
known dependence liability.

herein includes the free base, the hydrochloride salt and other

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-

*"Palliative cognitive support"* means an intervention with the

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a piperidine derivative and a high-affinity, selective
inverse agonist at the human histamine H3 receptor. Inverse agonism
at H3 elevates extracellular histamine and supports state-dependent
release of acetylcholine and dopamine in cortex, hippocampus, and
dorsal striatum. Pitolisant is approved by the FDA for the
treatment of excessive daytime sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy.
Pitolisant is metabolized primarily by CYP2D6 and CYP3A4. The
medication is a substrate and weak inhibitor of CYP3A4 and is not a
significant inducer of common cytochrome P450 enzymes. Pitolisant
is *not* an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor and is therefore expected
to avoid the peripheral cholinergic burden (nausea, diarrhea,
bradycardia) that limits the use of donepezil, rivastigmine, and
galantamine in frail prion-disease patients. Pitolisant is under
U.S. brand-patent protection through the late 2020s; clinical
deployment of the present invention will require coordination with
the marketing authorization holder.

#### 2.2 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. Activation of
the M1 receptor (a Gαq-coupled receptor concentrated in cerebral
cortex and hippocampus) facilitates cholinergic cognitive
enhancement post-synaptically and is independent of endogenous
acetylcholine availability. Activation of the M4 receptor (a Gαi-
coupled receptor concentrated in striatum) contributes to attenuation
of psychotomimetic and agitation symptoms. Xanomeline was developed
in the 1990s as a potential treatment for Alzheimer's-type major NCD
and showed cognitive and behavioral signal in clinical study; further
development at that time was limited by peripheral cholinergic
adverse effects (nausea, vomiting, salivation, sweating, diarrhea).
The combination of xanomeline with the peripherally restricted
muscarinic antagonist trospium chloride substantially mitigates
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects without antagonizing the
central M1/M4 effect, and the xanomeline–trospium combination
(Cobenfy®) is FDA-approved for the treatment of schizophrenia in
adults. Xanomeline is metabolized primarily by CYP2D6 and CYP3A4.
The xanomeline–trospium combination is under U.S. brand-patent
protection through the 2030s; clinical deployment of the present
invention will require coordination with the marketing authorization
holder.

#### 2.3 Trospium (optional)

Trospium chloride is a quaternary ammonium peripherally restricted
muscarinic antagonist that does not appreciably cross the
blood-brain barrier. Trospium is approved as a monotherapy for
overactive bladder and is an FDA-approved component of the
xanomeline–trospium fixed-dose combination Cobenfy® for
schizophrenia. In the present invention, trospium is included
optionally and at a daily dose effective to mitigate peripheral
cholinergic adverse effects without antagonizing the central
muscarinic effect of xanomeline.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-release
oral tablet at strengths of 4.5 mg and 17.8 mg (free-base

tablets at strengths up to 35.6 mg per unit are within the scope of
the invention.

Xanomeline (with or without trospium) is preferably formulated as
twice-daily oral capsules. The marketed Cobenfy® product is supplied

xanomeline-tartrate / trospium-chloride capsules; the present

oral intake becomes unreliable.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-tablet bottle of
pitolisant immediate-release oral tablets at 8.9 mg or 17.8 mg,
(b) a 60-capsule bottle of xanomeline-trospium 100 mg/20 mg
capsules, and (c) a printed insert with administration timing,
weight-band guidance for hepatic / renal adjustment, and titration
schedule.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adults (subjects ≥ 18 years and ≥ 50 kg) with PrD-NCD, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pitolisant 8.9 mg orally once daily on awakening for week 1, then
  17.8 mg once daily thereafter; maximum 35.6 mg/day.
* Xanomeline-trospium 50 mg/20 mg orally twice daily for the first
  three days, then 100 mg/20 mg twice daily for the next four days,
  then 125 mg/30 mg twice daily thereafter as tolerated; matching
  the prescribing information for Cobenfy® in schizophrenia.

Pediatric experience with either active is limited; use in subjects
< 18 years should be reserved for compassionate-use settings under
specialist supervision.

Hepatic adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B) and is not
recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).
Xanomeline-trospium is not recommended in moderate or severe hepatic
impairment.

Renal adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²).
Xanomeline-trospium is not recommended in moderate or severe renal
impairment.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP2D6 inhibitors (paroxetine, fluoxetine, bupropion) without
halving the pitolisant and xanomeline doses. Avoid co-administration
with strong CYP3A4 inducers. Avoid co-administration with potent
anticholinergic medications other than the trospium component.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects who
meet DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 criteria for Major or Mild Neurocognitive
Disorder due to Prion Disease. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: confirmed diagnosis of probable or
definite human prion disease per the European CJD Surveillance
Network (EuroCJD) or U.S. CDC criteria, supported by characteristic
clinical features and at least one of EEG (periodic sharp-wave
complexes), CSF (elevated 14-3-3, RT-QuIC positivity), or MRI
(cortical ribboning, basal-ganglia hyperintensity); MMSE 14–26 at
enrollment; sufficiently preserved swallowing and oral intake to
tolerate oral medication; informed consent provided by the subject
or, if the subject lacks capacity, by a legally authorized
representative. Exclusion criteria include known hypersensitivity to
either active, current treatment with a prohibited concomitant
medication (see § 5), severe hepatic or renal impairment, narrow-
angle glaucoma (a contraindication to trospium), urinary retention,
and gastric retention.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Cognitive decline in PrD-NCD is mediated by (a) loss of cortical
and hippocampal cholinergic tone, (b) loss of histaminergic and
noradrenergic arousal-system support, and (c) progressive synaptic
and neuronal injury caused by PrP^Sc accumulation. The present
invention addresses (a) and (b) without claiming to address (c).

Selective H3 inverse agonism by pitolisant elevates extracellular
histamine and supports state-dependent acetylcholine and dopamine
release in cortex, hippocampus, and dorsal striatum, with documented
clinical effect on psychomotor vigilance in narcolepsy populations.
This addresses the arousal-system contribution to cognitive
impairment.

M1/M4 muscarinic agonism by xanomeline directly facilitates
post-synaptic cholinergic signaling in cortex and hippocampus,
independent of endogenous acetylcholine availability, and is
therefore expected to retain efficacy in the setting of advanced
cholinergic neuronal loss where acetylcholinesterase inhibition has
diminished pharmacodynamic substrate. The trospium component
mitigates peripheral cholinergic adverse effects without antagonizing
the central muscarinic effect.

The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge
on the cortical–hippocampal cognitive circuit. Co-administration is
therefore expected to produce greater-than-additive palliative
benefit on cognition and arousal in PrD-NCD. The taxonomy-aware
receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 28,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding (³H-N-α-methylhistamine
displacement on rat cortical membranes; Ki for pitolisant
≈ 1.5 nM); H3 functional inverse agonism (cAMP accumulation in
CHO-K1 cells expressing human H3); M1 receptor binding (³H-NMS
displacement on human M1-CHO membranes); M1 functional agonism
(calcium mobilization in M1-CHO cells; EC₅₀ for xanomeline
≈ 100 nM); M4 binding and functional agonism in M4-CHO cells.

In-vivo behavioral paradigms useful for confirming the combination's
effect on the cortical–hippocampal cognitive substrate, in
non-prion rodent models, include: the Morris water maze; novel
object recognition; spatial alternation in T-maze; and 5-CSRTT for
sustained attention. In rodent models of cholinergic deficit (e.g.,
scopolamine challenge, basal forebrain lesion), a supra-additive
effect of the combination on Morris-water-maze platform-finding
latency, novel-object recognition discrimination index, and
5-CSRTT discrimination accuracy, relative to either monotherapy at
any individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof
point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9. Direct experiments
in prion-infected mice (e.g., RML-prion C57BL/6 inoculation) are
optional and are intended only to confirm symptomatic effect on
locomotor and arousal endpoints in the symptomatic phase, not to
assess disease-modification.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years; equal sex
distribution). Period 1: pitolisant 17.8 mg orally once daily for
7 days; full PK sampling on days 1 and 7. Period 2 (after 7-day
washout): xanomeline-trospium 100 mg/20 mg orally twice daily for
7 days; full PK sampling for both xanomeline and trospium on days
1 and 7. Period 3 (after 7-day washout): combination at the same
doses for 7 days; PK sampling for all three actives on days 1 and
7. The primary endpoint is the geometric mean ratio for AUC₀–τ and
Cmax of pitolisant and of xanomeline in combination versus
monotherapy, with 90 % confidence intervals contained within
0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm palliative-care study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group palliative-
care study of the combination in adults with PrD-NCD (FIG. 4).
Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 90 (30 per arm) given
the rarity of the indication; analyses are descriptive and effect
estimates are reported with 90 % confidence intervals.

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant 17.8 mg/day oral +
  xanomeline-trospium 100 mg/20 mg twice daily.
* Arm B (xanomeline-trospium monotherapy): xanomeline-trospium
  100 mg/20 mg twice daily + matching oral placebo.
* Arm C (placebo): matching oral placebo + matching capsule placebo.

All subjects receive standard-of-care palliative measures including
nutritional support, anti-myoclonic therapy as needed, and
caregiver-respite resources. The primary endpoint is the difference
between arms in the rate of MMSE decline (points/month) over 12
weeks. Secondary endpoints include CDR sum-of-boxes change,
ADCS-ADL change, mRS, Zarit Burden Interview change, and CGI-I.
Survival and time-to-loss-of-independence are exploratory endpoints.

### Example 4 — Worked patient case

A 64-year-old woman with a 4-week history of progressive cognitive
decline, myoclonus, and gait ataxia, MRI cortical ribboning and

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the palliative treatment
of Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder due to Prion Disease
comprising co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount
of the histamine H3 receptor inverse agonist pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of the M1/M4-preferring muscarinic
agonist xanomeline, optionally further co-administered with the
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist trospium chloride to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects, are disclosed. The
two CNS-active agents act on non-overlapping receptor classes that
converge on the cortical–hippocampal–striatal cognitive and arousal
circuits implicated in prion-disease neurocognitive decline, and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive palliative benefit on
cognition, arousal, and caregiver burden than either agent alone at
any individually achievable dose. The invention does not assert
disease-modification of the underlying prion proteinopathy.
