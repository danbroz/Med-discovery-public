## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Nightmare Disorder by co-administering
(a) an orexin receptor antagonist, in particular suvorexant, and
(b) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with 5-HT2C
antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine. The invention is
intended for subjects with recurrent dysphoric, vivid, or
threat-laden dream episodes causing sleep disturbance and daytime
distress, and addresses REM-associated nightmare burden, nocturnal
hyperarousal, and circadian destabilization.

## Background of the invention

Nightmare Disorder is defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by repeated
occurrences of extended, extremely dysphoric, and well-remembered
dreams that usually involve efforts to avoid threats to survival,
security, or physical integrity and that generally occur during the
second half of the major sleep episode. Awakening from such dreams
is usually accompanied by rapid orientation and recall of the dream,
but the cumulative disorder causes clinically significant distress,
sleep avoidance, daytime fatigue, and impaired function.

The prevalence of recurrent nightmares is meaningful across the
lifespan and is especially high in trauma-exposed populations,
subjects with anxiety disorders, depressive disorders, and other
sleep disorders. The clinical burden includes fragmented sleep,
fear of returning to sleep, impaired daytime function, and high
comorbidity with PTSD-spectrum symptoms. Existing care is often
fragmented across behavioral and pharmacologic approaches, and there
is no FDA-approved medication specifically labeled for Nightmare
Disorder as a primary indication.

The current standard of care is largely behavioral and symptomatic.
It consists primarily of imagery rehearsal therapy, sleep hygiene,
trauma-focused treatment where appropriate, and off-label
pharmacologic strategies in selected patients. No pharmaceutical
product is approved by the United States Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) specifically for the treatment of Nightmare
Disorder as a primary indication. Many agents borrowed from
insomnia, PTSD, or sedative practice do not directly address the
combination of nocturnal hyperarousal and circadian instability
that maintains recurrent nightmares.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, nightmare recurrence is associated with abnormal
nocturnal arousal and unstable sleep-state transitions. Orexin
signaling contributes to wake maintenance and arousal threshold
control. Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved
for insomnia that facilitates sleep onset and maintenance by
reducing orexin-driven wakefulness. In the present invention, the
suvorexant axis addresses the *abnormal nocturnal arousal and
sleep-fragmentation substrate* of Nightmare Disorder.

Second, nightmare persistence is often worsened by circadian
disorganization, fragmented sleep timing, and incomplete recovery
of restorative sleep architecture. Agomelatine is a melatonergic
antidepressant that acts as a potent agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and a neutral antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors. This
pharmacologic profile supports circadian resynchronization and
subjective sleep-quality improvement. The agomelatine axis
therefore addresses the *circadian-destabilization and sleep-
timing substrate* of Nightmare Disorder.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an orexin receptor antagonist in
combination with a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist with 5-HT2C
antagonism for Nightmare Disorder specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of orexin-driven
arousal instability (a suvorexant axis) and restoration of stable
circadian entrainment (an agomelatine axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for treatment of Nightmare Disorder. The
combination is expected to produce greater reduction in nightmare
frequency, nightmare-related awakenings, and daytime distress than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) suvorexant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) agomelatine or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, co-crystal,
or prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Nightmare Disorder in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of suvorexant and a therapeutically effective amount of
agomelatine. Co-administration may be simultaneous or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) suvorexant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Nightmare
Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in
conjunction with sleep-hygiene and behavioral nightmare
interventions where indicated.

The invention contemplates suvorexant in a daily dose of about
5 mg to about 20 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a
preferred adult dose of 10 mg to 20 mg nightly. The invention
contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about 25 mg to about
50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a preferred dose of
25 mg nightly and optional escalation to 50 mg nightly after about
2 weeks if clinically indicated.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with Nightmare Disorder, in nightmare frequency, nightmare-related
awakenings, nocturnal distress, and daytime fatigue, that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of suvorexant and
agomelatine administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Suvorexant"* means the dual orexin receptor antagonist approved
for insomnia. The term includes suvorexant free base, salts,
polymorphs, solvates, hydrates, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent orexin receptor antagonists
with a clinically comparable sleep-promoting profile.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Nightmare Disorder"* means the parasomnia / sleep disorder defined
in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by repeated extended, dysphoric, and
well-remembered dreams associated with clinically meaningful
distress or impairment.

*"Nightmare-frequency score"* means a clinically meaningful count or
severity rating of nightmare episodes across a defined interval.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of Nightmare Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed
herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least
one of: nightmare-frequency score, awakening burden, nightmare-
distress score, sleep-fragmentation index, daytime fatigue, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Suvorexant

Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia. By reducing orexin-mediated wake drive, it facilitates
sleep onset and maintenance. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of suvorexant is reduction of
abnormal nocturnal arousal that contributes to awakening from
dysphoric dream episodes.

#### 2.2 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant acting as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and a neutral antagonist at
5-HT2C receptors. This profile supports circadian resynchronization
and subjective sleep-quality improvement. In the present invention,
the principal mechanistic contribution of agomelatine is
stabilization of sleep timing and reduction of circadian
vulnerability to recurrent nightmare disruption.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise suvorexant and
agomelatine, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a suvorexant tablet and an
agomelatine tablet.

Excipients useful for the suvorexant composition include tablet
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and film-coating polymers
appropriate for low-dose bedtime administration. Excipients useful
for the agomelatine composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Suvorexant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime dosing. Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral
tablet suitable for bedtime administration.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
suvorexant tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of agomelatine tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to sleep scheduling and nightmare
tracking.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with Nightmare Disorder, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Suvorexant 5 mg orally at bedtime for about 3 nights, titratable
  to 10 mg and then to 20 mg nightly if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.
* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days,
  titratable to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.

Broad genus ranges include suvorexant 5–20 mg/day and agomelatine
25–50 mg/day. The preferred schedule administers both agents at
bedtime on a consistent nightly schedule.

Hepatic adjustment: clinically meaningful hepatic impairment is
exclusionary or requires careful monitoring because agomelatine is
associated with hepatotoxicity risk in susceptible patients.

Medication–medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors. Suvorexant should be used with
caution alongside other CNS depressants. The regimen is intended to
complement, not replace, behavioral nightmare interventions where
indicated.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
Nightmare Disorder. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age ≥ 12 years; recurrent dysphoric dream
episodes causing clinically meaningful distress or impairment; and
ability to participate in sleep-hygiene and nightmare tracking.
Exclusion criteria include untreated severe sleep-disordered
breathing, uncontrolled nocturnal seizures, severe hepatic disease,
and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Nightmare Disorder is maintained by two interacting layers:
abnormal nocturnal arousal that permits dysphoric dream episodes to
terminate in awakening, and circadian / sleep-architecture
instability that increases recurrence vulnerability.

Suvorexant addresses the arousal-instability layer through orexin
antagonism. Agomelatine addresses the circadian-vulnerability layer
through MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. The two axes are
non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the
nightmare-frequency phenotype.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: orexin receptor binding and antagonism assays
for suvorexant; MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays for
agomelatine; and combined sleep-state transition assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include sleep-fragmentation models, stress-dream / arousal models,
circadian-shift paradigms, and nightmare-relevant dysphoric-
arousal paradigms where validated. A supra-additive effect of the
combination on reduction of dysphoric awakening burden and sleep
fragmentation, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
suvorexant 10 mg nightly for 3 days; Period 2: agomelatine 25 mg
nightly for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, liver-function monitoring, and next-day sedation /
tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal
purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of clinically
meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in Nightmare Disorder (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): suvorexant + agomelatine.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): suvorexant + placebo for
  agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard behavioral
  nightmare management.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in nightmare-frequency
score at week 8. Secondary endpoints include awakening burden,
nightmare-distress score, sleep-fragmentation index, daytime
fatigue, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Nightmare Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the orexin antagonist
suvorexant and a therapeutically effective amount of the
melatonergic antidepressant agomelatine are disclosed. The two
agents act on non-overlapping nocturnal-arousal and circadian-
stability circuits implicated in recurrent dysphoric dream
episodes and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
symptomatic improvement in nightmare frequency, awakening burden,
and daytime fatigue than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.
