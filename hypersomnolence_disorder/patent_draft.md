## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Hypersomnolence Disorder by co-administering
(a) a histamine H3 receptor antagonist or inverse agonist, in
particular pitolisant, and (b) an orexin receptor agonist, in
particular Nag 26 or a pharmacologically equivalent OX2-preferring
orexin agonist. The invention is intended for subjects with
pathologic excessive sleepiness, prolonged sleep inertia, and
impaired daytime wakefulness, and addresses deficient arousal
support through two non-overlapping wake-promoting mechanisms. The
invention is not intended to encourage unsupervised use of research
compounds outside lawful specialist development pathways.

## Background of the invention

Hypersomnolence Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
condition of recurrent episodes of irrepressible need to sleep,
recurrent lapses into sleep within the same day, or prolonged main
sleep episode with persistent nonrestorative sleepiness despite
apparently adequate sleep duration. Subjects may experience long
sleep times, severe morning sleep inertia, cognitive fog, and major
functional impairment in school, work, and driving safety.

The prevalence of clinically meaningful hypersomnolence is lower
than that of insomnia but the burden is substantial. Subjects often
experience inability to sustain wakefulness, poor academic or
occupational performance, social withdrawal, mislabeling as
depression or laziness, and high treatment frustration. Compared to
narcolepsy, some subjects do not have cataplexy or REM-intrusion
syndromes but nonetheless have disabling excessive sleepiness.

The current standard of care is wake-promoting and empiric. It
consists primarily of stimulants, wake-promoting agents, sleep
schedule optimization, and treatment of comorbid sleep disorders.
No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for the broad syndrome of
Hypersomnolence Disorder as a primary indication. Existing agents
often fail to fully restore morning wakefulness or leave residual
sleep inertia despite improving subjective alertness.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses is the
absence of a defensible, mechanism-based regimen that combines
enhanced histaminergic wake support with direct orexin-agonist
replacement in a disorder of insufficient daytime arousal.

The present invention recognizes that two non-overlapping wake-
promoting axes can be combined to support daytime function. First,
the histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic auto- and heteroreceptor
that suppresses histamine release and modulates acetylcholine,
dopamine, and norepinephrine transmission. Selective inverse
agonism at H3 by pitolisant elevates extracellular histamine and
improves wakefulness without conventional stimulant scheduling. The
pitolisant axis therefore addresses the *histaminergic vigilance
and wake maintenance substrate* of Hypersomnolence Disorder.

Second, orexin signaling is central to maintenance of wakefulness
and stability of arousal state. Nag 26 is a research-compound label
used herein to denote a selective non-peptide orexin (OX2)
receptor agonist class member with wake-promoting activity. Orexin-
agonist pharmacology is mechanistically attractive for hypersomnia
because it directly augments the deficient arousal axis rather than
only disinhibiting downstream monoamines. The orexin-agonist axis
therefore addresses the *core orexinergic wake-drive insufficiency*
substrate of Hypersomnolence Disorder.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an H3 antagonist or inverse agonist
in combination with an orexin agonist for Hypersomnolence Disorder
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that histaminergic wake support (a
pitolisant H3 axis) and direct orexinergic wake promotion (a Nag 26
or equivalent OX2 agonist axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of Hypersomnolence Disorder. The
combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement
in excessive daytime sleepiness, wake maintenance, and sleep
inertia than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) Nag 26 or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, prodrug, or
pharmacologically equivalent OX2-preferring orexin agonist
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Hypersomnolence Disorder in a human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of pitolisant and a therapeutically effective
amount of Nag 26 or a pharmacologically equivalent OX2-preferring
orexin agonist. Co-administration may be simultaneous or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) Nag 26 or a pharmacologically equivalent OX2-preferring orexin
agonist in a second unitary dosage form, and (c) written
instructions for the treatment of Hypersomnolence Disorder
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily. The
invention contemplates Nag 26 or equivalent OX2 agonist in a daily
dose of about 0.1 mg to about 5 mg administered orally or by
another clinically appropriate route, with a preferred adult dose
range of about 0.3 mg to about 3 mg/day. The inventive feature is
the concurrent engagement of histaminergic wake support and direct
orexin agonism in a hypersomnolence syndrome.

The technical effect of the combination is improvement, in subjects
with Hypersomnolence Disorder, in daytime wakefulness, maintenance
of alertness, sleep inertia, and functional performance, greater
than would be obtained with either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse
agonist marketed as Wakix®. The term includes pitolisant free base,
hydrochloride or other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent H3 inverse agonists.

*"Nag 26"* means a selective non-peptide orexin (OX2) receptor
agonist research compound label. The term includes the named
research compound, its pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent OX2-preferring orexin
agonists that retain wake-promoting activity within the genus
defined herein.

*"Hypersomnolence Disorder"* means the disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by persistent excessive sleepiness, recurrent
sleep episodes, or prolonged nonrestorative sleep associated with
clinically meaningful impairment.

*"Daytime-sleepiness score"* means a clinically meaningful burden
of excessive daytime sleepiness, sleep attacks, or sleep inertia
quantified by structured clinician assessment or validated
instruments such as the Epworth Sleepiness Scale.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of Hypersomnolence Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed
herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least
one of: daytime-sleepiness score, maintenance of wakefulness,
sleep inertia score, functional performance, responder rate, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse agonist
that increases histaminergic wake drive and supports state-
dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of pitolisant is
attenuation of excessive daytime sleepiness and support of sustained
wakefulness.

#### 2.2 Nag 26

Nag 26 is a selective non-peptide orexin (OX2) receptor agonist
research compound label used in the present invention as a member of
an orexin-agonist wake-promoting class. In the present invention,
the principal mechanistic contribution of the Nag 26 axis is direct
orexinergic support of wakefulness and reduction of sleep inertia.

#### 2.3 Materials and sourcing posture

Because Nag 26 is a research-compound label with no established
commercial supply chain, material for the present invention may be
obtained by one or more of: (a) license or transfer from the
current rights holder or development sponsor; (b) GMP custom
synthesis of Nag 26 or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof;
(c) use of retained lawful development inventory; or (d)
substitution with a pharmacologically equivalent OX2-preferring
orexin agonist within the genus defined herein. The present
specification is drafted to support any of those lawful supply
pathways.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
Nag 26 or an equivalent OX2 agonist, together with at least one
pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The compositions may take
the form of a co-packaged kit of a pitolisant tablet and an oral
research-formulation or development-formulation orexin-agonist unit.

Excipients useful for the pitolisant composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the Nag 26
component include oral solid or liquid development-formulation
carriers, stabilizers, and excipients suitable for GMP custom
synthesis output or licensed development inventory.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
morning administration. Nag 26 is preferably formulated as an oral
research or development dosage form suitable for controlled
specialist dosing.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pitolisant tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of Nag 26 or equivalent
OX2 agonist units, and (c) written instructions aligned to daytime
wake-promoting use, sleep schedule optimization, and safety
monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with Hypersomnolence Disorder, the preferred
dosing regimen is:

* Pitolisant 4.5 mg orally once daily, titratable to 17.8 mg and
  then to 35.6 mg once daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.
* Nag 26 or equivalent OX2 agonist 0.1 mg to 5 mg daily, preferably
  in the morning or early wake period, with a preferred range of
  0.3 mg to 3 mg/day under specialist supervision.

The preferred embodiment is specialist-supervised because Nag 26 is
a research-compound label with no routine clinical safety database.
The regimen is not intended to replace evaluation for sleep apnea,
circadian disorder, narcolepsy, medication effects, or other
reversible contributors to hypersomnolence.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with Hypersomnolence Disorder. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: age >= 18 years; persistent excessive
daytime sleepiness or prolonged sleep inertia; clinically
meaningful functional impairment; and ability to participate in
specialist monitoring. Exclusion criteria include untreated severe
sleep-disordered breathing, uncontrolled cardiovascular disease,
active psychosis or mania worsened by wake promotion, and known
hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Hypersomnolence Disorder often includes two interacting layers:
insufficient histaminergic vigilance support and insufficient
orexinergic wake-drive.

Pitolisant addresses the histaminergic vigilance layer through H3
antagonism / inverse agonism. Nag 26 addresses the orexinergic
wake-drive layer through OX2 agonism. The two axes are non-
overlapping at the receptor level and converge on improved
wakefulness and reduced sleep inertia.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in hypersomnolence burden than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 35,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding and inverse-agonism assays
for pitolisant; OX2 receptor binding and agonism assays for Nag 26
or equivalent class members; and combined wake-maintenance network
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include excessive-sleepiness models, maintenance-of-wakefulness
tasks, sleep-inertia paradigms, and circadian-wake support assays.
A supra-additive effect of the combination on restoration of
wakefulness, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in Section 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
pitolisant 17.8 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: low-dose Nag 26 or
equivalent OX2 agonist exposure under specialist monitoring for
7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK sampling,
cardiovascular monitoring, sleepiness assessment, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained. The principal purpose is to establish
tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult Hypersomnolence Disorder (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant + Nag 26 or equivalent OX2
  agonist.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pitolisant + placebo for the orexin
  agonist.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in Epworth Sleepiness
Scale total at week 8. Secondary endpoints include maintenance of
wakefulness, sleep inertia score, functional performance, responder
rate, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of an H3 antagonist or inverse
agonist with an orexin agonist for Hypersomnolence Disorder
specifically. The closest hits are:

* Existing hypersomnolence patents disclose wake-promoting methods
  or class-specific approaches and do not teach the present dual-
  arousal combination.
* Generic orexin receptor chemistry and antagonist patents do not
  disclose the present wake-promoting orexin-agonist substitution
  framework.
* Generic pitolisant formulation patents disclose the active but
  not the present low-FTO orexin-agonist combination rationale.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (H3 antagonism /
inverse agonism + OX2 agonism), (ii) the specific actives
(pitolisant + Nag 26 or equivalent genus member), and (iii) the
specific indication (Hypersomnolence Disorder). No prior-art
disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on which the
present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Hypersomnolence Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of Nag 26 or a pharmacologically
equivalent OX2-preferring orexin agonist are disclosed. The two
agents act on non-overlapping histaminergic and orexinergic arousal
circuits and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in daytime sleepiness, wake maintenance, and sleep
inertia than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.
