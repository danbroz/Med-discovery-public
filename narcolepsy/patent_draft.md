## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Narcolepsy by co-administering (a) a histamine H3
receptor antagonist or inverse agonist and (b) an orexin receptor
agonist, in particular the combination of pitolisant and Nag 26 or a
pharmacologically equivalent OX2-preferring orexin agonist, in dosage
forms and on dosing schedules selected to improve daytime wakefulness,
reduce REM-intrusion symptoms, and decrease cataplexy burden without
reliance on stimulant escalation.

## Background of the invention

Narcolepsy is a chronic neurologic sleep-wake disorder characterized
by excessive daytime sleepiness, impaired maintenance of wakefulness,
sleep attacks, and in many subjects cataplexy, hypnagogic
hallucinations, sleep paralysis, and fragmented nocturnal sleep.
Narcolepsy type 1 is strongly associated with orexin deficiency,
whereas narcolepsy type 2 may present with less complete orexin loss
but overlapping wake-state instability. The disorder produces marked
functional impairment in school, work, driving safety, and quality of
life.

The current treatment landscape remains incomplete. Sodium oxybate and
low-sodium oxybate products can improve sleepiness and cataplexy but
require nighttime dosing, carry misuse-control burdens, and are not
appropriate for all subjects. Stimulants and wake-promoting agents may
improve sleepiness yet often leave residual REM-intrusion symptoms or
cataplexy untreated. Pitolisant is approved for excessive daytime
sleepiness and cataplexy in narcolepsy, but clinically meaningful
residual symptoms remain common even with optimized H3 inverse
agonism. There remains an unmet need for a combination regimen that
addresses both histaminergic vigilance support and the orexin deficit
that underlies core narcolepsy pathophysiology.

The mechanistic gap addressed by the present invention has two layers.
First, pitolisant increases histaminergic tone through H3 receptor
inverse agonism / antagonism and thereby improves cortical arousal,
state stability, and maintenance of wakefulness. Second, an orexin
agonist directly addresses the deficient orexinergic wake-drive that
defines narcolepsy, particularly narcolepsy type 1. The inventors
recognized that these two axes are pharmacologically non-overlapping
yet circuit-convergent: the H3 axis amplifies endogenous arousal
networks, while the orexin-agonist axis restores the upstream wake
drive that stabilizes transitions between sleep and wake.

Prior-art searching identified disclosures directed to orexin-pathway
macrocycles and piperidines, disclosures directed to histamine H3
inverse agonism, and older wake-promoting approaches such as
reboxetine for narcolepsy. However, no issued patent or published
application known to the inventors teaches the specific combination of
pitolisant with Nag 26 or another OX2-preferring orexin agonist for
the treatment of Narcolepsy, nor the dosing relationships, kit
configurations, and patient-selection criteria disclosed herein.

The present invention further recognizes that a combination regimen
using pitolisant with a research-stage orexin agonist should be
drafted with explicit lawful supply-chain language and with support
for equivalent OX2-preferring agonists within the same mechanistic
genus. This is both a practical development requirement and an
important written-description feature for later non-provisional
claiming.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) Nag 26 or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, stereoisomer, polymorph,
or prodrug thereof, or a pharmacologically equivalent OX2-preferring
orexin agonist, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Narcolepsy in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering a therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant and
a therapeutically effective amount of Nag 26 or an equivalent
OX2-preferring orexin agonist. Co-administration may be simultaneous
or sequential, provided that the two actives are administered within a
24-hour period and are titrated to provide overlapping wake-promoting
exposure during the subject's planned wake interval.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising (a)
pitolisant in a first dosage form, (b) Nag 26 or an equivalent
OX2-preferring orexin agonist in a second dosage form, and
(c) written instructions for morning or early-wake administration,
avoidance of late-day dosing that may impair nocturnal sleep, and
monitoring of sleepiness, cataplexy frequency, and REM-intrusion
symptoms.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg, preferably about 8.9 mg to about 35.6 mg,
administered orally once daily in the morning. The invention
contemplates Nag 26 or an equivalent OX2-preferring orexin agonist in
a daily dose of about 0.1 mg to about 5 mg, preferably about 0.3 mg
to about 3 mg, administered once daily or in divided daytime doses
under specialist supervision.

The technical effect of the combination is expected to be greater
than additive improvement in at least one endpoint selected from the
group consisting of Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS), Maintenance of
Wakefulness Test (MWT), weekly cataplexy rate, sleep paralysis
frequency, hypnagogic hallucination burden, Clinical Global
Impression-Improvement (CGI-I), and patient-reported functional
alertness, compared with either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Narcolepsy"* means narcolepsy type 1, narcolepsy type 2, or another
clinically recognized narcolepsy-spectrum disorder characterized by
excessive daytime sleepiness and instability of wake-state control,
with or without cataplexy.

*"Pitolisant"* means 1-{3-[3-(4-chlorophenyl)propoxy]propyl}piperidine
and pharmaceutically acceptable salts, especially the hydrochloride
salt, together with solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, and
metabolically equivalent forms that retain H3 inverse agonist /
antagonist activity.

*"Nag 26"* means the non-peptide research-compound label used herein as
an OX2-preferring orexin receptor agonist and includes salts,
solvates, stereoisomers, polymorphs, prodrugs, labeled forms, and
closely related analogues that retain therapeutically relevant OX2
agonism.

*"Equivalent OX2-preferring orexin agonist"* means a compound other
than Nag 26 that exhibits orexin receptor agonist activity with a
preference for OX2 signaling sufficient to improve wake stability in a
narcolepsy subject, including later-developed clinical candidates
within the same pharmacologic genus.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount that produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in one or more wakefulness or
REM-intrusion endpoints when used according to a regimen disclosed
herein.

### 2. Active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a selective histamine H3 receptor inverse agonist /
antagonist that increases histaminergic tone and supports
state-dependent release of acetylcholine, dopamine, and
norepinephrine. In the present invention, pitolisant provides a
vigilance-support and wake-maintenance axis that reduces daytime
sleepiness and helps stabilize wakefulness.

#### 2.2 Nag 26

Nag 26 is used herein as a selective OX2-preferring orexin receptor
agonist research compound label. In the present invention, the Nag 26
axis provides direct orexinergic wake-drive replacement or support,
thereby addressing the orexin-deficient substrate of narcolepsy more
directly than histaminergic amplification alone.

#### 2.3 Materials and sourcing posture

Because Nag 26 is a research-compound label without an established
routine commercial supply chain, material for the present invention
may be obtained by one or more lawful pathways including: (a) license,
assignment, or transfer from a current rights holder, sponsor, or
development organization; (b) GMP custom synthesis of Nag 26 or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof; (c) use of retained lawful
development inventory; or (d) substitution with a pharmacologically
equivalent OX2-preferring orexin agonist within the mechanistic genus
defined herein. The present specification is intentionally drafted to
support each of those pathways.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and Nag
26 or an equivalent OX2-preferring orexin agonist together with one or
more pharmaceutically acceptable excipients. The actives may be
formulated as separate dosage forms, as a co-packaged kit, or in a
unitary modified-release oral dosage form when compatible stability
and pharmacokinetic profiles permit.

Suitable excipients for pitolisant include diluents, binders,
disintegrants, glidants, lubricants, coating polymers, and pH
modifiers useful in oral tablet formulation. Suitable excipients for
the Nag 26 component include oral solid-formulation carriers,
development-formulation stabilizers, solubilizers, and capsule-fill
vehicles compatible with custom-synthesized or development-inventory
material.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as an immediate-release oral
tablet for once-daily morning administration. Nag 26 is preferably
formulated as an oral capsule, tablet, sachet, suspension, or other
development-formulation dosage form suitable for specialist-managed
titration.

A kit form of the invention comprises: (a) a 30-day supply of
pitolisant tablets; (b) a 30-day supply of Nag 26 or an equivalent
OX2-preferring orexin agonist units; and (c) written instructions
aligned to wake-period dosing, driving-safety counseling, symptom
tracking, and nocturnal-sleep preservation.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with Narcolepsy, a preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pitolisant 4.5 mg orally once daily, titratable to 8.9 mg,
  17.8 mg, and then 35.6 mg once daily if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.
* Nag 26 or equivalent OX2-preferring orexin agonist 0.1 mg to 5 mg
  per day, preferably 0.3 mg to 3 mg per day, given in the morning or
  early wake period.

In certain embodiments, the orexin-agonist component is administered
30 to 120 minutes before the time window in which the subject most
frequently experiences sleep attacks or difficulty maintaining
wakefulness. In other embodiments, the pitolisant is titrated first
and the orexin-agonist component is layered in after baseline
tolerability is established.

Late-day dosing that materially disrupts nocturnal sleep is generally
avoided. The preferred regimen is specialist-supervised because Nag 26
is a research-compound label with no routine clinical safety database.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adults and, in
some embodiments, adolescents with clinically confirmed Narcolepsy.
Preferred subjects have persistent excessive daytime sleepiness
despite behavioral optimization or inadequate control with a single
wake-promoting agent.

In certain embodiments the subject has narcolepsy type 1 with
cataplexy. In certain embodiments the subject has narcolepsy type 2
without cataplexy but with severe daytime functional impairment.

Exclusion criteria for preferred embodiments include untreated severe
sleep-disordered breathing, unstable cardiovascular disease,
uncontrolled mania or psychosis worsened by wake-promotion,
pregnancy when the orexin-agonist safety database is incomplete, and
inability to participate in specialist monitoring.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Narcolepsy represents a convergence of impaired upstream orexinergic
drive and unstable downstream vigilance circuitry. Pitolisant improves
histaminergic tone and widens the pharmacologic support of cortical
wake-state maintenance. Nag 26 or an equivalent OX2-preferring orexin
agonist restores or mimics the orexin signal that normally stabilizes
wakefulness and suppresses pathologic REM-state intrusion into wake.

The two mechanisms are complementary rather than redundant. Pitolisant
amplifies endogenous arousal output; the orexin agonist addresses the
deficient command signal that drives the network. Their convergence is
expected to reduce daytime sleepiness more effectively than either
monotherapy while also improving cataplexy and other REM-intrusion
symptoms that are incompletely addressed by H3 antagonism alone.

### 8. Administration timing and monitoring

The combination may be administered simultaneously or sequentially,
provided that the wake-support profiles overlap during the subject's
planned daytime activity period. Monitoring may include one or more of
ESS, MWT, weekly cataplexy rate, patient diary of sleep attacks, sleep
paralysis episodes, hypnagogic hallucinations, clinician-rated CGI-I,
and functional measures such as driving confidence or school / work
attendance.

The regimen is not intended to replace adequate nocturnal sleep,
scheduled naps when clinically appropriate, evaluation for coexisting
sleep apnea or circadian disorder, or standard safety counseling for
sleep-related driving risk.

### 9. Prophetic clinical study

A prophetic Phase 2 study enrolls adults with confirmed Narcolepsy and
baseline ESS >= 11. Subjects are randomized to: (a) pitolisant plus
Nag 26-equivalent OX2 agonist; (b) pitolisant plus placebo; or
(c) Nag 26-equivalent OX2 agonist plus placebo, for 8 weeks after a
2-week titration period.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in MWT mean sleep
latency. Key secondary endpoints include ESS, weekly cataplexy rate,
REM-intrusion composite score, Patient Global Impression-Improvement,
and CGI-I. The combination arm is expected to outperform each
monotherapy arm on at least MWT and ESS and, in narcolepsy type 1
subjects, on weekly cataplexy rate. FIGS. 3-5 are prophetic
illustrations of those expected outcomes.

### 10. Prior-art distinction

The closest prior-art references identified in the prior-art landscape
include:

* *Substituted piperidino compounds and related methods of treatment*
  and related macrocyclic orexin-pathway disclosures - these describe
  orexin-pathway chemistry but do not teach the specific combination
  of pitolisant with Nag 26 or an equivalent OX2 agonist in
  Narcolepsy.
* *Method of treatment with histamine-3 receptor inverse agonist* -
  discloses H3 inverse agonism, but does not teach orexin-agonist
  co-administration.
* *Use of reboxetine to treat narcolepsy* - discloses a different
  wake-promoting monotherapy and does not teach either pitolisant or
  an orexin agonist.

The present invention is distinct in the specific receptor classes
combined, the specific actives and genus language employed, and the
specialist-managed dosing relationships used to address residual
sleepiness and REM-intrusion symptoms in Narcolepsy.

sleepiness on pitolisant 17.8 mg/day, and weekly cataplexy despite

morning, titrated to 1 mg/day after one week. After 6 weeks the
subject reports fewer sleep attacks, improved afternoon alertness, and
reduced cataplexy frequency, with CGI-I rated 2 ("much improved").
PROPHETIC.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Narcolepsy comprising co-administration of a therapeutically effective
amount of the histamine H3 receptor inverse agonist / antagonist
pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount of the
OX2-preferring orexin agonist Nag 26, or a pharmacologically
equivalent orexin agonist, are disclosed. The two agents act on
non-overlapping receptor classes that converge on wake-stability
circuits implicated in Narcolepsy and are expected to produce
greater-than-additive improvement in daytime wakefulness and
REM-intrusion symptoms, including cataplexy, compared with either
agent alone at any individually achievable dose. The specification
further supports lawful sourcing and equivalent-genus substitution of
the orexin-agonist component.
