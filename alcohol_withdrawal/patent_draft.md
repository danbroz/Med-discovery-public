## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Alcohol Withdrawal by co-administering
(a) a cannabinoid CB1 receptor agonist, in particular
delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (Δ9-THC), and (b) a beta-adrenoceptor
antagonist, in particular betaxolol. The invention is directed to
selected adult subjects in medically supervised alcohol-withdrawal
management and addresses autonomic hyperarousal, insomnia, anorexia,
nausea, dysphoria, and symptom-triggered rescue burden after seizure-
and delirium-risk triage. The invention is adjunctive and is not
intended to replace benzodiazepines, phenobarbital, ICU-level care, or
treatment of delirium tremens.

## Background of the invention

Alcohol Withdrawal is the clinically significant syndrome that develops
after cessation or marked reduction of heavy and prolonged alcohol
use. The syndrome may include tremor, anxiety, insomnia, diaphoresis,
tachycardia, nausea, anorexia, agitation, perceptual disturbance,
seizures, and delirium tremens. The clinical burden ranges from mild
autonomic and affective distress to life-threatening complicated
withdrawal requiring intensive monitoring.

Real-world care for alcohol withdrawal is dominated by syndrome-
defining supervised detoxification protocols. Benzodiazepines remain
standard treatment for seizure and delirium-tremens prevention, with
phenobarbital, dexmedetomidine, clonidine, and other adjuncts used in
selected settings. However, even when high-risk GABAergic rescue is
available, a meaningful subset of patients continues to experience
residual insomnia, anorexia, nausea, dysphoria, tremor, tachycardia,
and treatment dropout risk. The workbook used in this project
explicitly under-represents detox protocols and monitoring workflows,
so the present invention is framed as adjunctive to, not competitive
with, evidence-based withdrawal management.

The mechanistic scaffold for alcohol withdrawal involves at least two
clinical layers relevant after initial risk stratification. One layer
is hyperadrenergic and autonomic, manifesting as tachycardia, tremor,
sweating, internal agitation, and somatic distress. Another layer is
reward-deficit and recovery-failure, manifesting as insomnia,
anorexia, nausea, dysphoria, and a strong drive to self-medicate.
Standard benzodiazepine protocols are essential for seizure-risk
management, but they do not always fully resolve these residual
symptom clusters and may not optimize appetite, subjective sleep
restoration, or continued retention in supervised care.

The mechanistic gap addressed by the present invention has two layers.
First, delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol, available clinically as oral
dronabinol, is a CB1 receptor agonist with known effects on appetite,
nausea, sleep, and affective tone. Although not a standard alcohol-
withdrawal medicine, a CB1 agonist offers a plausible route to
reducing dysphoria, poor intake, insomnia, and aversive internal
distress during the supervised withdrawal interval. Second, betaxolol
is a beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist that reduces heart
rate, adrenergic surge, and somatic autonomic distress.

The inventors recognized that these mechanisms are complementary in
selected medically supervised alcohol-withdrawal settings. The CB1
axis addresses reward-deficit, nausea, appetite loss, and sleep-
recovery failure. The beta1-antagonist axis addresses tremor,
tachycardia, and adrenergic overactivation. The combination is
therefore expected to produce greater improvement in mild-to-moderate,
autonomic-dominant, non-delirious alcohol-withdrawal symptom burden
than either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, provided
that standard seizure-risk management remains available.

Prior-art searching identified general alcohol-dependency treatment
references, alcohol-resistant formulation references, cannabinoid
formulation references, and generic withdrawal patents. Those
references do not teach the specific combination of delta-9-THC and
betaxolol for Alcohol Withdrawal, nor the narrow adjunctive framing
disclosed herein for symptom-dominant, medically supervised adult
withdrawal after seizure / delirium triage.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, prodrug, isomeric formulation, or
clinically equivalent CB1 agonist thereof and (b) betaxolol or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Alcohol Withdrawal in a selected human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering a therapeutically effective amount of
delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol and a therapeutically effective amount of
betaxolol during medically supervised withdrawal management after
initial assessment for seizure and delirium-tremens risk.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol in a first dosage form,
(b) betaxolol in a second dosage form, and (c) written instructions
for adjunctive use in medically supervised alcohol-withdrawal
management, including hemodynamic monitoring, rescue-protocol
availability, and exclusions for delirium tremens or uncontrolled
withdrawal seizures.

The invention contemplates delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol in a daily
dose of about 2.5 mg to about 60 mg administered orally in divided
doses, with a preferred dose range of about 5 mg to about 30 mg/day in
the acute withdrawal interval. The invention contemplates betaxolol in
a daily dose of about 2.5 mg to about 20 mg administered orally once
or twice daily, with a preferred dose of about 5 mg to about 10 mg/day.

The technical effect of the combination is expected to be greater than
additive improvement in one or more endpoints selected from the group
consisting of composite withdrawal severity, tachycardia burden,
tremor burden, sleep quality, appetite restoration, nausea burden,
benzodiazepine rescue burden, and Clinical Global Impression-
Improvement (CGI-I), compared with either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose in the selected supervised population.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol"* or *"Δ9-THC"* means the principal
psychoactive phytocannabinoid acting as a CB1 receptor agonist,
including synthetic dronabinol, oral formulations, and
pharmaceutically acceptable equivalents that reproduce clinically
relevant CB1 agonism.

*"Betaxolol"* means the beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist
approved in oral and ophthalmic formulations. The term includes
betaxolol free base, hydrochloride or other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, and prodrugs.

*"Alcohol Withdrawal"* means the withdrawal syndrome recognized in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 after cessation or marked reduction of heavy and
prolonged alcohol use, characterized by a clinically meaningful
combination of autonomic, affective, sleep, gastrointestinal, or
neurologic symptoms.

*"Selected supervised alcohol-withdrawal population"* means adult
subjects in medically supervised withdrawal management whose seizure
and delirium-tremens risk has been triaged and for whom adjunctive
management of autonomic distress, poor intake, insomnia, or dysphoria
is clinically appropriate.

*"Composite withdrawal severity"* means a clinician-rated or subject-
rated multi-domain score capturing tremor, autonomic arousal, sleep
disturbance, nausea, anorexia, agitation, and subjective distress,
including but not limited to CIWA-Ar-derived or study-specific
multi-domain composites.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a selected supervised alcohol-
withdrawal subject according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
composite withdrawal severity, tachycardia burden, tremor burden,
sleep score, appetite restoration, nausea burden, benzodiazepine
rescue burden, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject so
that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the subject
during the period in which the pharmacological effect of the other
agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two agents are
administered in the same dosage form, in separate dosage forms, at the
same time, or at different times within a window of about 24 hours.

### 2. Active agents

#### 2.1 Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol

Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol is a CB1 receptor agonist that modulates
appetite, nausea, reward physiology, affective tone, and sleep.
Clinically, oral synthetic delta-9-THC is available as dronabinol. In
the present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
delta-9-THC is support of appetite, nausea reduction, dysphoria
attenuation, and sleep recovery during the acute supervised withdrawal
window.

#### 2.2 Betaxolol

Betaxolol is a beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist used in
the treatment of hypertension and glaucoma. In oral use, betaxolol
reduces heart rate, adrenergic drive, and sympathetic symptoms. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
betaxolol is reduction of withdrawal-associated autonomic
hyperarousal, tremor-linked adrenergic activation, and internal
agitation.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol and betaxolol, together with at least one
pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the
form of a co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as an
oral dronabinol capsule / solution and a betaxolol oral tablet.

Excipients useful for the delta-9-THC composition include oral
solution vehicles, gelatin capsule excipients, lipid carriers, and
stabilizers suitable for controlled-substance pharmaceutical
handling. Excipients useful for the betaxolol composition include
diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers
suitable for oral tablet manufacture.

### 4. Dosage forms

Delta-9-THC is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or oral
solution suitable for divided dosing in a medically supervised setting.
Inhaled formulations are not preferred because they complicate dose
standardization and may mimic the route associated with use behavior.
Betaxolol is preferably formulated as an immediate-release oral tablet.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 5-day to 7-day supply of
oral delta-9-THC dosage units, (b) a 5-day to 7-day supply of
betaxolol tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to medically
supervised withdrawal management, hemodynamic monitoring, and
availability of symptom-triggered benzodiazepine or phenobarbital
rescue when clinically indicated.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with selected supervised Alcohol Withdrawal, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Delta-9-THC (e.g., oral dronabinol equivalent) 2.5 mg to 10 mg
  orally two to four times daily on days 1 to 5, titratable within a
  broad daily range of about 2.5 mg to about 60 mg according to
  appetite, nausea, sleep, dysphoria, and psychoactive tolerability.
* Betaxolol 2.5 mg to 10 mg orally once daily on day 1, titratable to
  about 20 mg/day according to heart rate, blood pressure, and
  tolerability.

The regimen is preferably limited to the acute withdrawal period
(for example, about 3 to 7 days) and administered only in a medically
supervised setting. In preferred embodiments the regimen is adjunctive
to a symptom-triggered benzodiazepine or phenobarbital protocol rather
than a substitute for it.

Hepatic adjustment: severe hepatic impairment, unstable severe alcohol-
related hepatitis, or decompensated liver failure is exclusionary.
Bradycardia, significant hypotension, decompensated heart failure, or
clinically relevant conduction abnormality are contraindications to
betaxolol use in this indication.

Medication-medication interactions: avoid co-administration of
delta-9-THC with other strong CNS depressants unless medically
necessary and supervised. Use caution with other beta blockers,
calcium-channel blockers, or QT-prolonging agents.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
presenting with clinically meaningful Alcohol Withdrawal after
cessation or marked reduction of heavy and prolonged alcohol use.
Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population include:
age ≥ 18 years; medically supervised withdrawal setting; mild-to-
moderate or autonomic-dominant alcohol-withdrawal symptom burden after
initial seizure / delirium triage; and availability of hemodynamic and
mental-status monitoring.

Exclusion criteria include active delirium tremens, uncontrolled
withdrawal seizures, need for ICU-level sedative infusion as the
primary intervention, severe psychosis or severe cannabinoid
sensitivity, marked bradycardia, severe hypotension, unstable
cardiovascular disease, pregnancy, and inability to comply with
supervised treatment.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Alcohol Withdrawal is fundamentally a state of reduced inhibitory
buffering, glutamatergic rebound, stress-system recruitment, and
hyperadrenergic physiology after alcohol cessation. Standard
benzodiazepine-based rescue addresses seizure and delirium risk, but a
selected subset of medically supervised patients continues to
experience residual autonomic distress, poor appetite, nausea,
insomnia, and dysphoria that complicate completion of detoxification.

Delta-9-THC addresses the reward-deficit and recovery-failure layer by
supporting appetite, nausea control, sleep, and affective tone.
Betaxolol addresses the autonomic layer through beta1-selective
reduction of sympathetic overdrive. The two axes are non-overlapping
at the receptor level and converge on the residual distress phenotype
of selected supervised alcohol withdrawal.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in autonomic-dominant and recovery-failure symptom
for this corrected combination, derived from the alcohol-withdrawal
taxonomy-aware pairwise shortlist, is 31, indicating mechanistic
compatibility above the reporting threshold for this indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: CB1 receptor binding and functional agonism assays
for delta-9-THC; beta1-adrenoceptor binding and antagonism assays for
betaxolol; and combined autonomic-signaling or stress-response assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include alcohol-withdrawal models, tremor / autonomic-activation
paradigms, sleep-disruption assays, intake suppression paradigms, and
withdrawal-distress models after ethanol cessation. A supra-additive
effect of the combination on autonomic distress, sleep disruption, and
intake restoration, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, supervised study in 24 healthy adult
volunteers or carefully screened medically stable volunteers. Period 1:
oral delta-9-THC equivalent 5 mg three times daily for 3 days;
Period 2: betaxolol 5 mg once daily for 3 days; Period 3: the
combination at the same doses for 3 days. Full PK sampling,
heart-rate and blood-pressure monitoring, orthostasis assessment, and
CNS tolerability assessments are obtained in each period. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK or cardiovascular interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of the
combination in selected medically supervised adult Alcohol Withdrawal
(FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120
(40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): delta-9-THC + betaxolol, together with
  symptom-triggered standard rescue care.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): delta-9-THC + placebo for betaxolol,
  together with symptom-triggered standard rescue care.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, together with symptom-triggered
  standard rescue care.

All subjects remain in medically supervised withdrawal management. The
primary endpoint is change from baseline in composite withdrawal
severity over days 1 to 5. Secondary endpoints include heart-rate
reduction, sleep score, appetite restoration, nausea burden,
benzodiazepine rescue burden, and CGI-I.

### Example 4 — Worked patient case

A 47-year-old adult with heavy daily alcohol use presents for
medically supervised withdrawal 14 hours after last drink with tremor,
tachycardia, nausea, anorexia, insomnia, and moderate CIWA-Ar burden,
but without active delirium tremens or withdrawal seizure. After
initial triage and availability of symptom-triggered benzodiazepine
rescue, the subject begins oral delta-9-THC 5 mg three times daily
and betaxolol 5 mg once daily. Over 4 days the subject demonstrates
reduced autonomic distress, improved ability to eat and sleep, and
lower rescue-medication burden; CGI-I is rated 2 ("much improved").

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Alcohol
Withdrawal comprising co-administration of a therapeutically effective
amount of the cannabinoid CB1 receptor agonist delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol and a therapeutically effective amount of the
beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist betaxolol are disclosed.
The two agents act on non-overlapping reward-deficit and autonomic-
hyperarousal pathways relevant to selected medically supervised alcohol
withdrawal and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in residual withdrawal distress, sleep, appetite, and
hemodynamic symptom burden compared with either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose. The regimen is adjunctive and is not a
substitute for seizure or delirium-tremens management.
