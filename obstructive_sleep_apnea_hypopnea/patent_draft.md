## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Obstructive Sleep Apnea Hypopnea (OSAH) by
co-administering (a) an orexin receptor antagonist, in particular
suvorexant, and (b) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with
5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine. The
invention is intended for carefully selected adult subjects with
stable, treated OSAH who remain on standard airway-management
therapy and who experience insomnia-like sleep fragmentation,
circadian dysregulation, or poor adherence to positive airway
pressure. The invention is not intended to replace CPAP, oral
appliance therapy, upper-airway intervention, or treatment of
untreated severe OSA.

## Background of the invention

Obstructive Sleep Apnea Hypopnea is a sleep-related breathing
disorder characterized by recurrent collapse or narrowing of the
upper airway during sleep, resulting in obstructive apneas or
hypopneas, oxygen desaturation, arousal, and sleep fragmentation.
The disorder is associated with daytime sleepiness, cardiovascular
risk, mood symptoms, impaired cognition, and reduced quality of
life.

The prevalence of OSAH is high and the burden is substantial
because many subjects have persistent sleep fragmentation and poor
tolerance of airway-management treatment even after diagnosis. The
clinical problem in treated subjects is often not only obstruction
per se, but also difficulty falling asleep with therapy, unstable
sleep timing, and recurrent removal or rejection of devices during
the night.

The current standard of care is airway- and anatomy-directed. It
consists primarily of positive airway pressure, oral appliances,
weight and positional interventions, and selected surgical or
device-based approaches. No pharmaceutical product is approved by
the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically
for the broad adjunctive treatment of OSAH-related sleep initiation
and adherence problems. A clinically meaningful gap remains for a
carefully selected pharmacologic regimen that improves sleep
continuity and target-window sleep initiation in treated patients
without replacing airway management.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, treated OSAH subjects frequently experience
persistent sleep initiation difficulty, device-associated arousal,
and circadian misalignment that undermine adherence. Suvorexant is
a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for insomnia that
facilitates sleep onset and maintenance by reducing orexin-driven
wakefulness. In selected stable and monitored sleep-disordered
breathing populations, the clinical question is patient selection,
not categorical impossibility of use. The suvorexant axis
therefore addresses the *wake-drive and sleep-initiation failure*
substrate of treated OSAH.

Second, OSAH subjects often also have disrupted sleep timing and
poor overnight recovery that worsen treatment tolerance. Agomelatine
is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent agonist at
MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors. This
combination supports circadian resynchronization and subjective
sleep-quality improvement. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses
the *circadian-disorganization and recovery-failure* substrate of
treated OSAH.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an orexin receptor antagonist in
combination with a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist having 5-HT2C
antagonism for OSAH specifically in selected, stable treated
subjects.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of orexin-driven
wake maintenance (a suvorexant axis) and restoration of circadian
alignment and sleep-recovery (an agomelatine axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for improving sleep
continuity and adherence in OSAH. The combination is expected to
produce greater improvement in sleep continuity and adherence to
airway therapy than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) suvorexant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) agomelatine or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, co-crystal,
or prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
OSAH in a selected adult subject in need thereof, comprising co-
administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of
suvorexant and a therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine
while the subject continues standard airway-management therapy.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) suvorexant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of OSAH according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with continued
positive airway pressure or other standard airway-management
therapy.

The invention contemplates suvorexant in a daily dose of about
5 mg to about 20 mg administered orally in the target sleep window,
with a preferred adult dose of 10 mg to 20 mg nightly. The
invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about 25 mg
to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a preferred
dose of 25 mg nightly and optional escalation to 50 mg nightly if
clinically indicated. The inventive feature is the concurrent
engagement of orexin-antagonist sleep-initiation support and
melatonergic circadian resynchronization in stable treated OSAH.

The technical effect of the combination is improvement, in selected
subjects with OSAH, in sleep continuity, target-window sleep onset,
and adherence to airway-management therapy, greater than the sum of
the individual effects of suvorexant and agomelatine administered
as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Suvorexant"* means the dual orexin receptor antagonist approved
for insomnia. The term includes suvorexant free base, salts,
polymorphs, solvates, hydrates, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent orexin receptor antagonists
with a clinically comparable sleep-promoting profile.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Obstructive Sleep Apnea Hypopnea"* or *"OSAH"* means a sleep-
related breathing disorder characterized by recurrent upper-airway
collapse or narrowing during sleep causing obstructive apneas or
hypopneas.

*"Stable treated OSAH subject"* means a subject with diagnosed OSAH
whose standard airway-management plan is established and whose
residual clinical problem includes sleep initiation, sleep timing,
or treatment tolerance rather than untreated severe obstruction.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a stable treated OSAH subject
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: sleep-
continuity score, PAP adherence, sleep-onset latency, daytime
fatigue, quality of life, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Suvorexant

Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia. By reducing orexin-mediated wake drive, it facilitates
sleep onset and maintenance. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of suvorexant is reduction of
wake-drive that interferes with sleep onset while using airway
therapy.

#### 2.2 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is stabilization of circadian timing and
overnight recovery.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise suvorexant and
agomelatine, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of a suvorexant tablet and an agomelatine tablet.

Excipients useful for the suvorexant composition include tablet
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and film-coating polymers
appropriate for bedtime administration. Excipients useful for the
agomelatine composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants,
lubricants, and coating polymers suitable for oral tablet
manufacture.

### 4. Dosage forms

Suvorexant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
target-window sleep initiation. Agomelatine is preferably
formulated as an oral tablet suitable for bedtime administration.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
suvorexant tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of agomelatine tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to ongoing PAP or airway therapy,
sleep timing, and adherence monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For selected stable treated OSAH subjects, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Suvorexant 5 mg orally in the target sleep window for about
  3 nights, titratable to 10 mg and then to 20 mg nightly if
  clinically indicated and tolerated.
* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days, titratable
  to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.

The regimen is not intended for untreated severe OSA, for acute
respiratory decompensation, or as a substitute for PAP or other
airway-management therapy.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to selected
stable treated OSAH subjects. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: age >= 18 years; diagnosed OSAH;
established airway-management plan; clinically meaningful sleep-
initiation difficulty, sleep fragmentation, or adherence problem;
and ability to participate in monitoring. Exclusion criteria
include untreated severe OSAH, severe respiratory instability,
severe hepatic disease, and known hypersensitivity to either
active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Treated OSAH often includes two interacting layers: persistent
wake-drive or insomnia-like sleep initiation failure, and circadian
or overnight recovery disruption that worsens adherence.

Suvorexant addresses the wake-drive layer through orexin
antagonism. Agomelatine addresses the circadian-recovery layer
through MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. The two axes are
non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on improved
sleep continuity and treatment tolerance.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: orexin receptor binding and antagonism assays
for suvorexant; MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays for
agomelatine; and combined sleep-state and adherence-tolerance
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include sleep-fragmentation paradigms, treated-OSA adherence models,
circadian-shift tasks, and wake-maintenance assays. A supra-additive
effect of the combination on restoration of target-window sleep and
treatment tolerance, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
suvorexant 10 mg nightly for 3 days; Period 2: agomelatine
25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days.
Full PK sampling, next-day sedation monitoring, liver-function
monitoring, and tolerability assessments are obtained.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in stable treated OSAH (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): suvorexant + agomelatine.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): suvorexant + placebo for agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with continued airway therapy.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in sleep-continuity
score at week 6. Secondary endpoints include PAP adherence,
sleep-onset latency, daytime fatigue, quality of life, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of stable
treated Obstructive Sleep Apnea Hypopnea comprising co-
administration of a therapeutically effective amount of suvorexant
and a therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine are disclosed.
The two agents act on non-overlapping wake-drive and sleep-recovery
circuits and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in sleep continuity and adherence to airway-management
therapy than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
