## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Schizoid Personality Disorder (ScPD) by
co-administering (a) a muscarinic M1/M4 receptor agonist and
(b) a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist, in particular the
combination of xanomeline and perospirone, optionally further with
a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic such as trospium to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects. The invention is
intended for adult subjects with ScPD who are concurrently
receiving structured psychotherapy or supportive treatment, and
addresses social detachment, restricted affect, low affiliative
motivation, and maladaptive salience assignment to interpersonal
engagement.

## Background of the invention

Schizoid Personality Disorder is a personality disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by a pervasive pattern of detachment from
social relationships and a restricted range of emotional expression
in interpersonal settings, beginning by early adulthood and present
across contexts. Characteristic features include neither desiring
nor enjoying close relationships, almost always choosing solitary
activities, little interest in sexual experiences with another
person, pleasure in few activities, lack of close friends or
confidants, apparent indifference to praise or criticism, and
emotional coldness, detachment, or flattened affectivity.

The prevalence of ScPD in the general population is low but the
clinical burden is meaningful because the disorder is associated
with severe social isolation, occupational under-engagement,
functional underachievement, low treatment seeking, and frequent
comorbidity with depression, anxiety, trauma-spectrum symptoms, and
other schizophrenia-spectrum personality traits. Although the
subject may appear indifferent, the long-term functional burden on
the individual and caregivers can be substantial.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological in the first
instance and consists of psychotherapy, skills support, and
management of comorbid disorders. No pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
for the treatment of ScPD as a primary indication. Where medication
is used, clinicians generally borrow from mood, anxiety, or
schizophrenia-spectrum practice, but those approaches are
inconsistently effective and may not directly improve social
motivation, cognitive-affective engagement, or interpersonal
responsiveness.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, ScPD includes impoverished social-cognitive
engagement, low affiliative salience, and restricted affective
expression, suggesting deficient cortical cholinergic signal
quality in networks supporting social cognition and motivated
attention. Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist
developed in the 1990s and more recently formulated with the
peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium as the FDA-approved
xanomeline–trospium combination (Cobenfy®) for schizophrenia in
adults. The muscarinic axis therefore addresses the
*social-cognitive engagement and affiliative-salience substrate*
of ScPD.

Second, ScPD also includes rigid interpersonal disengagement and, in
some subjects, low-grade aversive salience or oddity-linked social
withdrawal that overlap partially with the negative / attenuated
psychosis spectrum. Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic
approved in Japan for schizophrenia that acts as a D2 antagonist
with additional serotonergic properties. In the present invention,
the perospirone axis addresses the *maladaptive withdrawal-
maintaining salience and rigid disengagement substrate* of ScPD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an M1/M4 agonist in combination with
a D2 antagonist for ScPD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that muscarinic facilitation of
social-cognitive engagement (a xanomeline M1/M4 axis) and
dopaminergic stabilization of maladaptive withdrawal salience
(a perospirone D2 axis) are complementary and convergent substrates
for treatment of ScPD. The combination is expected to produce
greater symptomatic improvement in social engagement, emotional
expressivity, and functional participation than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) xanomeline or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) perospirone or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, optionally further comprising a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic agent such as trospium chloride, together with one
or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Schizoid Personality Disorder in an adult subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of xanomeline and a therapeutically effective
amount of perospirone, optionally further with a peripherally
restricted antimuscarinic. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) xanomeline or a salt thereof, optionally co-formulated with
trospium, in a first unitary dosage form, (b) perospirone or a
salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form, and (c) written
instructions for the treatment of ScPD according to a dosing
regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with psychotherapy or
supportive treatment.

The invention contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about
50 mg to about 250 mg administered orally as the free base or as
the tartrate salt in two divided doses; when xanomeline is
co-administered with trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is
about 40 mg to about 60 mg administered orally in two divided
doses. The invention contemplates perospirone in a daily dose of
about 4 mg to about 48 mg administered orally in one or two divided
doses, with a preferred dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in adult
subjects with ScPD, in social detachment, restricted affect,
interpersonal disengagement, and functional under-participation,
that is greater than the sum of the individual effects of
xanomeline and perospirone administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-
yl]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine, an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist. The term as used herein includes the free base,
the tartrate salt and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Trospium chloride"* or *"trospium"* means the quaternary ammonium
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist that does not
appreciably cross the blood-brain barrier and may be used to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline.

*"Schizoid Personality Disorder"* or *"ScPD"* means the personality
disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by a pervasive pattern of
detachment from social relationships and restricted range of
emotional expression in interpersonal settings.

*"Social-detachment / restricted-affect score"* means a clinically
meaningful burden of social withdrawal, low affiliative
motivation, limited pleasure in close relationships, and restricted
affective expression quantified by structured clinician assessment
or validated personality / symptom instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult subject for the
treatment of ScPD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
social-detachment / restricted-affect score, interpersonal-
function score, expressive-affect score, quality of life, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two or more active agents to a
subject so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted
on the subject during the period in which the pharmacological
effect of the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether
the agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. Activation of
the M1 receptor facilitates post-synaptic cholinergic cognitive
enhancement in cortex and hippocampus; activation of the M4
receptor contributes to stabilization of salience and behavioral
output in striatal circuits. Xanomeline reached the market in
fixed-dose combination with trospium chloride as Cobenfy® for
schizophrenia in adults and is under live U.S. brand-patent
protection via the marketed combination and related rights.

#### 2.2 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is reduction of low-grade salience misattribution and
withdrawal-maintaining dopaminergic reinforcement through D2
antagonism.

#### 2.3 Trospium (optional)

Trospium chloride is a quaternary ammonium peripherally restricted
muscarinic antagonist that does not appreciably cross the blood-
brain barrier. In the present invention, trospium may be included
at a daily dose effective to mitigate peripheral cholinergic
adverse effects of xanomeline without materially antagonizing the
central cognitive effect.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise xanomeline and
perospirone, optionally further comprising trospium, together with
at least one pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The compositions
may take the form of a co-packaged kit of two or three separate
dosage forms or a unitary fixed-dose combination.

The mass ratio of xanomeline (calculated as the free base) to
perospirone (calculated as the free base) is in the range of about
2:1 to about 50:1, preferably about 4:1 to about 20:1. When
trospium is included, the molar ratio of trospium to xanomeline is
approximately 1:1 to 1:3.

### 4. Dosage forms

Xanomeline (with or without trospium) is preferably formulated as
twice-daily oral capsules. Perospirone is preferably formulated as
an immediate-release oral tablet.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
xanomeline or xanomeline-trospium capsules, (b) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to
psychotherapy and symptom monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with ScPD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Xanomeline-trospium 50 mg/20 mg orally twice daily for the first
  three days, then 100 mg/20 mg twice daily for the next four days,
  then 125 mg/30 mg twice daily thereafter as tolerated.
* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily for 7 days, then 8 mg twice
  daily thereafter, with titration up to 12 mg twice daily if
  clinically needed.

Where trospium is omitted, xanomeline may be administered at an
equivalent free-base exposure range with heightened monitoring for
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects.

Hepatic adjustment: xanomeline-trospium is not recommended in
moderate or severe hepatic impairment. Severe renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP2D6 inhibitors without dose review of xanomeline and
perospirone. Avoid strong CYP3A4 inducers. QT monitoring should be
considered when other QT-prolonging agents are present.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with ScPD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age ≥ 18 years; clinician-confirmed ScPD;
clinically meaningful social detachment or restricted affect at
baseline; and capacity to participate in psychotherapy or
supportive treatment. Exclusion criteria include schizophrenia,
schizoaffective disorder, current manic episode, active psychotic
disorder, uncontrolled substance use disorder, severe hepatic or
renal impairment, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

ScPD is characterized by two interacting layers: low affiliative
salience and reinforcement of solitary, disengaged, or emotionally
restricted behavioral patterns. These layers lead to chronic social
distance and low participation in reciprocal relationships.

Xanomeline addresses the social-cognitive and affiliative-salience
layer through M1/M4 agonism. Perospirone addresses the withdrawal-
maintaining and low-grade maladaptive salience layer through D2
antagonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on the social-detachment / restricted-affect
phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in social engagement and emotional
expressivity than either monotherapy alone. The taxonomy-aware
efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 31,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: M1 receptor binding and calcium-mobilization
assays; M4 receptor functional assays; D2 receptor binding and
functional antagonism assays for perospirone; and combined
social-salience network assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include social-interaction tasks, affiliative preference tasks,
novelty-reward paradigms, and cognitive-flexibility measures.
No animal model is asserted to reproduce ScPD itself; rather, the
preclinical program supports convergent mechanisms of low social
salience and socially disengaged behavioral reinforcement.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
xanomeline-trospium 100 mg/20 mg twice daily for 7 days; Period 2:
perospirone titrated to 16 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the
combination for 7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and
tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal
purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of clinically
meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult ScPD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): xanomeline + perospirone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): perospirone + placebo for
  xanomeline.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with structured psychotherapy.

All subjects receive standardized psychotherapy or supportive
behavioral treatment focused on social participation, alliance
formation, and emotional expressivity. The primary endpoint is
change from baseline in social-detachment / restricted-affect score
at week 12. Secondary endpoints include interpersonal-function
score, expressive-affect score, responder rate, quality of life,
and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Schizoid Personality Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the muscarinic M1/M4 agonist
xanomeline and a therapeutically effective amount of the D2
antagonist perospirone, optionally further with the peripherally
restricted antimuscarinic trospium chloride, are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping social-cognitive engagement and
withdrawal-salience circuits implicated in detachment from social
relationships and restricted affect, and are expected to produce
greater-than-additive symptomatic improvement than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose.
