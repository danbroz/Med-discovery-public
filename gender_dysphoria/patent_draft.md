## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of reducing acute distress associated with Gender Dysphoria
by co-administering (a) a serotonergic tetracyclic agent having
5-HT2C / 5-HT2A antagonist properties, in particular mianserin, and
(b) a mu-opioid receptor agonist, in particular sufentanil, in a
specialist-supervised setting. The invention is intended only for
adult subjects with Gender Dysphoria who are already receiving,
being evaluated for, or electing gender-affirming clinical care,
and is directed to short-term relief of severe dysphoria-related
distress, panic, autonomic arousal, and sleep disruption. The
invention is explicitly not intended to alter gender identity or
gender expression and is not a substitute for gender-affirming
psychological, hormonal, or surgical care.

## Background of the invention

Gender Dysphoria is defined in DSM-5-TR as a marked incongruence
between one's experienced / expressed gender and assigned sex,
associated with clinically significant distress or impairment in
social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning. The
corresponding ICD-11 construct, Gender Incongruence, is placed in a
different diagnostic chapter, but the clinically relevant symptom
cluster of dysphoria-related distress remains a substantial source
of morbidity in many patients. In adults, the most burdensome
symptoms often include body-related distress, social pain, panic,
sleep disruption, physiological hyperarousal, and episodic crisis
states, especially during periods of delayed access to gender-
affirming care, hostile social exposure, or abrupt interruption in
care.

The prevalence of clinically meaningful gender dysphoria /
incongruence is difficult to quantify because ascertainment is
influenced by stigma, access to care, and evolving diagnostic
criteria. Nonetheless, the burden of dysphoria-related distress is
well established and includes increased anxiety, depression,
insomnia, self-harm risk, and crisis-driven healthcare utilization.

The current standard of care is gender-affirming and
multidisciplinary. It consists primarily of supportive psychotherapy,
social transition support where desired, gender-affirming hormone
therapy where indicated, and surgical interventions where desired
and clinically appropriate. No pharmaceutical product is approved by
the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically
for the treatment of gender dysphoria-related distress as a primary
indication. Where medications are used, they generally target
co-occurring depression, anxiety, insomnia, or panic rather than
the acute, body-linked dysphoria state itself.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, dysphoria-related distress frequently includes
intrusive preoccupation, anxious arousal, insomnia, and difficulty
down-regulating emotionally charged self-referential thought.
Mianserin is a tetracyclic antidepressant with antagonist activity
at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and H1
histamine receptors. In clinical use, mianserin has antidepressant,
anxiolytic, and sleep-promoting properties at oral doses commonly
in the range of about 30 mg to 90 mg/day, with lower doses often
used for sleep support. In the present invention, the mianserin
axis addresses the *persistent dysphoric preoccupation, sleep
disturbance, and hyperarousal substrate* of acute gender-dysphoria
states.

Second, severe dysphoria episodes may include an affective-pain
component analogous to extreme social pain or body-related distress.
The endogenous opioid system is deeply implicated in social-pain
processing and affective salience. Sufentanil is a potent
mu-opioid receptor agonist approved for analgesic use in specialist
medical settings and available in parenteral and sublingual /
transmucosal formulations. In the present invention, sufentanil is
not used as chronic mood management, but as a tightly time-limited,
specialist-supervised rescue agent to attenuate acute peaks of
dysphoria-related affective distress during crisis or destabilizing
care transitions. The sufentanil axis therefore addresses the
*acute affective-pain / crisis-intensity substrate* of severe
dysphoria episodes.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a 5-HT2C / 5-HT2A-antagonist
antidepressant in combination with a short-duration mu-opioid
agonist rescue strategy for gender dysphoria-related distress
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that chronic background reduction
of dysphoric preoccupation and hyperarousal (a mianserin axis) and
time-limited suppression of acute affective-pain crises
(a sufentanil axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for
short-term reduction of severe distress associated with Gender
Dysphoria in adults already engaged in gender-affirming care. The
combination is expected to produce a greater reduction in acute
dysphoria-related distress than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose. The invention is explicitly not
directed to changing identity, orientation, or gender expression.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) mianserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) sufentanil or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of reducing
acute distress associated with Gender Dysphoria in an adult subject
in need thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of mianserin and a
therapeutically effective amount of sufentanil. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with the sufentanil component
limited to specialist-supervised short-duration use.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) mianserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) sufentanil or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for reduction of acute
gender-dysphoria-related distress in an adult subject according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein, together with explicit warnings
that the regimen is adjunctive, adult-only, and not a substitute
for gender-affirming care.

The invention contemplates mianserin in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 90 mg administered orally once daily or in divided
doses, with a preferred adult dose of 15 mg to 60 mg/day and a
more preferred bedtime dose of 15 mg to 30 mg during initiation.
The invention contemplates sufentanil in a daily dose of about
5 mcg to about 360 mcg administered by sublingual or transmucosal
route in specialist-supervised divided doses, with a preferred
single rescue dose of 5 mcg to 30 mcg and a preferred maximum
continuous use interval of about 72 hours. The inventive feature is
the combination of a background dysphoria-stabilizing serotonergic
agent with a tightly limited opioid rescue strategy under gender-
affirming specialist supervision.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in adult
subjects with Gender Dysphoria, in acute dysphoria-distress
severity, panic intensity, autonomic arousal, and sleep disruption,
that is greater than the sum of the individual effects of
mianserin and sufentanil administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose, without asserting any change in
gender identity or expression.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Mianserin"* means the tetracyclic antidepressant
2-methyl-1,2,3,4,10,14b-hexahydrodibenzo[c,f]pyrazino[1,2-a]
azepine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof. The term
includes the hydrochloride and other pharmaceutically acceptable
salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically
labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues. Mianserin
is used internationally as an antidepressant and anxiolytic and
has clinically meaningful antagonist activity at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C,
alpha2-adrenergic, and H1 histamine receptors.

*"Sufentanil"* means the potent mu-opioid receptor agonist
N-[4-(methoxymethyl)-1-[2-(2-thienyl)ethyl]-4-piperidyl]-N-
phenylpropanamide or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof.
The term includes parenteral, sublingual, and transmucosal
formulations and clinically equivalent rescue formulations of
sufentanil used in medically supervised settings.

*"Gender Dysphoria"* means the DSM-5-TR syndrome of marked
incongruence between one's experienced / expressed gender and
assigned sex, associated with clinically significant distress or
impairment. The term, as used in this specification, refers only to
the distress syndrome and not to gender identity or gender
expression itself.

*"Acute dysphoria-related distress"* means a transient episode of
severe affective, autonomic, cognitive, or sleep-related distress
associated with Gender Dysphoria, including panic, body-related
distress, severe dysphoric rumination, and inability to sleep or
self-regulate.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult subject for the
reduction of acute distress associated with Gender Dysphoria
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: acute
dysphoria-distress score, panic intensity, autonomic-arousal
score, sleep quality, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Mianserin

Mianserin is a tetracyclic antidepressant used internationally for
major depressive disorder and insomnia-associated affective states.
Its pharmacology includes antagonism at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors,
antagonism at alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and antihistaminergic
activity. Clinically, mianserin is associated with reduction of
anxiety, dysphoric rumination, sleep latency, and hyperarousal. In
the present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
mianserin is reduction of the background dysphoric, intrusive, and
insomnia-linked component of acute gender-dysphoria-related
distress.

#### 2.2 Sufentanil

Sufentanil is a potent mu-opioid receptor agonist approved for
analgesic use in tightly supervised medical settings. Sublingual
and transmucosal formulations permit small-dose, short-duration
administration in monitored environments. In the present invention,
sufentanil is not used as chronic treatment of gender dysphoria,
but as a tightly limited rescue intervention during acute
dysphoria-related crises in specialist care, with explicit duration,
dose, and setting controls. The regimen is not intended for home
unsupervised chronic opioid administration.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise mianserin and
sufentanil, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as an oral mianserin tablet and
a sublingual / transmucosal sufentanil dosage unit intended for
specialist-supervised rescue use.

Excipients useful for the mianserin composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and film-coating polymers. Excipients useful
for the sufentanil composition include transmucosal-film or
sublingual-tablet carriers, oxygen scavengers, stabilizers, flavor
systems, and controlled-substance-compatible packaging.

### 4. Dosage forms

Mianserin is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
once-daily bedtime administration. Sufentanil is preferably
formulated as a small-volume sublingual tablet, transmucosal film,
or other mucosal rescue dosage form that can deliver low microgram
doses in a supervised setting.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
mianserin tablets, (b) a limited supply of supervised-use
sufentanil rescue dosage units, and (c) a printed insert with
explicit specialist-use restrictions, opioid-risk mitigation, and
continuation of affirming care instructions.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with acute distress associated with Gender
Dysphoria, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Mianserin 15 mg orally at bedtime for 7 days, titratable to
  30 mg and then 60 mg as clinically indicated.
* Sufentanil 5 mcg to 30 mcg by sublingual or transmucosal route
  in a specialist-supervised setting during acute dysphoria-related
  crisis windows, with a minimum interval between doses and a
  preferred maximum continuous-use interval of about 72 hours.

Broad genus ranges include mianserin 10–90 mg/day and sufentanil
5–360 mcg/day, although the preferred use of sufentanil is
substantially lower and time-limited. The regimen is intended only
for adults under specialist supervision. Chronic unsupervised
opioid exposure is outside the scope of the preferred embodiment.

Hepatic adjustment: mianserin should be reduced or avoided in
moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment. Sufentanil use is
contraindicated or requires heightened caution in severe
respiratory compromise, severe hepatic impairment, or clinically
significant concurrent CNS depressant use.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration of
sufentanil with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants
unless absolutely necessary in a monitored setting. Avoid strong
CYP3A4 modulators where clinically significant exposure changes are
expected.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with Gender Dysphoria-related distress. Inclusion criteria for the
preferred treatment population include: age ≥ 18 years; current
clinically meaningful dysphoria-related distress; engagement in or
active evaluation for gender-affirming clinical care; cognitive
capacity to participate in informed-consent processes; and access
to specialist supervision for any sufentanil rescue use. Exclusion
criteria include active opioid use disorder, uncontrolled substance
use disorder, severe respiratory disease, unsupervised setting,
pregnancy where clinically relevant, and any use of the regimen as
a substitute for or barrier to gender-affirming care. The invention
is not intended to alter gender identity, suppress gender
expression, or serve as conversion therapy.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Gender Dysphoria-related acute distress can include two interacting
layers: a persistent background layer of dysphoric rumination,
hyperarousal, and insomnia, and an acute crisis layer of affective
pain, panic, and autonomic escalation. These two layers are not
optimally addressed by a single conventional anxiolytic or
antidepressant alone.

Mianserin addresses the background dysphoric-rumination /
hyperarousal layer through serotonergic, alpha2-adrenergic, and H1
antagonist pharmacology. Sufentanil addresses the acute
affective-pain layer through short-duration mu-opioid receptor
agonism in a highly supervised rescue setting. The two axes are
non-overlapping in their principal mechanistic contribution and
converge on the acute dysphoria-distress phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in acute distress associated with Gender
Dysphoria than either monotherapy alone. This treatment is
adjunctive symptom management only and is not intended to alter
identity or replace gender-affirming care. Because opioid exposure
is involved, the method must remain specialist-supervised.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C binding assays and alpha2
adrenergic antagonism assays for mianserin; mu-opioid receptor
binding and functional agonism assays for sufentanil; and combined
stress / social-pain network assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include social-defeat or social-pain paradigms, hyperarousal /
insomnia paradigms, stress-potentiated startle, and affective-pain
readouts under strict ethics controls. No animal model is asserted
to reproduce Gender Dysphoria itself; rather, the preclinical
program supports convergent mechanisms of dysphoric rumination,
hyperarousal, and acute affective-pain escalation.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1:
mianserin titrated to 30 mg/day for 7 days; full PK sampling on
days 1 and 7. Period 2 (after washout): supervised sufentanil
sublingual microdosing over 24 to 72 hours with full PK and
respiratory monitoring. Period 3: combination with mianserin at
steady state and supervised sufentanil rescue dosing. The primary
endpoint is the geometric mean ratio for exposure parameters and a
demonstration of short-duration tolerability under monitored
conditions.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult Gender Dysphoria-related acute distress
(FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 60
(20 per arm), with all subjects receiving standard gender-affirming
care and psychotherapy as otherwise indicated.

* Arm A (combination): mianserin background therapy +
  specialist-supervised sufentanil rescue.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): mianserin background therapy +
  placebo rescue.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo background therapy + placebo rescue,
  in addition to standard affirming care.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in acute dysphoria-
distress score at week 4 or within a predefined rescue window,
with follow-up through week 12 for durability, sleep, and safety.
Secondary endpoints include panic intensity, sleep score,
autonomic-arousal score, responder rate, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for reducing acute distress
associated with Gender Dysphoria in adult subjects comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of
mianserin and a therapeutically effective amount of the mu-opioid
agonist sufentanil are disclosed. The two agents act on
non-overlapping background-dysphoria and acute affective-pain
circuits and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in dysphoria-related distress, panic intensity,
autonomic arousal, and sleep disruption than either agent alone at
any individually achievable dose when used as an adjunct to
specialist-supervised gender-affirming care. The regimen is
explicitly not intended to alter gender identity or gender
expression and is not a substitute for gender-affirming care.
