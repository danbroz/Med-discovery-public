## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Social Anxiety Disorder (SAD) by
co-administering (a) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with 5-HT1A
partial agonist activity, in particular vilazodone, and (b) a
melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with 5-HT2C antagonist
activity, in particular agomelatine. The invention is intended for
subjects with clinically significant fear of negative evaluation,
humiliation, or scrutiny in social or performance situations, and
addresses anticipatory social dread, autonomic social-threat
reactivity, post-event rumination, sleep disruption, and avoidance.
The invention is not intended to pathologize normal shyness,
introversion, or culturally appropriate reserve.

## Background of the invention

Social Anxiety Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
disorder characterized by marked fear or anxiety about one or more
social situations in which the individual is exposed to possible
scrutiny by others, such as social interactions, being observed, or
performing in front of others. The person fears that he or she will
act in a way or show anxiety symptoms that will be negatively
evaluated. The situations are avoided or endured with intense
distress.

The prevalence of SAD is clinically meaningful across adolescence
and adulthood and the burden is substantial. The disorder is
associated with school and occupational underperformance, reduced
social development, relationship avoidance, high comorbidity with
depression and substance use, and chronic sleep disruption before
anticipated social exposure. Even when subjects function externally,
the internal burden of pre-event dread and post-event rumination may
be severe.

The current standard of care is primarily psychotherapeutic and
serotonergic. It consists of cognitive-behavioral therapy,
exposure-based interventions, and use of SSRIs or related
antidepressants. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for
the combination of social-threat cognitive attenuation and
sleep-recovery support in SAD. A clinically meaningful gap remains
for subjects whose social fear is intensified by poor overnight
recovery and persistent next-day hyperarousal.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, SAD includes intense anticipatory threat
expectancy, fear of negative evaluation, and socially amplified
anxious cognition. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available
antidepressant that combines selective serotonin reuptake
inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual
mechanism supports attenuation of anxious distress and social-threat
expectancy while maintaining a familiar anxiety-relevant
pharmacology profile. The vilazodone axis therefore addresses the
*anxious-serotonergic and negative-evaluation expectancy*
substrate of SAD.

Second, many SAD subjects experience poor sleep before anticipated
social challenges and impaired nocturnal recovery after stressful
social exposure. Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that
acts as a potent agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist
at 5-HT2C receptors. This pharmacology supports circadian
resynchronization, improvement in sleep architecture, and reduction
of nocturnal hyperarousal. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses
the *sleep-recovery and social-performance recovery-failure*
substrate of SAD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist in combination with a melatonergic MT1 / MT2
agonist having 5-HT2C antagonism for SAD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of social-threat
expectancy (a vilazodone axis) and restoration of sleep-linked
recovery (an agomelatine axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of SAD. The combination is expected to
produce greater symptomatic improvement in social avoidance,
anticipatory anxiety, post-event rumination, and functional
participation than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) agomelatine or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, co-crystal,
or prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Social Anxiety Disorder in a human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective
amount of agomelatine.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of SAD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with exposure-based
or CBT-based treatment.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with
a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from
10 mg/day. The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose
of about 25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with
a preferred initiation dose of 25 mg nightly and optional
escalation to 50 mg nightly after about 2 weeks if clinically
indicated. The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of
serotonergic social-threat attenuation and melatonergic sleep-
recovery support.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with SAD, in anticipatory social anxiety, avoidance burden, post-
event rumination, and sleep disruption, that is greater than the
sum of the individual effects of vilazodone and agomelatine
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Social Anxiety Disorder"* or *"SAD"* means the anxiety disorder
defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by marked fear or anxiety about
possible scrutiny or negative evaluation in social situations.

*"Social-anxiety-severity total"* means a clinically meaningful
burden of social fear, anticipatory anxiety, avoidance, post-event
rumination, or role impairment quantified by structured clinician
assessment or validated social-anxiety instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of SAD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: social-
anxiety-severity total, social avoidance score, post-event
rumination, sleep-quality score, role-function score, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
social-threat expectancy and negative-evaluation fear.

#### 2.2 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is restoration of sleep continuity and
post-exposure recovery.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
agomelatine, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of a vilazodone tablet and an agomelatine tablet.

Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers. Excipients useful for the agomelatine
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and coating polymers suitable for oral tablet manufacture.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an oral tablet taken with
food. Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet
suitable for bedtime administration.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of agomelatine tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to exposure-based or CBT-based
social anxiety treatment and sleep support.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with SAD, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.
* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days, titratable
  to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.

The regimen is not intended to replace exposure-based or CBT-based
treatment, social-skills practice where indicated, or treatment of
substance-use coping strategies if present.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
SAD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 12 years; clinically meaningful fear of negative
evaluation or social scrutiny; and willingness to engage in
exposure-based or CBT-based treatment. Exclusion criteria include
psychotic or delusional fear states, severe hepatic disease, and
known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

SAD often includes two interacting layers: anticipatory fear of
negative evaluation and post-event rumination, and poor overnight
recovery that worsens next-day social threat sensitivity.

Vilazodone addresses the social-threat layer through SERT
inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Agomelatine addresses the
sleep-recovery layer through MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C
antagonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on the same social-avoidance phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in SAD burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 32,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays for
agomelatine; and combined social-threat and sleep-recovery assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include social-defeat or social-evaluation tasks, avoidance and
approach paradigms, post-stress rumination-like tasks, and sleep-
fragmentation assays. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
reduction of social avoidance and post-event rumination, relative to
either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the
principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in
Section 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: agomelatine
25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days.
Full PK sampling, liver-function monitoring, and tolerability
assessments are obtained.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult SAD (FIG. 4). Three arms,
1:1:1 randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + agomelatine.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with exposure-based
  psychotherapy.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in social-anxiety-
severity total at week 12. Secondary endpoints include social
avoidance score, post-event rumination, sleep-quality score,
role-function score, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Social
Anxiety Disorder comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective
amount of agomelatine are disclosed. The two agents act on non-
overlapping social-threat and sleep-recovery circuits and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in social
avoidance, anticipatory dread, and post-event rumination than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
