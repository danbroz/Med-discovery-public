## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Prolonged Grief Disorder (PGD) by
co-administering (a) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist
having 5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine, and
(b) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with 5-HT1A partial agonist
activity, in particular vilazodone. The invention is intended for
subjects with persistent, impairing grief after the applicable
diagnostic interval and addresses yearning, grief-related
preoccupation, sleep disruption, anxious rumination, and functional
stagnation while preserving attachment, autobiographical memory,
and grief-focused psychotherapy. The invention is not intended to
blunt normative acute bereavement.

## Background of the invention

Prolonged Grief Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
bereavement-related disorder characterized by persistent longing
for, or preoccupation with, the deceased together with clinically
meaningful grief-related distress and functional impairment beyond
the culturally expected and diagnostically relevant interval after
the loss. The syndrome may include identity disruption, disbelief,
avoidance of reminders, intense emotional pain, difficulty moving
forward in life, emotional numbness, loneliness, or a sense that
life is meaningless without the deceased.

The prevalence of PGD is clinically meaningful among bereaved
adults and is higher in trauma-linked or unexpectedly bereaved
populations. The burden includes persistent insomnia, social
withdrawal, occupational dysfunction, reduced engagement with
family or treatment, high comorbidity with depression, anxiety,
PTSD-like symptoms, and elevated suicide risk in severe cases.
While grief is a universal human experience, PGD represents a
persistent and impairing syndrome beyond normative mourning.

The current standard of care is primarily psychotherapeutic. It
consists of grief-focused psychotherapy, supportive treatment,
management of co-occurring depression or anxiety, and structured
follow-up. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United
States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for PGD as a
primary indication. Borrowed antidepressant strategies may reduce
comorbid symptoms but often do not directly address the combined
sleep, circadian, and grief-preoccupation phenotype that defines
PGD.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, PGD frequently involves circadian disruption,
early-morning awakening, fragmented sleep, reduced reward
entrainment, and inability to transition out of nocturnal
rumination states. Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant
that acts as a potent agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an
antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors. This combination supports
resynchronization of circadian timing, improvement in sleep
architecture, and disinhibition of frontal dopaminergic and
noradrenergic tone. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses the
*circadian, sleep-linked, and motivational-reengagement* substrate
of PGD.

Second, PGD also includes persistent yearning, intrusive grief-
related thought loops, anxious rumination, and reduced serotonergic
resilience in the face of reminder cues. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is
an orally available antidepressant that combines selective
serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A
receptor. This dual mechanism supports attenuation of anxious-
depressive symptoms and intrusive grief-related rumination while
maintaining a familiar antidepressant pharmacology profile. The
vilazodone axis therefore addresses the *anxious-serotonergic and
intrusive-grief cognition* substrate of PGD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
with 5-HT2C antagonism in combination with a serotonin reuptake
inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial agonist for PGD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that circadian restoration and
motivational re-engagement (an agomelatine axis) and anxious-
serotonergic attenuation of intrusive grief rumination (a
vilazodone axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for
treatment of PGD. The combination is expected to produce greater
symptomatic improvement in grief severity, sleep disturbance, and
functional re-engagement than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose, while preserving the subject's bond
to the deceased and not asserting erasure of grief memories or
normal attachment.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Prolonged Grief Disorder in a human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of agomelatine and a therapeutically effective
amount of vilazodone. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of PGD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with grief-focused
psychotherapy or supportive bereavement treatment.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a
preferred initiation dose of 25 mg nightly and a preferred
escalation to 50 mg nightly after about 2 weeks if clinically
indicated. The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of
about 10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with
food, with a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration
from 10 mg/day. The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement
of circadian-melatonergic regulation and serotonergic / 5-HT1A
stabilization in a persistent grief syndrome.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with PGD, in grief-severity burden, sleep disruption, intrusive
preoccupation with the deceased, and functional impairment, that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of agomelatine and
vilazodone administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Prolonged Grief Disorder"* or *"PGD"* means the bereavement-
related disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by persistent
longing for or preoccupation with the deceased, together with
clinically meaningful grief-related distress and functional
impairment beyond the applicable diagnostic interval after the loss.

*"Grief-severity score"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
yearning, preoccupation with the deceased, emotional pain, identity
disruption, avoidance, or inability to re-engage in life,
quantified by structured clinician assessment or validated grief
instruments such as PG-13 or equivalent scales.

*"Grief-focused psychotherapy"* means a structured psychotherapeutic
intervention designed to address maladaptive grief persistence,
avoidance, and re-engagement in life while preserving attachment
and memory of the deceased.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of PGD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: grief-
severity score, yearning / preoccupation score, sleep-quality
score, functional re-engagement score, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is restoration of circadian timing,
sleep continuity, and motivational re-engagement in PGD.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious distress and grief-related rumination. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of vilazodone is
reduction of intrusive grief-preoccupation and anxious-depressive
destabilization.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as an agomelatine tablet and a
vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the agomelatine composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers. Excipients useful for
the vilazodone composition include diluents (microcrystalline
cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders (hydroxypropyl
methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants (crospovidone, sodium
starch glycolate), lubricants (magnesium stearate), glidants
(colloidal silicon dioxide), and film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime administration. Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an
oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
agomelatine tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of vilazodone tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to grief-focused psychotherapy,
measurement-based follow-up, and sleep tracking.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with PGD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days, titratable
  to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.

Broad genus ranges include agomelatine 25-50 mg/day and vilazodone
10-40 mg/day. The preferred schedule administers agomelatine at
bedtime and vilazodone once daily with food.

Medication-medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors and requires hepatic monitoring in
appropriate patients. Vilazodone dose adjustment may be required
with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. The regimen is not intended to blunt
normal early bereavement reactions or to replace grief-focused
psychotherapy where indicated.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
PGD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 18 years; bereavement beyond the applicable
diagnostic interval; persistent yearning for or preoccupation with
the deceased together with clinically meaningful distress or
impairment; and willingness to participate in grief-focused
psychotherapy or structured bereavement care. Exclusion criteria
include acute normative grief not meeting diagnostic duration
criteria, untreated primary psychotic disorder or mania, severe
hepatic disease, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

PGD often includes two interacting layers: persistent grief-related
rumination and yearning that prevent cognitive-emotional
integration, and circadian / sleep disruption that sustains
fatigue, anhedonia, and inability to re-engage with life.

Agomelatine addresses the circadian-motivational layer through
MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. Vilazodone addresses the
rumination-anxiety layer through SERT inhibition and 5-HT1A partial
agonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on the same grief-severity phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in PGD burden than either monotherapy alone.
the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 31,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays
for agomelatine; SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; and combined stress-rumination modulation assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include stress and separation-distress models, circadian-shift
paradigms, sleep-fragmentation models, and reward-reengagement
paradigms. A supra-additive effect of the combination on reduction
of persistent distress and restoration of sleep-linked adaptation,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
agomelatine 25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone
titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, liver-function monitoring, ECG
monitoring, and tolerability assessments are obtained in each
period. The principal purpose is to establish tolerability and
absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult PGD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): agomelatine + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with grief-focused
  psychotherapy.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in a grief-severity
score at week 16. Secondary endpoints include yearning /
preoccupation score, sleep-quality score, functional re-engagement
score, responder rate, and CGI-I.

* PTSD- and stress-related patents disclose trauma-focused or
  stress-disorder strategies, not the present pair or the present

* Generic agomelatine and vilazodone formulation patents disclose

* Broad depressive-symptom patents do not disclose the present

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (MT1 / MT2 agonism +
5-HT2C antagonism + SERT inhibition + 5-HT1A partial agonism),
(ii) the specific actives (agomelatine + vilazodone), and
(iii) the specific indication (PGD). No prior-art disclosure
suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on which the present
invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Prolonged Grief Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping circadian-motivational and
intrusive-grief circuits implicated in persistent, impairing grief
and are expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in
grief severity, sleep disturbance, and functional re-engagement
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose while
preserving normative attachment and bereavement meaning.
