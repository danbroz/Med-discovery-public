## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Somatic Symptom Disorder (SSD) by
co-administering (a) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with 5-HT1A
partial agonist activity, in particular vilazodone, and (b) a
beta-adrenoceptor antagonist, in particular betaxolol. The
invention is intended for subjects with distressing somatic
symptoms accompanied by excessive symptom-focused thoughts,
feelings, or behaviors, and addresses anxious interpretation,
autonomic amplification, and bodily-signal hypervigilance while
continuing appropriate medical evaluation and care.

## Background of the invention

Somatic Symptom Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
condition in which one or more distressing somatic symptoms are
accompanied by excessive thoughts, feelings, or behaviors related
to the symptoms or associated health concerns. The diagnosis does
not require that the somatic symptoms be medically unexplained; the
clinical emphasis is on disproportionate and persistent symptom-
related distress, preoccupation, and functional impairment.

The prevalence and clinical burden of SSD are substantial in
primary care, specialty clinics, and consultation-liaison settings.
Subjects may experience chronic pain, fatigue, gastrointestinal
distress, dizziness, cardiopulmonary sensations, neurologic-type
complaints, or multisystem symptom clusters. Excessive symptom
monitoring, catastrophic interpretation of bodily signals, repeated
medical visits, functional disability, and high healthcare
utilization are common. Co-occurring anxiety, depression, trauma-
spectrum symptoms, and autonomic hyperarousal frequently intensify
the disorder.

The current standard of care is supportive and longitudinal. It
consists primarily of regular, structured medical follow-up,
psychotherapy, psychoeducation, treatment of co-occurring anxiety
or depression, and avoidance of unnecessary diagnostic escalation
once an appropriate medical assessment has been performed. No
pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for SSD as a primary
indication. A clinically meaningful gap remains for a regimen that
simultaneously reduces symptom-focused anxious interpretation and
the autonomic surges that reinforce bodily threat perception.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, SSD often includes intrusive body-focused
attention, symptom catastrophizing, intolerance of uncertainty, and
rigid expectation of worsening disease. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an
orally available antidepressant that combines selective serotonin
reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor.
This dual mechanism supports attenuation of anxious distress and
intrusive threat-related cognition while preserving antidepressant
utility. The vilazodone axis therefore addresses the *anxious-
serotonergic and symptom-interpretation amplification* substrate of
SSD.

Second, SSD often includes tachycardia, tremor, sweating, chest
tightness, gastrointestinal churn, or other adrenergic sensations
that are misread as evidence of serious illness and thereby
escalate distress. Betaxolol is a beta1-selective beta-
adrenoceptor antagonist approved for hypertension and glaucoma that
reduces heart rate and adrenergic surge. Relative to nonselective
beta blockers, the beta1-selective profile may offer a more
targeted autonomic intervention where bronchospasm risk or broad
nonselective blockade is undesirable. The betaxolol axis therefore
addresses the *autonomic-feedback and bodily-threat amplification*
substrate of SSD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist in combination with a beta1-selective
antagonist for SSD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that reduction of anxious symptom
interpretation (a vilazodone axis) and reduction of adrenergic
feedback escalation (a betaxolol axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for treatment of SSD. The combination is
expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement in somatic
distress, symptom preoccupation, and healthcare-utilization burden
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose, while
continuing appropriate medical evaluation and without asserting that
the underlying somatic symptoms are imaginary or medically
irrelevant.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) betaxolol or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, or prodrug thereof,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Somatic Symptom Disorder in a human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective
amount of betaxolol. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) betaxolol or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of SSD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein while continuing appropriate
medical care and structured follow-up.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with
a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from
10 mg/day. The invention contemplates betaxolol in a daily dose of
about 2.5 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once or twice
daily, with a preferred dose of 5 mg to 20 mg/day. The inventive
feature is the concurrent engagement of serotonergic threat-
modulation and beta1-selective autonomic stabilization in a subject
with SSD.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with SSD, in somatic-distress burden, symptom-focused preoccupation,
autonomic escalation, and healthcare-utilization intensity, and an
increase in functional stability, that is greater than the sum of
the individual effects of vilazodone and betaxolol administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Betaxolol"* means the beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor
antagonist approved in oral and ophthalmic formulations. The term
includes betaxolol free base, hydrochloride or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
and prodrugs.

*"Somatic Symptom Disorder"* or *"SSD"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11
condition in which one or more distressing somatic symptoms are
accompanied by excessive symptom-focused thoughts, feelings, or
behaviors.

*"Somatic-distress composite"* means a clinically meaningful
composite of somatic symptom burden, symptom preoccupation,
catastrophic interpretation, functional impairment, or healthcare
utilization associated with SSD.

*"Appropriate medical care"* means continued evaluation, monitoring,
and treatment of potentially relevant medical conditions according
to accepted clinical judgment, rather than withholding needed
medical assessment.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of SSD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: somatic-
distress composite, symptom-preoccupation score, autonomic-distress
score, healthcare-utilization burden, functional-impairment score,
or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious distress, intrusive threat-related cognition, and
catastrophic symptom interpretation. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of vilazodone is reduction of
body-focused anxious amplification in SSD.

#### 2.2 Betaxolol

Betaxolol is a beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist with
oral formulations suitable for autonomic stabilization. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
betaxolol is damping of adrenergic sensations that reinforce bodily
threat interpretation and recurrent reassurance-seeking.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
betaxolol, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a vilazodone tablet and a
betaxolol tablet.

Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers. Excipients useful for the betaxolol
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and coating polymers suitable for oral tablet manufacture.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an oral tablet taken with
food. Betaxolol is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable
for once-daily or twice-daily use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of betaxolol tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to structured follow-up,
symptom-tracking, and continued medical care.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with SSD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.
* Betaxolol 5 mg orally once daily, titratable to 10 mg and then to
  20 mg daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.

Broad genus ranges include vilazodone 10–40 mg/day and betaxolol
2.5–40 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses once-daily vilazodone
with food and once-daily betaxolol.

Medication–medication interactions: vilazodone dose adjustment may
be required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Betaxolol should be used
with caution in subjects with clinically meaningful bradycardia,
atrioventricular conduction disturbance, decompensated heart
failure, or bronchospasm risk. The regimen is not intended to
replace appropriate medical assessment of new or worsening somatic
symptoms.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
SSD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age ≥ 18 years; persistent distressing somatic symptoms
with excessive symptom-focused thoughts, feelings, or behaviors;
clinically meaningful distress or functional impairment; and
willingness to participate in structured follow-up. Exclusion
criteria include unexplained acute medical deterioration requiring
new urgent evaluation, severe bradyarrhythmia or another unsafe
beta-blocker contraindication, severe hepatic impairment, and known
hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

SSD often includes two interacting layers: anxious amplification
and catastrophic interpretation of bodily signals, and adrenergic
feedback that makes those signals feel urgent, dangerous, or
intractable.

Vilazodone addresses the interpretation-amplification layer through
SERT inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Betaxolol addresses the
autonomic-feedback layer through beta1-selective antagonism. The
two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on
the same somatic-distress phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in SSD burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; beta1-adrenoceptor binding and functional antagonism
assays for betaxolol; and combined autonomic-threat modulation
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include interoceptive-threat models, stress-reactivity paradigms,
autonomic-trigger models, and somatic-fear generalization paradigms.
A supra-additive effect of the combination on reduction of body-
signal threat responses, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: betaxolol
10 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, ECG monitoring, orthostatic vital signs, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult SSD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + betaxolol.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for betaxolol.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with collaborative SSD care.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in a somatic-distress
composite at week 12. Secondary endpoints include symptom-
preoccupation score, autonomic-distress score, healthcare-
utilization burden, functional-impairment score, and CGI-I.

* Generic neuropsychiatric or mood-disorder patents disclose broad
  treatment concepts, not the present pair or the present SSD

* Generic vilazodone formulation patents disclose the active and

  combination in SSD.
* Generic adrenergic-modulation filings do not disclose the

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (SERT inhibition /
5-HT1A partial agonism + beta1-selective antagonism), (ii) the
specific actives (vilazodone + betaxolol), and (iii) the specific
indication (SSD). No prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-
circuit rationale on which the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Somatic Symptom Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone and a
therapeutically effective amount of betaxolol are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping symptom-interpretation and
autonomic-feedback circuits implicated in excessive somatic
distress and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in somatic distress, symptom preoccupation, and
functional burden than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose while preserving appropriate medical evaluation.
