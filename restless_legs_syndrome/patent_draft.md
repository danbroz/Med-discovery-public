## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS) by co-administering
(a) an orexin receptor antagonist, in particular suvorexant, and
(b) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with 5-HT2C
antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine. The invention is
intended for subjects with the urge to move the legs and associated
evening or nocturnal discomfort, and addresses sleep-onset
disruption, nocturnal restlessness, circadian worsening of
symptoms, and augmentation-sensitive symptom burden. The invention
is not intended to replace iron repletion, neuropathy workup, or
other cause-directed management when indicated.

## Background of the invention

Restless Legs Syndrome is recognized in DSM-5-TR-associated sleep
disorder frameworks and in ICD-11 as a sensorimotor disorder
characterized by an urge to move the legs, usually accompanied by
uncomfortable sensations, that begins or worsens during rest, is
partially or totally relieved by movement, and is worse in the
evening or night. Sleep-onset difficulty and repeated nocturnal
disturbance are common consequences.

The prevalence of RLS is clinically meaningful and the burden is
substantial. The disorder is associated with chronic insomnia,
daytime fatigue, reduced work performance, mood symptoms, and major
quality-of-life impairment. Many patients also suffer from
augmentation or tolerance-like worsening on dopaminergic therapies
or incomplete relief on gabapentinoids.

The current standard of care is symptom-based and cause-informed. It
consists primarily of iron evaluation and replacement where
appropriate, alpha2delta ligands, dopamine agonists in selected
patients, opioid rescue in refractory cases, and sleep-hygiene
intervention. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United
States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for a dual
chronobiologic-plus-wake-drive approach to RLS. A clinically
meaningful gap remains for a regimen that reduces evening
hyperarousal and improves sleep continuity without relying solely
on dopaminergic escalation.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, RLS symptoms are strongly time-locked to the
evening and early night, implicating circadian vulnerability and
poor nocturnal recovery. Agomelatine is a melatonergic
antidepressant that acts as a potent agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors. This combination
supports circadian resynchronization and improved subjective sleep
quality. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses the *circadian
worsening and poor nocturnal recovery* substrate of RLS.

Second, RLS often includes persistent wakefulness and difficulty
transitioning from symptomatic restlessness into consolidated sleep.
Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia that facilitates sleep onset and maintenance by reducing
orexin-driven wakefulness. In the present invention, the suvorexant
axis addresses the *wake-drive and sleep-initiation failure*
substrate of RLS.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an orexin receptor antagonist in
combination with a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist having 5-HT2C
antagonism for RLS specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of evening
wake-drive (a suvorexant axis) and restoration of circadian sleep-
recovery timing (an agomelatine axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for treatment of RLS. The combination is
expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement in sleep-onset
latency, nocturnal restlessness, and evening symptom burden than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) suvorexant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) agomelatine or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, co-crystal,
or prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Restless Legs Syndrome in a human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of suvorexant and a therapeutically effective
amount of agomelatine.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) suvorexant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of RLS according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with cause-directed
evaluation and sleep support.

The invention contemplates suvorexant in a daily dose of about
5 mg to about 20 mg administered orally in the target sleep window,
with a preferred adult dose of 10 mg to 20 mg nightly. The
invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about 25 mg
to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a preferred
dose of 25 mg nightly and optional escalation to 50 mg nightly if
clinically indicated. The inventive feature is the concurrent
engagement of orexin-antagonist sleep-initiation support and
melatonergic circadian stabilization in RLS.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with RLS, in nocturnal symptom burden, sleep-onset latency,
nighttime awakenings, and daytime fatigue, that is greater than the
sum of the individual effects of suvorexant and agomelatine
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Suvorexant"* means the dual orexin receptor antagonist approved
for insomnia. The term includes suvorexant free base, salts,
polymorphs, solvates, hydrates, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent orexin receptor antagonists
with a clinically comparable sleep-promoting profile.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Restless Legs Syndrome"* or *"RLS"* means the sensorimotor
disorder characterized by an urge to move the legs that worsens at
rest, improves with movement, and is worse in the evening or night.

*"Evening RLS severity"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
urge, discomfort, sleep disruption, or rest-related worsening
quantified by structured clinician assessment or validated RLS
instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of RLS according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: evening RLS
severity, sleep-onset latency, augmentation burden, nocturnal
awakenings, daytime fatigue, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Suvorexant

Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia. By reducing orexin-mediated wake drive, it facilitates
sleep onset and maintenance. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of suvorexant is reduction of
wake-drive that prevents sleep initiation once evening RLS symptoms
have begun.

#### 2.2 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is stabilization of circadian timing
and overnight recovery in the evening-worsening RLS phenotype.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise suvorexant and
agomelatine, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of a suvorexant tablet and an agomelatine tablet.

Excipients useful for the suvorexant composition include tablet
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and film-coating polymers
appropriate for bedtime administration. Excipients useful for the
agomelatine composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants,
lubricants, and coating polymers suitable for oral tablet
manufacture.

### 4. Dosage forms

Suvorexant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
use in the target sleep window. Agomelatine is preferably
formulated as an oral tablet suitable for bedtime administration.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
suvorexant tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of agomelatine tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to evening symptom tracking, sleep
timing, and augmentation monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with RLS, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Suvorexant 5 mg orally in the target sleep window for about
  3 nights, titratable to 10 mg and then to 20 mg nightly if
  clinically indicated and tolerated.
* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days, titratable
  to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.

The regimen is not intended to replace iron repletion, neuropathy
evaluation, or management of augmentation from dopaminergic
therapies. It is also not intended to replace opioid rescue in
highly refractory specialist cases when otherwise clinically
appropriate.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
RLS. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 18 years; clinically meaningful evening or nocturnal
RLS symptoms; sleep-onset or sleep-maintenance impairment; and
willingness to participate in cause-directed evaluation. Exclusion
criteria include untreated severe sleep-disordered breathing, severe
hepatic disease, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

RLS often includes two interacting layers: evening-circadian
worsening of symptoms and persistent wake-drive that prevents
consolidated sleep once symptoms begin.

Suvorexant addresses the wake-drive layer through orexin
antagonism. Agomelatine addresses the circadian-worsening layer
through MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. The two axes are
non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on improved
nighttime symptom control and sleep continuity.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in RLS burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 36,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: orexin receptor binding and antagonism assays
for suvorexant; MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays for
agomelatine; and combined evening-arousal and sleep-state assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include rest-related movement-urge models, circadian symptom
timing paradigms, sleep-fragmentation tasks, and wake-maintenance
assays. A supra-additive effect of the combination on reduction of
evening symptom burden and improvement in sleep initiation, relative
to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the
principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in
Section 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
suvorexant 10 mg nightly for 3 days; Period 2: agomelatine
25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days.
Full PK sampling, liver-function monitoring, next-day sedation
monitoring, and tolerability assessments are obtained.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult RLS (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): suvorexant + agomelatine.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): suvorexant + placebo for agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard supportive care.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in evening RLS
severity at week 8. Secondary endpoints include sleep-onset
latency, augmentation burden, nocturnal awakenings, daytime
fatigue, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Restless Legs Syndrome comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of suvorexant and a
therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping wake-drive and circadian-recovery
circuits and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in evening symptom burden and sleep initiation than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
