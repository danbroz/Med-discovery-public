## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating compulsive and related disorders by
co-administering (a) a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse agonist /
antagonist and (b) a serotonergic antidepressant having serotonin
reuptake inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonist activity, in
particular the combination of pimavanserin and vilazodone. The
invention is intended for subjects with primary obsessive-
compulsive and related disorders or clinically mixed compulsive-
spectrum phenotypes, and addresses intrusive salience tagging,
compulsive drive, perseverative attention, and repetitive behavior
burden across category boundaries.

## Background of the invention

The obsessive-compulsive and related disorders chapter of DSM-5-TR
and the corresponding ICD-11 group encompasses a transdiagnostic
family of syndromes characterized by intrusive preoccupation,
compulsive acts, repetitive body-focused behaviors, or rigid
salience misassignment. This family includes obsessive-compulsive
disorder, body dysmorphic disorder, hoarding disorder,
trichotillomania, excoriation disorder, and closely related
presentations. In routine practice, many subjects exhibit mixed or
cross-over phenotypes that do not stay neatly confined to one
diagnostic box over time.

The prevalence and clinical burden of this compulsive-spectrum
family are substantial. Core impairments include intrusive
preoccupation, repetitive rituals or body-focused repetitive
behavior, shame, sleep interference, high treatment burden, social
avoidance, poor quality of life, and reduced work or school
function. Comorbid anxiety, depression, trauma-spectrum symptoms,
and substance use disorders are common. Existing category-specific
pharmacotherapy is also imperfectly transferable across the family,
especially when a subject has a mixed or shifting compulsive
phenotype.

The current standard of care is category-specific and often
behavioral first. It includes exposure and response prevention for
OCD-spectrum disorders, habit-reversal and stimulus control for
body-focused repetitive behaviors, and supportive psychotherapy for
related presentations. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the umbrella
category of compulsive and related disorders as a transdiagnostic
construct. Conventional serotonergic monotherapy is frequently used,
but it is not consistently sufficient in subjects whose symptoms
reflect both anxious-compulsive distress and rigid salience
pathology.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, compulsive-spectrum disorders include intrusive
distress, anxious amplification, and repetitive thought loops that
are strongly linked to serotonergic tone and 5-HT1A-regulated
anxious-compulsive cognition. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally
available antidepressant that combines selective serotonin reuptake
inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual
mechanism supports attenuation of anxious-compulsive symptomatology
and intrusive ideation. The vilazodone axis therefore addresses the
*obsessional distress and anxious-compulsive amplification*
substrate of compulsive-spectrum disorders.

Second, compulsive-spectrum disorders also include pathologic
salience tagging, rigid cue weighting, and perseverative
attentional capture around feared, desired, or incomplete internal
and external stimuli. Pimavanserin (Nuplazid®) is a selective
5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist approved by the FDA for
hallucinations and delusions associated with Parkinson disease
psychosis. Pimavanserin has no clinically meaningful D2 antagonism
and therefore can modify 5-HT2A-mediated salience processing
without adding direct dopaminergic motor burden. The pimavanserin
axis therefore addresses the *maladaptive salience and
perseverative-attention* substrate shared across compulsive and
related disorders.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist /
antagonist in combination with a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist for the umbrella category of compulsive and
related disorders as a transdiagnostic therapeutic construct.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of obsessional
distress (a vilazodone HTR1A / SERT axis) and attenuation of
maladaptive salience tagging (a pimavanserin 5-HT2A axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of the
compulsive-spectrum family. The combination is expected to produce
greater symptomatic improvement across intrusive, compulsive, and
repetitive-behavior dimensions than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pimavanserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) vilazodone or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating a
compulsive and related disorder in a human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of pimavanserin and a therapeutically effective
amount of vilazodone. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of a compulsive and
related disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in
conjunction with evidence-based behavioral treatment.

The invention contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose of about
17 mg to about 34 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 34 mg/day. The invention contemplates
vilazodone in a daily dose of about 10 mg to about 40 mg
administered orally once daily with food, with a preferred dose of
20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from 10 mg/day.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in
subjects with compulsive and related disorders, in intrusive
preoccupation, compulsive severity, repetitive-behavior frequency,
and caregiver burden, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of pimavanserin and vilazodone administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically equivalent
5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Compulsive and related disorder"* or *"compulsive-spectrum
disorder"* means a disorder or mixed phenotype within the
obsessive-compulsive and related disorders family of DSM-5-TR / ICD-11,
including but not limited to obsessive-compulsive disorder, body
dysmorphic disorder, hoarding disorder, trichotillomania,
excoriation disorder, and mixed or shifting phenotypes across those
categories.

*"Transdiagnostic compulsive-severity score"* means a clinically
meaningful burden of intrusive preoccupation, compulsive drive, or
repetitive behavior quantified by a validated scale adapted to the
dominant phenotype, or by a transdiagnostic composite harmonizing
such scales.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of a compulsive-spectrum disorder according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in
at least one of: transdiagnostic compulsive-severity score,
intrusive-preoccupation score, repetitive-behavior frequency,
quality of life, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. It has no clinically meaningful
affinity for dopamine D2 receptors. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of pimavanserin is attenuation
of maladaptive salience tagging and rigid cue-weighting that
contribute to intrusive and repetitive symptom persistence across
the compulsive-spectrum family.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious-compulsive symptomatology and intrusive ideation. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
vilazodone is reduction of obsessional distress and anxious-
compulsive amplification across mixed compulsive phenotypes.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pimavanserin and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a pimavanserin capsule and a
vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the pimavanserin composition include capsule
fill excipients, diluents, disintegrants, and stabilizers suitable
for the marketed pimavanserin formulation profile. Excipients useful
for the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or tablet
suitable for once-daily dosing. Vilazodone is preferably formulated
as an immediate-release oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pimavanserin capsules or tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to
behavioral treatment and symptom monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with a compulsive-spectrum
disorder, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pimavanserin 34 mg orally once daily.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.

Broad genus ranges include pimavanserin 17–34 mg/day and
vilazodone 10–40 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses once-daily
pimavanserin and once-daily vilazodone with food.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: severe hepatic or renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this transdiagnostic indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers without review of pimavanserin
exposure. Vilazodone dose adjustment may be required with strong
CYP3A4 inhibitors. Corrected QT interval monitoring should be
considered when other QT-prolonging medications are used.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
compulsive-spectrum disorders. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: age ≥ 12 years; clinician-confirmed
obsessive-compulsive or related disorder phenotype; clinically
meaningful intrusive-preoccupation or repetitive-behavior burden at
baseline; and participation in or willingness to engage in
evidence-based behavioral treatment. Exclusion criteria include
primary psychotic disorders, active mania, severe hepatic or renal
impairment, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Compulsive-spectrum disorders share two interacting layers despite
differences in outward phenotype: intrusive, distressing
preoccupation and rigid salience tagging of internal or external
cues that drive repetitive acts.

Vilazodone addresses the anxious-compulsive amplification layer
through SERT inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Pimavanserin
addresses the maladaptive salience / perseverative-attention layer
through 5-HT2A inverse agonism. The two axes are non-overlapping
at the receptor level and converge on the transdiagnostic
compulsive-symptom phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement across multiple compulsive-spectrum disorders
and mixed phenotypes than either monotherapy alone. The taxonomy-
receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A binding and inverse-agonism assays for
pimavanserin; SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; and combined salience / repetitive-behavior network
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include marble-burying, reversal learning, compulsive grooming,
cue-triggered repetitive-behavior tasks, and perseveration
paradigms. A supra-additive effect of the combination on intrusive
and repetitive symptom proxies, relative to either monotherapy at
any individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof
point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
pimavanserin 34 mg once daily for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone
titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult compulsive-spectrum
disorders (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 180
(60 per arm), stratified by dominant phenotype.

* Arm A (combination): pimavanserin + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for
  pimavanserin.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with evidence-based
  behavioral treatment.

All subjects receive standardized behavioral treatment appropriate
to their dominant phenotype. The primary endpoint is change from
baseline in a transdiagnostic compulsive-severity composite at
week 12. Secondary endpoints include intrusive-preoccupation score,
repetitive-behavior frequency, responder rate, quality of life,
and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of a
compulsive and related disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the selective 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist pimavanserin and a therapeutically effective
amount of the serotonin reuptake inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial
agonist vilazodone are disclosed. The two agents act on non-
overlapping salience-tagging and obsessional-distress circuits
shared across the compulsive-spectrum family and are expected to
produce greater-than-additive symptomatic improvement in intrusive
preoccupation and repetitive-behavior severity than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose.
