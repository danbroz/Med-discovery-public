## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Premature Ejaculation (PE) by co-administering
(a) a serotonergic tetracyclic agent having antagonist activity at
5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, in particular mianserin, and
(b) a mu-opioid receptor agonist, in particular sufentanil, in a
restricted, session-aligned rescue regimen. The invention is
intended for adult male subjects with persistent premature
ejaculation causing clinically meaningful distress, and is directed
to increasing ejaculatory latency, improving perceived control, and
reducing distress without chronic unsupervised opioid use.

## Background of the invention

Premature Ejaculation is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
male sexual dysfunction characterized by ejaculation that occurs
earlier than desired, often within about one minute of vaginal
penetration in lifelong generalized forms or with marked reduction
in latency in acquired forms, together with inability to delay
ejaculation on most or all sexual occasions and clinically
significant distress or interpersonal difficulty.

The prevalence of PE is clinically meaningful and the burden is
substantial. The disorder is associated with shame, avoidance of
sexual intimacy, relationship conflict, anticipatory anxiety,
reduced self-efficacy, and in some subjects secondary erectile or
desire problems driven by fear of failure. Existing therapies
include behavioral methods, topical local anesthetics, and chronic
or on-demand serotonergic agents, but many subjects remain
dissatisfied because of delayed onset, adverse effects, or limited
perceived control.

The current standard of care is heterogeneous. It consists
primarily of behavioral strategies, topical desensitizing
approaches, psychotherapy, and off-label or region-specific
serotonergic pharmacotherapy. No pharmaceutical product is approved
by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically
for PE as a primary indication. Existing chronic SSRI-based
strategies may help some men but often introduce daily medication
burden or sexual side effects, creating a need for alternative,
mechanism-based control strategies.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, PE frequently includes performance anxiety,
pre-ejaculatory urgency, intrusive self-monitoring, and unstable
serotonergic regulation of ejaculatory control. Mianserin is a
tetracyclic antidepressant with antagonist activity at 5-HT2A /
5-HT2C receptors, alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and H1 histamine
receptors. Non-SRI antidepressant strategies have been proposed in
the PE literature as alternatives to chronic SSRI exposure. In the
present invention, the mianserin axis addresses the *anxious-
urgency and baseline ejaculatory-control instability* substrate of
PE.

Second, a subset of PE appears to involve intense acute sensory
urgency and low threshold for reflexive ejaculatory completion. The
endogenous opioid system participates in sensory salience,
reinforcement, and modulation of orgasmic timing. Sufentanil is a
potent mu-opioid receptor agonist approved for analgesic use in
tightly supervised medical settings and available in parenteral and
sublingual / transmucosal formulations. Although chronic or high-
dose opioid exposure is generally associated with sexual
dysfunction, the present invention is based on the recognition that
*very low-dose, intermittent, time-limited, session-aligned*
mu-opioid agonism, administered only on planned sexual-attempt
days, can blunt acute ejaculatory urgency and increase ejaculatory
latency in selected subjects when combined with background
stabilization by mianserin.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C-antagonist
antidepressant in combination with low-dose rescue mu-opioid
agonism for PE specifically.

The present invention recognizes that reduction of baseline
performance-anxiety and ejaculatory-control instability (a
mianserin axis) and tightly limited attenuation of acute sensory
urgency (a sufentanil mu-opioid rescue axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for treatment of PE in selected adult male
subjects. The combination is expected to produce greater
improvement in ejaculatory latency, perceived control, and distress
reduction than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose, while explicitly avoiding chronic unsupervised opioid
exposure.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) mianserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) sufentanil or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Premature Ejaculation in an adult male subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of mianserin and a therapeutically effective
amount of sufentanil, wherein the sufentanil component is used as a
session-aligned, as-needed rescue intervention during planned
sexual activity attempts and is not used as chronic daily opioid
therapy.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) mianserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) sufentanil or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Premature
Ejaculation according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
together with explicit warnings on opioid risk, restricted use
frequency, and supervision requirements.

The invention contemplates mianserin in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 90 mg administered orally once daily or in divided
doses, with a preferred dose of 15 mg to 60 mg/day and a preferred
bedtime initiation dose of 15 mg to 30 mg/day. The invention
contemplates sufentanil in a single rescue dose of about 2.5 mcg to
about 20 mcg by sublingual or transmucosal route administered about
15 to 90 minutes before a planned sexual attempt, with a preferred
maximum daily exposure of about 40 mcg and a preferred maximum use
frequency of about 4 attempt-days per week. The inventive feature
is the combination of chronic background reduction of ejaculatory
urgency with a tightly limited, low-dose, session-aligned rescue
intervention for ejaculatory control.

The technical effect of the combination is an increase, in adult
male subjects with PE, in ejaculatory latency and perceived
ejaculatory control, and a reduction in distress, that is greater
than the sum of the individual effects of mianserin and sufentanil
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Mianserin"* means the tetracyclic antidepressant
2-methyl-1,2,3,4,10,14b-hexahydrodibenzo[c,f]pyrazino[1,2-a]
azepine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof. The term
includes the hydrochloride and other pharmaceutically acceptable
salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically
labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Sufentanil"* means the potent mu-opioid receptor agonist
N-[4-(methoxymethyl)-1-[2-(2-thienyl)ethyl]-4-piperidyl]-N-
phenylpropanamide or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof.
The term includes parenteral, sublingual, and transmucosal
formulations and clinically equivalent low-dose rescue formulations
of sufentanil used in tightly controlled settings.

*"Premature Ejaculation"* or *"PE"* means the male sexual
dysfunction defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by ejaculation that
occurs sooner than desired on most or all partnered sexual
encounters together with reduced perceived control and clinically
significant distress.

*"Intravaginal ejaculatory latency time"* or *"IELT"* means the
elapsed time from vaginal penetration to ejaculation in partnered
sexual activity, or analogous latency metrics in structured sexual-
activity assessments.

*"Session-aligned rescue dosing"* means administration of a rescue
medication within a defined interval, preferably about 15 to
90 minutes, before a planned sexual attempt, with explicit limits
on frequency, total dose, and supervision.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult male subject for the
treatment of PE according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
IELT, perceived control, distress score, partner-reported
satisfaction, satisfying sexual events, or investigator-rated
CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Mianserin

Mianserin is a tetracyclic antidepressant with antagonist activity
at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and H1
histamine receptors. In the present invention, the principal
mechanistic contribution of mianserin is reduction of baseline
anxious urgency and stabilization of ejaculatory control without
chronic SSRI exposure.

#### 2.2 Sufentanil

Sufentanil is a potent mu-opioid receptor agonist approved for
analgesic use in tightly supervised settings. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of sufentanil is
acute, time-limited dampening of sensory urgency and delay of
ejaculatory completion during planned sexual attempts. The
preferred embodiment is a restricted rescue regimen, not chronic
daily opioid therapy.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise mianserin and
sufentanil, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a mianserin tablet and a low-
dose transmucosal sufentanil rescue unit.

Excipients useful for the mianserin composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and film-coating polymers
suitable for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the
sufentanil rescue component include mucoadhesive carriers,
stabilizers, permeation-support excipients, and low-dose unit
delivery matrices appropriate for sublingual or buccal use.

### 4. Dosage forms

Mianserin is preferably formulated as an oral tablet administered
once daily, preferably at bedtime. Sufentanil is preferably
formulated as a low-dose sublingual or buccal dosage form suitable
for restricted rescue use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
mianserin tablets, (b) a limited number of low-dose sufentanil
rescue units consistent with a maximum use frequency of about
4 attempt-days per week, and (c) written instructions aligned to
session-aligned rescue dosing, safety monitoring, and opioid-risk
warnings.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult male subjects with PE, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Mianserin 15 mg orally at bedtime for about 7 days, titratable to
  30 mg and then to 60 mg nightly if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.
* Sufentanil about 2.5 mcg to about 20 mcg by sublingual or buccal
  route about 15 to 90 minutes before a planned consensual sexual
  activity attempt, with preferred maximum use frequency of about
  4 attempt-days per week and a preferred maximum daily exposure of
  about 40 mcg.

The preferred embodiment expressly excludes chronic daily opioid
therapy. Rescue doses are aligned to planned attempt days and
should be accompanied by counseling on sedation risk, respiratory
risk, and secure storage.

Medication-medication interactions: concomitant use of other CNS
depressants, benzodiazepines, alcohol misuse, or other opioids is
disfavored or exclusionary because of additive sedation and
respiratory-depression risk. The preferred clinical embodiment
requires screening for opioid use disorder and respiratory risk
before initiation.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult male
subjects with PE. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age >= 18 years; persistent PE causing
clinically meaningful distress; willingness to track planned sexual-
attempt outcomes; and ability to follow restricted rescue-use
instructions. Exclusion criteria include opioid use disorder or
high misuse risk, clinically meaningful respiratory instability,
severe hepatic impairment, uncontrolled major psychiatric
decompensation, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

PE in selected adult male subjects often includes two interacting
layers: baseline anxious urgency and low control threshold, and
acute sensory urgency that rapidly triggers ejaculatory completion.

Mianserin addresses the baseline urgency layer through 5-HT2A /
5-HT2C antagonism and related serotonergic / anxiolytic
modulation. Sufentanil addresses the acute sensory-urgency layer
through a tightly limited, session-aligned mu-opioid rescue
intervention. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor
level and converge on improved ejaculatory control.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in ejaculatory latency and control than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 27,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C binding and antagonism assays
for mianserin; mu-opioid receptor binding and signaling assays for
sufentanil; and combined ejaculatory-control / sensory-urgency
paradigms where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include sexual-reflex latency models, sensory-urgency paradigms,
reward / anxiety interaction tasks, and serotonergic sexual-control
models. A supra-additive effect of the combination on prolongation
of ejaculatory latency, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult male volunteers (age 21-55 years). Period 1:
mianserin 15 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: single low-dose
sublingual sufentanil rescue administration on two supervised study
days; Period 3: the combination. Full PK sampling, sedation
monitoring, pulse oximetry, adverse-event monitoring, and
abuse-liability assessments are obtained. The principal purpose is
to establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult PE (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): mianserin + session-aligned low-dose
  sufentanil rescue.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): mianserin + placebo rescue.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo background + placebo rescue.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in IELT across weeks
5-8. Secondary endpoints include perceived control, distress score,
partner-reported satisfaction, satisfying sexual events, and CGI-I.

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of a 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C-antagonist
antidepressant with a low-dose rescue mu-opioid agonist for PE
specifically. The closest hits are:

* Premature-ejaculation patents involving modafinil, botulinum
  neurotoxin, or other interventions disclose different mechanisms
  and do not teach the present pair or the present session-aligned
  rescue architecture.
* Non-SRI antidepressant and sexual-dysfunction filings suggest
  alternative serotonergic strategies but do not disclose the
  present low-dose sufentanil combination.
* Sufentanil dosage-form patents disclose pain-management rescue
  formulations and do not teach PE use.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (5-HT2A / 5-HT2C
antagonism + mu-opioid agonism), (ii) the specific actives
(mianserin + sufentanil), and (iii) the specific session-aligned
use for PE. No prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-
circuit rationale on which the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Premature Ejaculation comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of mianserin and a therapeutically
effective amount of sufentanil are disclosed, wherein the
mianserin provides background reduction of ejaculatory urgency and
the sufentanil provides a low-dose, session-aligned rescue
intervention on planned sexual-attempt days. The two agents act on
non-overlapping urgency-control and sensory-salience circuits and
are expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in
ejaculatory latency and perceived control than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose.
