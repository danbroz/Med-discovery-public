## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Catatonic Disorder Due to Another Medical
Condition (CD-AMC) by co-administering (a) a muscarinic M1/M4
receptor agonist and (b) a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist, in
particular the combination of xanomeline and perospirone,
optionally further with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic
such as trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse
effects. The invention addresses catatonic motoric and behavioral
signs, psychomotor freezing or excitement, and associated
caregiver / medical burden in subjects whose catatonic syndrome is
attributable to a defined medical condition; it does not assert
disease-modification of the underlying medical condition itself.

## Background of the invention

Catatonic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition is recognized
in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a syndrome in which a clinically
meaningful catatonic presentation is judged to be the direct
physiologic consequence of a medical condition. The syndrome may
include stupor, mutism, posturing, waxy flexibility, negativism,
echolalia, echopraxia, agitation not influenced by external
stimuli, grimacing, stereotypy, or mannerisms. The underlying
medical condition may include neurologic disease, autoimmune
encephalitis, seizure disorder, metabolic encephalopathy, endocrine
disorder, infectious CNS process, or other recognized medical
causes.

The prevalence of CD-AMC is difficult to estimate because catatonia
is under-recognized and often coded under the primary medical
condition or under broader delirium / encephalopathy labels.
Nonetheless, the clinical burden is substantial. Catatonia may
produce profound functional immobility, inability to eat or drink,
severe caregiver burden, aspiration risk, pressure injury, and
marked medical instability. Although benzodiazepines and
electroconvulsive therapy are established treatments for many
catatonic syndromes, there remains an unmet need for adjunctive,
mechanism-based pharmacologic strategies for selected medically
caused catatonic presentations, particularly those with recurring
or partial-response patterns.

The current standard of care is supportive and etiology-directed. It
consists primarily of urgent medical stabilization, treatment of
the underlying medical condition, lorazepam challenge / treatment,
and electroconvulsive therapy when indicated. No pharmaceutical
product is approved by the United States Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) specifically for CD-AMC as a primary
indication. Existing pharmacologic management is not mechanism-
matched to all catatonic phenotypes and may be limited by sedation,
incomplete response, or recurrence.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, many catatonic presentations appear to involve
frontal-striatal dysregulation with impaired initiation, motor
release, and behavioral gating that may benefit from muscarinic
facilitation. Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist
developed in the 1990s and more recently formulated with the
peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium as the FDA-approved
xanomeline–trospium combination (Cobenfy®) for schizophrenia in
adults. The muscarinic axis therefore addresses the
*frontocortical-striatal gating and behavioral-initiation substrate*
of catatonia.

Second, some catatonic syndromes include dopaminergically
disorganized salience and motor-output imbalance, especially in
agitated or mixed catatonic states. Perospirone is an atypical
antipsychotic approved in Japan for schizophrenia that acts as a D2
antagonist with additional serotonergic properties. In the present
invention, the D2-antagonist axis addresses the *motor-output
disorganization and salience dysregulation* substrate of CD-AMC.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an M1/M4 agonist in combination with
a D2 antagonist for CD-AMC specifically.

The present invention recognizes that muscarinic facilitation of
behavioral initiation and cortical gating (a xanomeline M1/M4 axis)
and stabilization of dopaminergic motor-output dysregulation
(a perospirone D2 axis) are complementary and convergent substrates
for treatment of CD-AMC. The combination is expected to produce
greater reduction in catatonic symptom burden than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose, without asserting
disease-modification of the underlying medical condition.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) xanomeline or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) perospirone or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, optionally further comprising a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic agent such as trospium chloride, together with one
or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Catatonic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition in a human
subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject
a therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline and a
therapeutically effective amount of perospirone, optionally further
with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) xanomeline or a salt thereof, optionally co-formulated with
trospium, in a first unitary dosage form, (b) perospirone or a
salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form, and (c) written
instructions for the treatment of CD-AMC according to a dosing
regimen disclosed herein while continuing standard care for the
underlying medical condition.

The invention contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about
50 mg to about 250 mg administered orally as the free base or as
the tartrate salt in two divided doses; when xanomeline is
co-administered with trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is
about 40 mg to about 60 mg administered orally in two divided
doses. The invention contemplates perospirone in a daily dose of
about 4 mg to about 48 mg administered orally in one or two divided
doses, with a preferred dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in
subjects with CD-AMC, in catatonic symptom severity, psychomotor
freezing or agitation, and caregiver / medical burden, that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of xanomeline and
perospirone administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-
yl]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine, an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist. The term as used herein includes the free base,
the tartrate salt and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Trospium chloride"* or *"trospium"* means the quaternary ammonium
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist that does not
appreciably cross the blood-brain barrier and may be used to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline.

*"Catatonic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition"* or
*"CD-AMC"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 syndrome in which a
catatonic syndrome with motoric, behavioral, and speech
abnormalities is the direct physiologic consequence of a defined
medical condition.

*"Catatonic-symptom composite"* means a clinically meaningful burden
of stupor, mutism, negativism, posturing, agitation, echophenomena,
or related motoric catatonic signs quantified by structured
clinician assessment or validated catatonia rating scales.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the
treatment of CD-AMC according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
catatonic-symptom composite, motoric subscore, oral intake,
immobility duration, caregiver / medical burden, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two or more active agents to a
subject so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted
on the subject during the period in which the pharmacological
effect of the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether
the agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. Activation of
the M1 receptor facilitates cortical and hippocampal signal
integration, while activation of the M4 receptor contributes to
behavioral gating in striatal circuits. Xanomeline reached the
market in fixed-dose combination with trospium chloride as Cobenfy®
for schizophrenia in adults and is under live U.S. brand-patent
protection via the marketed combination and related rights.

#### 2.2 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is reduction of motor-output disorganization and
dopaminergic salience imbalance in catatonic states.

#### 2.3 Trospium (optional)

Trospium chloride is a quaternary ammonium peripherally restricted
muscarinic antagonist that does not appreciably cross the blood-
brain barrier. In the present invention, trospium may be included
at a daily dose effective to mitigate peripheral cholinergic
adverse effects of xanomeline without materially antagonizing the
central behavioral-gating effect.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise xanomeline and
perospirone, optionally further comprising trospium, together with
at least one pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The compositions
may take the form of a co-packaged kit of two or three separate
dosage forms or a unitary fixed-dose combination.

The mass ratio of xanomeline (calculated as the free base) to
perospirone (calculated as the free base) is in the range of about
2:1 to about 50:1, preferably about 4:1 to about 20:1. When
trospium is included, the molar ratio of trospium to xanomeline is
approximately 1:1 to 1:3.

### 4. Dosage forms

Xanomeline (with or without trospium) is preferably formulated as
twice-daily oral capsules. Perospirone is preferably formulated as
an immediate-release oral tablet. Subjects unable to swallow in the
acute catatonic phase may receive the regimen after stabilization
and restoration of oral intake, or via alternative enteral
administration where pharmaceutically feasible.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
xanomeline or xanomeline-trospium capsules, (b) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to
inpatient or closely supervised catatonia management.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with CD-AMC, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Xanomeline-trospium 50 mg/20 mg orally twice daily for the first
  three days, then 100 mg/20 mg twice daily for the next four days,
  then 125 mg/30 mg twice daily thereafter as tolerated.
* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily for 7 days, then 8 mg twice
  daily thereafter, with titration up to 12 mg twice daily if
  clinically needed.

Where trospium is omitted, xanomeline may be administered at an
equivalent free-base exposure range with heightened monitoring for
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects.

Hepatic adjustment: xanomeline-trospium is not recommended in
moderate or severe hepatic impairment. Severe renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP2D6 inhibitors without dose review of xanomeline and
perospirone. Avoid strong CYP3A4 inducers. QT monitoring should be
considered when other QT-prolonging agents are present. Standard
lorazepam or ECT rescue pathways remain available and are not
displaced by the present invention.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
CD-AMC. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: clinician-confirmed catatonic syndrome attributable to a
defined medical condition; clinically meaningful catatonic signs at
baseline; medical stabilization sufficient for protocolized drug
administration; and active management of the underlying medical
condition. Exclusion criteria include malignant catatonia requiring
different emergent management, unstable autonomic crisis, inability
to safely administer oral medication, severe hepatic or renal
impairment, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

CD-AMC reflects a severe failure of motoric and behavioral
initiation / gating, often with episodes of stupor, mutism,
negativism, or agitation. A single pharmacologic axis may be
insufficient when both cholinergic network dysfunction and
dopaminergic motor-output dysregulation are present.

Xanomeline addresses the cortical / striatal gating layer through
M1/M4 agonism. Perospirone addresses the motor-output
disorganization and salience imbalance layer through D2 antagonism.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on the catatonic-symptom phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in catatonic symptom burden and improved return
of functional behavior than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 35,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: M1 receptor binding and calcium-mobilization
assays; M4 receptor functional assays; D2 receptor binding and
functional antagonism assays for perospirone; and combined
behavioral-gating network assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include immobility / motor-initiation tasks, catatonia-like models
where validated, prepulse inhibition, and disease-relevant
encephalitic or metabolic models producing motoric shutdown or
agitated motor dyscontrol. A supra-additive effect of the
combination on behavioral initiation and reduction of catatonic
motor signs, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
xanomeline-trospium 100 mg/20 mg twice daily for 7 days; Period 2:
perospirone titrated to 16 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the
combination for 7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and
tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal
purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of clinically
meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult CD-AMC (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 60 (20 per arm), given the acuity and
relative rarity of the syndrome.

* Arm A (combination): xanomeline + perospirone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): perospirone + placebo for
  xanomeline.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard-of-care medical
  management.

All subjects continue aggressive management of the underlying
medical condition and access to lorazepam / ECT rescue where
necessary. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in
Bush–Francis Catatonia Rating Scale composite at week 4.
Secondary endpoints include motoric subscore, oral intake,
immobility duration, caregiver / medical burden, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of an M1/M4 agonist with a D2
antagonist for CD-AMC specifically. The closest hits are:

* *Antiglucocorticoids for the treatment of catatonia* — discloses a
different mechanistic approach to catatonia and does not teach the
present xanomeline + perospirone combination.
* *Methods and compositions for treatment of disorders ameliorated
  by muscarinic receptor activation* and *Analogs of xanomeline*
  disclose muscarinic agonism but do not teach perospirone
  co-administration or catatonia due to another medical condition.
* Generic psychosis / schizophrenia filings on perospirone or
  related D2 agents do not teach the present combination or
  catatonic syndrome.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (M1/M4 agonism +
D2 antagonism), (ii) the specific actives (xanomeline +
perospirone), and (iii) the specific indication (Catatonic
Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition). No prior-art
disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on which the
present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Catatonic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
muscarinic M1/M4 agonist xanomeline and a therapeutically effective
amount of the D2 antagonist perospirone, optionally further with
the peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium chloride, are
disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping motoric / behavioral
gating and dopaminergic output circuits implicated in medically
caused catatonia and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
symptomatic improvement than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose while continuing standard care for the underlying
medical condition.
