# Fixed-dose pharmaceutical products for treating Intermittent Explosive Disorder with perospirone and oxymetazoline

**U.S. Provisional Patent Application under 35 U.S.C. 111(b)**

## Cross-reference to related applications

None. This specification is prepared as a first-filed U.S. provisional application and does not claim priority to any earlier application.

## Field of the invention

The invention relates to fixed-dose pharmaceutical products, fixed-dose combination product packages, methods of treatment, kits, dose-ratio embodiments, release-profile embodiments, and manufacturing embodiments for treating Intermittent Explosive Disorder with perospirone and oxymetazoline.

## Background of the invention

Intermittent Explosive Disorder is a central-nervous-system, psychiatric, neurocognitive, sleep, substance-use, or mental-health condition with remaining need for better-tolerated, more adherent, and more precisely monitored pharmacologic regimens.

The present disclosure converts a coadministration concept into a fixed-dose medication product posture. The active agents are selected to engage DRD2 antagonism + α2A agonism. The fixed-dose posture is limited by route feasibility, component contribution, compatibility, stability, release performance, safety monitoring, and written-description support.

This specification does not represent completed combination testing. Statements marked MODELED are based on repository metadata, public-source search links, known pharmacologic context, and drafting inference. Statements marked PROPHETIC describe prospective work. Statements marked DATA NEEDED identify information that must be generated or verified before relying on broader claim scope.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a multi-unit fixed labeled regimen comprising fixed amounts of perospirone and oxymetazoline for use in treating Intermittent Explosive Disorder.

The route-feasibility classification for this application is **multi_unit_fixed_regimen**. MODELED: the active routes differ (perospirone: oral; oxymetazoline: intranasal), so the fixed-dose product is drafted as a fixed labeled regimen with route-specific dosage units in one package rather than as an implausible co-formulated unit.

In one embodiment, the product is fixed-dose combination product package with oral perospirone unit and intranasal oxymetazoline unit. Dose strengths are disclosed as low, standard, and high fixed-dose levels derived from individual-active dose contexts, subject to label review, pharmacokinetic review, tolerability review, and component-contribution confirmation.

In additional aspects, the invention provides methods of treatment, kit instructions, manufacturing routes, stability protocols, dissolution or route-performance protocols, and component-contribution study designs.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

"Intermittent Explosive Disorder" means the disorder as defined by DSM-5-TR, ICD-11, or an equivalent clinical diagnostic framework, including only subtypes and severity levels supported by the disclosure.

"Fixed-dose pharmaceutical product" means a product in which predetermined amounts of two active agents are supplied in a single physical dosage form or in route-specific dosage units packaged and labeled for a fixed regimen.

"Single physical dosage form" means a tablet, capsule, film, spray, injection, depot, or other dosage unit in which both active agents are contained in one administered unit.

"Fixed labeled regimen" means written instructions that specify the strength, route, timing, titration, monitoring, contraindication checks, and discontinuation criteria for the packaged active agents.

"MODELED" means a statement derived from computational, repository, mechanistic, public-source, or pharmacologic rationale without completed combination testing.

"PROPHETIC" means a prospective example, study design, manufacturing protocol, or endpoint framework that has not been carried out for the disclosed fixed-dose product.

"DATA NEEDED" means evidence that is not presently represented by this package and must be generated or verified before final filing reliance, clinical use, or non-provisional claim expansion.

### 2. Active agents

Perospirone is the first active agent. Repository metadata identifies this active as a D2/5-HT2A antagonist (atypical antipsychotic), with reference route oral, reference dosage form immediate-release tablet, divided dosing, dose context 4 to 48 mg/day, metabolism or clearance CYP3A4, and safety liability EPS; hyperprolactinemia; weight gain.

Oxymetazoline is the second active agent. Repository metadata identifies this active as a α1A/α2A adrenergic agonist, with reference route intranasal, reference dosage form metered-dose nasal spray, dose context 0.5 to 2 mg/day, metabolism or clearance minimal hepatic; predominantly excreted unchanged, and safety liability rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa).

A pharmaceutically acceptable salt, hydrate, solvate, polymorph, isotopically labeled form, prodrug, or co-crystal is included only for embodiments with written-description, manufacturing, analytical, and clinical-pharmacology support.

### 3. Fixed-dose product strategy

The fixed-dose product strategy is fixed-dose combination product package with oral perospirone unit and intranasal oxymetazoline unit.

MODELED: the active routes differ (perospirone: oral; oxymetazoline: intranasal), so the fixed-dose product is drafted as a fixed labeled regimen with route-specific dosage units in one package rather than as an implausible co-formulated unit.

For same-route embodiments, a single physical fixed-dose dosage form may be used when compatibility, stability, release, content-uniformity, and bioavailability support are available. For mixed-route embodiments, the product is a package with route-specific dosage units and a fixed labeled regimen. For development-asset embodiments, clinical and CMC support are DATA NEEDED before any clinical-use assertion.

### 4. Dose ratios and strengths

MODELED: fixed-dose strengths are derived from repository individual-active dose contexts and are not completed combination dose-ranging results.

Nominal ratio range: 4 mg to 48 mg perospirone per 0.5 mg to 2 mg oxymetazoline.

| Strength level | Nominal daily fixed strength |
|---|---|
| low | 4 mg perospirone / 0.5 mg oxymetazoline |
| standard | 26 mg perospirone / 1.25 mg oxymetazoline |
| high | 48 mg perospirone / 2 mg oxymetazoline |

The disclosed strengths may be adjusted for renal function, hepatic function, age, body weight, sex, pharmacogenomic status, interacting medications, route-specific bioavailability, tolerability, and regulatory label constraints.

### 5. Compatibility, stability, and release

### 8. Safety, interactions, and monitoring

Administration includes baseline medication review, contraindication screening, adverse-event monitoring, endpoint tracking, and route-specific counseling.

* perospirone: EPS; hyperprolactinemia; weight gain
* oxymetazoline: rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa)
* perospirone metabolism/clearance: CYP3A4
* oxymetazoline metabolism/clearance: minimal hepatic; predominantly excreted unchanged
* route-specific administration errors are mitigated by unit-of-use packaging and fixed labeled timing instructions

### 9. Subject populations

Preferred subjects have confirmed Intermittent Explosive Disorder, a defined baseline endpoint burden, and inadequate response, intolerance, residual symptoms, relapse risk, adherence limitations, or functional impairment after at least one standard-care intervention, unless a protocol justifies first-line investigational use.

Exclusion criteria include high-acuity psychiatric or medical states requiring immediate intervention, contraindications to either active agent, unsafe interacting medications, pregnancy or lactation concerns where relevant, inability to follow route-specific instructions, and inability to complete the monitoring plan.
