## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Intermittent Explosive Disorder (IED) by
co-administering (a) a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist, in
particular perospirone, and (b) an alpha2A-adrenergic agonist, in
particular oxymetazoline, preferably in a rapid-onset intranasal or
other fast-delivery rescue formulation. The invention is intended
for subjects with recurrent impulsive aggressive outbursts and
addresses hostile salience, attack-like rage escalation, autonomic
surge, and failure of behavioral inhibition. The invention does not
contemplate chronic unsupervised overuse of topical nasal
decongestant dosing.

## Background of the invention

Intermittent Explosive Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and
ICD-11 as a disorder of recurrent behavioral outbursts representing
failure to control aggressive impulses, as manifested by verbal or
physical aggression out of proportion to provocation or by more
severe aggressive episodes causing damage or assaultive behavior.
The outbursts are impulsive, not premeditated, and are not
committed to achieve a tangible objective.

The prevalence of clinically meaningful impulsive aggression is
substantial and the burden of IED is high. The disorder is
associated with injuries, legal problems, school and occupational
impairment, family conflict, shame, healthcare utilization, and
comorbidity with mood disorders, anxiety disorders, substance use,
ADHD, trauma-spectrum symptoms, and personality pathology. Many
subjects describe a rising physiologic surge, narrowing of
attention, and rapid transition from irritability to explosive
behavior.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological in the first
instance and consists primarily of psychotherapy, anger-management
skills, environmental modification, and treatment of comorbid
conditions. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United
States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for the
treatment of IED as a primary indication. Borrowed pharmacologic
strategies from mood, antipsychotic, or serotonergic practice are
heterogeneous and often fail to provide both baseline reduction in
aggressive salience and rapid containment of autonomic escalation.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, IED includes hostile attribution, aggressive
salience misassignment, and failure to inhibit aggressive action
once triggered. Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic approved
in Japan for schizophrenia that functions as a D2 receptor
antagonist with additional serotonergic properties. In the present
invention, the perospirone axis addresses the *aggressive salience,
hostile attribution, and action-release* substrate of IED.

Second, many explosive episodes are preceded by rapid adrenergic
surge manifested as facial flushing, tachycardia, tremor, chest
tightness, escalating tension, and narrowing of behavioral control.
Oxymetazoline is an imidazoline alpha-adrenergic agonist with
recognized alpha2A activity and established intranasal formulation
pathways. The present invention is based on the recognition that a
tightly limited, rapid-onset, prodrome-aligned alpha2A agonist
intervention can dampen escalating autonomic arousal and buy time
for behavioral inhibition before an explosive outburst becomes
fully expressed. The oxymetazoline axis therefore addresses the
*adrenergic-escalation and rapid de-control* substrate of IED.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a D2 antagonist in combination with
an alpha2A agonist for IED specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of aggressive
salience and hostile action-readiness (a perospirone D2 axis) and
attenuation of prodromal adrenergic escalation (an oxymetazoline
alpha2A axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for
treatment of IED. The combination is expected to produce greater
reduction in outburst frequency, outburst intensity, and time-to-
de-escalation than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose, while expressly avoiding chronic unsupervised
nasal overuse and not replacing emergency intervention for
imminent violence risk.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) perospirone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) oxymetazoline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating IED
in a human subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to
the subject a therapeutically effective amount of perospirone and a
therapeutically effective amount of oxymetazoline, wherein the
oxymetazoline component is preferably used as a rapid-onset rescue
intervention during prodromal anger-escalation periods and is not
used as chronic unrestricted daily nasal therapy.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) perospirone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) oxymetazoline or a salt thereof in a second rapid-onset dosage
form, and (c) written instructions for the treatment of IED
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, together with
warnings on cardiovascular risk, rebound-congestion risk, and
restricted use frequency.

The invention contemplates perospirone in a daily dose of about
4 mg to about 48 mg administered orally in one or two divided doses,
with a preferred dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day. The invention
contemplates oxymetazoline in a rescue dose of about 25 mcg to
about 200 mcg intranasally or by another fast-delivery route
administered during prodromal escalation or up to about 30 minutes
before a foreseeable high-risk trigger, with a preferred use
frequency of about 4 escalation-days per week or less. The
inventive feature is the combination of baseline D2-mediated
aggression-salience control with rapid alpha2A-mediated autonomic
de-escalation.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with IED, in explosive-outburst frequency, explosive-intensity
severity, autonomic-escalation burden, and violence-related
functional impairment, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of perospirone and oxymetazoline administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Oxymetazoline"* means the imidazoline alpha-adrenergic agonist
commonly formulated for intranasal or topical use. The term as used
herein includes oxymetazoline free base, hydrochloride or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and rapid-onset delivery
formats suitable for restricted rescue use.

*"Intermittent Explosive Disorder"* or *"IED"* means the DSM-5-TR /
ICD-11 disorder of recurrent impulsive aggressive outbursts that
are grossly out of proportion to provocation and not premeditated
for tangible gain.

*"Outburst-frequency score"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
verbal or physical aggressive outbursts over a defined interval as
quantified by structured clinician assessment, caregiver report, or
validated aggression instruments.

*"Prodromal escalation"* means the interval before a fully
expressed aggressive outburst during which autonomic activation,
subjective rage build-up, or behavioral warning signs indicate high
imminent risk of explosive behavior.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of IED according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
outburst-frequency score, outburst-intensity score, time-to-
de-escalation, autonomic-escalation severity, caregiver burden, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is attenuation of aggressive salience, hostile
attribution, and action-release tendencies.

#### 2.2 Oxymetazoline

Oxymetazoline is an imidazoline alpha-adrenergic agonist with
recognized alpha2A activity and rapid intranasal formulation
pathways. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of oxymetazoline is acute damping of adrenergic surge
and support of de-escalation when used in a tightly limited rescue
format.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise perospirone and
oxymetazoline, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of an oral perospirone dosage form and an
intranasal or other rapid-onset oxymetazoline rescue dosage form.

Excipients useful for the perospirone composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the
oxymetazoline rescue component include buffered aqueous carriers,
stabilizers, isotonic agents, preservatives where appropriate, and
metered-delivery devices suitable for low-dose rescue use.

### 4. Dosage forms

Perospirone is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
one or two daily doses. Oxymetazoline is preferably formulated as a
metered intranasal spray or other fast-delivery rescue unit.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, (b) a limited number of oxymetazoline rescue
units consistent with restricted prodrome-aligned use, and
(c) written instructions aligned to anger-management skills,
trigger tracking, and safety planning.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with IED, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily, titratable to 8 mg twice
  daily and then to 12 mg twice daily if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.
* Oxymetazoline about 25 mcg to about 200 mcg intranasally during
  prodromal escalation or up to about 30 minutes before a
  foreseeable high-risk trigger, with preferred maximum use
  frequency of about 4 escalation-days per week or less.

The preferred embodiment expressly excludes chronic unrestricted
multiple-times-daily decongestant use. Rescue administration is
intended to be episodic and linked to identifiable escalation risk.

Medication-medication interactions: perospirone carries
antipsychotic-class monitoring considerations including sedation,
orthostasis, and extrapyramidal effects. Oxymetazoline should be
used with caution in subjects with uncontrolled hypertension,
cardiac arrhythmia, narrow-angle glaucoma, or other unsafe
adrenergic sensitivity. The regimen is not intended to replace
emergency intervention where there is imminent risk of serious
violence.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
IED. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 12 years; recurrent impulsive aggressive outbursts
out of proportion to provocation; clinically meaningful distress or
functional impairment; and willingness to participate in behavioral
treatment and trigger tracking. Exclusion criteria include active
mania, uncontrolled substance intoxication or withdrawal as the
primary cause of outbursts, severe cardiovascular instability,
untreated primary psychotic disorder driving aggression, and known
hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

IED often includes two interacting layers: hostile salience and
failure of behavioral inhibition, and rapid adrenergic escalation
that drives loss of control once anger begins to rise.

Perospirone addresses the hostile-salience layer through D2
antagonism. Oxymetazoline addresses the adrenergic-escalation layer
through alpha2A agonist rescue signaling. The two axes are non-
overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the same
explosive-outburst phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in IED burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 28,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: D2 receptor binding and functional antagonism
assays for perospirone; alpha2A receptor binding and functional
agonism assays for oxymetazoline; and combined salience /
de-escalation network assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include aggression and frustration paradigms, provoked attack-like
behavior models, autonomic-escalation tasks, and startle / stress-
recovery paradigms. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
reduction of aggressive outbursts and acceleration of de-escalation,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
perospirone 8 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: supervised intranasal
oxymetazoline rescue administration on repeated study days; Period
3: the combination. Full PK sampling, cardiovascular monitoring,
sedation monitoring, and tolerability assessments are obtained. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult IED (FIG. 4). Three arms,
1:1:1 randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): perospirone + prodrome-aligned oxymetazoline
  rescue.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): perospirone + placebo rescue.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo tablets + placebo rescue, with
  structured behavioral treatment.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in outburst-frequency
score over 12 weeks. Secondary endpoints include outburst-intensity
score, time-to-de-escalation, autonomic-escalation severity,
caregiver burden, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of a D2 antagonist with an alpha2A
agonist for IED specifically. The closest hits are:

* Oxymetazoline formulation patents disclose nasal or topical
  formulations and do not teach the present psychiatric de-
  escalation use.
* Generic aggression-treatment patents disclose other mechanisms
  and do not teach the present pair or prodrome-aligned rescue
  architecture.
* Antipsychotic formulation patents disclose perospirone dosage
  forms and not the present convergent-circuit rationale in IED.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (D2 antagonism +
alpha2A agonism), (ii) the specific actives (perospirone +
oxymetazoline), and (iii) the specific indication (IED). No
prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on
which the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Intermittent Explosive Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of perospirone and a
therapeutically effective amount of oxymetazoline are disclosed,
wherein perospirone provides background reduction of aggressive
salience and oxymetazoline provides a restricted, rapid-onset,
prodrome-aligned de-escalation intervention. The two agents act on
non-overlapping aggressive-salience and autonomic-escalation
circuits and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in explosive outburst frequency and intensity than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
