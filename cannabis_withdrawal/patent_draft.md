## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Cannabis Withdrawal by co-administering (a) a
cannabinoid CB1 receptor agonist, in particular delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol (Δ9-THC), and (b) a beta-adrenoceptor
antagonist, in particular betaxolol. The invention is intended for
medically supervised withdrawal management after cessation or
reduction of cannabis use and addresses irritability, sleep
disturbance, autonomic arousal, appetite loss, dysphoria, and
craving-linked distress.

## Background of the invention

Cannabis Withdrawal is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
clinically meaningful withdrawal syndrome that develops after
cessation of heavy and prolonged cannabis use. Common symptoms
include irritability, anger or aggression, nervousness or anxiety,
sleep difficulty, decreased appetite or weight loss, restlessness,
depressed mood, and physical symptoms such as abdominal pain,
tremor, sweating, fever, chills, or headache. Symptoms typically
begin within about 24 hours of cessation, peak over the first
several days, and may persist for one to three weeks or longer.

The prevalence and clinical significance of cannabis withdrawal are
substantial because of the widespread use of cannabis and the
frequency with which withdrawal symptoms drive relapse. The
syndrome is associated with distress, insomnia, impaired treatment
retention, irritability, and repeated return to cannabis use as a
form of self-medication. There is currently no FDA-approved
medication specifically for cannabis withdrawal as a primary
indication.

The current standard of care is supportive and symptom-based. It
consists primarily of reassurance, sleep hygiene, hydration,
nutrition support, and management of co-occurring psychiatric
symptoms. Various off-label pharmacologic approaches have been
studied, but no clearly established medication standard has
emerged. As a result, there remains a meaningful need for a
mechanism-based regimen that can reduce the severity of the core
withdrawal syndrome and support early abstinence.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, cannabis withdrawal is a state of CB1 receptor
under-stimulation after cessation of chronic exogenous cannabinoid
exposure, producing dysphoria, appetite loss, sleep disturbance,
and craving. Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol, available clinically as
oral dronabinol, is a CB1 receptor agonist. Human laboratory
studies have shown that oral dronabinol can suppress cannabis
withdrawal in a dose-dependent manner, with published regimens in
the approximate range of 30 mg/day to 120 mg/day. The CB1 agonist
axis therefore addresses the *core reward-deficit, anorexia, sleep
disturbance, and craving substrate* of cannabis withdrawal.

Second, cannabis withdrawal also includes sympathetic and autonomic
activation manifested as restlessness, tremor, sweating, internal
agitation, and physiologic distress. Betaxolol is a beta1-selective
beta-adrenoceptor antagonist approved for hypertension and glaucoma
that reduces heart rate, adrenergic surge, and somatic distress.
Relative to nonselective beta blockers, the beta1-selective
profile may offer a more tolerable cardiovascular-autonomic
intervention in medically supervised settings. The betaxolol axis
therefore addresses the *adrenergic-autonomic substrate* of
cannabis withdrawal.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a CB1 agonist in combination with a
beta1-selective antagonist for Cannabis Withdrawal specifically.

The present invention recognizes that cannabinoid replacement /
stabilization of CB1 under-stimulation (a Δ9-THC CB1 axis) and
beta1-selective suppression of autonomic hyperarousal
(a betaxolol axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for
the symptomatic treatment of Cannabis Withdrawal. The combination
is expected to produce a greater reduction in withdrawal distress,
sleep disruption, craving, and appetite loss than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, prodrug, isomeric formulation, or
clinically equivalent CB1 agonist thereof, and (b) betaxolol or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, or prodrug thereof,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Cannabis Withdrawal in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol and a therapeutically effective
amount of betaxolol. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol or a salt thereof in a first
unitary dosage form, (b) betaxolol or a salt thereof in a second
unitary dosage form, and (c) written instructions for the
treatment of Cannabis Withdrawal according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein in a medically supervised setting.

The invention contemplates delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol in a daily
dose of about 2.5 mg to about 120 mg administered orally in divided
doses, with a preferred dose range of 10 mg to 60 mg/day in divided
doses during the acute withdrawal interval. The invention
contemplates betaxolol in a daily dose of about 2.5 mg to about
40 mg administered orally once or twice daily, with a preferred
dose of 5 mg to 20 mg/day. The inventive feature is the concurrent
engagement of the CB1 and beta1-adrenergic axes during the acute
withdrawal window in a cannabis-specific syndrome.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with Cannabis Withdrawal, in composite withdrawal severity,
irritability, sleep disturbance, appetite loss, autonomic arousal,
and craving-linked distress, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol and betaxolol
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol"* or *"Δ9-THC"* means the principal
psychoactive phytocannabinoid acting as a CB1 receptor agonist,
including synthetic dronabinol, oral formulations, and
pharmaceutically acceptable equivalents that reproduce clinically
relevant CB1 agonism.

*"Betaxolol"* means the beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor
antagonist approved in oral and ophthalmic formulations. The term
includes betaxolol free base, hydrochloride or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
and prodrugs.

*"Cannabis Withdrawal"* means the withdrawal syndrome recognized in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 after cessation or reduction of heavy and
prolonged cannabis use, characterized by irritability, sleep
difficulty, decreased appetite, anxiety, restlessness, depressed
mood, craving, and / or associated physical symptoms.

*"Composite withdrawal severity"* means a clinician-rated or
subject-rated multi-domain score capturing irritability, sleep
disturbance, appetite loss, autonomic arousal, and craving.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the
treatment of Cannabis Withdrawal according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in
at least one of: composite withdrawal severity, sleep score,
appetite restoration, irritability score, craving-linked distress,
or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol

Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol is a CB1 receptor agonist that
modulates appetite, reward physiology, affective tone, and sleep.
Clinically, oral synthetic delta-9-THC is available as dronabinol.
Human laboratory studies have shown that oral dronabinol can
suppress cannabis withdrawal in a dose-dependent manner and improve
appetite and subjective withdrawal symptoms. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of delta-9-THC
is reversal of CB1 under-stimulation during acute cannabis
withdrawal.

#### 2.2 Betaxolol

Betaxolol is a beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist used in
the treatment of hypertension and glaucoma. In oral use, betaxolol
reduces heart rate, adrenergic drive, and sympathetic symptoms. In
the present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
betaxolol is reduction of withdrawal-associated autonomic
hyperarousal and internal agitation.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol and betaxolol, together with at least one
pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The compositions may take
the form of a co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as
an oral dronabinol capsule / solution and a betaxolol oral tablet.

Excipients useful for the delta-9-THC composition include oral
solution vehicles, gelatin capsule excipients, lipid carriers, and
stabilizers suitable for controlled-substance pharmaceutical
handling. Excipients useful for the betaxolol composition include
diluents (microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate),
binders (povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Delta-9-THC is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or oral
solution suitable for divided dosing in a medically supervised
setting. Inhaled formulations are not preferred because they
complicate dose standardization and may mimic the route associated
with use behavior. Betaxolol is preferably formulated as an
immediate-release oral tablet.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 7-day supply of oral
delta-9-THC dosage units, (b) a 7-day supply of betaxolol tablets,
and (c) written instructions aligned to medically supervised
withdrawal management and symptom monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with Cannabis Withdrawal, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Delta-9-THC (e.g., oral dronabinol equivalent) 5 mg orally
  three times daily on day 1, titratable to 10 mg four times daily
  as clinically needed, with a broad daily range of 2.5 mg to
  120 mg.
* Betaxolol 5 mg orally once daily on day 1, titratable to 10 mg
  or 20 mg/day according to heart rate, blood pressure, and
  tolerability.

The regimen is preferably limited to the acute withdrawal period
(for example, about 3 to 10 days) and administered only in a
medically supervised setting.

Hepatic adjustment: severe hepatic impairment or unstable
cardiovascular disease is exclusionary. Bradycardia, significant
hypotension, decompensated heart failure, or clinically relevant
conduction abnormality are contraindications to betaxolol use in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration of
delta-9-THC with other strong CNS depressants unless medically
necessary and supervised. Use caution with other beta blockers,
calcium-channel blockers, or QT-prolonging agents.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
presenting with clinically meaningful Cannabis Withdrawal after
cessation or reduction of heavy and prolonged cannabis use.
Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population include:
age ≥ 18 years; recent cessation or reduction of cannabis use;
clinically meaningful withdrawal symptoms including irritability,
sleep disturbance, appetite loss, or craving; and availability of
medically supervised monitoring. Exclusion criteria include active
cannabis intoxication requiring acute management, active psychosis,
severe bradycardia, severe hypotension, unstable cardiovascular
disease, pregnancy, and inability to comply with supervised
treatment.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Cannabis Withdrawal is fundamentally a state of CB1 under-
stimulation after cessation of chronic exogenous cannabinoid
exposure. The syndrome has two interacting layers: a reward-deficit
layer producing dysphoria, appetite loss, and craving, and an
autonomic-arousal layer producing irritability, restlessness, and
somatic tension.

Delta-9-THC addresses the first layer by restoring CB1 agonism in a
controlled and taperable manner. Betaxolol addresses the second
layer through beta1-selective reduction of sympathetic overdrive.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on the same cannabis-withdrawal distress phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in acute cannabis-withdrawal burden than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: CB1 receptor binding and functional agonism
assays for delta-9-THC; beta1-adrenoceptor binding and antagonism
assays for betaxolol; and combined stress-hormone / autonomic
signaling assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include cannabinoid-withdrawal models, appetite / feeding assays,
sleep-disruption paradigms, and withdrawal-like irritability /
distress paradigms. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
withdrawal-like irritability, sleep disruption, and appetite loss,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1: oral
delta-9-THC equivalent 10 mg three times daily for 3 days;
Period 2: betaxolol 10 mg once daily for 3 days; Period 3: the
combination at the same doses for 3 days. Full PK sampling,
heart-rate and blood-pressure monitoring, and CNS tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK or
cardiovascular interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult Cannabis Withdrawal (FIG. 4). Three arms,
1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): delta-9-THC + betaxolol.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): delta-9-THC + placebo for betaxolol.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard supportive care.

All subjects remain in medically supervised withdrawal management.
The primary endpoint is change from baseline in composite
withdrawal severity over days 1–5. Secondary endpoints include
sleep score, appetite restoration, irritability score,
craving-linked distress, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Cannabis Withdrawal comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol
and a therapeutically effective amount of the beta1-selective
antagonist betaxolol are disclosed. The two agents act on
non-overlapping CB1-mediated reward-deficit and beta1-mediated
autonomic-arousal circuits implicated in acute cannabis withdrawal
and are expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in
withdrawal severity, sleep disruption, appetite loss, irritability,
and craving-linked distress than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose in a medically supervised setting.
