## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Delirium by co-administering (a) a histamine H3
receptor antagonist or inverse agonist and (b) a muscarinic M1/M4
receptor agonist, in particular the combination of pitolisant and
xanomeline, optionally further with a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic such as trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic
adverse effects. The invention is intended for medically supervised
subjects with delirium after initiation of etiologic workup and
supportive management, particularly in selected hypoactive or mixed
delirium states with impaired attention and arousal. The invention
is not intended to replace treatment of the underlying cause,
management of alcohol-withdrawal delirium, or emergency containment
of uncontrolled severe agitation.

## Background of the invention

Delirium is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as an acute disorder
of attention, awareness, and cognition that develops over a short
period, tends to fluctuate, and arises as a direct physiologic
consequence of one or more medical causes. The syndrome may present
in hyperactive, hypoactive, or mixed forms, with impaired attention,
disorganized thinking, fluctuating arousal, disturbed sleep-wake
cycle, and severe functional destabilization.

The prevalence of delirium is clinically meaningful across
hospitalized medical, surgical, critically ill, and older-adult
populations. The burden is substantial because delirium is
associated with longer hospitalization, functional decline,
caregiver distress, institutionalization risk, and mortality.
Hypoactive forms in particular are frequently under-recognized and
may manifest as impaired wakefulness, attentional collapse, and
severe apathy-like disengagement rather than overt agitation.

The current standard of care is etiologic and supportive. It
consists primarily of identification and treatment of precipitating
medical causes, reorientation, sleep optimization, mobilization,
avoidance of deliriogenic medications, and in selected cases short-
term symptomatic pharmacotherapy. No pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
specifically for delirium as a primary indication. Existing off-
label antipsychotic use often aims at agitation control rather than
restoration of attention and arousal, and may worsen cognitive
dulling in selected subjects.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses is the
absence of a defensible, mechanism-based symptomatic regimen for
the attention-plus-arousal phenotype of delirium, especially in
hypoactive or mixed forms. A single-agent approach often leaves one
dimension undertreated: a purely wake-promoting intervention may not
restore cortical cholinergic signal quality, while a purely
cholinergic strategy may not overcome severe vigilance collapse.

The present invention recognizes that two non-overlapping CNS axes
can be combined to support attention and arousal in delirium.
First, the histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic auto- and
heteroreceptor that suppresses histamine release and modulates the
release of acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine. Selective
inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant elevates extracellular
histamine and supports wakefulness and attention. The pitolisant
axis therefore addresses the *hypoarousal, vigilance collapse, and
attention fluctuation* substrate of delirium.

Second, muscarinic M1 receptor signaling facilitates cortical and
hippocampal cognition, while M4 signaling contributes to behavioral
stabilization. Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist
developed in the 1990s and more recently formulated with the
peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium as the FDA-approved
xanomeline-trospium combination (Cobenfy®) for schizophrenia in
adults. The xanomeline axis therefore addresses the *cortical
cholinergic attentional and cognitive-integration substrate* of
delirium.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an H3 antagonist or inverse agonist
in combination with a muscarinic M1/M4 agonist for delirium
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance- and arousal-
enabled cognitive support (a pitolisant H3 axis) and post-synaptic
muscarinic facilitation of cortical attention (a xanomeline M1/M4
axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of
delirium. The combination is expected to produce greater
symptomatic benefit in attention, arousal, and delirium severity
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose,
without asserting cure of the precipitating medical condition.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, (b) xanomeline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, and (c) optionally a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic agent such as trospium chloride in an amount
effective to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Delirium in a human subject in need thereof, comprising co-
administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of
pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline,
optionally further with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic,
while continuing etiologic and supportive delirium management.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) xanomeline or a salt thereof, optionally co-formulated with
trospium, in a second unitary dosage form, and (c) written
instructions for symptomatic treatment of delirium according to a
dosing regimen disclosed herein in a supervised medical setting.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily. The
invention contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about 50 mg to
about 250 mg administered orally as the free base or as the tartrate
salt in two divided doses; when xanomeline is co-administered with
trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is about 40 mg to about
60 mg administered orally in two divided doses.

The technical effect of the combination is improvement, in
selected subjects with delirium, in attention, arousal stability,
and delirium-severity burden, greater than would be obtained with
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse
agonist marketed as Wakix®. The term includes pitolisant free base,
hydrochloride or other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent H3 inverse agonists.

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-
yl]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine, an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist. The term as used herein includes the free base,
the tartrate salt and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Trospium chloride"* or *"trospium"* means the quaternary ammonium
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist that does not
appreciably cross the blood-brain barrier and may be used to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline.

*"Delirium"* means the acute disorder of attention, awareness, and
cognition defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11, including hypoactive and
mixed presentations, but excluding acute alcohol-withdrawal delirium
and untreated primary intoxication states in the preferred
embodiment.

*"Delirium-severity composite"* means a clinically meaningful burden
of inattention, fluctuating arousal, disorganized thinking, or
sleep-wake disruption quantified by structured clinician assessment
or validated delirium instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of delirium according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
delirium-severity composite, attention score, arousal stability,
sleep-wake normalization, caregiver burden, or investigator-rated
CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two or more active agents to a
subject so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted
on the subject during the period in which the pharmacological
effect of the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether
the two agents are administered in the same dosage form, in
separate dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times
within a window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse agonist
that increases histaminergic wake drive and supports state-
dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of pitolisant is
attenuation of hypoarousal, vigilance collapse, and attentional
instability in selected delirium presentations.

#### 2.2 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
xanomeline is support of cortical attentional control and cognitive
integration during delirium.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
xanomeline, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of a pitolisant tablet and a xanomeline or xanomeline-trospium
dosage form.

Excipients useful for the pitolisant composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the xanomeline
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and stabilizing excipients appropriate for muscarinic agonist oral
delivery.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
morning administration. Xanomeline is preferably formulated as an
oral immediate-release or modified-release dosage form administered
in divided doses. The preferred commercial-development embodiment
contemplates xanomeline co-administered with trospium to mitigate
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a short-course supply of
pitolisant tablets, (b) a short-course supply of xanomeline or
xanomeline-trospium units, and (c) written instructions aligned to
supervised delirium management and etiologic workup.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For selected medically supervised adult subjects with delirium, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pitolisant 4.5 mg orally once daily, titratable to 17.8 mg and
  then to 35.6 mg once daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.
* Xanomeline 50 mg orally twice daily, titratable to 100 mg twice
  daily if clinically indicated and tolerated; when paired with
  trospium, trospium is administered at about 20 mg to 30 mg twice
  daily.

The regimen is not intended to replace treatment of infection,
metabolic derangement, drug toxicity, hypoxia, alcohol withdrawal,
or other precipitating etiologies. The preferred embodiment is use
in selected supervised hypoactive or mixed delirium after initial
medical stabilization begins.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to selected adult
subjects with delirium. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: age >= 18 years; delirium diagnosed by
structured clinical assessment; impaired attention or arousal
instability; and active etiologic management already underway.
Exclusion criteria include uncontrolled severe hyperactive delirium
requiring immediate alternate containment, alcohol-withdrawal
delirium, untreated intoxication states as the primary driver,
severe cholinergic intolerance, unsafe cardiac conduction
abnormality, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Delirium often includes two interacting layers: impaired arousal /
vigilance and reduced cholinergic cortical signal quality, producing
fluctuating inattention and cognitive disorganization.

Pitolisant addresses the vigilance-arousal layer through H3
antagonism / inverse agonism. Xanomeline addresses the cholinergic-
attention layer through M1/M4 agonism. The two axes are non-
overlapping at the receptor level and converge on improved
attention and arousal stability.

additive reduction in delirium burden than either monotherapy

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding and inverse-agonism assays
for pitolisant; M1 and M4 receptor binding and signaling assays for
xanomeline; and combined arousal / attentional network assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include delirium-like inflammatory or postoperative attention
models, wake-maintenance paradigms, attentional-fluctuation tasks,
and sleep-wake instability assays. A supra-additive effect of the
combination on restoration of attention and arousal stability,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
pitolisant 17.8 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: xanomeline or
xanomeline-trospium for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, orthostatic vital signs,
and tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in selected adult delirium (FIG. 4). Three arms,
1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant + xanomeline.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pitolisant + placebo for xanomeline.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard delirium
  management.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in delirium-severity
composite at day 7. Secondary endpoints include attention score,
arousal stability, sleep-wake normalization, caregiver burden, and
CGI-I.

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and

angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of an H3 antagonist or inverse
agonist with a muscarinic M1/M4 agonist for delirium specifically.
The closest hits are:

* Existing delirium patents disclose other mechanisms such as
  glucocorticoid-receptor strategies or device-based approaches and
  do not teach the present pair.
* Generic muscarinic-activation patents disclose disorders
  ameliorated by muscarinic receptor activation but not the present
  arousal-plus-cholinergic combination.
* Generic H3 inverse agonist patents disclose wake-promoting agents
  but not the present delirium indication.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (H3 antagonism /
inverse agonism + M1/M4 agonism), (ii) the specific actives
(pitolisant + xanomeline), and (iii) the specific indication
(selected delirium). No prior-art disclosure suggests the
convergent-circuit rationale on which the present invention is
based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
selected delirium comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of pitolisant and a therapeutically effective
amount of xanomeline are disclosed, optionally with trospium to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects. The two agents act
on non-overlapping arousal-support and cortical-cholinergic
circuits and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in attention, wakefulness, and delirium severity than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
