## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD) by
co-administering (a) an orexin receptor antagonist, in particular
suvorexant, and (b) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with
5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine. The
invention is intended for adult subjects with recurrent dream-
enactment behavior and REM-sleep-without-atonia physiology, and
addresses REM-state instability, nocturnal arousal, sleep
fragmentation, and circadian dysregulation. The invention is not
intended to diagnose or reverse an underlying synucleinopathy or
other neurodegenerative disease.

## Background of the invention

REM Sleep Behavior Disorder is a parasomnia characterized by loss of
normal REM-sleep muscle atonia with associated dream enactment
behavior. Subjects may shout, punch, kick, leap from bed, or
perform other complex motor acts during vivid dreams, often causing
injury to themselves or their bed partners. The diagnosis is
preferably confirmed by video polysomnography showing REM sleep
without atonia together with clinically compatible nocturnal
behaviors.

The prevalence of idiopathic and secondary RBD is lower than that
of simple insomnia or common parasomnias, but the clinical burden
is substantial because of injury risk, sleep disruption, and the
association with alpha-synuclein neurodegenerative disorders such
as Parkinson disease, dementia with Lewy bodies, and multiple
system atrophy. Many subjects have co-existing insomnia, mood
symptoms, obstructive sleep apnea, antidepressant exposure, or
other sleep-state destabilizers that worsen dream enactment.

The current standard of care is supportive and incompletely
effective. It includes environmental safety measures, treatment of
co-existing sleep disorders, review of medications that can worsen
RBD, and selected off-label use of melatonin or clonazepam. No
pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for RBD as a primary
indication. A clinically meaningful gap remains for a regimen that
improves nocturnal state stability and sleep architecture without
being framed as a sedative-only approach.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, RBD reflects instability across REM-state
control, nocturnal arousal thresholds, and transitions between REM,
wakefulness, and motor disinhibition. Orexin signaling contributes
to wake maintenance and state-transition pressure. Suvorexant is a
dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for insomnia that
facilitates sleep onset and maintenance by reducing orexin-driven
wakefulness. In the present invention, the suvorexant axis
addresses the *nocturnal arousal and REM-state-instability*
substrate of RBD.

Second, RBD is frequently worsened by sleep fragmentation,
irregular sleep timing, and circadian destabilization. Agomelatine
is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent agonist at
MT1 and MT2 receptors and a neutral antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. This pharmacologic profile supports circadian
resynchronization and subjective sleep-quality improvement. The
agomelatine axis therefore addresses the *circadian-disorganization
and sleep-architecture instability* substrate of RBD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an orexin receptor antagonist in
combination with a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist with 5-HT2C
antagonism for RBD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of orexin-driven
state-transition pressure (a suvorexant axis) and restoration of
stable circadian entrainment (an agomelatine axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of RBD. The
combination is expected to produce greater reduction in dream-
enactment frequency, injury risk, and sleep fragmentation than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose, while
continuing standard neurologic follow-up and bedroom-safety
measures.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) suvorexant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) agomelatine or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, co-crystal,
or prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating REM
Sleep Behavior Disorder in an adult human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of suvorexant and a therapeutically effective
amount of agomelatine. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) suvorexant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of RBD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with bedroom-
safety planning and evaluation / treatment of co-occurring sleep
disorders.

The invention contemplates suvorexant in a daily dose of about
5 mg to about 20 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a
preferred adult dose of 10 mg to 20 mg nightly. The invention
contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about 25 mg to about
50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a preferred dose of
25 mg nightly and optional escalation to 50 mg nightly after about
2 weeks if clinically indicated. The inventive feature is the
concurrent engagement of orexin-antagonist state-stability support
and melatonergic circadian resynchronization in a subject with RBD.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with RBD, in dream-enactment episode frequency, injury / near-
injury burden, REM-related sleep disruption, and bed-partner
disturbance, that is greater than the sum of the individual effects
of suvorexant and agomelatine administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Suvorexant"* means the dual orexin receptor antagonist approved
for insomnia. The term includes suvorexant free base, salts,
polymorphs, solvates, hydrates, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent orexin receptor antagonists
with a clinically comparable sleep-promoting profile.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"REM Sleep Behavior Disorder"* or *"RBD"* means a parasomnia
characterized by dream enactment behavior associated with REM sleep
without atonia, including idiopathic RBD and RBD occurring in
association with recognized neurologic conditions or medication
exposure.

*"Dream-enactment episode frequency"* means the number of
clinically meaningful dream-enactment events over a defined
interval, as recorded by the subject, bed partner, caregiver,
validated sleep diary, or laboratory observation.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult human subject for the
treatment of RBD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
dream-enactment episode frequency, injury / near-injury burden,
bed-partner disruption, REM-without-atonia burden, sleep-
fragmentation index, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Suvorexant

Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia. By reducing orexin-mediated wake drive, it facilitates
sleep onset and maintenance. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of suvorexant is reduction of
abnormal nocturnal arousal pressure and REM-state destabilization
that contribute to dream-enactment events.

#### 2.2 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant acting as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and a neutral antagonist at
5-HT2C receptors. This profile supports circadian resynchronization
and subjective sleep-quality improvement. In the present invention,
the principal mechanistic contribution of agomelatine is
stabilization of sleep timing and reduction of circadian
vulnerability to recurrent RBD events.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise suvorexant and
agomelatine, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a suvorexant tablet and an
agomelatine tablet.

Excipients useful for the suvorexant composition include tablet
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and film-coating polymers
appropriate for bedtime administration. Excipients useful for the
agomelatine composition include diluents (microcrystalline
cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders (povidone,
hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants (crospovidone,
sodium starch glycolate), lubricants (magnesium stearate), and
coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Suvorexant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime dosing. Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral
tablet suitable for bedtime administration.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
suvorexant tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of agomelatine tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to sleep scheduling, bedroom-
safety planning, and treatment of co-occurring sleep disorders.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with RBD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Suvorexant 5 mg orally at bedtime for about 3 nights, titratable
  to 10 mg and then to 20 mg nightly if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.
* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days,
  titratable to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.

Broad genus ranges include suvorexant 5–20 mg/day and agomelatine
25–50 mg/day. The preferred schedule administers both agents at
bedtime on a consistent nightly schedule.

Hepatic adjustment: clinically meaningful hepatic impairment is
exclusionary or requires careful monitoring because agomelatine is
associated with hepatotoxicity risk in susceptible patients.

Medication–medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors. Suvorexant should be used with
caution alongside other CNS depressants. The regimen is intended to
complement, not replace, environmental safety measures and
treatment of co-occurring sleep disorders such as obstructive sleep
apnea where present.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with RBD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age ≥ 18 years; clinician-confirmed dream enactment
behavior, preferably with video-polysomnographic evidence of REM
sleep without atonia; clinically meaningful distress, injury risk,
or bed-partner disruption; and ability to participate in safety
measures and sleep tracking. Exclusion criteria include untreated
severe sleep-disordered breathing, uncontrolled nocturnal seizures,
severe hepatic disease, and known hypersensitivity to either
active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

RBD is maintained by two interacting layers: REM-state instability
with abnormal motor disinhibition and arousal pressure, and
circadian / sleep-architecture instability that increases event
recurrence.

Suvorexant addresses the state-instability layer through orexin
antagonism. Agomelatine addresses the circadian-vulnerability layer
through MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. The two axes are
non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the same
dream-enactment phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in RBD burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 36,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: orexin receptor binding and antagonism assays
for suvorexant; MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays for
agomelatine; and combined sleep-state transition assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include sleep-fragmentation models, REM-atonia / REM-state
instability models, circadian-shift paradigms, and EMG-defined
sleep-state motor-disinhibition paradigms. A supra-additive effect
of the combination on reduction of abnormal REM-associated motor
activity, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
suvorexant 10 mg nightly for 3 days; Period 2: agomelatine 25 mg
nightly for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, liver-function monitoring, next-day sedation monitoring,
and nocturnal polysomnographic safety measurements are obtained in
each period. The principal purpose is to establish tolerability and
absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult RBD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 90 (30 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): suvorexant + agomelatine.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): agomelatine + placebo for
  suvorexant.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard safety and
  sleep-hygiene management.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in weekly dream-
enactment episode frequency at week 8. Secondary endpoints include
injury / near-injury rate, bed-partner disruption score, REM-
without-atonia burden, sleep-fragmentation index, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of an orexin antagonist with a
melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist having 5-HT2C antagonism for RBD
specifically. The closest hits are:

* Generic sleep-disorder and insomnia patents disclose orexin-
  antagonist or melatonergic approaches but not the present pair
  for RBD.
* Agomelatine association patents disclose melatonergic sleep-
  regulation strategies but do not teach suvorexant co-
  administration for dream-enactment disorders.
* RBD-adjacent sleep-treatment patents do not disclose the present
  specific actives together or the present convergent-circuit
  rationale.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (orexin antagonism +
MT1 / MT2 agonism + 5-HT2C antagonism), (ii) the specific actives
(suvorexant + agomelatine), and (iii) the specific indication
(RBD). No prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit
rationale on which the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of REM
Sleep Behavior Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the orexin antagonist
suvorexant and a therapeutically effective amount of the
melatonergic antidepressant agomelatine are disclosed. The two
agents act on non-overlapping REM-state-instability and circadian-
stability circuits implicated in dream-enactment behavior and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive symptomatic improvement
in dream-enactment frequency, injury burden, and sleep continuity
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
