## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to
HIV Infection (HIV-NCD; HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder,
HAND spectrum) by co-administering (a) a histamine H3 receptor
antagonist or inverse agonist and (b) a muscarinic M1/M4 receptor
agonist, in particular the combination of pitolisant and
xanomeline, optionally further with a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic such as trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic
adverse effects. The invention addresses symptomatic cognitive
impairment, daytime fatigue / attention fluctuation, and caregiver /
functional burden in subjects with HIV-related neurocognitive
decline while continuing antiretroviral therapy; it does not assert
antiviral activity or eradication of HIV reservoirs.

## Background of the invention

Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to HIV Infection is
recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a neurocognitive syndrome
attributable to human immunodeficiency virus infection. In modern
practice the syndrome falls within the broader concept of HIV-
associated neurocognitive disorders (HAND), spanning asymptomatic
neurocognitive impairment, mild neurocognitive disorder, and HIV-
associated dementia. The affected cognitive domains commonly
include complex attention, psychomotor speed, executive function,
learning and memory, and functional independence.

Despite major advances in antiretroviral therapy (ART), HAND
remains prevalent. In cohorts on stable ART and even in subjects
with suppressed plasma viral load, mild neurocognitive impairment
remains common and may affect a substantial minority of patients.
These subjects frequently experience slowed processing speed,
attention fluctuation, memory inefficiency, fatigue, reduced work
capacity, and increased caregiver burden. Unlike opportunistic CNS
infections or uncontrolled encephalopathy, the residual syndrome in
treated HIV is often chronic, subtle, and under-addressed.

The current standard of care is supportive and etiology-directed. It
consists primarily of maintaining virologic suppression with ART,
optimizing sleep and mood, minimizing anticholinergic or sedating
polypharmacy, treating vascular and metabolic comorbidities, and
using rehabilitation and caregiver supports. No pharmaceutical
product is approved by the United States Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) specifically for the cognitive syndrome of
HIV-NCD as a primary indication. Where clinicians do use
cognition-directed medications, they typically borrow agents from
other neurocognitive or arousal indications, with inconsistent
benefit and tolerability.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses is the
absence of a defensible, mechanism-based symptomatic regimen for
cognitive and arousal impairment in HIV-NCD that does not rely on
acetylcholinesterase inhibition alone. HIV-related neurocognitive
impairment frequently involves reduced cortical cholinergic tone,
frontostriatal attentional inefficiency, fatigue, and fluctuating
attention. A single-agent approach often leaves one half of the
phenotype untreated.

The present invention recognizes that two non-overlapping
cholinergic-support mechanisms can be combined to support cognition
and daytime functioning in HIV-NCD. First, the histamine H3
receptor is a presynaptic auto- and heteroreceptor that, when
constitutively active, suppresses the release of histamine and of
acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine in cortex, striatum,
and hippocampus. Selective inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant
(Wakix®) elevates extracellular histamine and supports state-
dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine without the abuse
liability of classic stimulants. The H3 axis therefore addresses
the *vigilance, attention, and fatigue substrate* of HIV-NCD.

Second, the muscarinic M1 receptor is a Gαq-coupled postsynaptic
receptor concentrated in cerebral cortex and hippocampus that
mediates a substantial portion of cholinergic facilitation of
cognition, while the M4 receptor is a Gαi-coupled receptor
concentrated in striatal circuits and contributes to behavioral
stabilization. Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist
developed in the 1990s and more recently formulated with the
peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium as the FDA-approved
xanomeline–trospium combination (Cobenfy®) for schizophrenia in
adults. The xanomeline axis therefore addresses the *cortical–
hippocampal cholinergic substrate* of HIV-NCD and, unlike
acetylcholinesterase inhibition, does not require fully preserved
endogenous acetylcholine release to the same degree.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an H3 antagonist or inverse agonist
in combination with an M1/M4 agonist for HIV-NCD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance- and arousal-
enabled cognition (a pitolisant H3 axis) and post-synaptic
muscarinic facilitation of cortical cognition (a xanomeline M1/M4
axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for symptomatic
cognitive support in HIV-NCD. The combination is expected to
produce greater symptomatic cognitive and functional benefit than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose, by acting
on two non-overlapping CNS axes that converge on the same
frontostriatal–cortical cognitive circuit. The invention does not
assert antiviral activity or eradication of HIV reservoirs.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, (b) xanomeline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, and (c) optionally a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic agent such as trospium chloride in an amount
effective to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to HIV Infection in a
human subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to the
subject a therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline, optionally further
with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic. Co-administration
may be simultaneous, in a unitary dosage form or in separate dosage
forms, or sequential, with each agent administered within a window
of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) xanomeline, optionally co-formulated with trospium, in a second
unitary dosage form, and (c) written instructions for the
symptomatic treatment of HIV-NCD according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein while continuing ART.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily. The
invention contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about 50 mg to
about 250 mg administered orally as the free base or as the
tartrate salt in two divided doses; when xanomeline is co-
administered with trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is
about 40 mg to about 60 mg administered orally in two divided
doses. The mass ratio of xanomeline to pitolisant (calculated on
free-base equivalents) ranges broadly from about 5:1 to about
200:1, and is preferably from about 10:1 to about 50:1.

The technical effect of the combination is attenuation, in subjects
with HIV-NCD, of cognitive slowing and attention impairment as
measured by validated cognitive instruments such as the Montreal
Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), the International HIV Dementia Scale
(IHDS), Symbol Digit Modalities Test (SDMT), or attention /
executive function composites, together with improvement in
daytime alertness and reduction in caregiver burden, each greater
than would be obtained with either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the compound 1-{3-[3-(4-chlorophenyl)propoxy]
propyl}piperidine, also known as BF2.649, marketed in the United
States as Wakix® and in the European Union as Wakix®. Pitolisant is
a selective inverse agonist of the human histamine H3 receptor with
a binding affinity (Ki) of approximately 0.16 nM. The term as used
herein includes the free base, the hydrochloride salt and other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-
yl]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine, an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist. The term as used herein includes the free base,
the tartrate salt and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Trospium chloride"* or *"trospium"* means the quaternary ammonium
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist that does not
appreciably cross the blood-brain barrier and may be used to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline.

*"Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to HIV Infection"* or
*"HIV-NCD"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 neurocognitive syndrome in
which clinically meaningful cognitive decline is attributable to
human immunodeficiency virus infection, including the clinical HAND
spectrum to the extent it maps to those classifications.

*"Symptomatic cognitive support"* means a treatment effect directed
toward cognition, alertness, executive function, or functional
independence without asserted antiviral activity, eradication of
HIV reservoirs, or disease-modification of HIV infection itself.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the
symptomatic treatment of HIV-NCD according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in,
or attenuation of worsening of, at least one of: MoCA, IHDS, SDMT,
attention / executive composite, caregiver burden, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two or more active agents to a
subject so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted
on the subject during the period in which the pharmacological
effect of the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether
the agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a piperidine derivative and a high-affinity, selective
inverse agonist at the human histamine H3 receptor. Inverse agonism
at H3 elevates extracellular histamine and supports state-dependent
release of acetylcholine and dopamine in cortex, hippocampus, and
striatal circuits. Pitolisant is approved by the FDA for the
treatment of excessive daytime sleepiness in adults with
narcolepsy. Pitolisant is metabolized primarily by CYP2D6 and
CYP3A4. It is not an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor and therefore
avoids the direct peripheral cholinergic burden of classic
cholinesterase inhibitors.

#### 2.2 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. Activation of
the M1 receptor facilitates post-synaptic cholinergic cognitive
enhancement in cortex and hippocampus and is not wholly dependent
on endogenous acetylcholine availability. Activation of the M4
receptor contributes to behavioral stabilization in striatal
circuits. Xanomeline reached the market in fixed-dose combination
with trospium chloride as Cobenfy® for schizophrenia in adults and
is under live U.S. brand-patent protection via the marketed
combination and related rights.

#### 2.3 Trospium (optional)

Trospium chloride is a quaternary ammonium peripherally restricted
muscarinic antagonist that does not appreciably cross the blood-
brain barrier. In the present invention, trospium may be included
at a daily dose effective to mitigate peripheral cholinergic
adverse effects of xanomeline without materially antagonizing the
central cognitive effect.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
xanomeline, optionally further comprising trospium, together with at
least one pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The compositions
may take the form of: (a) a co-packaged kit of two or three
separate dosage forms (pitolisant tablet + xanomeline-trospium
fixed-dose capsule); (b) a unitary fixed-dose combination dosage
form including all three actives; or (c) a co-packaged kit of
pitolisant tablets and the marketed xanomeline-trospium product.

The mass ratio of xanomeline (calculated as the free base) to
pitolisant (calculated as the free base) is in the range of about
5:1 to about 200:1, preferably about 10:1 to about 50:1. When
trospium is included, the molar ratio of trospium to xanomeline is
approximately 1:1 to 1:3.

### 4. Dosage forms

Xanomeline (with or without trospium) is preferably formulated as
twice-daily oral capsules. The marketed Cobenfy® product is
supplied as 50 mg/20 mg, 100 mg/20 mg, and 125 mg/30 mg
xanomeline-tartrate / trospium-chloride capsules; the present
invention contemplates use of those strengths and equivalent
strengths of xanomeline alone where peripheral cholinergic burden is
acceptable. Pitolisant is preferably formulated as a once-daily
immediate-release oral tablet at strengths of 4.5 mg and 17.8 mg
(free-base equivalent), matching the marketed product.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with HIV-NCD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pitolisant 8.9 mg orally once daily on awakening for week 1, then
  17.8 mg once daily thereafter; maximum 35.6 mg/day.
* Xanomeline-trospium 50 mg/20 mg orally twice daily for the first
  three days, then 100 mg/20 mg twice daily for the next four days,
  then 125 mg/30 mg twice daily thereafter as tolerated.

Where trospium is omitted, xanomeline may be administered at an
equivalent free-base exposure range with heightened monitoring for
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects.

Hepatic adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in
subjects with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B) and is not
recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).
Xanomeline-trospium is not recommended in moderate or severe
hepatic impairment.

Renal adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²).
Xanomeline-trospium is not recommended in moderate or severe renal
impairment.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP2D6 inhibitors without dose review of pitolisant and
xanomeline. Avoid co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inducers.
Avoid co-administration with potent anticholinergics other than the
trospium component. Stable ART is required and ART modification for
the purpose of this invention is not contemplated.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects who
meet DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 criteria for major or mild NCD due to HIV
infection. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: documented HIV infection; stable ART for at
least 12 weeks; plasma viral suppression or otherwise clinically
stable HIV management where possible; clinician-confirmed major or
mild neurocognitive syndrome attributable to HIV infection; and
sufficient swallowing and oral intake to tolerate oral medication.
Exclusion criteria include uncontrolled CNS opportunistic infection,
active delirium, uncontrolled psychosis, severe hepatic or renal
impairment, narrow-angle glaucoma, urinary retention, gastric
retention, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

HIV-NCD is characterized by a mixed phenotype of reduced cortical
cholinergic tone, frontostriatal inefficiency, psychomotor slowing,
daytime fatigue, and attentional fluctuation that often persists
despite virologic suppression on ART. A single-agent approach rarely
addresses all of these features.

Pitolisant elevates extracellular histamine and supports state-
dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine in cortex,
hippocampus, and striatal circuits, thereby improving vigilance,
fatigue, and attentional stability. Xanomeline directly facilitates
post-synaptic muscarinic signaling in cortex and hippocampus,
thereby supporting cognition even when endogenous acetylcholine
release is reduced or dysregulated. The two axes are non-overlapping
at the receptor level and converge on the same frontostriatal–
cortical cognitive circuit.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in cognition, alertness, and caregiver-

(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 28,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: M1 receptor binding and calcium-mobilization
assays; M4 receptor functional assays; H3 receptor binding and
inverse-agonism assays; and combined cortical-network signaling
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms useful for confirming the combination's effect on
the frontostriatal cognitive substrate include HIV-protein or
neuroinflammation models of cognitive impairment, novel object
recognition, reversal learning, and 5-choice serial reaction time
task for attention. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
attentional accuracy, processing-speed proxies, and cognitive task
performance, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1: pitolisant
17.8 mg orally once daily for 7 days; full PK sampling on days 1
and 7. Period 2 (after washout): xanomeline-trospium 100 mg/20 mg
twice daily for 7 days; full PK sampling for all actives on days 1
and 7. Period 3: the combination at the same doses for 7 days. The
primary endpoint is the geometric mean ratio for AUC₀–τ and Cmax of
pitolisant and xanomeline in combination versus monotherapy, with
90 % confidence intervals contained within 0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adults with HIV-NCD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant + xanomeline-trospium.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): xanomeline-trospium + matching
  pitolisant placebo.
* Arm C (placebo): matching placebo + standard-of-care HIV
  management.

All subjects continue stable ART throughout the study. The primary
endpoint is change from baseline in MoCA / IHDS composite at week
12. Secondary endpoints include SDMT, daytime alertness score,
caregiver burden, functional independence, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Major
or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to HIV Infection comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
histamine H3 receptor inverse agonist pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of the muscarinic M1/M4-preferring
agonist xanomeline, optionally further with the peripherally
restricted antimuscarinic trospium chloride, are disclosed. The
two CNS-active agents act on non-overlapping receptor classes that
converge on frontostriatal–cortical cognitive and arousal circuits
implicated in HIV-associated neurocognitive impairment and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive symptomatic cognitive
benefit than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose while continuing stable antiretroviral therapy.
