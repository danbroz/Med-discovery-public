## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Specific Phobia by co-administering (a) a
melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist having 5-HT2C antagonist
activity, in particular agomelatine, and (b) a serotonin reuptake
inhibitor with 5-HT1A partial agonist activity, in particular
vilazodone. The invention is intended for subjects with marked fear
or anxiety about a circumscribed object or situation, and
addresses anticipatory dread, cue-bound autonomic arousal, sleep
disruption before exposure, avoidance, and engagement with graded
exposure therapy. The invention is not intended to suppress
reasonable fear of objectively dangerous stimuli or to replace
exposure-based behavioral treatment.

## Background of the invention

Specific Phobia is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as an anxiety
disorder characterized by marked fear or anxiety about a specific
object or situation, such as animals, heights, blood / injury,
storms, flying, enclosed spaces, injections, or vomiting-related
cues. The phobic object or situation almost always provokes
immediate fear or anxiety and is actively avoided or endured with
intense distress.

The prevalence of Specific Phobia is high relative to many other
psychiatric disorders and the burden can be substantial when the
phobia interferes with work, medical care, travel, education, or
family function. Although some phobias remain narrow, others can
drive severe avoidance, panic-like symptoms, sleep disruption
before anticipated exposures, and major restriction of life
activities.

The current standard of care is primarily behavioral. It consists
of graded exposure therapy, psychoeducation, and in selected cases
general anxiolytic or antidepressant treatment when comorbid or
broader anxiety symptoms are present. No pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
specifically for the treatment of Specific Phobia as a primary
indication. Yet many subjects continue to experience intense
anticipatory dread, poor sleep before exposure, and inability to
engage meaningfully in exposure-based therapy.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, specific phobias include strong cue-bound threat
expectancy, intrusive catastrophic ideation, and rapid autonomic
arousal when the feared stimulus is anticipated or encountered.
Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available antidepressant that
combines selective serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial
agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual mechanism supports
attenuation of anxious distress and threat-related ideation while
maintaining an anxiety-relevant pharmacology profile. The
vilazodone axis therefore addresses the *anxious-serotonergic and
cue-threat expectancy* substrate of Specific Phobia.

Second, many subjects with specific phobias experience circadian
and sleep disruption before anticipated exposure opportunities,
leading to poor next-day coping and higher physiologic reactivity.
Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. This combination supports resynchronization of circadian
timing, improvement in sleep architecture, and reduction of
nighttime hyperarousal. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses
the *sleep-linked anticipatory reactivity and recovery-failure*
substrate of Specific Phobia.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
with 5-HT2C antagonism in combination with a serotonin reuptake
inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial agonist for Specific Phobia
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of cue-bound
threat expectancy (a vilazodone axis) and restoration of sleep-
linked recovery before exposure (an agomelatine axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of Specific
Phobia. The combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic
improvement in avoidance, anticipatory fear, and exposure
tolerance than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Specific Phobia in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of agomelatine and a therapeutically effective amount of
vilazodone. Co-administration may be simultaneous or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Specific Phobia
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with
graded exposure therapy.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a
preferred initiation dose of 25 mg nightly and optional escalation
to 50 mg nightly after about 2 weeks if clinically indicated. The
invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about 10 mg to
about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with a
preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from 10 mg/day.
The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of melatonergic
sleep-recovery support and serotonergic cue-threat attenuation in a
stimulus-specific fear syndrome.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with Specific Phobia, in phobia-severity burden, anticipatory fear,
cue-bound autonomic distress, and avoidance behavior, that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of agomelatine and
vilazodone administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Specific Phobia"* means the anxiety disorder defined in DSM-5-TR
and ICD-11 by marked fear or anxiety about a specific object or
situation that provokes immediate fear and is avoided or endured
with intense distress.

*"Phobia-severity total"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
avoidance, anticipatory dread, cue-triggered distress, or exposure
intolerance quantified by structured clinician assessment or
validated phobia instruments.

*"Graded exposure therapy"* means a structured intervention in which
contact with the feared stimulus is approached progressively to
reduce avoidance and catastrophic expectancy.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of Specific Phobia according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
phobia-severity total, anticipatory fear score, exposure tolerance,
autonomic-distress score, avoidance frequency, or investigator-
rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is restoration of sleep continuity and
reduction of pre-exposure nighttime hyperarousal.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
cue-bound fear expectancy and autonomic distress. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of vilazodone is
reduction of anticipatory fear and catastrophic ideation.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of an agomelatine tablet and a vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the agomelatine composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the vilazodone
composition include diluents (microcrystalline cellulose, lactose
monohydrate), binders (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone),
disintegrants (crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime administration. Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an
oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
agomelatine tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of vilazodone tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to exposure hierarchy planning,
sleep stabilization, and cue-specific symptom tracking.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with Specific Phobia, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days, titratable
  to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.

Broad genus ranges include agomelatine 25-50 mg/day and vilazodone
10-40 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses bedtime agomelatine and
once-daily vilazodone with food.

Medication-medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors and requires hepatic monitoring in
appropriate patients. Vilazodone dose adjustment may be required
with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. The regimen is intended to
complement, not replace, graded exposure therapy.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
Specific Phobia. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age >= 12 years; clinically meaningful
stimulus-specific fear or avoidance; and willingness to participate
in graded exposure or structured behavioral treatment. Exclusion
criteria include psychotic or delusional fear states, severe hepatic
disease, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Specific Phobia often includes two interacting layers: cue-bound
catastrophic expectancy and autonomic fear, and poor sleep /
recovery before anticipated exposure opportunities.

Vilazodone addresses the cue-threat layer through SERT inhibition
and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Agomelatine addresses the sleep-
recovery layer through MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on the same avoidance phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in Specific Phobia burden than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 31,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays for
agomelatine; and combined cue-threat and sleep-recovery assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include fear-conditioning tasks, cue-exposure models,
extinction-learning assays, and sleep-fragmentation paradigms. A
supra-additive effect of the combination on reduction of cue-bound
fear and improvement of exposure tolerance, relative to either
monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the principal
in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
agomelatine 25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone
titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, liver-function monitoring, and
tolerability assessments are obtained in each period. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult Specific Phobia (FIG. 4).
Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): agomelatine + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with graded exposure therapy.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in phobia-severity
total at week 10. Secondary endpoints include anticipatory fear
score, exposure tolerance, autonomic-distress score, avoidance
frequency, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Specific Phobia comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of agomelatine and a therapeutically effective
amount of vilazodone are disclosed. The two agents act on non-
overlapping cue-threat and sleep-recovery circuits implicated in
stimulus-specific fear and are expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in anticipatory fear, exposure tolerance, and
avoidance than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.
