## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Parkinson's Disease Neurocognitive Disorder (PD-
NCD) by co-administering (a) a histamine H3 receptor antagonist or
inverse agonist and (b) a muscarinic receptor agonist, in particular
the combination of pitolisant and xanomeline, optionally together with
trospium or another peripherally restricted anticholinergic to limit
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects.

## Background of the invention

Parkinson's Disease Neurocognitive Disorder comprises the mild or
major cognitive disorder associated with Parkinson's disease and is
characterized by deficits in attention, executive function,
visuospatial processing, memory retrieval, alertness, and functional
independence. Many subjects further experience daytime sleepiness,
cognitive fluctuations, hallucinations, or caregiver-dependent
functional decline. The clinical burden is high and grows as motor and
non-motor disease progress.

The current treatment landscape is limited. Cholinesterase inhibitors
may provide modest symptomatic benefit but are often constrained by
tolerability and are not consistently sufficient for attention or
daytime-alertness deficits. Dopaminergic regimen adjustment can
improve or worsen cognition depending on the subject, and dopamine-
blocking approaches risk motor worsening. There remains a need for a
non-dopamine-blocking combination that addresses both cortical
cholinergic deficiency and wake-state instability in PD-NCD.

The mechanistic gap addressed by the present invention has two layers.
First, pitolisant increases histaminergic tone through H3 inverse
agonism / antagonism and supports state-dependent release of
acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine, improving alertness and
task engagement. Second, xanomeline is a centrally active muscarinic
agonist with M1/M4-preferring clinical activity that strengthens
cortical cholinergic signaling relevant to attention, memory encoding,
and executive processing. The inventors recognized that these two axes
are complementary and non-redundant: pitolisant improves the waking
state in which cognition occurs, while xanomeline improves the quality
of cortical cholinergic processing within that state.

This combination is particularly attractive in PD-NCD because it can
be framed without dopamine receptor blockade. The invention therefore
targets cognition, alertness, and functional participation while
avoiding the primary motor-worsening concern associated with many
central dopamine-blocking strategies.

Prior-art searching identified muscarinic-receptor activation
references such as *Methods and compositions for treatment of
disorders ameliorated by muscarinic receptor activation* and
Parkinson's disease treatment references involving levodopa-based
regimens or other unrelated mechanisms. Those references do not teach
the specific combination of pitolisant and xanomeline for PD-NCD, nor
the optional use of a peripheral anticholinergic mitigator such as
trospium to improve tolerability.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) xanomeline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, optionally together with trospium chloride or another
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist, and further together
with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Parkinson's Disease Neurocognitive Disorder in a human subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering a therapeutically effective
amount of pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount of
xanomeline. In certain embodiments, trospium chloride is further
administered to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects while
preserving central efficacy.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pitolisant in a first dosage form, (b) xanomeline in a second
dosage form, optionally packaged with trospium chloride in a third
dosage form, and (c) written instructions for use in a subject with
PD-NCD, including guidance regarding concomitant stable dopaminergic
therapy, blood-pressure monitoring, and assessment of cognition and
daytime alertness.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered once daily. The invention
contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about 50 mg to about
250 mg, preferably in divided doses. In certain embodiments trospium
chloride is administered in a total daily dose of about 20 mg to about
60 mg.

The technical effect of the combination is expected to be greater than
additive improvement in one or more endpoints selected from the group
consisting of Parkinson's Disease-Cognitive Rating Scale (PD-CRS),
Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), daytime-alertness measures,
Clinical Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I), caregiver burden, and
functional participation, while minimizing motor worsening associated
with dopamine-blocking approaches.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Parkinson's Disease Neurocognitive Disorder"* or *"PD-NCD"* means
the mild or major neurocognitive disorder associated with Parkinson's
disease, including cognitive syndromes characterized by impairment in
attention, executive function, visuospatial performance, memory, or
functional cognition.

*"Pitolisant"* means the H3 inverse agonist / antagonist and its
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues that
retain clinically relevant H3 activity.

*"Xanomeline"* means xanomeline and pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, and metabolically equivalent
forms that retain clinically relevant muscarinic receptor agonist
activity.

*"Peripheral anticholinergic mitigator"* means trospium chloride or
another antimuscarinic agent with limited central nervous system
penetration used to reduce peripheral cholinergic adverse effects
associated with xanomeline administration.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount that produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in one or more PD-NCD endpoints when
used according to a regimen disclosed herein.

### 2. Active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant increases histaminergic tone and supports wakefulness,
attention, and state-dependent cortical neurotransmitter release. In
the present invention, its principal contribution is improvement in
vigilance, daytime alertness, and the behavioral availability required
for cognitive function.

#### 2.2 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is a centrally active muscarinic receptor agonist with
M1/M4-preferring clinical activity. In the present invention, its
principal contribution is enhancement of cortical cholinergic signal
quality relevant to attention, memory, and executive function.

#### 2.3 Optional trospium chloride

Trospium chloride may optionally be administered with xanomeline to
reduce peripheral cholinergic adverse effects such as nausea,
diarrhea, abdominal cramping, sweating, or urinary urgency, while
preserving central cholinergic efficacy due to limited blood-brain
barrier penetration.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
xanomeline, optionally together with trospium chloride, plus one or
more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients. The actives
may be administered as separate oral dosage forms, as a co-packaged
kit, or as a synchronized blister-card system aligned to morning and
evening dosing.

Suitable excipients include common diluents, binders, disintegrants,
lubricants, capsule-fill agents, coating systems, and release-control
polymers appropriate for oral solid dosage forms. In preferred
embodiments pitolisant is given in the morning and xanomeline in
divided doses to balance central efficacy and tolerability.

### 4. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with PD-NCD, a preferred regimen comprises:

* Pitolisant 4.5 mg to 35.6 mg orally once daily, preferably in the
  morning.
* Xanomeline 50 mg to 250 mg daily, preferably in divided doses.
* Optional trospium chloride 20 mg to 60 mg daily in divided doses.

In certain embodiments pitolisant is titrated first to establish
tolerability and alertness benefit, after which xanomeline is added at
a low dose and increased gradually. In certain embodiments xanomeline
and trospium are co-initiated.

The subject's dopaminergic Parkinson's regimen is preferably stable at
baseline so that cognitive and alertness changes can be attributed to
the present invention rather than to major levodopa adjustment.

### 5. Subject populations

Preferred subjects are adults with clinician-confirmed Parkinson's
disease and mild or major neurocognitive disorder manifested by
attention deficits, executive dysfunction, cognitive fluctuation,
visuospatial impairment, caregiver-reported cognitive decline, or
daytime sleepiness that interferes with function.

Preferred embodiments include subjects with persistent cognitive
symptoms despite optimized standard Parkinson's management or despite
cholinesterase inhibitor exposure. In certain embodiments the subject
has fluctuating alertness that limits rehabilitation, exercise,
therapy, or daily independence.

Exclusion criteria for preferred embodiments include uncontrolled
psychosis, acute delirium, unstable severe orthostatic hypotension,
uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma, severe gastrointestinal
intolerance to cholinergic therapy, or inability to participate in
monitoring.

### 6. Mechanistic rationale

PD-NCD often combines a cortical cholinergic deficit with impaired
daytime arousal. A subject who is insufficiently alert may perform
poorly even when cholinergic tone is partially restored, while a
subject with improved arousal but no cholinergic support may remain
limited by poor encoding, attention, and cognitive control.

Pitolisant addresses the arousal layer through H3 antagonism / inverse
agonism. Xanomeline addresses the cholinergic processing layer through
central muscarinic agonism. The combination is therefore expected to
produce larger improvements in cognition and functional alertness than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

Because the mechanism is non-dopamine-blocking, the combination is
further expected to avoid the direct motor worsening risk associated
with D2 antagonism. This feature is especially valuable in Parkinson's
disease populations.

### 7. Administration and monitoring

Monitoring may include PD-CRS, MoCA, Epworth Sleepiness Scale or other
daytime-alertness measures, caregiver diary, orthostatic vital signs,
bowel tolerance, urinary symptoms, and hallucination burden. In
certain embodiments motor status is followed with MDS-UPDRS Part III
to confirm that cognitive benefit is not accompanied by unacceptable
motor decline.

The regimen is not intended to replace levodopa-based treatment,
movement-disorder specialty care, nonpharmacologic cognitive support,
or management of acute delirium or severe psychosis when present.

### 8. Prophetic clinical study

A prophetic Phase 2 study enrolls adults with Parkinson's disease,
baseline PD-CRS impairment, and stable dopaminergic treatment.
Subjects are randomized to: (a) pitolisant plus xanomeline, optionally
with trospium; (b) pitolisant plus placebo; or (c) placebo plus
standard Parkinson's care, for 12 weeks after a titration phase.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in PD-CRS total score.
Key secondary endpoints include MoCA, daytime alertness, CGI-I,
caregiver burden, and safety / tolerability including hallucination
burden and orthostasis. The combination arm is expected to show the
largest improvement in cognition and daytime function, without
clinically meaningful worsening in motor scores. FIGS. 3-5 are
prophetic illustrations of those expected outcomes.

### 9. Prior-art distinction

The closest prior-art references identified in the prior-art
landscape include:

* *Methods and compositions for treatment of disorders ameliorated by
  muscarinic receptor activation* - disclose muscarinic activation but
  do not teach co-administration with pitolisant for PD-NCD.
* Levodopa / carbidopa / entacapone Parkinson's references - disclose
  dopaminergic therapy, not the H3 plus muscarinic combination
  disclosed herein.
* Other Parkinson's disease treatment disclosures - do not teach the
  specific non-dopamine-blocking pitolisant + xanomeline combination
  nor the optional peripheral-anticholinergic mitigation strategy.

The present invention is distinct in the specific receptor classes
combined, the specific actives chosen, and the symptom-layered
strategy of coupling vigilance support with cortical cholinergic
enhancement in PD-NCD.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Parkinson's Disease Neurocognitive Disorder comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
histamine H3 receptor inverse agonist / antagonist pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of the muscarinic receptor agonist
xanomeline are disclosed, optionally together with trospium chloride
or another peripherally restricted anticholinergic to reduce
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects. The two agents act on non-
overlapping vigilance and cholinergic-processing pathways relevant to
PD-NCD and are expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement
in cognition and daytime function compared with either agent alone at
any individually achievable dose without relying on dopamine
receptor blockade.
