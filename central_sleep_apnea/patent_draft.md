## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Central Sleep Apnea (CSA) by co-administering
(a) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with 5-HT2C
antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine, and (b) an orexin
receptor antagonist, in particular suvorexant. The invention is
intended for carefully selected adult subjects with stable CSA who
remain on standard respiratory management and who experience sleep-
wake instability, circadian dysregulation, insomnia-like sleep
fragmentation, or poor tolerance of ventilatory support. The
invention is not intended to replace respiratory support, adaptive
servo-ventilation, phrenic nerve stimulation, or treatment of
unstable central respiratory failure.

## Background of the invention

Central Sleep Apnea is a sleep-related breathing disorder
characterized by repetitive episodes of reduced or absent
respiratory effort during sleep, resulting in recurrent central
apneas or hypopneas. CSA may occur in association with heart
failure, neurologic disease, opioid exposure, high-altitude
periodic breathing, treatment-emergent sleep apnea, idiopathic
forms, or other recognized disorders of respiratory drive and sleep
state stability.

The prevalence of clinically meaningful CSA is lower than that of
obstructive sleep apnea but the burden is substantial because the
disorder often occurs in medically vulnerable populations. Subjects
may experience severe sleep fragmentation, daytime fatigue,
unrefreshing sleep, morning headache, poor adherence to nocturnal
ventilatory support, cardiovascular stress, and caregiver burden.
Unlike simple insomnia, the complaint exists in a setting of
instability of respiratory drive and sleep-state transitions.

The current standard of care is respiratory- and etiology-directed.
It consists primarily of treatment of the underlying condition,
positive airway pressure or adaptive servo-ventilation in selected
cases, oxygen therapy, medication adjustment, phrenic nerve
stimulation in selected patients, and avoidance of respiratory
destabilizers. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United
States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for CSA as a
primary indication. Yet many stable, treated subjects continue to
experience marked sleep fragmentation, circadian misalignment, and
difficulty tolerating or sustaining respiratory therapy.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses is not a
failure of respiratory-support technology itself, but the lack of a
carefully selected pharmacologic regimen that improves sleep-state
stability, circadian alignment, and adherence-supporting sleep
quality without being framed as a substitute for respiratory
management.

First, CSA is frequently worsened by unstable sleep-state
transitions, recurrent arousal, and poor continuity of sleep.
Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. This combination supports circadian resynchronization,
improves subjective sleep quality, and can reduce nocturnal
hypervigilance. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses the
*circadian-disorganization and sleep-fragmentation substrate* of
CSA.

Second, wake-maintenance pressure and maladaptive nocturnal arousal
may worsen instability in selected stable CSA subjects who are
already receiving respiratory support. Suvorexant is a dual orexin
receptor antagonist approved for insomnia that facilitates sleep
onset and maintenance by reducing orexin-driven wakefulness. Formal
respiratory-safety studies have been conducted in selected sleep-
disordered-breathing and chronic respiratory populations, indicating
that the key question is selection of a stable, monitored
subpopulation. In the present invention, the suvorexant axis
addresses the *maladaptive nocturnal wake-drive and sleep-initiation
failure* substrate of CSA in carefully selected, respiratorily
stable subjects.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
with 5-HT2C antagonism in combination with an orexin receptor
antagonist for CSA specifically in selected, stable subjects
receiving standard respiratory management.

The present invention recognizes that restoration of circadian
alignment and stable sleep timing (an agomelatine axis) and
attenuation of orexin-driven wake-maintenance pressure (a
suvorexant axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for
improving sleep continuity and respiratory-treatment tolerance in
CSA. The combination is expected to produce greater improvement in
sleep continuity, sleep-onset latency, and adherence-supporting
sleep quality than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose, without asserting replacement of respiratory
support or direct correction of unstable respiratory drive.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) suvorexant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Central Sleep Apnea in a selected adult subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of agomelatine and a therapeutically effective
amount of suvorexant while the subject continues standard
respiratory management.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) suvorexant or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of CSA according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with continued
ventilatory support, sleep monitoring, and respiratory follow-up.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a
preferred dose of 25 mg nightly and optional escalation to
50 mg nightly after about 2 weeks if clinically indicated. The
invention contemplates suvorexant in a daily dose of about 5 mg to
about 20 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a preferred adult
dose of 10 mg to 20 mg nightly. The inventive feature is the
concurrent engagement of melatonergic circadian resynchronization
and orexin-antagonist sleep-state stabilization in a stable,
monitored CSA population.

The technical effect of the combination is improvement, in selected
subjects with CSA, in sleep continuity, subjective sleep quality,
sleep-onset latency with respiratory support in place, and
respiratory-treatment adherence, that is greater than the sum of
the individual effects of agomelatine and suvorexant administered
as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Suvorexant"* means the dual orexin receptor antagonist approved
for insomnia. The term includes suvorexant free base, salts,
polymorphs, solvates, hydrates, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent orexin receptor antagonists
with a clinically comparable sleep-promoting profile.

*"Central Sleep Apnea"* or *"CSA"* means a sleep-related breathing
disorder characterized by repetitive episodes of reduced or absent
respiratory effort during sleep due to instability of central
respiratory drive.

*"Stable CSA subject"* means a subject whose CSA has been medically
characterized, who is on an established respiratory-management plan
if indicated, and who is not in unstable central respiratory
failure, acute intoxication-related respiratory depression, or
other acute respiratory decompensation.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a stable adult subject for the
treatment of CSA according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
sleep-continuity score, respiratory-support adherence, sleep-onset
latency, daytime fatigue, quality of life, or investigator-rated
CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is stabilization of circadian timing
and reduction of sleep-state fragmentation in stable CSA.

#### 2.2 Suvorexant

Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia. By reducing orexin-mediated wake drive, it facilitates
sleep onset and maintenance. In the present invention, suvorexant
is used only in selected, stable CSA subjects under close
monitoring and as an adjunct to respiratory support rather than as
a stand-alone respiratory treatment.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
suvorexant, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as an agomelatine tablet and a
suvorexant tablet.

Excipients useful for the agomelatine composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers. Excipients useful for
the suvorexant composition include tablet binders, disintegrants,
lubricants, and film-coating polymers appropriate for low-dose
bedtime administration.

### 4. Dosage forms

Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime administration. Suvorexant is preferably formulated as an
oral tablet suitable for bedtime dosing.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
agomelatine tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of suvorexant tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to continued respiratory support,
symptom monitoring, and sleep scheduling.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For selected stable adult subjects with CSA, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days,
  titratable to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.
* Suvorexant 5 mg orally at bedtime for about 3 nights, titratable
  to 10 mg and then to 20 mg nightly if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.

The preferred embodiment requires that the subject continue
positive airway pressure, adaptive servo-ventilation, oxygen,
phrenic nerve stimulation, or other standard respiratory support as
indicated. The regimen is not preferred for unstable respiratory
control, opioid intoxication, or decompensated medically driven CSA.

Hepatic adjustment: clinically meaningful hepatic impairment is
exclusionary or requires careful monitoring because agomelatine is
associated with hepatotoxicity risk in susceptible patients.
Severe cardiovascular instability, uncontrolled decompensated heart
failure, or unstable respiratory status is exclusionary.

Medication-medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors. Suvorexant should be used with
caution alongside other CNS depressants. The regimen is not
intended to replace respiratory support or to treat acute central
respiratory decompensation.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to stable adult
subjects with CSA. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age >= 18 years; clinician-confirmed CSA;
stable respiratory-management plan in place if indicated;
clinically meaningful sleep fragmentation or sleep-initiation
difficulty interfering with adherence or quality of sleep; and
ability to participate in monitoring. Exclusion criteria include
unstable central respiratory failure, acute opioid intoxication or
withdrawal as the primary respiratory driver, severe decompensated
heart failure without stable respiratory plan, severe hepatic
disease, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

CSA in appropriately selected subjects often includes two
clinically linked but distinct problems: circadian / sleep-state
instability that fragments sleep, and maladaptive wake-drive that
undermines tolerance of respiratory therapy.

Agomelatine addresses the circadian-instability layer through
MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. Suvorexant addresses the
wake-drive layer through orexin antagonism. The two axes are
non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the
adherence-relevant sleep phenotype rather than on respiratory-drive
replacement per se.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in sleep continuity and respiratory-treatment
tolerance than either monotherapy alone. The taxonomy-aware pair
score for this combination, derived from the master receptor-
efficacy summary (`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`),
is 38, indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting
threshold for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays
for agomelatine; orexin receptor binding and antagonism assays for
suvorexant; and combined sleep-state stability assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include sleep-fragmentation models, circadian-shift paradigms,
respiratory-instability models under stable monitored conditions,
and arousal-threshold models. Because respiratory safety is central
to the indication, the preclinical program should explicitly
distinguish stable monitored respiratory settings from decompensated
settings and exclude the latter from the preferred clinical
embodiment.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
agomelatine 25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: suvorexant 10 mg
nightly for 3 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, liver-function monitoring, nocturnal oximetry,
capnography, and tolerability assessments are obtained in each
period. The principal purpose is to establish tolerability and
absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in stable adult CSA (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 90 (30 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): agomelatine + suvorexant.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): agomelatine + placebo for suvorexant.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard respiratory
  management.

All subjects continue prescribed respiratory support. The primary
endpoint is change from baseline in sleep-continuity score at week
6. Secondary endpoints include adherence to respiratory support,
sleep-onset latency, daytime fatigue, quality of life, and CGI-I.

* Sleep-apnea treatment patents such as *Sultiame for the treatment

* Generic orexin-antagonist insomnia patents disclose wake-drive

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (MT1 / MT2 agonism +
5-HT2C antagonism + orexin antagonism), (ii) the specific actives
(agomelatine + suvorexant), and (iii) the specific indication
(stable CSA treated adjunctively, not as primary respiratory
therapy). No prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit
rationale on which the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the adjunctive
treatment of Central Sleep Apnea in stable adult subjects receiving
respiratory management comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and a
therapeutically effective amount of suvorexant are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping circadian-stability and wake-
drive circuits implicated in sleep fragmentation and poor
respiratory-treatment tolerance, and are expected to produce
greater-than-additive improvement in sleep continuity and adherence
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
