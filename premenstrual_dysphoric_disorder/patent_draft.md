## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD) by
co-administering (a) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with
5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine, and
(b) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with 5-HT1A partial agonist
activity, in particular vilazodone. The invention is intended for
reproductive-age subjects with severe cyclical luteal-phase mood and
somatic symptoms causing clinically meaningful distress, and
addresses premenstrual irritability, mood lability, anxiety, sleep
disruption, and functional impairment. The invention is not
intended to pathologize normative mild premenstrual symptoms.

## Background of the invention

Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and
ICD-11 as a cyclic disorder in which severe affective and physical
symptoms occur during the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle and
improve after the onset of menses. Characteristic symptoms include
marked affective lability, irritability or anger, depressed mood,
anxiety or tension, together with associated sleep, concentration,
energy, appetite, or physical symptoms.

The prevalence of PMDD is clinically meaningful among reproductive-
age subjects and the burden is substantial. The disorder is
associated with work and school impairment, interpersonal conflict,
caregiver burden within families, reduced quality of life, and
elevated risk of mood destabilization. Symptoms are often sharply
time-locked to the luteal phase and may include severe evening
irritability and disturbed sleep before menses.

The current standard of care is primarily serotonergic and
gynecologic. It consists of SSRIs, oral contraceptive or hormonal
approaches in selected cases, cycle tracking, psychotherapy, and
lifestyle measures. No combination pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
specifically targeting both luteal-phase sleep/circadian disruption
and anxious-affective PMDD symptoms together. A clinically
meaningful gap remains for a regimen that addresses both overnight
recovery and daytime mood dysregulation during the symptomatic
window.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, PMDD frequently includes circadian disruption,
sleep fragmentation, and poor overnight recovery during the luteal
phase. Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a
potent agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. This combination supports circadian resynchronization,
improves subjective sleep quality, and may reduce nocturnal
hyperarousal. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses the *luteal-
phase sleep-disruption and recovery-failure* substrate of PMDD.

Second, PMDD also includes anxious-affective lability, irritability,
and intrusive negative cognition during the symptomatic premenstrual
window. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available antidepressant
that combines selective serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial
agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual mechanism supports
attenuation of anxious and depressive symptoms while maintaining an
established reproductive-age anxiety/mood pharmacology profile. The
vilazodone axis therefore addresses the *anxious-serotonergic and
affective-lability* substrate of PMDD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
with 5-HT2C antagonism in combination with a serotonin reuptake
inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial agonist for PMDD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that restoration of sleep-linked
recovery in the luteal phase (an agomelatine axis) and attenuation
of anxious-affective PMDD symptoms (a vilazodone axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of PMDD. The
combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement
in mood lability, irritability, sleep disruption, and functional
impairment than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating PMDD
in a human subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to
the subject a therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone, preferably in a
cycle-timed regimen aligned to the luteal phase or in a continuous
regimen with luteal-phase intensification.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of PMDD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with cycle
tracking and supportive care.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime during the
luteal phase or continuously in selected embodiments, with a
preferred initiation dose of 25 mg nightly. The invention
contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about 10 mg to about
40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with continuous or
luteal-phase-targeted dosing depending on clinical response. The
inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of melatonergic
sleep-recovery support and serotonergic affective stabilization in
the premenstrual symptomatic window.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with PMDD, in luteal-phase irritability, mood lability, sleep
disturbance, and functional impairment, that is greater than the
sum of the individual effects of agomelatine and vilazodone
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder"* or *"PMDD"* means the cyclic
disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 in which severe affective
and physical symptoms occur during the luteal phase and remit with
menses.

*"PMDD symptom severity"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
luteal-phase affective lability, irritability, anxiety, depressed
mood, sleep disturbance, or associated functional impairment
quantified by structured clinician assessment or validated PMDD
instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of PMDD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: PMDD symptom
severity, irritability score, sleep-quality score, role-function
score, responder rate, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is improvement of luteal-phase sleep
continuity and overnight emotional recovery.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
luteal-phase anxious and depressive lability. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of vilazodone is
reduction of PMDD-related mood destabilization.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of an agomelatine tablet and a vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the agomelatine composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the vilazodone
composition include diluents (microcrystalline cellulose, lactose
monohydrate), binders (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone),
disintegrants (crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime administration. Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an
oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a cycle-aligned supply of
agomelatine tablets, (b) a cycle-aligned supply of vilazodone
tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to menstrual-cycle
tracking and symptomatic-window dosing.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For reproductive-age subjects with PMDD, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime during the luteal phase,
  titratable to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated, or
  continuously in selected embodiments with luteal-phase emphasis.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food, titratable to
  20 mg or 40 mg daily, administered continuously or initiated in
  the luteal phase according to clinical need.

The preferred embodiment includes prospective symptom and cycle
tracking. The regimen is not intended to replace gynecologic
evaluation, endocrine assessment when indicated, or evidence-based
cycle-timed psychotherapy or supportive care.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to reproductive-
age subjects with PMDD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: cyclic luteal-phase symptom pattern
confirmed by prospective tracking; clinically meaningful distress or
role impairment; and willingness to participate in cycle-based
monitoring. Exclusion criteria include bipolar-spectrum mania,
pregnancy when contraindications apply, severe hepatic disease, and
known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

PMDD often includes two interacting layers: luteal-phase affective
lability and serotonergic vulnerability, and sleep / circadian
disruption that worsens next-day emotional regulation.

Agomelatine addresses the sleep-recovery layer through MT1 / MT2
agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. Vilazodone addresses the affective-
lability layer through SERT inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on the same cycle-bound symptom phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in PMDD burden than either monotherapy alone.
the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 31,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays
for agomelatine; SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; and hormone-state-dependent mood-regulation assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include cycle-sensitive affective-behavior models, sleep-
fragmentation paradigms, and stress-reactivity assays. A supra-
additive effect of the combination on reduction of luteal-phase
symptom severity, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in Section 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy reproductive-age adult volunteers. Period 1:
agomelatine 25 mg nightly during a defined luteal-phase window;
Period 2: vilazodone titrated to 20 mg/day; Period 3: the
combination. Full PK sampling, liver-function monitoring, and
tolerability assessments are obtained. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in PMDD over three menstrual cycles (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): agomelatine + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo with standard supportive care.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in PMDD symptom
severity over the luteal phase. Secondary endpoints include
irritability score, sleep-quality score, role-function score,
responder rate, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping sleep-recovery and affective-
stabilization circuits implicated in luteal-phase symptom
expression and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in PMDD symptom severity than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.
