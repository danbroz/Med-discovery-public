## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Obsessive Compulsive Disorder (OCD) by
co-administering (a) a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse agonist /
antagonist, in particular pimavanserin, and (b) a serotonergic
antidepressant with serotonin reuptake inhibition and 5-HT1A
partial agonist activity, in particular vilazodone. The invention
is intended for subjects with intrusive obsessions and compulsions,
and addresses obsessional distress, compulsive drive, maladaptive
salience tagging, reassurance-seeking or ritualized behavior, and
functional impairment while continuing evidence-based behavioral
treatment such as exposure and response prevention.

## Background of the invention

Obsessive Compulsive Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11
as a disorder characterized by obsessions, compulsions, or both.
Obsessions are intrusive and unwanted thoughts, urges, or images
that cause marked anxiety or distress; compulsions are repetitive
behaviors or mental acts that the individual feels driven to
perform in response to an obsession or according to rigid rules.
The syndrome may include contamination fears, checking, symmetry /
ordering concerns, taboo intrusive thoughts, hoarding-related
features, or other repetitive obsessional phenomena.

The prevalence of OCD is clinically meaningful and the burden is
substantial. The disorder is associated with major impairment in
school, work, family life, and quality of life; high rates of
depression and suicidality; severe time consumption by rituals; and
reduced ability to sustain relationships or caregiving roles. Even
with available treatment, many subjects remain partially symptomatic
or treatment resistant.

The current standard of care is primarily serotonergic and
behavioral. It consists of high-dose SSRIs or clomipramine,
exposure and response prevention (ERP), and in more refractory
cases antipsychotic augmentation or other off-label strategies. No
pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for a combination strategy
that directly targets both serotonergic anxious-compulsive distress
and non-dopaminergic salience distortion in OCD. A clinically
meaningful gap remains for a mechanism-based regimen that may
improve obsessional severity without relying solely on D2-heavy
antipsychotic augmentation.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, obsessional distress and compulsive drive are
strongly linked to serotonergic tone and the regulation of anxious-
compulsive cognition. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available
antidepressant that combines selective serotonin reuptake
inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual
mechanism supports attenuation of anxious-compulsive
symptomatology and intrusive ideation. The vilazodone axis
therefore addresses the *obsessional distress and anxious-
compulsive amplification* substrate of OCD.

Second, OCD also includes pathologic salience tagging, rigid
internal cue weighting, and perseverative attentional capture
around feared or incomplete internal or external stimuli.
Pimavanserin (Nuplazid®) is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist /
antagonist approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions
associated with Parkinson disease psychosis. Pimavanserin has no
clinically meaningful D2 antagonism and therefore can modify
5-HT2A-mediated salience processing without adding direct
dopaminergic motor burden. The pimavanserin axis therefore
addresses the *maladaptive salience and perseverative-attention*
substrate of OCD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist /
antagonist in combination with a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist for OCD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of obsessional
distress (a vilazodone HTR1A / SERT axis) and attenuation of
maladaptive salience tagging (a pimavanserin 5-HT2A axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of OCD. The
combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement
in obsessive severity, compulsive frequency, and functional burden
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pimavanserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) vilazodone or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating OCD
in a human subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to
the subject a therapeutically effective amount of pimavanserin and
a therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of OCD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with ERP or other
evidence-based behavioral treatment.

The invention contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose of about
17 mg to about 34 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 34 mg/day. The invention contemplates
vilazodone in a daily dose of about 10 mg to about 40 mg
administered orally once daily with food, with a preferred dose of
20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from 10 mg/day. The inventive
feature is the concurrent engagement of 5-HT2A salience regulation
and 5-HT1A / serotonin-transporter-mediated reduction of obsessional
distress.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with OCD, in obsessional severity, compulsive frequency, intrusive-
thought burden, and functional impairment, that is greater than the
sum of the individual effects of pimavanserin and vilazodone
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically equivalent
5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Obsessive Compulsive Disorder"* or *"OCD"* means the disorder
defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by obsessions, compulsions, or both,
causing distress, time consumption, or impairment.

*"Y-BOCS total"* means a clinically meaningful burden of obsession
and compulsion severity quantified by the Yale-Brown Obsessive
Compulsive Scale or a functionally equivalent structured measure.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of OCD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: Y-BOCS total,
obsession subscale, compulsion subscale, responder rate,
role-function score, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. It has no clinically meaningful
affinity for dopamine D2 receptors. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of pimavanserin is attenuation
of maladaptive salience tagging and perseverative attentional
capture.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
obsessional distress and anxious-compulsive ideation. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
vilazodone is reduction of obsession-related distress and urge
amplification.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pimavanserin and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of a pimavanserin capsule or tablet and a vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the pimavanserin composition include capsule
fill excipients, diluents, disintegrants, and stabilizers suitable
for marketed pimavanserin formulation profiles. Excipients useful
for the vilazodone composition include diluents (microcrystalline
cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders (hydroxypropyl
methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants (crospovidone, sodium
starch glycolate), lubricants (magnesium stearate), glidants
(colloidal silicon dioxide), and film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or tablet
suitable for once-daily use. Vilazodone is preferably formulated as
an oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pimavanserin capsules or tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to ERP or
other exposure-based behavioral treatment.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with OCD, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Pimavanserin 17 mg orally once daily, titratable to 34 mg once
  daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.

The regimen is not intended to replace ERP or other evidence-based
behavioral treatment, and it is not intended as a D2-heavy
antipsychotic strategy.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
OCD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 12 years; clinically meaningful obsessions or
compulsions; and willingness to participate in ERP or other
behavioral treatment. Exclusion criteria include untreated primary
psychotic disorder, severe hepatic impairment, clinically
significant baseline QT prolongation, and known hypersensitivity to
either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

OCD often includes two interacting layers: obsession-related
distress and anxious-compulsive drive, and maladaptive salience
tagging that makes cues feel incomplete, dangerous, or impossible
to ignore.

Vilazodone addresses the distress / urge layer through SERT
inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Pimavanserin addresses the
salience layer through selective 5-HT2A inverse agonism /
antagonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on the same obsession-compulsion phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in OCD burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 32,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A binding and inverse-agonism assays for
pimavanserin; SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; and combined salience / compulsivity network assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include compulsive checking models, reversal-learning paradigms,
marble-burying or compulsive-grooming tasks, and cue-triggered
perseveration models. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
reduction of compulsive behavior and obsession-related distress,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in Section 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
pimavanserin 34 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone titrated to
20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability assessments are obtained.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult OCD (FIG. 4). Three arms,
1:1:1 randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pimavanserin + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for pimavanserin.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with ERP.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in Y-BOCS total at
week 12. Secondary endpoints include obsession subscale, compulsion
subscale, responder rate, role-function score, and CGI-I.

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Obsessive Compulsive Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of pimavanserin and a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping salience-tagging and obsessional-
distress circuits and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in obsessive severity and compulsive burden than either
agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
