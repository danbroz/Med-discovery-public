## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Psychotic Disorder Due to Another Medical
Condition (PD-AMC) by co-administering (a) a dopamine D2 receptor
antagonist and (b) a muscarinic M1/M4 receptor agonist, in
particular the combination of perospirone and xanomeline,
optionally further with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic
such as trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse
effects. The invention addresses hallucinations, delusions,
behavioral disorganization, and associated cognitive burden in
subjects whose psychotic syndrome is attributable to a defined
medical condition; it does not assert disease-modification of the
underlying medical condition itself.

## Background of the invention

Psychotic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition is recognized in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a psychotic syndrome in which prominent
hallucinations or delusions, and in some cases disorganized
behavior or thinking, are judged to be the direct physiologic
consequence of a medical condition. The underlying medical
condition may include neurologic disease, endocrine disease,
autoimmune or inflammatory conditions, epilepsy, infectious CNS
processes, neoplastic syndromes, metabolic disorders, or other
recognized medical causes of psychosis.

The prevalence of PD-AMC is difficult to estimate because the
syndrome is usually coded under the primary medical condition or a
broader encephalopathic syndrome. Nonetheless, the clinical burden
is substantial. Subjects may experience hallucinations, delusions,
agitation, severe mistrust, behavioral disruption, caregiver
distress, and reduced adherence to the treatment of the underlying
medical condition. Unlike primary schizophrenia-spectrum disorders,
the course is often shaped by the activity of the medical disease,
inflammatory state, medication exposure, and systemic physiologic
stress.

The current standard of care is supportive and etiology-directed. It
consists primarily of treating the underlying medical condition to
the extent possible, removing provoking agents where feasible, and
using antipsychotic medications symptomatically when necessary. No
pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for PD-AMC as a primary
indication. Conventional D2-blocking antipsychotic monotherapy is
often limited by cognitive dulling, motor adverse effects,
orthostasis, or interaction with the medical fragility of the
subject, while also failing to address the cognitive and
cholinergic dimension of the syndrome.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, the psychotic symptoms of PD-AMC frequently
require attenuation of dopaminergic salience misattribution and
behavioral disorganization. Perospirone is an atypical
antipsychotic approved in Japan for schizophrenia that functions as
a D2 receptor antagonist with additional serotonergic properties.
In the present invention, the D2-antagonist axis addresses the
*hallucinatory / delusional salience and behavioral-disorganization*
substrate of PD-AMC.

Second, many medical-cause psychoses occur in subjects who also
have attention, executive, or cognitive vulnerability. Muscarinic
M1 receptor signaling facilitates cortical and hippocampal
cognition, while M4 signaling contributes to antipsychotic effects
through striatal mechanisms. Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist developed in the 1990s and more recently
formulated with the peripherally restricted antimuscarinic
trospium as the FDA-approved xanomeline–trospium combination
(Cobenfy®) for schizophrenia in adults. The xanomeline axis
therefore addresses the *muscarinic antipsychotic and cognitive
support substrate* of PD-AMC and offers a complementary route to
psychosis reduction that is mechanistically distinct from D2
blockade.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a D2 antagonist in combination with
an M1/M4 agonist for PD-AMC specifically.

The present invention recognizes that dopaminergic stabilization of
psychotic salience (a perospirone D2 axis) and muscarinic
facilitation of antipsychotic and cognitive control
(a xanomeline M1/M4 axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of PD-AMC. The combination is expected to
produce greater reduction in hallucinations, delusions, and
behavioral disorganization than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose, with better preservation of cognition
than D2-heavy escalation alone. The invention does not assert
disease-modification of the underlying medical condition.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) perospirone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) xanomeline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, optionally further comprising a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic agent such as trospium chloride, together with one
or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Psychotic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition in a human
subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject
a therapeutically effective amount of perospirone and a
therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline, optionally further
with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) perospirone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) xanomeline, optionally co-formulated with trospium, in a second
unitary dosage form, and (c) written instructions for the
treatment of PD-AMC according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein
while continuing standard care for the underlying medical
condition.

The invention contemplates perospirone in a daily dose of about
4 mg to about 48 mg administered orally in one or two divided doses,
with a preferred adult dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day. The invention
contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about 50 mg to about
250 mg administered orally as the free base or as the tartrate salt
in two divided doses; when xanomeline is co-administered with
trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is about 40 mg to about
60 mg administered orally in two divided doses.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in
subjects with PD-AMC, in hallucination severity, delusion
severity, behavioral disorganization, and caregiver burden, that
is greater than the sum of the individual effects of perospirone
and xanomeline administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-
yl]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine, an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist. The term as used herein includes the free base,
the tartrate salt and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Trospium chloride"* or *"trospium"* means the quaternary ammonium
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist that does not
appreciably cross the blood-brain barrier and may be used to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline.

*"Psychotic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition"* or
*"PD-AMC"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 syndrome in which prominent
hallucinations or delusions, with or without associated behavioral
disorganization, are the direct physiologic consequence of a
defined medical condition.

*"Psychotic-symptom composite"* means a clinically meaningful burden
of hallucinations, delusions, suspiciousness, or disorganization
quantified by structured clinician assessment or validated psychosis
rating instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the
treatment of PD-AMC according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
psychotic-symptom composite, hallucination severity, delusion
severity, disorganization score, caregiver burden, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two or more active agents to a
subject so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted
on the subject during the period in which the pharmacological
effect of the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether
the agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is reduction of hallucination / delusion salience and
behavioral disorganization through D2 antagonism. Perospirone is
generally used in adult schizophrenia at oral doses in the range of
about 8 mg to 48 mg/day.

#### 2.2 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. Activation of
the M1 receptor facilitates cortical and hippocampal cognitive
function, while activation of the M4 receptor contributes to
antipsychotic effects in striatal circuits. Xanomeline reached the
market in fixed-dose combination with trospium chloride as Cobenfy®
for schizophrenia in adults and is under live U.S. brand-patent
protection via the marketed combination and related rights.

#### 2.3 Trospium (optional)

Trospium chloride is a quaternary ammonium peripherally restricted
muscarinic antagonist that does not appreciably cross the blood-
brain barrier. In the present invention, trospium may be included
at a daily dose effective to mitigate peripheral cholinergic
adverse effects of xanomeline without materially antagonizing the
central antipsychotic and cognitive effect.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise perospirone and
xanomeline, optionally further comprising trospium, together with
at least one pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The compositions
may take the form of a co-packaged kit of two or three separate
dosage forms or a unitary fixed-dose combination.

The mass ratio of xanomeline (calculated as the free base) to
perospirone (calculated as the free base) is in the range of about
2:1 to about 50:1, preferably about 4:1 to about 20:1. When
trospium is included, the molar ratio of trospium to xanomeline is
approximately 1:1 to 1:3.

### 4. Dosage forms

Xanomeline (with or without trospium) is preferably formulated as
twice-daily oral capsules. Perospirone is preferably formulated as
an immediate-release oral tablet.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
xanomeline or xanomeline-trospium capsules, (b) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to
medical-management continuation and psychosis monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with PD-AMC, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Xanomeline-trospium 50 mg/20 mg orally twice daily for the first
  three days, then 100 mg/20 mg twice daily for the next four days,
  then 125 mg/30 mg twice daily thereafter as tolerated.
* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily for 7 days, then 8 mg twice
  daily thereafter, with titration up to 12 mg twice daily if
  clinically needed.

Where trospium is omitted, xanomeline may be administered at an
equivalent free-base exposure range with heightened monitoring for
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects.

Hepatic adjustment: xanomeline-trospium is not recommended in
moderate or severe hepatic impairment. Severe renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP2D6 inhibitors without dose review of xanomeline and
perospirone. Avoid strong CYP3A4 inducers. QT monitoring should be
considered when other QT-prolonging agents are present.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with PD-AMC. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age ≥ 18 years; clinician-confirmed psychotic
syndrome attributable to a defined medical condition; clinically
meaningful hallucinations or delusions at baseline; and stable
management of the underlying medical condition for at least 4 weeks
where clinically possible. Exclusion criteria include primary
schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder without a medical-cause
etiology, active delirium requiring different emergent management,
severe hepatic or renal impairment, and known hypersensitivity to
either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

PD-AMC includes two interacting symptom layers: psychotic salience
misattribution and the cognitive / attentional vulnerability that
often accompanies medical-cause psychosis.

Perospirone addresses the first layer through D2 antagonism,
reducing hallucination / delusion salience and behavioral
disorganization. Xanomeline addresses the second layer, and may
also contribute directly to antipsychotic control, through M1/M4
muscarinic agonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the
receptor level and converge on the psychotic-symptom phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in hallucinations, delusions, and cognitive
stability than either monotherapy alone. The taxonomy-aware pair
score for this combination, derived from the master receptor-
efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 37,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: D2 receptor binding and functional antagonism
assays for perospirone; M1 receptor binding and calcium-
mobilization assays; M4 receptor functional assays; and combined
salience / cognitive-control network assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include psychosis-like behavioral models, prepulse inhibition,
novel object recognition, and disease-relevant neurologic or
inflammatory models producing psychosis-like phenotypes. A supra-
additive effect of the combination on psychosis-related salience
and cognitive stabilization, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
xanomeline-trospium 100 mg/20 mg twice daily for 7 days; Period 2:
perospirone titrated to 16 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the
combination for 7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and
tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal
purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of clinically
meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult PD-AMC (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): perospirone + xanomeline.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): perospirone + placebo for
  xanomeline.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard-of-care medical
  management.

All subjects continue treatment of the underlying medical
condition. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in
psychotic-symptom composite at week 12. Secondary endpoints include
hallucination severity, delusion severity, disorganization score,
caregiver burden, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Psychotic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the D2
antagonist perospirone and a therapeutically effective amount of
the muscarinic M1/M4 agonist xanomeline, optionally further with
the peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium chloride, are
disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping psychotic-salience
and cognitive-control circuits implicated in medically caused
hallucinations and delusions and are expected to produce greater-
than-additive symptomatic improvement than either agent alone at
any individually achievable dose while continuing standard care for
the underlying medical condition.
