## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Anorexia Nervosa (AN) by co-administering
(a) a serotonergic tetracyclic agent having antagonist activity at
5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, in particular mianserin, and
(b) a cannabinoid CB1 receptor agonist, in particular
delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (Δ9-THC), preferably as an orally
administered pharmaceutical formulation such as dronabinol. The
invention is intended for adult subjects, and in certain specialist-
supervised embodiments older adolescents where legally and
clinically appropriate, with Anorexia Nervosa who are concurrently
receiving nutritional rehabilitation and evidence-based eating-
disorder care. The invention is not intended to replace refeeding,
medical monitoring, or psychotherapy, and is not directed to
general appetite enhancement, obesity, recreational cannabis use, or
non-medical weight manipulation.

## Background of the invention

Anorexia Nervosa is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as an eating
disorder characterized by restriction of energy intake relative to
requirements leading to significantly low body weight, intense fear
of gaining weight or becoming fat, and disturbance in the way in
which one's body weight or shape is experienced, undue influence of
body weight or shape on self-evaluation, or persistent lack of
recognition of the seriousness of low body weight.

The prevalence of AN is lower than that of broader anxiety or mood
disorders, but the burden is among the highest in psychiatry
because of severe medical instability, high relapse risk,
functional collapse, and elevated mortality. Subjects may experience
severe dietary restraint, meal-related panic, hyperactivity,
obsessive weight and shape cognition, sleep disruption, early
satiety, dysphoria, and marked aversion to nutritional
rehabilitation. Family burden and healthcare utilization are often
substantial.

The current standard of care is multidisciplinary and medically
intensive. It consists primarily of nutritional rehabilitation,
weight restoration, psychotherapy such as family-based treatment or
cognitive-behavioral therapy for eating disorders, and close
medical monitoring. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for
the treatment of AN as a primary indication. Existing off-label
medication approaches are inconsistent and often fail to
simultaneously address restrictive-drive anxiety, sleep
disturbance, and low appetitive reward value of eating.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, AN frequently includes intense meal-related
anxiety, rigid restrictive cognition, disturbed sleep, and reduced
drive toward intake despite physiologic need. Mianserin is a
tetracyclic antidepressant with antagonist activity at 5-HT2A /
5-HT2C receptors, alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and H1 histamine
receptors. This pharmacology offers a route to reduction of
restrictive anxious activation, improvement in sleep, and relief of
5-HT2C-linked appetite inhibition. The mianserin axis therefore
addresses the *restrictive-drive, anxiogenic, and serotonergic
appetite-inhibition* substrate of AN.

Second, AN also includes low incentive salience of food, poor meal
reward, early satiety-related aversion, and reduced hedonic drive
toward eating. Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (Δ9-THC), available
clinically as dronabinol, is a CB1 receptor agonist with known
effects on appetite, nausea, reward tone, and meal-related hedonic
salience. In specialist medical settings, cannabinoid agonism
provides a mechanistically distinct route to increase appetite and
food-related motivation. The Δ9-THC axis therefore addresses the
*appetitive-reward and low intake-drive* substrate of AN.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C-antagonist
antidepressant in combination with a CB1 agonist for AN
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that reduction of restrictive
anxious drive (a mianserin axis) and enhancement of food-related
appetitive salience (a Δ9-THC CB1 axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for treatment of AN. The combination is
expected to produce greater improvement in nutritional-rehabilitation
tolerance, meal completion, weight-restoration trajectory, and
eating-disorder distress than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose, while remaining strictly adjunctive
to specialist eating-disorder care.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) mianserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, prodrug, or clinically equivalent CB1 agonist thereof,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Anorexia Nervosa in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of mianserin and a therapeutically effective amount of delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol, preferably in an orally administered
pharmaceutical formulation. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) mianserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol or a salt thereof in a second
unitary dosage form, and (c) written instructions for the treatment
of AN according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction
with nutritional rehabilitation, medical monitoring, and
psychotherapy.

The invention contemplates mianserin in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 90 mg administered orally once daily or in divided
doses, with a preferred dose of 15 mg to 60 mg/day and a preferred
bedtime initiation dose of 15 mg to 30 mg/day. The invention
contemplates delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol in a daily dose of about
1 mg to about 20 mg administered orally in divided doses, with a
preferred dose range of about 2.5 mg to about 10 mg/day in divided
doses as dronabinol or an equivalent pharmaceutical oral
formulation. The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of
serotonergic restriction-relief and CB1-mediated appetitive
salience support during nutritional rehabilitation.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with AN, in restrictive meal-related distress, food aversion, and
nutritional-rehabilitation resistance, and an increase in appetite,
meal completion, and weight-restoration trajectory, that is greater
than the sum of the individual effects of mianserin and delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Mianserin"* means the tetracyclic antidepressant
2-methyl-1,2,3,4,10,14b-hexahydrodibenzo[c,f]pyrazino[1,2-a]
azepine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof. The term
includes the hydrochloride and other pharmaceutically acceptable
salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically
labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol"* or *"Δ9-THC"* means the principal
psychoactive phytocannabinoid acting as a CB1 receptor agonist,
including oral dronabinol and other pharmaceutically controlled
oral or transmucosal formulations with clinically relevant CB1
agonism.

*"Anorexia Nervosa"* or *"AN"* means the eating disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by restriction leading to significantly low
body weight together with intense fear of gaining weight or
disturbance in body weight / shape experience.

*"Weight-restoration / meal-completion composite"* means a
clinically meaningful measure of meal completion, caloric
acceptance, weight-restoration trajectory, appetite, or treatment
tolerance during structured eating-disorder care.

*"Specialist eating-disorder care"* means multidisciplinary care
including nutritional rehabilitation, psychotherapy, and medical
monitoring appropriate to the severity of the eating disorder.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of AN according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: weight-
restoration / meal-completion composite, appetite score,
meal-related anxiety, eating-disorder psychopathology score,
responder rate, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Mianserin

Mianserin is a tetracyclic antidepressant with antagonist activity
at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and H1
histamine receptors. In the present invention, the principal
mechanistic contribution of mianserin is reduction of restrictive
anxious drive, meal-related distress, and 5-HT2C-linked appetite
inhibition.

#### 2.2 Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol

Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol is a CB1 receptor agonist available in
controlled pharmaceutical form as dronabinol. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol is enhancement of appetite, reward salience of
food, and tolerance of nutritional rehabilitation. The preferred
embodiment is a specialist-supervised oral pharmaceutical
formulation rather than smoked cannabis.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise mianserin and
delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol, together with at least one
pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The compositions may take
the form of a co-packaged kit of a mianserin tablet and an oral
dronabinol or equivalent CB1-agonist dosage form.

Excipients useful for the mianserin composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and film-coating polymers
suitable for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the
delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol component include oil-based oral
capsule carriers, soft-gel matrices, or other pharmaceutical oral
vehicles appropriate for controlled cannabinoid dosing.

### 4. Dosage forms

Mianserin is preferably formulated as an oral tablet administered
once daily, preferably at bedtime. Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol is
preferably formulated as an oral capsule, solution, or other
controlled oral dosage form.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
mianserin tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of oral delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol units, and (c) written instructions aligned to
nutritional rehabilitation, meal support, medical monitoring, and
controlled-substance handling.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with AN in specialist care, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Mianserin 15 mg orally at bedtime for about 7 days, titratable to
  30 mg and then to 60 mg nightly if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.
* Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol about 1 mg to about 10 mg per day
  orally in divided doses, preferably initiated at 2.5 mg once or
  twice daily and titrated according to appetite response,
  tolerability, and psychiatric monitoring.

The preferred embodiment expressly excludes uncontrolled smoked or
recreational cannabis use. The cannabinoid component is
specialist-supervised, pharmaceutical, and integrated into a
structured refeeding plan.

Medication-medication interactions: coadministration with other CNS
depressants, sedatives, or intoxicants requires caution.
Psychiatric monitoring for anxiety, dissociation, or psychosis-
adjacent effects is required because cannabinoids may worsen such
symptoms in susceptible subjects. The regimen is not intended to
replace medical stabilization, inpatient care when indicated, or
refeeding protocols.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
AN. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 18 years or older adolescent subjects only where
legally and clinically appropriate under specialist supervision;
diagnosis of AN; active participation in nutritional rehabilitation;
and clinically meaningful appetite suppression, meal-related
distress, or poor weight-restoration trajectory. Exclusion criteria
include active psychotic disorder, severe substance-use disorder,
uncontrolled cardiovascular instability, severe hepatic impairment,
pregnancy where cannabinoid exposure is contraindicated, and known
hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

AN often includes two interacting layers: restrictive anxiety and
aversive control over intake, and low appetitive reward or drive
toward food.

Mianserin addresses the restrictive-anxiety layer through 5-HT2A /
5-HT2C antagonism and related serotonergic disinhibition.
Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol addresses the low-appetite and
reward-deficit layer through CB1 agonism. The two axes are
non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on improved meal
tolerance and weight-restoration capacity.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in AN treatment tolerance than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 33,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C binding and antagonism assays
for mianserin; CB1 receptor binding and agonism assays for delta-
9-tetrahydrocannabinol; and combined appetite-reward assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include restricted-feeding models, anxiety-linked intake suppression
models, reward-deficit paradigms, and meal-engagement tasks. A
supra-additive effect of the combination on intake normalization and
meal-related distress reduction, relative to either monotherapy at
any individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof
point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 21-55 years). Period 1:
mianserin 15 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: oral delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol on repeated study days; Period 3: the
combination. Full PK sampling, sedation monitoring, psychiatric
tolerability monitoring, appetite assessments, and adverse-event
monitoring are obtained. The principal purpose is to establish
tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult AN receiving specialist care (FIG. 4).
Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): mianserin + oral delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): mianserin + placebo cannabinoid.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo + specialist eating-disorder care.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in a weight-
restoration / meal-completion composite at week 12. Secondary
endpoints include appetite score, meal-related anxiety,
eating-disorder psychopathology score, responder rate, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of a 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C-antagonist
antidepressant with a CB1 agonist for AN specifically. The closest
hits are:

* Cannabinoid appetite and weight-management patents disclose
  cannabinoid compositions but do not teach the present pair or the
  present specialist-supervised anorexia use.
* Eating-disorder patents involving glutamatergic or other
  mechanisms disclose different pharmacology and not the present
  convergent-circuit rationale.
* Generic cannabinoid plus antipsychotic or serotonergic filings do
  not disclose the present integration into nutritional
  rehabilitation.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (5-HT2A / 5-HT2C
antagonism + CB1 agonism), (ii) the specific actives (mianserin +
delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol), and (iii) the specific indication
(AN). No prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit
rationale on which the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Anorexia Nervosa comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of mianserin and a therapeutically effective
amount of delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol are disclosed. The two
agents act on non-overlapping restrictive-drive and appetitive-
reward circuits implicated in poor nutritional-rehabilitation
tolerance and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in appetite, meal completion, and weight-restoration
trajectory than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose while remaining adjunctive to specialist eating-disorder care.
