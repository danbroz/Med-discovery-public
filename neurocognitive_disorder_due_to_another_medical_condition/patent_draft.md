## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to
Another Medical Condition (NCD-AMC) by co-administering
(a) a muscarinic M1/M4 receptor agonist and (b) a histamine H3
receptor antagonist or inverse agonist, in particular the
combination of xanomeline and pitolisant, optionally further with a
peripherally restricted antimuscarinic such as trospium to mitigate
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects. The invention addresses
cognitive symptoms, daytime alertness, and caregiver burden in
subjects whose neurocognitive syndrome is attributable to a defined
underlying medical condition; it does not assert disease-
modification of the underlying medical condition itself.

## Background of the invention

Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Another Medical
Condition is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a syndrome in
which clinically significant cognitive decline is attributable to
the direct physiologic effects of a non-degenerative or otherwise
defined medical condition. The affected cognitive domains may
include complex attention, executive function, learning and memory,
language, perceptual-motor function, and social cognition. The
underlying medical condition may include endocrine disorders,
autoimmune disease, metabolic disease, infectious or inflammatory
conditions, epilepsy, multiple sclerosis, systemic lupus
erythematosus, HIV-related but non-primary neurocognitive injury,
hypoxic / anoxic insults, hepatic encephalopathy, renal disease,
or other recognized medical causes of persistent neurocognitive
impairment.

The prevalence of NCD-AMC is difficult to estimate because it is an
umbrella diagnosis that cuts across many underlying diseases and is
often coded by the primary medical condition rather than by the
neurocognitive syndrome itself. Nonetheless, the clinical burden is
material. These subjects frequently experience loss of executive
function, slowed processing speed, impaired new learning, reduced
functional independence, and increased caregiver burden. Unlike
primary neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer's disease or
Lewy body disease, the clinical course in NCD-AMC is often shaped
by the status of the underlying medical condition, treatment
exposure, inflammatory state, and systemic physiologic stress.

The current standard of care is supportive and etiology-directed.
It consists primarily of treating the underlying medical condition
to the extent possible, optimizing sleep and mood, minimizing
anticholinergic or sedating polypharmacy, and using rehabilitation
and caregiver supports. No pharmaceutical product is approved by
the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically
for the treatment of NCD-AMC as a primary indication. Where
clinicians do use cognition-directed medications, they typically
borrow acetylcholinesterase inhibitors or stimulants from other
indications, with inconsistent benefit and tolerability.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses is the
absence of a defensible, mechanism-based symptomatic regimen for
cognitive and arousal impairment in NCD-AMC that does not rely on
acetylcholinesterase inhibition alone. NCD-AMC frequently involves
a mixed phenotype of reduced cortical cholinergic tone, impaired
arousal-system support, and fluctuating attention. A single-agent
approach often leaves one half of the phenotype untreated: a purely
pro-cholinergic intervention may worsen peripheral cholinergic
burden, while a purely wake-promoting intervention may fail to
restore cortical signal quality.

The present invention recognizes that two non-overlapping
cholinergic-support mechanisms can be combined to support cognition
and daytime functioning in NCD-AMC. First, the muscarinic M1
receptor is a Gαq-coupled postsynaptic receptor concentrated in
cerebral cortex and hippocampus that mediates a substantial portion
of cholinergic facilitation of cognition; the M4 receptor is a
Gαi-coupled receptor concentrated in striatum and contributes to
behavioral stabilization. Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist developed in the 1990s and more recently
formulated with the peripherally restricted antimuscarinic
trospium as the FDA-approved xanomeline–trospium combination
(Cobenfy®) for schizophrenia in adults. The xanomeline axis
therefore addresses the *cortical–hippocampal cholinergic
substrate* of NCD-AMC and, unlike acetylcholinesterase inhibition,
does not require preserved endogenous acetylcholine release to the
same degree.

Second, the histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic auto- and
heteroreceptor that, when constitutively active, suppresses the
release of histamine from histaminergic neurons and the release of
acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine from cortical,
striatal, and hippocampal neurons. Selective inverse agonism at H3
by pitolisant (Wakix®, FDA-approved for excessive daytime
sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy) elevates extracellular
histamine and supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine and
dopamine without the abuse liability of stimulant scheduling. The
H3 axis therefore addresses the *vigilance and arousal substrate*
of cognitive impairment in NCD-AMC.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a muscarinic M1/M4 agonist in
combination with an H3 antagonist or inverse agonist for NCD-AMC
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that post-synaptic muscarinic
facilitation of cortical cognition (a xanomeline M1/M4 axis) and
vigilance- and arousal-enabled cognitive support (a pitolisant H3
axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of
NCD-AMC. The combination is expected to produce greater
symptomatic cognitive and functional benefit than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose, by acting on two non-
overlapping CNS axes that converge on the same cortical–limbic
cognitive circuit. The invention does not assert disease-
modification of the underlying medical condition.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) xanomeline or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, (b) pitolisant or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, and (c) optionally a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic agent such as trospium chloride in an amount
effective to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Another Medical
Condition in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of xanomeline and a therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant,
optionally further with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic.
Co-administration may be simultaneous, in a unitary dosage form or
in separate dosage forms, or sequential, with each agent
administered within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) xanomeline or a salt thereof, optionally co-formulated with
trospium, in a first unitary dosage form, (b) pitolisant or a salt
thereof in a second unitary dosage form, and (c) written
instructions for the symptomatic treatment of NCD-AMC according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein.

The invention contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about
50 mg to about 250 mg, administered orally as the free base or as
the tartrate salt in two divided doses; when xanomeline is
co-administered with trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is
about 40 mg to about 60 mg administered orally in two divided doses,
matching the marketed xanomeline-tartrate / trospium-chloride
product (Cobenfy®). The invention contemplates pitolisant in a
daily dose of about 4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally
once daily. The mass ratio of xanomeline to pitolisant (calculated
on free-base equivalents) ranges broadly from about 5:1 to about
200:1, and is preferably from about 10:1 to about 50:1. The dose-
equivalent molar relationship and the mechanism-complementarity are
the inventive features, not the absolute mass ratio.

The technical effect of the combination is (a) attenuation, in
subjects with NCD-AMC, of decline or fluctuation on validated
cognitive instruments such as the Mini-Mental State Examination
(MMSE), Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), or Repeatable Battery
for the Assessment of Neuropsychological Status (RBANS); (b)
improvement in complex attention and executive function; and
(c) reduction in caregiver burden, each greater than would be
obtained with either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-
yl]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine, an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist. The term as used herein includes the free base,
the tartrate salt and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Pitolisant"* means the compound 1-{3-[3-(4-chlorophenyl)propoxy]
propyl}piperidine, also known as BF2.649, marketed in the United
States as Wakix® and in the European Union as Wakix®. Pitolisant is
a selective inverse agonist of the human histamine H3 receptor with
a binding affinity (Ki) of approximately 0.16 nM. The term as used
herein includes the free base, the hydrochloride salt and other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Trospium chloride"* or *"trospium"* means the quaternary ammonium
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist that does not
appreciably cross the blood-brain barrier and may be used to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline.

*"Major or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Another Medical
Condition"* or *"NCD-AMC"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 neurocognitive
syndrome in which clinically meaningful cognitive decline is
attributable to the direct physiologic effects of a defined
underlying medical condition, excluding primary neurodegenerative
disorders separately classified elsewhere.

*"Symptomatic cognitive support"* means a treatment effect directed
toward cognition, alertness, executive function, or functional
independence without asserted disease-modification of the underlying
medical condition.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the
symptomatic treatment of NCD-AMC according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in,
or attenuation of worsening of, at least one of: MMSE, MoCA, RBANS
attention index, executive-function measure, caregiver burden, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two or more active agents to a
subject so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted
on the subject during the period in which the pharmacological
effect of the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether
the agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. Activation of
the M1 receptor facilitates post-synaptic cholinergic cognitive
enhancement in cortex and hippocampus and is not wholly dependent on
endogenous acetylcholine availability. Activation of the M4 receptor
contributes to behavioral stabilization in striatal circuits.
Xanomeline was developed in the 1990s as a potential treatment for
Alzheimer's-type major NCD and more recently reached the market in
fixed-dose combination with the peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic trospium chloride as Cobenfy® for schizophrenia in
adults. Xanomeline is under live U.S. brand-patent protection via
the marketed combination and related rights.

#### 2.2 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a piperidine derivative and a high-affinity, selective
inverse agonist at the human histamine H3 receptor. Inverse agonism
at H3 elevates extracellular histamine and supports state-dependent
release of acetylcholine and dopamine in cortex, hippocampus, and
dorsal striatum. Pitolisant is approved by the FDA for the
treatment of excessive daytime sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy.
Pitolisant is metabolized primarily by CYP2D6 and CYP3A4. It is not
an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor and therefore avoids the direct
peripheral cholinergic mechanism used by donepezil, rivastigmine,
and galantamine.

#### 2.3 Trospium (optional)

Trospium chloride is a quaternary ammonium peripherally restricted
muscarinic antagonist that does not appreciably cross the
blood-brain barrier. In the present invention, trospium may be
included at a daily dose effective to mitigate peripheral
cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline without antagonizing the
central cognitive effect.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise xanomeline and
pitolisant, optionally further comprising trospium, together with at
least one pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The compositions
may take the form of: (a) a co-packaged kit of two or three
separate dosage forms (pitolisant tablet + xanomeline-trospium
fixed-dose capsule); (b) a unitary fixed-dose combination dosage
form including all three actives; or (c) a co-packaged kit of
pitolisant tablets and the marketed xanomeline-trospium product.

The mass ratio of xanomeline (calculated as the free base) to
pitolisant (calculated as the free base) is in the range of about
5:1 to about 200:1, preferably about 10:1 to about 50:1. When
trospium is included, the molar ratio of trospium to xanomeline is
approximately 1:1 to 1:3, matching the ratio validated in the
marketed combination.

Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of xanomeline include the
tartrate, fumarate, hydrochloride, and citrate salts.
Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of pitolisant include the
hydrochloride, fumarate, succinate, citrate, and tartrate salts.
Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of trospium include the chloride
and bromide.

### 4. Dosage forms

Xanomeline (with or without trospium) is preferably formulated as
twice-daily oral capsules. The marketed Cobenfy® product is
supplied as 50 mg/20 mg, 100 mg/20 mg, and 125 mg/30 mg
xanomeline-tartrate / trospium-chloride capsules; the present
invention contemplates use of those strengths and equivalent
strengths of xanomeline alone where peripheral cholinergic burden is
acceptable. Pitolisant is preferably formulated as a once-daily
immediate-release oral tablet at strengths of 4.5 mg and 17.8 mg
(free-base equivalent), matching the marketed product.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-tablet bottle of
pitolisant immediate-release tablets, (b) a 60-capsule bottle of
xanomeline-trospium capsules, and (c) a printed insert with
administration timing, titration schedule, and monitoring guidance.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with NCD-AMC, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pitolisant 8.9 mg orally once daily on awakening for week 1, then
  17.8 mg once daily thereafter; maximum 35.6 mg/day.
* Xanomeline-trospium 50 mg/20 mg orally twice daily for the first
  three days, then 100 mg/20 mg twice daily for the next four days,
  then 125 mg/30 mg twice daily thereafter as tolerated, matching
  the prescribing-information titration logic for Cobenfy®.

Where trospium is omitted, xanomeline may be administered at an
equivalent free-base exposure range with heightened monitoring for
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects.

Hepatic adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in
subjects with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B) and is not
recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).
Xanomeline-trospium is not recommended in moderate or severe
hepatic impairment.

Renal adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²).
Xanomeline-trospium is not recommended in moderate or severe renal
impairment.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP2D6 inhibitors without dose review of pitolisant and
xanomeline. Avoid co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inducers.
Avoid co-administration with potent anticholinergics other than the
trospium component.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects who
meet DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 criteria for major or mild NCD due to
another medical condition. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: clinician-confirmed NCD attributable
to a defined underlying medical condition; MMSE 14–28 or equivalent
MoCA range at enrollment; sufficient swallowing and oral intake to
tolerate oral medication; and stable management of the underlying
medical condition for at least 4 weeks where clinically possible.
Exclusion criteria include primary neurodegenerative disorders more
appropriately classified elsewhere, active psychosis unrelated to
the medical condition, severe hepatic or renal impairment, narrow-
angle glaucoma, urinary retention, gastric retention, and known
hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Cognitive dysfunction in NCD-AMC is frequently mediated by a mixed
phenotype of reduced cortical cholinergic tone, impaired arousal-
system support, and fluctuating attention. A single-agent approach
rarely addresses all three features.

Xanomeline directly facilitates post-synaptic muscarinic signaling
in cortex and hippocampus, thereby supporting cognition even when
endogenous acetylcholine availability is reduced or unreliable.
Pitolisant elevates extracellular histamine and supports
state-dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine in cortex and
hippocampus, thereby improving vigilance and attentional stability.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge
on the same cortical–hippocampal cognitive circuit.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in cognition, alertness, and functional
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 28,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: M1 receptor binding and calcium-mobilization
assays; M4 receptor functional assays; H3 receptor binding and
inverse-agonism assays; and combined cortical-network signaling
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms useful for confirming the combination's effect on
the cortical–hippocampal cognitive substrate include: the Morris
water maze; novel object recognition; delayed alternation; and
5-choice serial reaction time task for sustained attention. In
rodent models of cholinergic deficit or medically induced cognitive
impairment, a supra-additive effect of the combination on spatial
memory, attentional accuracy, and executive switching, relative to
either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the
principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in
§ 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1: pitolisant
17.8 mg orally once daily for 7 days; full PK sampling on days 1
and 7. Period 2 (after washout): xanomeline-trospium 100 mg/20 mg
twice daily for 7 days; full PK sampling for all actives on days 1
and 7. Period 3: the combination at the same doses for 7 days. The
primary endpoint is the geometric mean ratio for AUC₀–τ and Cmax of
pitolisant and xanomeline in combination versus monotherapy, with
90 % confidence intervals contained within 0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adults with NCD-AMC (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant + xanomeline-trospium.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): xanomeline-trospium + matching
  pitolisant placebo.
* Arm C (placebo): matching placebo + standard-of-care medical
  management.

All subjects continue standard-of-care treatment of the underlying
medical condition. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in
MMSE / MoCA composite at week 12. Secondary endpoints include RBANS
attention index, an executive-function measure, caregiver burden,
functional independence, and CGI-I.

**20.** Use of xanomeline and pitolisant in the manufacture of a
medicament for the symptomatic treatment of Major or Mild

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Major
or Mild Neurocognitive Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition
comprising co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount
of the muscarinic M1/M4-preferring agonist xanomeline and a
therapeutically effective amount of the histamine H3 receptor
inverse agonist pitolisant, optionally further with the
peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium chloride, are
disclosed. The two CNS-active agents act on non-overlapping
receptor classes that converge on cortical–hippocampal–striatal
cognitive and arousal circuits implicated in medically driven
neurocognitive impairment, and are expected to produce greater-
than-additive symptomatic cognitive benefit than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose without asserting
disease-modification of the underlying medical condition.
