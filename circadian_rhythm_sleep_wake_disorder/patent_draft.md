## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Circadian Rhythm Sleep Wake Disorder (CRSWD) by
co-administering (a) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with
5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine, and
(b) an orexin receptor antagonist, in particular suvorexant. The
invention is intended for subjects with clinically meaningful
misalignment between the endogenous circadian rhythm and the desired
or required sleep-wake schedule and addresses delayed sleep onset,
unstable sleep timing, sleep fragmentation in the target sleep
window, and impaired daytime functioning. The invention is not
intended to replace light-based chronotherapy, scheduling
intervention, or safety-critical wakefulness management.

## Background of the invention

Circadian Rhythm Sleep Wake Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and
ICD-11 as a group of disorders in which the timing of the sleep-
wake cycle is out of alignment with the external environment or the
individual's desired schedule, leading to insomnia symptoms,
excessive sleepiness, or both. The group includes delayed sleep-
wake phase disorder, advanced sleep-wake phase disorder, irregular
sleep-wake rhythm disorder, non-24-hour sleep-wake rhythm disorder,
and shift work type.

The prevalence of CRSWD is clinically meaningful across adolescent,
adult, and medically complex populations, with burden varying by
subtype. The disorder is associated with school and occupational
impairment, social isolation, fatigue, mood symptoms, poor
medication adherence, impaired academic or job performance, and in
shift-work or non-24 contexts, chronic circadian stress. The
clinical problem is not always simple insomnia; rather, it is
failure of sleep to occur at the biologically or behaviorally
appropriate time.

The current standard of care is primarily behavioral and timing-
based. It consists of light therapy, schedule manipulation,
dark-phase protection, timed melatonin or melatonin agonists,
chronotherapy, and treatment of comorbid mood or anxiety symptoms.
Only limited subtype-specific products are approved in certain
jurisdictions, and no single pharmaceutical product is approved by
the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the broad
CRSWD spectrum as a unified indication. Many subjects continue to
experience persistent difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep in
the desired window even when timing interventions are correctly
applied.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, CRSWD frequently includes failure of circadian
entrainment or instability of phase position relative to the
intended sleep schedule. Agomelatine is a melatonergic
antidepressant that acts as a potent agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors. This combination
supports circadian resynchronization, improves subjective sleep
quality, and may facilitate alignment of the sleep episode to the
target clock time. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses the
*phase-alignment and circadian-timing substrate* of CRSWD.

Second, many CRSWD subjects also exhibit persistent orexin-driven
wake maintenance that prevents sleep initiation within the desired
sleep window even when phase-directed interventions are underway.
Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia that facilitates sleep onset and maintenance by reducing
orexin-driven wakefulness. The suvorexant axis therefore addresses
the *wake-drive and target-window sleep-initiation failure*
substrate of CRSWD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
with 5-HT2C antagonism in combination with an orexin receptor
antagonist for CRSWD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that restoration of circadian
alignment (an agomelatine axis) and attenuation of orexin-driven
wake pressure during the target sleep window (a suvorexant axis)
are complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of CRSWD.
The combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic
improvement in phase alignment, sleep onset in the desired window,
sleep continuity, and daytime function than either agent alone at
any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) suvorexant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Circadian Rhythm Sleep Wake Disorder in a human subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and a
therapeutically effective amount of suvorexant. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other, and preferably
timed so that both actives support the desired sleep window.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) suvorexant or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of CRSWD according
to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with schedule-
based chronotherapy.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally in relation to the target
sleep phase, preferably at bedtime or shortly before the desired
sleep onset. The invention contemplates suvorexant in a daily dose
of about 5 mg to about 20 mg administered orally in the target
sleep window, with a preferred adult dose of 10 mg to 20 mg. The
inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of melatonergic
phase alignment and orexin-antagonist sleep-initiation support in a
misaligned circadian state.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with CRSWD, in sleep-onset latency relative to the target schedule,
sleep fragmentation within the target sleep window, and daytime
functional impairment, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of agomelatine and suvorexant administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Suvorexant"* means the dual orexin receptor antagonist approved
for insomnia. The term includes suvorexant free base, salts,
polymorphs, solvates, hydrates, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent orexin receptor antagonists
with a clinically comparable sleep-promoting profile.

*"Circadian Rhythm Sleep Wake Disorder"* or *"CRSWD"* means a
disorder of sleep-wake timing in which the endogenous circadian
rhythm is misaligned with the desired or required schedule,
including delayed, advanced, irregular, non-24-hour, and shift-work
subtypes.

*"Target-window sleep-alignment score"* means a clinically
meaningful measure of alignment between the desired / required sleep
window and the achieved sleep episode, including phase position,
sleep onset, and sleep continuity within the target window.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of CRSWD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: target-window
sleep-alignment score, sleep-onset latency, daytime sleepiness,
social / occupational functioning, responder rate, or investigator-
rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is support of circadian phase alignment
and stabilization of the intended sleep period.

#### 2.2 Suvorexant

Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia. By reducing orexin-mediated wake drive, it facilitates
sleep onset and maintenance. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of suvorexant is reduction of
wake-drive that interferes with sleep onset in the desired sleep
window.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
suvorexant, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as an agomelatine tablet and a
suvorexant tablet.

Excipients useful for the agomelatine composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the suvorexant
composition include tablet binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and
film-coating polymers appropriate for bedtime administration.

### 4. Dosage forms

Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
timed administration before the desired sleep period. Suvorexant is
preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for use in the
target sleep window.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
agomelatine tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of suvorexant tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to light exposure, schedule
stabilization, and time-based dosing.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with CRSWD, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Agomelatine 25 mg orally in relation to the desired sleep phase,
  titratable to 50 mg if clinically indicated.
* Suvorexant 5 mg orally in the target sleep window for about
  3 nights, titratable to 10 mg and then to 20 mg nightly if
  clinically indicated and tolerated.

The preferred embodiment requires that dosing be aligned to the
desired sleep opportunity rather than to clock time alone.
Shift-work embodiments are limited to subjects attempting sleep in
the planned off-duty sleep window and are not intended for use
before safety-critical wake periods.

Medication-medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors and requires hepatic monitoring in
appropriate patients. Suvorexant should be used with caution
alongside other CNS depressants. The regimen is intended to
complement, not replace, light-based chronotherapy and schedule
intervention.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
CRSWD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 12 years; clinically meaningful circadian
misalignment causing insomnia or daytime sleepiness; and ability to
participate in schedule-based intervention. Exclusion criteria
include untreated severe sleep-disordered breathing, severe hepatic
disease, unsafe occupational need for immediate wakefulness after
dosing, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

CRSWD often includes two interacting layers: failure of circadian
phase alignment to the desired schedule, and persistent wake-drive
that prevents sleep from occurring when the subject attempts to
sleep in the target window.

Agomelatine addresses the phase-alignment layer through MT1 / MT2
agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. Suvorexant addresses the wake-drive
layer through orexin antagonism. The two axes are non-overlapping
at the receptor level and converge on the same sleep-timing
phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in CRSWD burden than either monotherapy alone.
the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 38,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays
for agomelatine; orexin receptor binding and antagonism assays for
suvorexant; and combined circadian / sleep-state assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include circadian-shift paradigms, light-cycle re-entrainment
models, sleep-onset tasks, and excessive sleepiness / wake-drive
models. A supra-additive effect of the combination on restoration
of target-window sleep alignment, relative to either monotherapy at
any individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof
point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
agomelatine 25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: suvorexant 10 mg
nightly for 3 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, liver-function monitoring, next-day sedation monitoring,
and tolerability assessments are obtained in each period. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult CRSWD (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm), stratified
by major CRSWD subtype.

* Arm A (combination): agomelatine + suvorexant.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): agomelatine + placebo for
  suvorexant.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with behavioral chronotherapy.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in target-window
sleep-alignment score at week 8. Secondary endpoints include sleep-
onset latency, daytime sleepiness, social / occupational
functioning, responder rate, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
having 5-HT2C antagonism with an orexin receptor antagonist for
CRSWD specifically. The closest hits are:

* Circadian rhythm sleep disorder and sleep-disturbance patents
  disclose other timing-based or melatonergic approaches and do not
  teach the present pair.
* Generic agomelatine and suvorexant formulation patents disclose
  the actives and dosage forms, not the present CRSWD use.
* Generic insomnia filings do not disclose the present phase-
  alignment plus target-window sleep-initiation rationale.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (MT1 / MT2 agonism +
5-HT2C antagonism + orexin antagonism), (ii) the specific actives
(agomelatine + suvorexant), and (iii) the specific indication
(CRSWD). No prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit
rationale on which the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Circadian Rhythm Sleep Wake Disorder comprising co-administration
of a therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and a
therapeutically effective amount of suvorexant are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping circadian-alignment and wake-
drive circuits implicated in mistimed sleep and are expected to
produce greater-than-additive improvement in target-window sleep
alignment, sleep onset, and daytime function than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose.
