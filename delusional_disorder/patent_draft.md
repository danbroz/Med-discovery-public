## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Delusional Disorder (DD) by co-administering
(a) a muscarinic M1 receptor agonist / M1-M4-preferring agonist and
(b) a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist, in particular the
combination of xanomeline and perospirone, optionally further with
a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic such as trospium to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects. The invention is
intended for adult subjects with persistent delusional conviction
in the absence of the full syndrome of schizophrenia, and addresses
delusional preoccupation, conviction rigidity, suspiciousness, and
social or functional disruption without asserting treatment of
delirium, acute intoxication, or broad chronic schizophrenia-
spectrum maintenance disease.

## Background of the invention

Delusional Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
psychotic disorder characterized by one or more delusions of at
least one month duration, with relative preservation of overall
functioning and without the prominent disorganization, negative
symptoms, or persistent hallucination burden typical of
schizophrenia. Subtypes include persecutory, jealous, erotomanic,
grandiose, somatic, mixed, and unspecified forms.

The prevalence of DD is lower than that of schizophrenia but the
clinical burden is substantial. The disorder is associated with
marital and family disruption, litigation or conflict, healthcare
overuse in somatic subtypes, occupational impairment, social
withdrawal, violence risk in a subset of persecutory or jealous
cases, and high treatment resistance because conviction rigidity can
reduce insight and engagement.

The current standard of care is supportive and inconsistently
effective. It consists primarily of antipsychotic trials, careful
therapeutic alliance building, psychotherapy when tolerated, and
treatment of comorbid mood or anxiety symptoms. No pharmaceutical
product is approved by the United States Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) specifically for DD as a primary indication.
Conventional D2-blocking monotherapy may reduce delusional
intensity in some subjects but often leaves persistent conviction
rigidity and cognitive inflexibility, while exposing subjects to
motor, sedative, or metabolic burden.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, DD frequently requires attenuation of
dopaminergic salience misattribution and fixation of false belief.
Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic approved in Japan for
schizophrenia that functions as a D2 receptor antagonist with
additional serotonergic properties. In the present invention, the
perospirone axis addresses the *delusional salience and belief-
conviction reinforcement* substrate of DD.

Second, persistent delusional belief in DD is often marked by rigid
cognitive-perceptual organization and reduced capacity to update
beliefs in response to contradicting evidence. Muscarinic M1
receptor signaling facilitates cortical and hippocampal cognition,
while M4 signaling contributes to antipsychotic effects through
striatal mechanisms. Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic
agonist developed in the 1990s and more recently formulated with
the peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium as the
FDA-approved xanomeline-trospium combination (Cobenfy®) for
schizophrenia in adults. The xanomeline axis therefore addresses
the *cognitive flexibility and muscarinic antipsychotic substrate*
of DD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a D2 antagonist in combination with
an M1/M4 agonist for DD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that dopaminergic stabilization of
delusional salience (a perospirone D2 axis) and muscarinic
facilitation of cognitive flexibility and antipsychotic control (a
xanomeline M1/M4 axis) are complementary and convergent substrates
for treatment of DD. The combination is expected to produce greater
reduction in delusional conviction, preoccupation, suspiciousness,
and associated functional burden than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) xanomeline or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) perospirone or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, optionally further comprising a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic agent such as trospium chloride, together with one
or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Delusional Disorder in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of xanomeline and a therapeutically effective amount of
perospirone, optionally further with a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic. Co-administration may be simultaneous or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) xanomeline, optionally co-formulated with trospium, in a first
unitary dosage form, (b) perospirone or a salt thereof in a second
unitary dosage form, and (c) written instructions for the treatment
of DD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in
conjunction with alliance-preserving psychotherapy or supportive
management.

The invention contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about
50 mg to about 250 mg administered orally as the free base or as
the tartrate salt in two divided doses; when xanomeline is
co-administered with trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is
about 40 mg to about 60 mg administered orally in two divided
doses. The invention contemplates perospirone in a daily dose of
about 4 mg to about 48 mg administered orally in one or two divided
doses, with a preferred adult dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day. The
inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of M1/M4 and D2
pathways in persistent delusional belief states.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with DD, in delusional conviction, delusional preoccupation,
suspiciousness, and functional impairment, that is greater than the
sum of the individual effects of xanomeline and perospirone
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-
yl]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine, an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist. The term as used herein includes the free base,
the tartrate salt and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Trospium chloride"* or *"trospium"* means the quaternary ammonium
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist that does not
appreciably cross the blood-brain barrier and may be used to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline.

*"Delusional Disorder"* or *"DD"* means the psychotic disorder
defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by one or more persistent delusions,
typically with relative preservation of overall functioning outside
the direct impact of the delusion.

*"Delusional-conviction score"* means a clinically meaningful burden
of fixed belief conviction, preoccupation, referential or
persecutory certainty, or resistance to corrective information,
quantified by structured clinician assessment or validated
delusional-belief instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of DD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
delusional-conviction score, delusional-preoccupation score,
suspiciousness / hostility burden, social-function score, insight
score, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two or more active agents to a
subject so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted
on the subject during the period in which the pharmacological
effect of the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether
the two agents are administered in the same dosage form, in
separate dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times
within a window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
xanomeline is improvement of cognitive flexibility and muscarinic
antipsychotic signaling in persistent delusional states.

#### 2.2 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is attenuation of delusional salience and reinforcement
of false belief conviction.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise xanomeline and
perospirone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a xanomeline or xanomeline-
trospium capsule and a perospirone tablet.

Excipients useful for the xanomeline composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and stabilizing excipients
appropriate for muscarinic agonist oral delivery. Excipients useful
for the perospirone composition include diluents, binders,
disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable for oral
tablet manufacture.

### 4. Dosage forms

Xanomeline is preferably formulated as an oral immediate-release or
modified-release dosage form administered in divided doses. The
preferred commercial-development embodiment contemplates xanomeline
co-administered with trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic
adverse effects. Perospirone is preferably formulated as an oral
tablet suitable for one or two daily doses.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
xanomeline or xanomeline-trospium units, (b) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to
measurement-based reassessment and supportive psychotherapy.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with DD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Xanomeline 50 mg orally twice daily, titratable to 100 mg or
  125 mg twice daily if clinically indicated and tolerated; when
  paired with trospium, trospium is administered at about 20 mg to
  30 mg twice daily.
* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily, titratable to 8 mg twice
  daily and then up to 12 mg twice daily if clinically indicated
  and tolerated.

Broad genus ranges include xanomeline 50-250 mg/day, trospium
40-60 mg/day when used, and perospirone 4-48 mg/day. The preferred
embodiment contemplates maintenance over the period required to
reduce conviction rigidity and improve function.

Medication-medication interactions: cholinergic adverse effects,
orthostasis, QT-related risk, sedation, and extrapyramidal effects
should be monitored. The regimen is not intended to replace workup
for delirium, intoxication, or medical-cause psychosis where
indicated.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with DD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 18 years; one or more persistent delusions;
relative preservation of overall functioning outside the direct
impact of the delusion; and willingness to participate in alliance-
preserving psychotherapy or supportive management. Exclusion
criteria include active delirium, substance-induced psychosis,
medical-cause psychosis requiring primary etiologic management,
established schizophrenia with prominent chronic disorganization,
severe cholinergic intolerance, unsafe cardiac conduction
abnormality, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

DD often includes two interacting layers: dopaminergic reinforcement
of fixed false belief and reduced cognitive flexibility / belief-
updating capacity that sustains conviction rigidity.

Perospirone addresses the first layer through D2 antagonism.
Xanomeline addresses the second through muscarinic M1/M4 agonism.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on the persistent delusional phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in DD burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 34,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: D2 receptor binding and functional antagonism
assays for perospirone; M1 and M4 receptor binding and signaling
assays for xanomeline; and combined salience and cognitive-
flexibility network assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include dopaminergic salience models, belief-updating or cognitive-
flexibility paradigms, sensorimotor-gating tasks, and social-
interpretation bias models. A supra-additive effect of the
combination on reduction of delusion-like salience and restoration
of belief flexibility, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
perospirone 8 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: xanomeline or
xanomeline-trospium for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, orthostatic vital signs,
and tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult DD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): xanomeline + perospirone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): perospirone + placebo for xanomeline.
* Arm C (active monotherapy): xanomeline (optionally with
  trospium) + placebo for perospirone.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in delusional-
conviction score at week 16. Secondary endpoints include
delusional-preoccupation score, suspiciousness / hostility burden,
social-function score, insight score, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of a D2 antagonist with an M1/M4
agonist for DD specifically. The closest hits are:

* Muscarinic-activation patents disclose disorders ameliorated by
  muscarinic receptor activation but do not teach the present pair
  or the present delusional-disorder indication.
* Generic antipsychotic-combination patents disclose broad
  psychosis treatment concepts and do not teach the present
  delusional-conviction syndrome.
* Schizophrenia and psychosis filings do not disclose the present
  convergent-circuit rationale tailored to DD.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (M1/M4 agonism + D2
antagonism), (ii) the specific actives (xanomeline + perospirone),
and (iii) the specific indication (DD). No prior-art disclosure
suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on which the present
invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Delusional Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline and a
therapeutically effective amount of perospirone are disclosed,
optionally with trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse
effects. The two agents act on non-overlapping muscarinic
cognitive-flexibility and dopaminergic delusional-salience circuits
and are expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in
delusional conviction and preoccupation than either agent alone at
any individually achievable dose.
