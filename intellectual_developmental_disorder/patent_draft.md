## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Intellectual Developmental Disorder (IDD) by
co-administering (a) a histamine H3 receptor antagonist or inverse
agonist and (b) an oxytocin receptor agonist, in particular the
combination of pitolisant and oxytocin, in dosage forms and on
dosing schedules timed to caregiver-mediated adaptive-skills and
learning interventions.

## Background of the invention

Intellectual Developmental Disorder, termed Intellectual Disability
in many clinical contexts, is a neurodevelopmental condition
defined in the *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental
Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision* (DSM-5-TR) by deficits in
general mental abilities, impairment in adaptive functioning that
results in failure to meet developmental and sociocultural
standards for personal independence and social responsibility, and
onset during the developmental period. The corresponding
International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11)
classification is 6A00. The disorder may arise from genetic,
chromosomal, metabolic, prenatal, perinatal, environmental, or
idiopathic causes and is frequently accompanied by language,
motor, behavioral, or social-communication impairments.

The estimated population prevalence of IDD is approximately 1 % to
3 % worldwide, with higher prevalence in low-resource settings and
in populations with increased burden of perinatal injury,
nutritional deficiency, genetic syndromes, or social deprivation.
IDD is associated with persistent limitations in academic
achievement, independent living, occupational participation,
community functioning, and self-care. The burden on caregivers and
educational systems is substantial and lifelong. Common comorbid
conditions include autism spectrum disorder, attention-deficit /
hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), epilepsy, speech and language
disorders, developmental coordination disorder, anxiety, and sleep
disturbance.

The current standard of care for IDD is non-pharmacological and
supportive. It consists primarily of individualized education,
speech-language therapy, occupational therapy, caregiver training,
behavioral intervention, supported communication, and adaptive-
skills programming. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment
of IDD as a core cognitive-adaptive syndrome. Off-label
pharmacotherapy in IDD generally targets comorbid symptoms such as
irritability, ADHD, aggression, or sleep problems rather than the
core learning and adaptive-function phenotype. As a result, there
remains an unmet need for a medication regimen that can improve the
learning-readiness, caregiver-directed attention, and adaptive-
skills acquisition that underlie functional outcome in IDD.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, adaptive learning in IDD depends on sustained
attention, state-appropriate cortical arousal, and the ability to
encode and consolidate task-relevant information during repeated
teaching trials. These processes are regulated in part by
histaminergic, cholinergic, and dopaminergic signaling in
prefrontal cortex, hippocampus, striatum, and thalamocortical
attention networks. The histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic
auto- and heteroreceptor that, when constitutively active,
suppresses the release of histamine and of acetylcholine, dopamine,
and norepinephrine in cortex and striatum. Selective inverse
agonism at H3 by pitolisant (Wakix®), an FDA-approved treatment for
excessive daytime sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy, elevates
extracellular histamine and supports state-dependent release of
acetylcholine and dopamine without the abuse liability of classic
stimulant scheduling. The H3 axis therefore addresses the
*vigilance, task engagement, and learning-readiness substrate* of
IDD, but does not directly address the caregiver-bonding and social
reinforcement processes by which repeated instruction is made
salient and emotionally meaningful.

Second, oxytocin receptor (OXTR) signaling in prefrontal cortex,
amygdala, ventral tegmental area, hypothalamus, and superior
temporal sulcus modulates trust, social salience, caregiver-
directed attention, affiliative motivation, and responsiveness to
socially contingent reinforcement. Intranasal oxytocin has been
investigated in autism spectrum disorder, Prader–Willi syndrome,
schizophrenia, and other neurodevelopmental or social-cognitive
conditions, with mechanism-consistent signals on social attention,
eye gaze, and caregiver-directed engagement. The OXTR axis
therefore addresses the *caregiver-linked social-reinforcement
substrate* of adaptive-skills acquisition in IDD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an oxytocin receptor agonist in
combination with an H3 receptor antagonist or inverse agonist for
IDD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance-enabled learning
trials (an H3 antagonist axis) and caregiver-directed social
reinforcement during those trials (an OXTR agonist axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for adaptive-skills
acquisition in IDD. The combination is expected to produce greater
improvement in adaptive functioning and learning-related endpoints
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose, by
acting on two non-overlapping CNS axes that converge on the same
task-relevant cortical–limbic learning circuit.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) oxytocin or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, analogue, fragment, or
prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Intellectual Developmental Disorder in a subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of pitolisant and a therapeutically effective
amount of oxytocin. Co-administration may be simultaneous, in a
unitary dosage form or in separate dosage forms, or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) oxytocin or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form, and
(c) written instructions for the treatment of IDD in a pediatric,
adolescent, or adult subject according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, with the oxytocin actuation explicitly timed to
within 30 minutes of a structured adaptive-skills or caregiver-
mediated learning session.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 8.9 to 17.8 mg/day and a preferred
pediatric dose weight-banded as set forth herein. The invention
contemplates oxytocin in a daily dose of about 8 IU to about
80 IU administered intranasally in divided doses, with a preferred
dose of 24 IU twice daily timed to teaching or practice sessions.
The mass ratio of pitolisant to oxytocin (calculated on the
free-base equivalents) ranges broadly from about 1:200,000 to about
1:5,000, and is preferably from about 1:50,000 to about 1:20,000;
the dose-equivalent molar relationship and the session-aligned
timing relationship are the inventive features, not the absolute
mass ratio.

The technical effect of the combination is an increase, in subjects
with IDD, in adaptive-skills acquisition rate, caregiver-directed
task engagement, and functional outcome as measured by validated
adaptive-function instruments such as the Vineland Adaptive
Behavior Scales, 3rd ed. (Vineland-3), the Adaptive Behavior
Assessment System, 3rd ed. (ABAS-3), and investigator-rated
Clinical Global Impression — Improvement (CGI-I), that is greater
than the sum of the individual effects of pitolisant and oxytocin
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.
The combination is stimulant-free, opioid-free, and has no known
dependence liability.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the compound 1-{3-[3-(4-chlorophenyl)propoxy]
propyl}piperidine, also known as BF2.649, marketed in the United
States as Wakix® and in the European Union as Wakix®. Pitolisant is
a selective inverse agonist of the human histamine H3 receptor with
a binding affinity (Ki) of approximately 0.16 nM. The term as used
herein includes the free base, the hydrochloride salt and other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Oxytocin"* means the cyclic nonapeptide of sequence
H-Cys-Tyr-Ile-Gln-Asn-Cys-Pro-Leu-Gly-NH₂ with a disulfide bond
between Cys¹ and Cys⁶. The term as used herein includes the free
peptide and its pharmaceutically acceptable acetate or citrate
salts, glycosylated or PEGylated derivatives that retain agonism
at the human OXTR with EC₅₀ within ten-fold of native oxytocin,
peptidic agonists such as carbetocin, and small-molecule OXTR
agonists such as TC OT 39, WAY 267,464, LIT-001, or such other
agents that produce ≥ 50 % maximal calcium mobilization at the
human OXTR at concentrations ≤ 1 µM in a standard fluorometric
calcium-flux assay.

*"Intellectual Developmental Disorder"* or *"IDD"* means the
disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 (6A00), characterized by
deficits in intellectual functions and adaptive functioning with
onset during the developmental period, and includes the mild,
moderate, severe, and profound adaptive-functioning levels where
clinically appropriate.

*"Adaptive-skills acquisition"* means measurable improvement in the
subject's daily living, communication, or socialization abilities
as reflected on a validated adaptive-behavior instrument or by
standardized structured observation.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a subject for the treatment of
IDD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
Vineland-3 Adaptive Behavior Composite, ABAS-3 general adaptive
composite, caregiver-directed task engagement, or investigator-
rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

*"Session-aligned dosing"* means administration of intranasal
oxytocin within a window of about 30 minutes prior to or during a
scheduled adaptive-skills or caregiver-mediated teaching session,
with the purpose of aligning the central oxytocin pharmacodynamic
window with the period of socially reinforced learning.

### 2. The two active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a piperidine derivative and a high-affinity,
selective inverse agonist at the human histamine H3 receptor. The
H3 receptor is a presynaptic autoreceptor and heteroreceptor that,
when constitutively active, suppresses the release of histamine
from histaminergic neurons and the release of acetylcholine,
dopamine, and norepinephrine from cortical, striatal, and
hippocampal neurons. Inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant elevates
extracellular histamine and supports state-dependent release of
acetylcholine and dopamine in prefrontal, striatal, and thalamic
networks that support task engagement and learning readiness.
Pitolisant is approved by the FDA for the treatment of excessive
daytime sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy. Pitolisant is
metabolized primarily by CYP2D6 and CYP3A4. The compound is a
substrate and weak inhibitor of CYP3A4 and is not a significant
inducer of common cytochrome P450 enzymes. Pitolisant remains under
live U.S. brand-patent protection through the late 2020s.

#### 2.2 Oxytocin

Oxytocin is the endogenous cyclic nonapeptide synthesized in the
paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei of the hypothalamus.
Oxytocin acts at the OXTR, a Gαq-coupled receptor that mobilizes
intracellular calcium and modulates social cognition, attachment,
caregiver-directed attention, trust, and stress reactivity.
Synthetic oxytocin (Pitocin®, Syntocinon®) is approved in many
jurisdictions for obstetric indications. Investigational use of
intranasal oxytocin in neurodevelopmental and social-cognitive
indications is supported by a substantial peer-reviewed
literature. Native oxytocin is freely available from multiple
suppliers and has no live U.S. composition-of-matter patent
restriction; the present invention does not require any
proprietary OXTR agonist. The intranasal route is preferred to
maximize central nervous system exposure relative to peripheral
OXTR engagement.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-
release oral tablet at strengths of 4.5 mg and 17.8 mg
(free-base equivalent), matching the marketed product.
Modified-release oral tablets at strengths up to 35.6 mg per unit
are within the scope of the invention.

Oxytocin is preferably formulated as a metered-dose intranasal
spray delivering 4 IU per actuation, with permeation enhancement
to maximize central nervous system bioavailability. Sublingual
films, buccal tablets, and depot subcutaneous formulations are
within the scope of the invention. Intravenous and intramuscular
dosage forms are appropriate for inpatient initiation but not for
chronic ambulatory use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-tablet bottle of
pitolisant immediate-release oral tablets at the appropriate
weight-band strength, (b) a 30-day supply of an intranasal
oxytocin spray device, and (c) a printed insert with administration
timing aligned to scheduled adaptive-skills and caregiver-mediated
teaching sessions.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adults (subjects ≥ 18 years and ≥ 50 kg), the preferred dosing
regimen is pitolisant 8.9 mg once daily for one week followed by
17.8 mg once daily, taken on awakening, plus intranasal oxytocin
24 IU twice daily, timed to within 30 minutes of scheduled
adaptive-skills training or caregiver-mediated learning sessions.
Maximum recommended pitolisant dose is 35.6 mg/day; maximum
recommended oxytocin dose is 80 IU/day in three or four divided
actuations.

For pediatric and adolescent subjects, weight-banded dosing is
preferred:

| Body weight | Pitolisant (mg/day, oral) | Oxytocin (IU/day, intranasal) |
|---|---|---|
| 20–< 30 kg | 4.5 | 16 (8 IU twice daily) |
| 30–< 50 kg | 8.9 | 24 (12 IU twice daily) |
| ≥ 50 kg | 8.9 → 17.8 (titration) | 24–48 |

Hepatic adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in
subjects with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B) and is not
recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C). No
oxytocin dose adjustment is required for hepatic impairment.

Renal adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²). No
oxytocin adjustment is required.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP2D6 inhibitors (paroxetine, fluoxetine, bupropion)
without halving the pitolisant dose. Avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inducers. No clinically significant
medication–medication interactions are expected between pitolisant
and intranasal oxytocin.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects who
meet DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 criteria for IDD. Inclusion criteria for
the preferred treatment population include: developmental onset of
the disorder; standardized evidence of impairment in intellectual
and adaptive functioning; age ≥ 6 years; availability of a stable
caregiver or support person able to participate in structured
teaching sessions; and absence of an uncontrolled neurologic or
medical condition that would independently preclude the proposed
learning regimen. Mild and moderate IDD are preferred initial
target populations, but the invention is not limited thereto.
Comorbid autism spectrum disorder, ADHD, developmental coordination
disorder, speech and language disorders, and epilepsy are not
exclusionary provided they are clinically stable. Exclusion
criteria include known hypersensitivity to either active, current
treatment with a prohibited concomitant medication (see § 5),
pregnancy or breastfeeding where clinically relevant, inability to
comply with intranasal administration, and severe uncontrolled
behavioral dysregulation that prevents structured teaching.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Functional outcome in IDD depends less on static IQ measures than
on the subject's ability to participate in repeated teaching
trials, attend to caregivers and instructors, encode the task
requirements, and generalize the acquired skill across settings.

Selective H3 inverse agonism by pitolisant addresses the vigilance,
arousal, and task-engagement half of that problem by elevating
central histamine and supporting state-dependent acetylcholine and
dopamine release in prefrontal, thalamic, and striatal circuits.
OXTR agonism by intranasal oxytocin addresses the social-
reinforcement half of the problem by increasing caregiver-directed
attention, affiliative salience, and responsiveness to praise and
contingent support. The two axes are non-overlapping at the
receptor level and converge on the cortical–limbic–striatal circuit
that supports learning under socially reinforced conditions.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in adaptive-skills acquisition and caregiver-
directed learning behavior than either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding (³H-N-α-methylhistamine
displacement on rat cortical membranes; Ki for pitolisant
≈ 1.5 nM); H3 functional inverse agonism (cAMP accumulation in
CHO-K1 cells expressing human H3); OXTR binding
(³H-oxytocin displacement on human OXTR-CHO membranes; Kd for
oxytocin ≈ 1 nM); and OXTR functional agonism (calcium
mobilization in OXTR-CHO cells; EC₅₀ for oxytocin ≈ 5–20 nM).

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for confirming the combination's
effect on learning readiness and caregiver-linked reinforcement
include: five-choice serial reaction time task for sustained
attention; novel object recognition and reversal learning for
acquisition and updating; social preference and caregiver-cue
approach paradigms; and juvenile dyadic social-learning paradigms.
A supra-additive effect of the combination on learning task
acquisition and caregiver-cue salience, relative to either
monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the principal
in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years; equal sex
distribution). Period 1: pitolisant 17.8 mg orally once daily for
7 days; full PK sampling on days 1 and 7. Period 2 (after 7-day
washout): intranasal oxytocin 24 IU twice daily for 7 days; full
PK sampling on days 1 and 7 (including exploratory biomarkers if
validated). Period 3 (after 7-day washout): combination at the
same doses for 7 days; PK sampling for both agents on days 1 and
7. The primary endpoint is the geometric mean ratio for AUC₀–τ and
Cmax of pitolisant in combination versus monotherapy, with 90 %
confidence intervals contained within 0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in pediatric and adolescent IDD (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm) with
80 % power to detect a clinically meaningful between-arm
difference in Vineland-3 Adaptive Behavior Composite at week 12,
assuming a standard deviation consistent with prior developmental
intervention literature and a two-sided α of 0.05.

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant weight-banded oral dosing +
  intranasal oxytocin twice daily.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pitolisant weight-banded oral dosing +
  matching intranasal placebo.
* Arm C (placebo): matching oral placebo + matching intranasal
  placebo.

All subjects receive a standardized, manualized adaptive-skills
intervention of equal frequency, intensity, and duration (for
example, three 45-minute sessions weekly over 12 weeks) delivered
by trained therapists and caregivers, aligned to the oxytocin
actuation window. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in
Vineland-3 Adaptive Behavior Composite at week 12. Secondary
endpoints include ABAS-3 general adaptive composite, structured
task-engagement scores, caregiver burden, responder rate, and
CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of an H3 receptor antagonist or
inverse agonist with an OXTR agonist for IDD specifically. The
closest hits are:

* *Treatment of autism and similar disorders* and *Intranasal
  carbetocin formulations and methods for the treatment of autism* —
  disclose oxytocin-pathway approaches in autism-spectrum
  indications; they do not teach pitolisant or H3 antagonism and do
  not teach IDD as a treatment population.
* *Oxytocin and opioid antagonists for treatment of social
  dysfunction disorder* — discloses oxytocin with a different
  pharmacologic partner, not with an H3 antagonist, and does not
  teach IDD.
* *Methods for the treatment of neurodegenerative disorders* and
  *Methods of treating developmental disorders with gaboxadol* —
  disclose different active classes and do not teach the present
  pitolisant + oxytocin combination.
* *Methods of improving behavioral therapies* — discloses oxytocin
  as an adjunct to behavioral treatment generically; it does not
  teach H3 inverse agonism, does not teach pitolisant, and does not
  teach IDD.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (H3 inverse agonism /
antagonism + OXTR agonism), (ii) the specific actives
(pitolisant + oxytocin), and (iii) the specific indication
(IDD as a disorder of intellectual and adaptive functioning with
developmental onset). No prior-art disclosure suggests the
convergent-circuit rationale on which the present invention is
based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Intellectual Developmental Disorder comprising co-administration of
a therapeutically effective amount of the histamine H3 receptor
inverse agonist pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount
of the oxytocin receptor agonist oxytocin, in dosage forms and on
dosing schedules timed to structured adaptive-skills and caregiver-
mediated learning sessions, are disclosed. The two agents act on
non-overlapping receptor classes that converge on cortical–limbic–
striatal learning and social-reinforcement circuits implicated in
adaptive-skills acquisition in Intellectual Developmental
Disorder, and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in adaptive functioning and caregiver-directed learning
behavior than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.
