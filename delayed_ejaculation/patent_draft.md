## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Delayed Ejaculation by co-administering
(a) a serotonergic tetracyclic agent having antagonist activity at
5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, in particular mianserin, and
(b) a mu-opioid receptor agonist, in particular sufentanil, in a
restricted, as-needed rescue regimen. The invention is intended for
adult male subjects with persistent delayed ejaculation or
anorgasmia causing distress, including medication-associated forms,
and is directed to reducing orgasmic latency and improving
ejaculatory completion. The invention is not intended for chronic
unsupervised opioid use.

## Background of the invention

Delayed Ejaculation is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a male
sexual dysfunction characterized by marked delay in ejaculation,
marked infrequency or absence of ejaculation, or a marked decrease
in sensation of ejaculatory inevitability on nearly all or all
(approximately 75–100 %) occasions of partnered sexual activity,
persisting for at least 6 months and causing clinically significant
distress. Although precise thresholds vary among studies, the
syndrome typically involves a substantial prolongation of orgasmic
latency or inability to reach ejaculation despite adequate
stimulation and desire.

The prevalence of Delayed Ejaculation is generally estimated at
about 1 % to 4 % of men, with higher prevalence in older men and in
subjects with concomitant medical, neurologic, psychiatric, or
medication-related contributors. Common etiologies include SSRI and
antipsychotic exposure, diabetes-related neuropathy, endocrinologic
disorders, pelvic surgery, psychogenic inhibition, relationship
stress, and idiopathic causes. The clinical burden includes
avoidance of sexual intimacy, frustration, relationship distress,
anhedonia, and loss of adherence to otherwise useful psychiatric
medications when delayed ejaculation is medication-induced.

The current standard of care is heterogeneous and unsatisfactory.
It consists primarily of focused history and examination, treatment
of reversible causes, behavioral modification, psychotherapy, and
empiric off-label medication trials. No pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
specifically for Delayed Ejaculation as a primary indication.
Existing pharmacologic ideas in the literature are fragmented and
often extrapolated from the better-studied opposite condition,
premature ejaculation. Consequently, there remains a substantial
unmet need for a mechanism-based regimen that can restore orgasmic
completion in selected patients with delayed ejaculation.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, delayed ejaculation and anorgasmia are often
maintained by excessive serotonergic inhibition of orgasmic
completion, particularly where the syndrome is associated with
serotonergic antidepressant exposure or central inhibitory tone.
Mianserin is a tetracyclic antidepressant with antagonist activity
at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and H1
histamine receptors. Clinical literature has shown that mianserin
can improve serotonin-reuptake-inhibitor-induced sexual
dysfunction, especially anorgasmia. The mianserin axis therefore
addresses the *serotonergic orgasm-inhibition substrate* of
Delayed Ejaculation and may also improve sleep and anxiety that
exacerbate performance inhibition.

Second, a subset of delayed ejaculation appears to involve a high
orgasmic threshold in which the pleasurable salience of genital
stimulation is insufficient to trigger ejaculatory completion even
when desire is present. The endogenous opioid system participates
in orgasmic reward, sensory integration, and sexual reinforcement.
Sufentanil is a potent mu-opioid receptor agonist approved for
analgesic use in tightly supervised medical settings and available
in parenteral and sublingual / transmucosal formulations. Although
chronic or high-dose opioid exposure is generally associated with
sexual dysfunction, the present invention is based on the
counter-intuitive recognition that *very low-dose, intermittent,
time-limited* mu-opioid agonism, administered only as a rescue
adjunct on planned sexual-attempt days, can lower the acute
orgasmic threshold in selected subjects when combined with
background reduction of serotonergic inhibitory tone by mianserin.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C-antagonist
antidepressant in combination with low-dose rescue mu-opioid
agonism for Delayed Ejaculation specifically.

The present invention recognizes that removal of serotonergic
braking on orgasmic completion (a mianserin 5-HT2 axis) and
tightly limited enhancement of acute orgasmic salience
(a sufentanil mu-opioid rescue axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for treatment of Delayed Ejaculation in
selected adult male subjects. The combination is expected to
produce greater improvement in ejaculatory completion and orgasmic
latency than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose, while explicitly avoiding chronic unsupervised opioid
exposure.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) mianserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) sufentanil or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Delayed Ejaculation in an adult male subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of mianserin and a therapeutically effective
amount of sufentanil, wherein the sufentanil component is used as
an as-needed rescue intervention during planned sexual-activity
attempts and is not used as chronic daily opioid therapy.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) mianserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) sufentanil or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Delayed
Ejaculation in an adult male subject according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, together with explicit warnings on opioid risk,
restricted use frequency, and supervision requirements.

The invention contemplates mianserin in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 90 mg administered orally once daily or in divided
doses, with a preferred dose of 15 mg to 60 mg/day and a preferred
bedtime initiation dose of 15 mg to 30 mg/day. The invention
contemplates sufentanil in a single rescue dose of about 5 mcg to
about 30 mcg by sublingual or transmucosal route administered
about 15 to 90 minutes before a planned sexual attempt, with a
preferred maximum daily exposure of about 60 mcg and a preferred
maximum use frequency of about 4 attempt-days per week. The
inventive feature is the combination of chronic background
reduction of serotonergic inhibitory tone with a tightly limited,
low-dose, as-needed rescue intervention for orgasmic completion.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in adult
male subjects with Delayed Ejaculation, in orgasmic latency and
rate of ejaculation failure, and an increase in successful
ejaculatory completion during partnered sexual activity or
masturbatory attempts, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of mianserin and sufentanil administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Mianserin"* means the tetracyclic antidepressant
2-methyl-1,2,3,4,10,14b-hexahydrodibenzo[c,f]pyrazino[1,2-a]
azepine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof. The term
includes the hydrochloride and other pharmaceutically acceptable
salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically
labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Sufentanil"* means the potent mu-opioid receptor agonist
N-[4-(methoxymethyl)-1-[2-(2-thienyl)ethyl]-4-piperidyl]-N-
phenylpropanamide or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof.
The term includes parenteral, sublingual, and transmucosal
formulations and clinically equivalent low-dose rescue formulations
of sufentanil used in tightly controlled settings.

*"Delayed Ejaculation"* means the male sexual dysfunction defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by marked delay in ejaculation, marked
infrequency or absence of ejaculation, or markedly reduced
sensation of ejaculatory inevitability on nearly all or all sexual
activity occasions, persisting for at least 6 months and causing
clinically significant distress.

*"Successful ejaculatory completion"* means ejaculation occurring
within a clinically acceptable timeframe during a planned partnered
sexual activity or masturbatory attempt, accompanied by subjective
orgasmic completion.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult male subject for the
treatment of Delayed Ejaculation according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in
at least one of: successful ejaculatory completion rate, orgasmic
latency, orgasmic intensity, distress score, partner-reported
satisfaction, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Mianserin

Mianserin is a tetracyclic antidepressant used internationally for
depressive and anxiety-related conditions. Its pharmacology
includes antagonist activity at 5-HT2A and 5-HT2C receptors,
alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and H1 histamine receptors. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
mianserin is removal of serotonergic inhibitory tone that may
contribute to delayed orgasm and ejaculation, especially in
medication-associated or anxiety-linked forms of the disorder.
Clinical literature has reported improvement in serotonin
reuptake-inhibitor-induced sexual dysfunction with mianserin,
including prominent anorgasmia phenotypes.

#### 2.2 Sufentanil

Sufentanil is a potent mu-opioid receptor agonist approved for
analgesic use in tightly supervised medical settings. Sublingual
and transmucosal formulations permit precise low-microgram dosing.
In the present invention, sufentanil is not used as chronic
maintenance therapy for sexual function. Instead, it is used only
as a tightly limited, low-dose, as-needed rescue intervention
designed to lower the acute orgasmic threshold during planned
sexual attempts. The regimen is explicitly not intended for daily
unsupervised opioid exposure or for subjects with opioid use
disorder.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise mianserin and
sufentanil, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as an oral mianserin tablet and
a low-dose sublingual / transmucosal sufentanil dosage unit for
restricted rescue use.

Excipients useful for the mianserin composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and film-coating polymers. Excipients useful
for the sufentanil composition include transmucosal-film or
sublingual-tablet carriers, oxygen scavengers, stabilizers,
flavoring systems, and controlled-substance-compatible packaging.

### 4. Dosage forms

Mianserin is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
once-daily bedtime administration. Sufentanil is preferably
formulated as a small-volume sublingual tablet, transmucosal film,
or other mucosal rescue dosage form that can deliver low microgram
doses.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
mianserin tablets, (b) a limited supply of low-dose rescue
sufentanil dosage units, and (c) a printed insert with
administration timing, restricted-use instructions, opioid-risk
mitigation, and partner / sexual-attempt logging guidance.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult male subjects with Delayed Ejaculation, the preferred
dosing regimen is:

* Mianserin 15 mg orally at bedtime for 7 days, titratable to
  30 mg and then to 60 mg as clinically indicated.
* Sufentanil 5 mcg to 15 mcg by sublingual or transmucosal route
  administered about 15 to 90 minutes before a planned sexual
  attempt, with an optional second 5 mcg to 15 mcg rescue dose if
  no ejaculatory completion has occurred after a clinically
  appropriate interval and no dose-limiting sedation or respiratory
  depression is present.

Broad genus ranges include mianserin 10–90 mg/day and sufentanil
5–360 mcg/day. The preferred embodiment limits sufentanil exposure
to a maximum of about 60 mcg in 24 hours and no more than about
4 attempt-days per week. Chronic daily rescue use beyond about
72 hours of continuous exposure is not preferred and is outside the
most defensible embodiment of the invention.

Hepatic adjustment: mianserin should be reduced or avoided in
moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment. Sufentanil should be avoided
or used only with heightened caution in severe respiratory disease,
significant hepatic impairment, sleep apnea, or clinically
significant concurrent CNS depressant use.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration of
sufentanil with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants
unless absolutely necessary in a monitored setting. Avoid strong
CYP3A4 modulators where clinically significant sufentanil exposure
changes are expected.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult male
subjects with Delayed Ejaculation. Inclusion criteria for the
preferred treatment population include: age ≥ 18 years; persistent
Delayed Ejaculation or anorgasmia for at least 6 months; clinically
meaningful distress; intact erectile capacity sufficient for sexual
activity; and absence of a reversible primary endocrine or
urologic cause that is more appropriately corrected directly.
Subjects with antidepressant-associated delayed ejaculation are a
preferred subgroup. Exclusion criteria include active opioid use
disorder, chronic opioid therapy, severe respiratory disease,
severe cardiovascular instability, severe hepatic disease,
untreated hypogonadism, uncontrolled diabetes-related neuropathy,
and inability to comply with restricted as-needed opioid use.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Delayed Ejaculation is maintained in many subjects by two
interacting mechanisms: excessive serotonergic inhibition of
orgasmic completion and an elevated orgasmic threshold in which
genital stimulation does not adequately engage the central reward /
completion network.

Mianserin addresses the first component by antagonizing 5-HT2A /
5-HT2C-mediated serotonergic braking, thereby facilitating orgasmic
completion and reducing medication-associated orgasm delay. The
second component is addressed, in a tightly limited and as-needed
fashion, by very low-dose sufentanil rescue dosing that is
hypothesized to transiently enhance orgasmic salience and lower the
acute threshold for ejaculatory completion. Although chronic or
high-dose opioid exposure is associated with sexual dysfunction,
the present invention is directed specifically to *low-dose,
intermittent, non-chronic* rescue administration in selected
subjects.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in ejaculatory completion and orgasmic latency
this combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C binding assays and alpha2
adrenergic antagonism assays for mianserin; mu-opioid receptor
binding and functional agonism assays for sufentanil; and combined
sensory-salience / reward-network assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include male copulatory-behavior paradigms measuring ejaculation
latency, mounting and intromission patterns, genital-sensory
threshold paradigms, and reward-salience paradigms under strict
ethics controls. A non-monotonic or U-shaped response to very low
versus higher mu-opioid exposure may be assessed, with the present
invention directed to the low-dose facilitative window rather than
the high-dose inhibitory window. A supra-additive effect of the
combination on successful ejaculatory completion, relative to
either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the
principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in
§ 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult male volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1:
mianserin titrated to 30 mg/day for 7 days; full PK sampling on
days 1 and 7. Period 2 (after washout): supervised low-dose
sufentanil mucosal challenge with respiratory and sedation
monitoring over 24 hours. Period 3: combination with mianserin at
steady state and supervised low-dose sufentanil challenge. The
primary endpoint is tolerability, absence of clinically meaningful
PK interaction, and confirmation of a low-dose safety window.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult male Delayed Ejaculation (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 90 (30 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): mianserin background therapy +
  low-dose as-needed sufentanil rescue.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): mianserin background therapy +
  placebo rescue.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo background therapy + placebo rescue.

The primary endpoint is successful ejaculatory completion rate at
week 8. Secondary endpoints include orgasmic latency, subjective
orgasmic intensity, distress score, partner-reported satisfaction,
and CGI-I. Rescue-dose use frequency and all opioid-related adverse
events are tracked as key safety endpoints.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of a 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C-antagonist
antidepressant with low-dose as-needed sufentanil rescue for
Delayed Ejaculation specifically. The closest hits are:

* Multiple patents directed to *premature ejaculation* treatment,
  including modafinil-, botulinum-toxin-, and other ejaculation-
  delaying strategies, teach the opposite physiologic goal and do
  not disclose the present combination.
* *Methods of using rapid-onset selective serotonin reuptake
  inhibitors for treating sexual dysfunction* disclose serotonergic
  strategies but do not teach mianserin plus sufentanil and do not
  teach Delayed Ejaculation specifically.
* *Method of delaying ejaculation* is directed to prolonging
  ejaculation latency, not reducing it, and therefore teaches away
  from the present objective.
* Sufentanil pain-formulation patents disclose pain-treatment
  dosage forms and do not teach sexual-function indication or the
  present low-dose rescue framing.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific mechanistic pair (5-HT2 antagonism + low-dose
mu-opioid rescue), (ii) the specific actives (mianserin +
sufentanil), and (iii) the specific indication (Delayed Ejaculation
as a distressing orgasmic-completion disorder). No prior-art
disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on which the
present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Delayed Ejaculation in adult male subjects comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of
mianserin and a therapeutically effective amount of low-dose
as-needed sufentanil are disclosed. The two agents act on
non-overlapping orgasm-inhibition and orgasmic-threshold circuits
implicated in delayed or absent ejaculation and are expected to
produce greater-than-additive improvement in successful ejaculatory
completion and orgasmic latency than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose, while explicitly avoiding chronic
daily opioid therapy.
