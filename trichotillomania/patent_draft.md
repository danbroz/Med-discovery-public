## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Trichotillomania (Hair-Pulling Disorder) by
co-administering (a) a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse agonist /
antagonist and (b) a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist, in
particular the combination of pimavanserin and perospirone. The
invention is intended for adolescent and adult subjects with
clinically meaningful repetitive hair pulling who are concurrently
receiving behavioral treatment, and addresses urge build-up,
cue-triggered pulling, ritualized pulling behavior, and post-pull
relief reinforcement.

## Background of the invention

Trichotillomania is an obsessive-compulsive and related disorder
defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by recurrent pulling out of one's
hair, resulting in hair loss, repeated attempts to decrease or stop
the behavior, and clinically significant distress or impairment.
The pulling may involve scalp hair, eyebrows, eyelashes, beard, or
other body sites and is often preceded by tension, boredom, or
aversive internal states and followed by relief, gratification, or
reduced tension. The behavior may occur with or without full
awareness and may be embedded in ritualized sequences.

The prevalence of Trichotillomania is generally estimated in the
low single digits, but the disorder is under-recognized and
substantially impairing. It is associated with shame, social
avoidance, cosmetic burden, skin damage, trichobezoar risk in
ingestive subtypes, and high comorbidity with anxiety, depression,
obsessive-compulsive symptoms, body-focused repetitive behaviors,
and substance use disorders.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological in the first
instance and consists primarily of habit-reversal training,
stimulus control, acceptance- and awareness-based behavioral
interventions, and treatment of comorbid disorders. No
pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for the treatment of
Trichotillomania as a primary indication. Off-label pharmacotherapy
is heterogeneous and often disappointing, creating a need for a
more mechanistically coherent strategy.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, Trichotillomania involves maladaptive salience
assignment to internal sensory urges and to hair-pulling cues, with
rigid attentional capture and repetitive action release. Pimavanserin
(Nuplazid®) is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. Although developed for another
indication, its selective 5-HT2A mechanism provides a way to
attenuate cue-linked salience and repetitive ideation without
adding direct D2 blockade burden.

Second, Trichotillomania also includes a dopaminergically
reinforced tension-relief habit loop in which the act of pulling
reduces aversive internal tension and becomes self-reinforcing.
Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic approved in Japan for
schizophrenia that acts as a D2 antagonist with additional
serotonergic properties. In the present invention, the D2 axis
addresses the *habit reinforcement and repetitive action-release*
substrate of Trichotillomania.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist /
antagonist in combination with a D2 antagonist for Trichotillomania
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of cue-linked
salience and intrusive pull-related focus (a pimavanserin 5-HT2A
axis) and attenuation of the tension-relief reinforcement loop
(a perospirone D2 axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of Trichotillomania. The combination is
expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement in hair-pulling
severity than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pimavanserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) perospirone or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Trichotillomania in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of pimavanserin and a therapeutically effective amount of
perospirone. Co-administration may be simultaneous or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) perospirone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Trichotillomania
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with
habit-reversal or other behavioral treatment.

The invention contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose of about
17 mg to about 34 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 34 mg/day. The invention contemplates
perospirone in a daily dose of about 4 mg to about 48 mg
administered orally in one or two divided doses, with a preferred
dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day. The inventive feature is the concurrent
engagement of 5-HT2A-linked salience modulation and D2-linked habit
reinforcement attenuation in a body-focused repetitive behavior.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in
subjects with Trichotillomania, in pulling frequency, urge
severity, cue-triggered pulling, and functional burden, that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of pimavanserin and
perospirone administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, tartrate or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically
equivalent 5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Trichotillomania"* or *"Hair-Pulling Disorder"* means the
obsessive-compulsive and related disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and
ICD-11 by recurrent hair pulling resulting in hair loss, repeated
attempts to decrease or stop the behavior, and clinically
significant distress or impairment.

*"Hair-pulling-severity score"* means a clinically meaningful burden
of pulling frequency, urge severity, resistance difficulty, or
associated distress quantified by structured clinician assessment
or validated trichotillomania instruments such as the MGH-HPS.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of Trichotillomania according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
MGH-HPS total, pull frequency, urge severity, quality of life, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. It has no clinically meaningful
affinity for dopamine D2 receptors. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of pimavanserin is attenuation
of cue-linked salience, intrusive repetitive focus, and rigid
attentional capture associated with hair-pulling urges.

#### 2.2 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is attenuation of dopaminergically reinforced
repetitive action release and habit-loop persistence in hair
pulling.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pimavanserin and
perospirone, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as a
pimavanserin capsule and a perospirone tablet.

Excipients useful for the pimavanserin composition include capsule
fill excipients, diluents, disintegrants, and stabilizers suitable
for the marketed pimavanserin formulation profile. Excipients useful
for the perospirone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone), lubricants (magnesium stearate), and coating
polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or tablet
suitable for once-daily dosing. Perospirone is preferably
formulated as an immediate-release oral tablet.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pimavanserin capsules or tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to
habit-reversal or other behavioral treatment and symptom
monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with Trichotillomania, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pimavanserin 34 mg orally once daily.
* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily for 7 days, then 8 mg twice
  daily thereafter, with titration up to 12 mg twice daily if
  clinically needed.

Broad genus ranges include pimavanserin 17–34 mg/day and
perospirone 4–48 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses once-daily
pimavanserin with one or two divided perospirone doses.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: severe hepatic or renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers without review of pimavanserin
exposure. Corrected QT interval monitoring should be considered
when other QT-prolonging medications are used.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
Trichotillomania. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age ≥ 12 years; clinician-confirmed
Trichotillomania; clinically meaningful pulling severity at
baseline; and participation in or willingness to engage in
behavioral treatment such as habit-reversal training. Exclusion
criteria include primary psychotic disorders, active mania,
severe hepatic or renal impairment, and known hypersensitivity to
either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Trichotillomania includes at least two interacting symptom layers:
cue- and urge-linked salience amplification, and dopaminergically
reinforced tension-release behavior. Together these maintain
chronic repetitive pulling despite repeated attempts to stop.

Pimavanserin addresses the salience / cue-capture layer through
5-HT2A inverse agonism. Perospirone addresses the repetitive
reinforcement / action-release layer through D2 antagonism. The two
axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the
hair-pulling-severity phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in pulling frequency and urge severity than
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 39,
indicating especially strong mechanistic compatibility for the
indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A binding and inverse-agonism assays for
pimavanserin; D2 receptor binding and functional antagonism assays
for perospirone; and combined repetitive-behavior salience-network
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include grooming / overgrooming paradigms, cue-triggered repetitive
behavior tasks, compulsive motor-output paradigms, and salience-
capture measures. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
repetitive grooming-like behavior and cue-linked action release,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
pimavanserin 34 mg once daily for 7 days; Period 2: perospirone
titrated to 16 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult Trichotillomania
(FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120
(40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pimavanserin + perospirone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pimavanserin + placebo for
  perospirone.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with structured behavioral
  treatment.

All subjects receive standardized behavioral treatment. The primary
endpoint is change from baseline in MGH-HPS total at week 12.
Secondary endpoints include pull frequency, urge severity,
responder rate, quality of life, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Trichotillomania comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of the selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist /
antagonist pimavanserin and a therapeutically effective amount of
the D2 antagonist perospirone are disclosed. The two agents act on
non-overlapping urge-salience and repetitive-habit circuits
implicated in hair-pulling disorder and are expected to produce
greater-than-additive symptomatic improvement in pulling frequency
and urge severity than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.
