## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Oppositional Defiant Disorder (ODD) by
co-administering (a) a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist, in
particular perospirone, and (b) an alpha2A-adrenergic agonist, in
particular oxymetazoline, preferably in a rapid-onset intranasal or
other fast-delivery rescue formulation. The invention is intended
for pediatric and adolescent subjects with chronic angry / irritable
mood, argumentative / defiant behavior, and vindictiveness, and
addresses hostile salience, escalation-prone defiance, autonomic
surge, and failure of behavioral inhibition while continuing parent-
management, school-based, and family-based treatment. The
invention is not intended as punitive pharmacologic control or as a
replacement for psychosocial intervention.

## Background of the invention

Oppositional Defiant Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11
as a disorder characterized by a pattern of angry / irritable mood,
argumentative / defiant behavior, or vindictiveness toward
authority figures and others. Typical features include frequent
temper loss, touchiness or easy annoyance, anger, active defiance
of rules or requests, deliberate annoyance of others, blaming of
others for mistakes, and spiteful or vindictive behavior.

The prevalence of ODD is clinically meaningful in pediatric
psychiatry and developmental-behavioral practice. The burden
includes family conflict, school exclusion, impaired peer
relationships, caregiver burnout, risk of progression to more
severe externalizing disorders, and high comorbidity with ADHD,
anxiety, trauma-spectrum symptoms, mood symptoms, and learning or
language problems. Many affected children show rapid autonomic and
behavioral escalation from frustration to defiance or rage.

The current standard of care is primarily psychosocial. It consists
of parent-management training, family therapy, school-based
behavioral plans, treatment of comorbid conditions, and structured
skills interventions. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for
the treatment of ODD as a primary indication. Borrowed medications
from ADHD, antipsychotic, or mood practice may attenuate severe
aggression or irritability in selected cases, but often fail to
combine baseline reduction in hostile salience with a rapid
de-escalation mechanism for prodromal episodes.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, ODD frequently includes hostile attribution,
reactive defiance, and failure to inhibit oppositional action once
frustration is triggered. Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic
approved in Japan for schizophrenia that functions as a D2 receptor
antagonist with additional serotonergic properties. In the present
invention, the perospirone axis addresses the *hostile salience,
irritable defiance, and action-release* substrate of ODD.

Second, many oppositional episodes are preceded by rapid adrenergic
surge manifested as facial flushing, tachycardia, escalating
tension, tearfulness or rage, and narrowing of behavioral control.
Oxymetazoline is an imidazoline alpha-adrenergic agonist with
recognized alpha2A activity and established intranasal formulation
pathways. The present invention is based on the recognition that a
tightly limited, rapid-onset, prodrome-aligned alpha2A agonist
intervention can dampen escalating autonomic arousal and create a
window for behavioral redirection before a defiant outburst becomes
fully expressed. The oxymetazoline axis therefore addresses the
*adrenergic-escalation and rapid de-control* substrate of ODD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a D2 antagonist in combination with
an alpha2A agonist for ODD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of hostile
salience and action-readiness (a perospirone D2 axis) and
attenuation of prodromal adrenergic escalation (an oxymetazoline
alpha2A axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for
treatment of ODD. The combination is expected to produce greater
reduction in oppositional-episode frequency, outburst intensity,
and caregiver burden than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose, while expressly avoiding chronic unsupervised
nasal overuse and not replacing parent-management treatment.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) perospirone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) oxymetazoline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating ODD
in a human subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to
the subject a therapeutically effective amount of perospirone and a
therapeutically effective amount of oxymetazoline, wherein the
oxymetazoline component is preferably used as a rapid-onset rescue
intervention during prodromal escalation periods and is not used as
chronic unrestricted daily nasal therapy.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) perospirone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) oxymetazoline or a salt thereof in a second rapid-onset dosage
form, and (c) written instructions for the treatment of ODD
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, together with
warnings on cardiovascular risk, rebound-congestion risk, and
restricted use frequency.

The invention contemplates perospirone in a daily dose of about
4 mg to about 48 mg administered orally in one or two divided doses
in adult-equivalent embodiments, with pediatric embodiments using
lower weight-adjusted starting doses under specialist supervision.
The invention contemplates oxymetazoline in a rescue dose of about
25 mcg to about 200 mcg intranasally or by another fast-delivery
route in adult-equivalent embodiments, with pediatric embodiments
using lower age-appropriate metered rescue doses under specialist
supervision. The inventive feature is the combination of baseline
D2-mediated hostile-salience control with rapid alpha2A-mediated
autonomic de-escalation.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with ODD, in oppositional-episode frequency, oppositional-intensity
severity, autonomic-escalation burden, and caregiver accommodation
burden, that is greater than the sum of the individual effects of
perospirone and oxymetazoline administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Oxymetazoline"* means the imidazoline alpha-adrenergic agonist
commonly formulated for intranasal or topical use. The term as used
herein includes oxymetazoline free base, hydrochloride or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and rapid-onset delivery
formats suitable for restricted rescue use.

*"Oppositional Defiant Disorder"* or *"ODD"* means the disorder
defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by a persistent pattern of angry /
irritable mood, argumentative / defiant behavior, or vindictiveness.

*"Oppositional-episode frequency"* means the number of clinically
meaningful defiant or explosive oppositional episodes over a
defined interval as quantified by structured clinician assessment,
caregiver report, teacher report, or validated externalizing
behavior instruments.

*"Prodromal escalation"* means the interval before a fully
expressed oppositional outburst during which autonomic activation,
subjective anger build-up, or behavioral warning signs indicate
high imminent risk of defiant behavioral escalation.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of ODD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
oppositional-episode frequency, defiance-intensity score,
time-to-de-escalation, caregiver accommodation burden,
school-function score, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is attenuation of hostile salience, irritability, and
action-release tendencies.

#### 2.2 Oxymetazoline

Oxymetazoline is an imidazoline alpha-adrenergic agonist with
recognized alpha2A activity and rapid intranasal formulation
pathways. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of oxymetazoline is acute damping of adrenergic surge
and support of behavioral de-escalation when used in a tightly
limited rescue format.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise perospirone and
oxymetazoline, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of an oral perospirone dosage form and an
intranasal or other rapid-onset oxymetazoline rescue dosage form.

Excipients useful for the perospirone composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the
oxymetazoline rescue component include buffered aqueous carriers,
stabilizers, isotonic agents, preservatives where appropriate, and
metered-delivery devices suitable for low-dose rescue use.

### 4. Dosage forms

Perospirone is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
one or two daily doses. Oxymetazoline is preferably formulated as a
metered intranasal spray or other fast-delivery rescue unit.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, (b) a limited number of oxymetazoline rescue
units consistent with restricted prodrome-aligned use, and
(c) written instructions aligned to parent-management strategies,
school supports, and family safety planning.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For pediatric and adolescent subjects with ODD, the preferred
embodiment uses age- and weight-adjusted perospirone dosing under
specialist supervision together with restricted oxymetazoline
rescue dosing during prodromal escalation. Adult-equivalent genus
ranges include perospirone 4-48 mg/day and oxymetazoline rescue
doses of 25-200 mcg. Pediatric embodiments use lower initial doses
and age-appropriate metered rescue formulations.

The preferred embodiment expressly excludes chronic unrestricted
multiple-times-daily decongestant-style use. Rescue administration
is intended to be episodic and linked to identifiable escalation
risk.

Medication-medication interactions: perospirone carries
antipsychotic-class monitoring considerations including sedation,
orthostasis, and extrapyramidal effects. Oxymetazoline should be
used with caution in subjects with uncontrolled hypertension,
cardiac arrhythmia, narrow-angle glaucoma, or other unsafe
adrenergic sensitivity. The regimen is not intended to replace
protective intervention where there is imminent risk of serious harm.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to pediatric and
adolescent subjects with ODD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: developmentally persistent
angry / irritable or defiant behavior causing clinically meaningful
impairment; recurrent escalation episodes; and willingness of the
subject or caregivers to participate in parent-management, family-
based, or school-based treatment. Exclusion criteria include
primary psychotic disorder, severe cardiovascular instability,
active substance intoxication as the primary driver of behavior,
and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

ODD often includes two interacting layers: hostile attribution and
defiant action-readiness, and rapid adrenergic escalation that
drives loss of behavioral control once frustration builds.

Perospirone addresses the hostile-salience layer through D2
antagonism. Oxymetazoline addresses the adrenergic-escalation layer
through alpha2A agonist rescue signaling. The two axes are non-
overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the same
oppositional-outburst phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in ODD burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 29,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: D2 receptor binding and functional antagonism
assays for perospirone; alpha2A receptor binding and functional
agonism assays for oxymetazoline; and combined salience /
de-escalation network assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include frustrative-nonreward paradigms, irritability and anger
tasks, autonomic-escalation tasks, and child-relevant social
challenge models. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
reduction of oppositional episodes and acceleration of de-
escalation, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, age-stratified study in older
adolescent volunteers under specialist monitoring to establish
tolerability of low-dose perospirone and low-dose intranasal
oxymetazoline rescue exposure. Full PK sampling, cardiovascular
monitoring, and tolerability assessments are obtained. The
principal purpose is to establish pediatric-appropriate exposure
and safety parameters.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in pediatric and adolescent ODD (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): perospirone + prodrome-aligned oxymetazoline
  rescue.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): perospirone + placebo rescue.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo + parent-management / school-based
  treatment.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in oppositional-
episode frequency over 12 weeks. Secondary endpoints include
defiance-intensity score, time-to-de-escalation, caregiver
accommodation burden, school-function score, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of a D2 antagonist with an alpha2A
agonist for ODD specifically. The closest hits are:

* Existing ODD / conduct patents disclose different chemotypes and
  do not teach the present pair or prodrome-aligned rescue
  architecture.
* Oxymetazoline formulation patents disclose nasal or topical
  formulations and not the present externalizing-disorder use.
* Generic aggression-treatment patents do not disclose the present
  convergent-circuit rationale in ODD.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (D2 antagonism +
alpha2A agonism), (ii) the specific actives (perospirone +
oxymetazoline), and (iii) the specific indication (ODD). No prior-
art disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on which
the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Oppositional Defiant Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of perospirone and a
therapeutically effective amount of oxymetazoline are disclosed,
wherein perospirone provides baseline reduction of hostile salience
and oxymetazoline provides a restricted, rapid-onset, prodrome-
aligned de-escalation intervention. The two agents act on non-
overlapping hostile-salience and autonomic-escalation circuits and
are expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in
oppositional-episode frequency and escalation severity than either
agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
