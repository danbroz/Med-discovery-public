## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Paranoid Personality Disorder (PPD) by
co-administering (a) a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist and (b) a
serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse agonist / antagonist, in
particular the combination of perospirone and pimavanserin. The
invention is intended for adult subjects with PPD who are
concurrently receiving structured psychotherapy or supportive
behavioral treatment, and addresses pervasive suspiciousness,
maladaptive salience tagging, interpersonal mistrust, and
hostility-prone cognitive rigidity.

## Background of the invention

Paranoid Personality Disorder is a personality disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by a pervasive distrust and suspiciousness of
others such that their motives are interpreted as malevolent,
beginning by early adulthood and present in a variety of contexts.
Characteristic features include unjustified suspicions that others
are exploiting, harming, or deceiving the individual; preoccupation
with unjustified doubts about the loyalty or trustworthiness of
friends or associates; reluctance to confide in others because of
unwarranted fear that information will be used maliciously; reading
hidden demeaning or threatening meanings into benign remarks or
events; persistent bearing of grudges; and recurrent unjustified
suspicions regarding the fidelity of spouse or sexual partner.

The prevalence of PPD in the general population is low to modest,
depending on sampling and diagnostic method, but the disorder is
clinically consequential because it is associated with chronic
interpersonal conflict, poor treatment engagement, occupational
disruption, and increased risk of hostile or litigious responses to
perceived slights. Comorbid anxiety, depression, substance use
disorders, trauma-related symptoms, and other personality-disorder
traits may coexist.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological in the first
instance and consists of psychotherapy, supportive treatment, and
management of comorbid disorders. No pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
for the treatment of PPD as a primary indication. Where medication
is used, clinicians generally borrow from anxiety, mood, or
psychotic-spectrum practice, but those approaches are inconsistently
effective and may bring sedation, metabolic burden, or extrapyramidal
risk without directly addressing the core suspiciousness phenotype.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, the behavioral rigidity and hostility-prone
misinterpretation in PPD can involve dopaminergic reinforcement of
salience and perseverative attribution. Perospirone is an atypical
antipsychotic approved in Japan for schizophrenia that functions as
a D2 receptor antagonist with additional serotonergic properties.
In the present invention, the D2-antagonist axis addresses the
*reinforced suspicious interpretation and hostility-prone
behavioral output* substrate of PPD.

Second, the core phenotype of PPD includes rigid, maladaptive
assignment of salience and threat meaning to ambiguous social
stimuli. Pimavanserin (Nuplazid®) is a selective 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist approved by the FDA for hallucinations and
delusions associated with Parkinson disease psychosis. Pimavanserin
has no clinically meaningful D2 antagonism and therefore can modify
5-HT2A-mediated cortical salience physiology without directly
increasing dopaminergic motor burden. The selective 5-HT2A axis
therefore addresses the *maladaptive suspicious salience and
interpretive rigidity* substrate of PPD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a D2 antagonist in combination with
a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist for PPD
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of dopaminergic
reinforcement of suspicious interpretation (a perospirone D2 axis)
and attenuation of 5-HT2A-mediated maladaptive salience tagging
(a pimavanserin axis) are complementary and convergent substrates
for treatment of PPD. The combination is expected to produce
greater symptomatic improvement in suspiciousness, interpersonal
rigidity, and hostility-prone functioning than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) perospirone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) pimavanserin or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Paranoid Personality Disorder in an adult subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of perospirone and a therapeutically effective
amount of pimavanserin. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) perospirone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of PPD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with psychotherapy
or supportive treatment.

The invention contemplates perospirone in a daily dose of about
4 mg to about 48 mg administered orally in one or two divided doses,
with a preferred adult dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day. The invention
contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose of about 17 mg to about
34 mg administered orally once daily, with a preferred adult dose
of 34 mg/day. The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of
the D2 and 5-HT2A axes in a subject with pervasive suspiciousness
and malevolent attribution style.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in adult
subjects with PPD, in suspiciousness severity, hostile
misinterpretation, interpersonal rigidity, and caregiver / partner
burden, that is greater than the sum of the individual effects of
perospirone and pimavanserin administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, tartrate or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically
equivalent 5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Paranoid Personality Disorder"* or *"PPD"* means the personality
disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by a pervasive distrust and
suspiciousness of others such that their motives are interpreted as
malevolent, beginning by early adulthood and present across
contexts.

*"Suspiciousness severity"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
mistrust, threat attribution, hostility-prone misinterpretation,
or interpersonal rigidity, quantified by structured clinician
assessment or validated personality / symptom instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult subject for the
treatment of PPD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
suspiciousness-severity score, hostility / misinterpretation score,
interpersonal-function score, partner / caregiver burden, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is D2 antagonism directed toward reduction of
suspicious interpretation reinforcement and hostile behavioral
output. Perospirone is generally used in adult schizophrenia at
oral doses in the range of about 8 mg to 48 mg/day.

#### 2.2 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. It has no clinically meaningful
affinity for dopamine D2 receptors. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of pimavanserin is modification
of maladaptive salience assignment and suspicious meaning-making
without adding direct D2 burden. The labeled adult dose is 34 mg
orally once daily, with no dose titration required.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise perospirone and
pimavanserin, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as a perospirone
tablet and a pimavanserin capsule or tablet.

Excipients useful for the perospirone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone), lubricants (magnesium stearate), and coating
polymers. Excipients useful for the pimavanserin composition
include capsule fill excipients, diluents, disintegrants, and
stabilizers suitable for the marketed pimavanserin formulation
profile.

### 4. Dosage forms

Perospirone is preferably formulated as an immediate-release oral
tablet. Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or
tablet suitable for once-daily dosing.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of pimavanserin capsules
or tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to psychotherapy,
social-function goals, and adherence monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with PPD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily for 7 days, then 8 mg twice
  daily thereafter, with titration up to 12 mg twice daily if
  clinically needed.
* Pimavanserin 34 mg orally once daily.

Broad genus ranges include perospirone 4–48 mg/day and
pimavanserin 17–34 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses a morning
and evening perospirone regimen and a once-daily pimavanserin
regimen at a consistent clock time.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: severe hepatic or renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers without review of pimavanserin
exposure. Corrected QT interval monitoring should be considered
when other QT-prolonging medications are used.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with PPD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age ≥ 18 years; clinician-confirmed PPD; clinically
meaningful suspiciousness or interpersonal rigidity at baseline;
and capacity to participate in psychotherapy or supportive
behavioral treatment. Exclusion criteria include schizophrenia,
delusional disorder, active psychotic disorder, active mania,
uncontrolled substance use disorder, severe hepatic or renal
impairment, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

PPD is characterized by two interacting layers: maladaptive
threat-salience assignment and reinforcement of rigid suspicious
interpretation. These layers often lead to persistent hostility,
mistrust, and low treatment engagement.

Perospirone addresses the reinforcement / behavioral-output layer
through D2 antagonism. Pimavanserin addresses the maladaptive
salience and suspiciousness layer through 5-HT2A inverse agonism
without direct D2 burden. The two axes are non-overlapping at the
receptor level and converge on the suspiciousness-severity
phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in suspiciousness, hostility-prone
misinterpretation, and interpersonal rigidity than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 31,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: D2 receptor binding and functional antagonism
assays for perospirone; 5-HT2A binding and inverse-agonism assays
for pimavanserin; and combined salience-network assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include social-threat overattribution paradigms, reversal learning,
fear generalization, and rigid-attention or salience-misattribution
models. No animal model is asserted to reproduce PPD itself;
rather, the preclinical program supports convergent mechanisms of
maladaptive suspicious salience and rigid interpersonal threat
attribution.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
perospirone titrated to 16 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2:
pimavanserin 34 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult PPD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): perospirone + pimavanserin.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pimavanserin + placebo for
  perospirone.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with structured psychotherapy.

All subjects receive standardized psychotherapy or supportive
behavioral treatment focused on interpersonal interpretation,
rigidity, and alliance formation. The primary endpoint is change
from baseline in suspiciousness-severity score at week 12.
Secondary endpoints include hostility / misinterpretation score,
interpersonal-function score, responder rate, partner / caregiver
burden, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Paranoid Personality Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the D2 antagonist perospirone
and a therapeutically effective amount of the 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist pimavanserin are disclosed. The two agents act
on non-overlapping suspicious-salience and reinforcement circuits
implicated in pervasive mistrust and malevolent attribution, and
are expected to produce greater-than-additive symptomatic
improvement in suspiciousness severity, hostility-prone
misinterpretation, and interpersonal rigidity than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose.
