## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Agoraphobia by co-administering (a) a serotonin
reuptake inhibitor with 5-HT1A partial agonist activity, in
particular vilazodone, and (b) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor
agonist with 5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular
agomelatine. The invention is intended for subjects with marked
fear or anxiety about situations in which escape might be
difficult, help might not be available, or panic-like symptoms
might occur, and addresses anticipatory anxiety, situational
avoidance, sleep disruption, autonomic hyperarousal, and exposure-
therapy engagement.

## Background of the invention

Agoraphobia is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a disorder
characterized by marked fear or anxiety about two or more
situational domains such as using public transportation, being in
open spaces, being in enclosed spaces, standing in line or being in
a crowd, or being outside the home alone. The feared situations are
avoided, require the presence of a companion, or are endured with
intense distress because of concern that escape may be difficult or
that help may not be available if panic-like or other embarrassing
symptoms occur.

The prevalence of Agoraphobia is clinically meaningful and the
burden is substantial. The disorder is associated with severe
avoidance, restricted independence, social and occupational
disability, depression, secondary substance use, caregiver burden,
and impaired medical access when subjects avoid leaving the home.
Although often linked to panic disorder, agoraphobia may persist
independently and frequently involves both anticipatory dread and
entrenched behavioral avoidance.

The current standard of care is primarily psychotherapeutic and
serotonergic. It consists of exposure-based cognitive-behavioral
therapy, panic-management skills, treatment of co-occurring anxiety
or depression, and trial use of SSRIs or related antidepressants.
No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for the treatment of
Agoraphobia as a primary indication. Conventional serotonergic
treatment may reduce generalized anxiety but often leaves persistent
sleep disruption, nocturnal anticipatory fear, and exposure-linked
autonomic fragility insufficiently treated.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, agoraphobia includes intense anticipatory threat
expectancy, panic-like somatic vigilance, and avoidance-related
anxious cognition. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available
antidepressant that combines selective serotonin reuptake
inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual
mechanism supports attenuation of anxious distress and intrusive
threat-related ideation while maintaining an established anxiety-
relevant pharmacology profile. The vilazodone axis therefore
addresses the *anxious-serotonergic and panic-expectancy*
substrate of Agoraphobia.

Second, agoraphobia is frequently worsened by circadian disruption,
poor sleep, early-morning dread, and reduced physiologic recovery
after exposure attempts. Agomelatine is a melatonergic
antidepressant that acts as a potent agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors. This combination
supports resynchronization of circadian timing, improvement in
sleep architecture, and reduction of nocturnal hyperarousal. The
agomelatine axis therefore addresses the *circadian, sleep-linked,
and recovery-failure* substrate of Agoraphobia.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist in combination with a melatonergic MT1 / MT2
agonist with 5-HT2C antagonism for Agoraphobia specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of panic-
expectancy and anxious-cognitive amplification (a vilazodone axis)
and restoration of circadian stability and exposure-recovery sleep
(an agomelatine axis) are complementary and convergent substrates
for treatment of Agoraphobia. The combination is expected to
produce greater symptomatic improvement in situational avoidance,
anticipatory fear, and functional mobility than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) agomelatine or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, co-crystal,
or prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Agoraphobia in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective amount of
agomelatine. Co-administration may be simultaneous or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Agoraphobia
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with
exposure-based psychotherapy or structured mobility practice.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with
a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from
10 mg/day. The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose
of about 25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with
a preferred dose of 25 mg nightly and optional escalation to
50 mg nightly after about 2 weeks if clinically indicated. The
inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of serotonergic
panic-expectancy reduction and melatonergic sleep / recovery
stabilization in agoraphobic avoidance.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with Agoraphobia, in situational-avoidance burden, anticipatory
anxiety, panic-like distress, and functional mobility impairment,
that is greater than the sum of the individual effects of
vilazodone and agomelatine administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Agoraphobia"* means the anxiety disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and
ICD-11 by marked fear or anxiety about situations in which escape
may be difficult or help unavailable if panic-like or other
embarrassing symptoms occur, leading to avoidance, companion
dependence, or severe distress.

*"Agoraphobia-severity total"* means a clinically meaningful burden
of situational avoidance, anticipatory dread, panic-like distress,
or mobility restriction quantified by structured clinician
assessment or validated agoraphobia instruments.

*"Exposure-based psychotherapy"* means a structured intervention in
which feared situations are approached in a graded, planned manner
to reduce avoidance and catastrophic expectancy.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of Agoraphobia according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
agoraphobia-severity total, situational-avoidance score,
panic-distress score, mobility radius, responder rate, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
panic-expectancy, anxious distress, and avoidance-related threat
cognition. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of vilazodone is reduction of anticipatory threat and
panic-like anxious amplification.

#### 2.2 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is restoration of circadian timing,
sleep continuity, and recovery capacity after exposure-related
distress.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
agomelatine, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a vilazodone tablet and an
agomelatine tablet.

Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers. Excipients useful for the agomelatine
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and coating polymers suitable for oral tablet manufacture.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an oral tablet taken with
food. Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet
suitable for bedtime administration.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of agomelatine tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to exposure-based psychotherapy,
mobility tracking, and sleep scheduling.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with Agoraphobia, the preferred
dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.
* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days,
  titratable to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.

Broad genus ranges include vilazodone 10-40 mg/day and agomelatine
25-50 mg/day. The preferred schedule administers vilazodone once
daily with food and agomelatine at bedtime.

Medication-medication interactions: vilazodone dose adjustment may
be required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Agomelatine should not
be used with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors and requires hepatic
monitoring in appropriate patients. The regimen is intended to
complement, not replace, exposure-based therapy or other functional
mobility intervention.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
Agoraphobia. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age >= 12 years; marked fear or anxiety across
two or more agoraphobic situational domains; clinically meaningful
distress or mobility impairment; and willingness to participate in
exposure-based or structured behavioral treatment. Exclusion
criteria include untreated primary psychotic disorder, severe
hepatic disease, known hypersensitivity to either active, and
active unstable substance intoxication as the main driver of
avoidance.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Agoraphobia often includes two interacting layers: panic-like
anticipatory threat expectancy that narrows behavioral range, and
poor sleep / circadian recovery that sustains daytime hypervigilance
and reduces exposure tolerance.

Vilazodone addresses the panic-expectancy layer through SERT
inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Agomelatine addresses the
recovery-failure layer through MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C
antagonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on the same avoidance phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in Agoraphobia burden than either monotherapy

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays for
agomelatine; and combined sleep-threat modulation assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include avoidance learning models, panic-vulnerability paradigms,
circadian-shift tasks, and extinction / exposure-response models. A
supra-additive effect of the combination on reduction of avoidance
and improvement in post-exposure recovery, relative to either
monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the principal
in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2:
agomelatine 25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 3: the combination
for 7 days. Full PK sampling, liver-function monitoring, and
tolerability assessments are obtained in each period. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult Agoraphobia (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + agomelatine.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for
  agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with exposure-based
  psychotherapy.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in agoraphobia-
severity total at week 16. Secondary endpoints include situational-
avoidance score, panic-distress score, mobility radius, responder
rate, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Agoraphobia comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective
amount of agomelatine are disclosed. The two agents act on non-
overlapping panic-expectancy and circadian-recovery circuits
implicated in situational avoidance and are expected to produce
greater-than-additive improvement in agoraphobia severity,
mobility, and anticipatory distress than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.
