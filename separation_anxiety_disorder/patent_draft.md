## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Separation Anxiety Disorder (SAD) by
co-administering (a) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist
having 5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine, and
(b) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with 5-HT1A partial agonist
activity, in particular vilazodone. The invention is intended for
children, adolescents, and adults with developmentally excessive
fear or anxiety concerning separation from attachment figures, and
addresses nighttime separation distress, anticipatory anxiety,
reassurance seeking, school / activity refusal, and caregiver-
linked avoidance while continuing family-based or exposure-based
treatment. The invention is not intended to pathologize normative
transient developmental separation protest.

## Background of the invention

Separation Anxiety Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as
a condition in which developmentally inappropriate and excessive
fear or anxiety concerning separation from those to whom the
individual is attached leads to distress or impairment. Typical
features include excessive distress when anticipating or
experiencing separation, persistent worry about losing attachment
figures or about harm befalling them, reluctance or refusal to go
out, be alone, or sleep away from attachment figures, repeated
nightmares about separation, and physical complaints when
separation is anticipated.

The prevalence of SAD is clinically meaningful in childhood and can
persist into adolescence and adulthood. The burden includes school
avoidance, reduced autonomy, social restriction, sleep disruption,
high family stress, impaired developmental progress, and
comorbidity with generalized anxiety, panic, depressive symptoms,
and trauma-spectrum symptoms. Adults with SAD may experience major
relationship strain, reduced occupational functioning, and
dependency-driven avoidance.

The current standard of care is primarily psychotherapeutic. It
consists of family-based intervention, cognitive-behavioral therapy,
graded separation exposure, school reintegration planning where
needed, and trial use of SSRIs or related antidepressants. No
pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for the treatment of SAD as
a primary indication. Conventional serotonergic treatment may
reduce generalized anxiety but often leaves persistent nighttime
distress, caregiver-linked reassurance loops, and poor sleep
recovery insufficiently treated.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, SAD includes circadian and sleep-related
vulnerability, especially bedtime fear, nighttime reassurance
seeking, sleep refusal, and poor physiologic recovery after
separation stress. Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant
that acts as a potent agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an
antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors. This combination supports
resynchronization of circadian timing, improvement in sleep
architecture, and reduction of nocturnal hyperarousal. The
agomelatine axis therefore addresses the *circadian, sleep-linked,
and nighttime separation-distress* substrate of SAD.

Second, SAD also includes anxious attachment threat expectancy,
catastrophic worry regarding harm to attachment figures, and strong
reassurance-seeking loops. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally
available antidepressant that combines selective serotonin reuptake
inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual
mechanism supports attenuation of anxious distress and intrusive
threat-related cognition while maintaining a familiar anxiety-
relevant pharmacology profile. The vilazodone axis therefore
addresses the *anxious-serotonergic and attachment-threat
amplification* substrate of SAD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
with 5-HT2C antagonism in combination with a serotonin reuptake
inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial agonist for SAD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that restoration of sleep-linked
recovery and circadian stability (an agomelatine axis) and
attenuation of anxious attachment-threat expectancy (a vilazodone
axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of
SAD. The combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic
improvement in separation distress, reassurance-seeking, school or
activity avoidance, and nighttime functioning than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Separation Anxiety Disorder in a human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of agomelatine and a therapeutically effective
amount of vilazodone. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of SAD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with family-based,
caregiver-mediated, or exposure-based treatment.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime in adult
subjects, with pediatric or adolescent embodiments using lower
weight-adjusted starting doses under specialist supervision. The
invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about 10 mg to
about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food in adult
subjects, with pediatric or adolescent embodiments using lower
weight-adjusted starting doses under specialist supervision. The
inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of circadian-
melatonergic regulation and serotonergic / 5-HT1A stabilization in
separation-linked anxiety.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with SAD, in separation-distress severity, nighttime separation
fear, school / activity refusal, reassurance-seeking, and mobility /
independence impairment, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of agomelatine and vilazodone administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Separation Anxiety Disorder"* or *"SAD"* means the DSM-5-TR /
ICD-11 condition characterized by developmentally excessive fear or
anxiety concerning separation from attachment figures.

*"Separation-anxiety severity total"* means a clinically meaningful
burden of distress on separation, worry about harm to attachment
figures, school or activity refusal, bedtime separation distress,
or reassurance-seeking, quantified by structured clinician
assessment or validated separation-anxiety instruments.

*"Family-based or exposure-based treatment"* means an intervention
that reduces accommodation, builds graded separation tolerance, and
supports age-appropriate independence.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of SAD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: separation-
anxiety severity total, school / activity attendance, bedtime
separation-distress score, caregiver accommodation burden,
responder rate, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is reduction of bedtime separation
distress, restoration of sleep continuity, and circadian
stabilization.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
attachment-threat expectancy and reassurance-seeking anxiety. In
the present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
vilazodone is reduction of anxious separation-related cognition and
distress.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as an agomelatine tablet and a
vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the agomelatine composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the vilazodone
composition include diluents (microcrystalline cellulose, lactose
monohydrate), binders (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone),
disintegrants (crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime administration. Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an
oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
agomelatine tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of vilazodone tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to bedtime routines, caregiver
accommodation reduction, and graded separation practice.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with SAD, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days, titratable
  to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated in adults and older
  adolescents.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated in adults and older adolescents.

Broad genus ranges include agomelatine 25-50 mg/day and vilazodone
10-40 mg/day in adult embodiments. Pediatric embodiments may use
lower weight-adjusted starting doses under specialist supervision
without changing the inventive feature of the combination.

Medication-medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors and requires hepatic monitoring in
appropriate patients. Vilazodone dose adjustment may be required
with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. The regimen is intended to
complement, not replace, family-based or exposure-based treatment.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to children,
adolescents, and adults with SAD. Inclusion criteria for the
preferred treatment population include: developmental age suitable
for diagnosis; clinically meaningful separation distress or
avoidance; impairment in sleep, school attendance, mobility, or
independence; and willingness of the subject or caregivers to
participate in behavioral treatment. Exclusion criteria include
normative transient developmental separation protest, unsafe or
unstable caregiving environment requiring primary protective
intervention, severe hepatic disease, and known hypersensitivity to
either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

SAD often includes two interacting layers: anxious attachment-
threat expectancy with repetitive reassurance seeking, and poor
sleep / circadian recovery that worsens nighttime distress and
reduces next-day independence.

Agomelatine addresses the sleep-recovery layer through MT1 / MT2
agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. Vilazodone addresses the attachment-
threat layer through SERT inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on the same separation-avoidance phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in SAD burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 31,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays
for agomelatine; SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; and combined attachment-threat and sleep-stability
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include maternal / attachment separation paradigms, avoidance and
proximity-seeking tasks, sleep-fragmentation models, and anxiety-
recovery assays. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
reduction of separation-distress and nighttime disruption, relative
to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the
principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in
§ 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
agomelatine 25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone
titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, liver-function monitoring, and
tolerability assessments are obtained in each period. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in child, adolescent, and adult SAD (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm), stratified
by age cohort.

* Arm A (combination): agomelatine + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for
  agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with family-based or exposure-
  based treatment.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in separation-anxiety
severity total at week 12. Secondary endpoints include school /
activity attendance, bedtime separation-distress score, caregiver
accommodation burden, responder rate, and CGI-I.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (MT1 / MT2 agonism +
5-HT2C antagonism + SERT inhibition + 5-HT1A partial agonism),
(ii) the specific actives (agomelatine + vilazodone), and
(iii) the specific indication (SAD). No prior-art disclosure
suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on which the present
invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Separation Anxiety Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping sleep-recovery and attachment-
threat circuits implicated in excessive separation distress and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in separation
anxiety severity, nighttime separation fear, and functional
independence than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.
