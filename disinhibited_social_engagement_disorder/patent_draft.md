## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder (DSED)
by co-administering (a) an oxytocin receptor agonist and (b) an
α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor partial agonist, in particular
the combination of oxytocin and encenicline. The invention is
intended for pediatric subjects with a current diagnosis of DSED
who are receiving stable caregiving and attachment-focused
behavioral treatment, and addresses indiscriminate social
approach, reduced checking back with caregivers, and diminished
social-boundary processing.

## Background of the invention

Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder is a trauma- and stressor-
related disorder recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11, arising in the
context of grossly insufficient caregiving. The core phenotype is a
pattern of behavior in which the child actively approaches and
interacts with unfamiliar adults and exhibits at least two of the
following: reduced or absent reticence in approaching unfamiliar
adults; overly familiar verbal or physical behavior not consistent
with culturally sanctioned or age-appropriate social boundaries;
diminished or absent checking back with an adult caregiver after
venturing away, even in unfamiliar settings; and willingness to go
off with an unfamiliar adult with minimal or no hesitation. The
child must have experienced a pattern of extremes of insufficient
care, including social neglect or deprivation, repeated changes of
primary caregivers that limit stable attachments, or rearing in
unusual settings that severely limit opportunities to form selective
attachments. The diagnosis is made only when the child has a
developmental age of at least 9 months.

The prevalence of DSED in the general population is low, but the
condition is enriched in populations with histories of severe
institutional deprivation, multiple foster placements, or chronic
neglect. The disorder is associated with persistent social-boundary
failure, exposure to exploitation risk, impaired caregiver bonding,
and long-term functional difficulties in school and peer contexts.
Comorbid attention-deficit / hyperactivity disorder (ADHD),
language disorder, trauma-related symptoms, and emotional /
behavioral dysregulation are common.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological. It consists
primarily of restoring stable caregiving, attachment-based
intervention, trauma-informed parent training, and supportive
behavioral scaffolding directed toward selective attachment and
safe social boundaries. No pharmaceutical product is approved by
the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the
treatment of DSED as a primary indication. Off-label medication use
in this population generally targets comorbid symptoms such as
sleep, agitation, or ADHD, and does not directly address the core
indiscriminate social-approach phenotype.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, DSED is characterized by disturbed assignment of
social salience to caregivers versus strangers. Oxytocin receptor
(OXTR) signaling in the hypothalamus, amygdala, superior temporal
sulcus, insula, and ventral striatum contributes to social
salience, trust calibration, gaze to socially relevant cues, and
caregiver-directed approach. Intranasal oxytocin has been
investigated in autism spectrum disorder, social dysfunction, and
other neurodevelopmental conditions, with mechanism-consistent
signals on social attention and social cue salience. The oxytocin
axis therefore addresses the *caregiver salience and attachment-
related social cue* substrate of DSED, but does not directly
address attentional filtering and boundary monitoring.

Second, α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor signaling in prefrontal
cortex, hippocampus, thalamus, and sensory-gating networks
modulates attentional filtering, novelty processing, and the
ability to distinguish salient from irrelevant social stimuli.
Encenicline (EVP-6124) is a brain-penetrant α7 nicotinic receptor
partial agonist developed for cognitive impairment in schizophrenia
and Alzheimer's disease, with adult clinical studies generally
using oral once-daily doses in the range of about 0.1 mg to
1.0 mg/day. Although late-stage development was discontinued after
program failure and gastrointestinal safety signals, encenicline
and related α7 agonists remain mechanistically attractive for
conditions characterized by impaired salience assignment and social
cue filtering. The α7 axis therefore addresses the *attentional
gating and novelty-filtering* substrate of DSED.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an oxytocin receptor agonist in
combination with an α7 nicotinic partial agonist for DSED
specifically, and none discloses such a combination for the
core indiscriminate-social-approach phenotype.

The present invention recognizes that caregiver-directed social
salience (an oxytocin axis) and attentional gating / social-boundary
filtering (an α7 nicotinic axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of DSED. The combination is expected to
produce a greater improvement in selective attachment behavior,
caregiver checking-back behavior, and stranger-boundary behavior
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) oxytocin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, analogue, fragment, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) encenicline or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder in a pediatric subject in
need thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of oxytocin and a therapeutically
effective amount of encenicline. Co-administration may be
simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered within a
window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) oxytocin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) encenicline or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of DSED in a
pediatric subject according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
including administration timed to caregiver-mediated attachment
intervention sessions.

The invention contemplates oxytocin in a daily dose of about
4 IU to about 48 IU administered intranasally in divided doses,
with a preferred dose of 8 IU to 12 IU twice daily depending on
body weight. The invention contemplates encenicline in a daily dose
of about 0.01 mg to about 2.0 mg administered orally once daily,
with a preferred pediatric dose of about 0.05 mg to about
0.6 mg/day depending on body weight. The mass ratio of oxytocin to
encenicline is not the inventive feature; rather, the inventive
feature is the concurrent engagement of the OXTR and α7 nicotinic
axes during attachment-learning windows.

The technical effect of the combination is an increase, in
pediatric subjects with DSED, in selective attachment behavior,
caregiver checking-back behavior, and age-appropriate reticence
toward unfamiliar adults, as measured by caregiver-report and
structured observation, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of oxytocin and encenicline administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Oxytocin"* means the endogenous cyclic nonapeptide hormone and
neuropeptide that acts as an agonist at the human oxytocin receptor
(OXTR). The term as used herein includes the native peptide, its
pharmaceutically acceptable acetate or citrate salts, peptidic
analogues such as carbetocin, fragments retaining OXTR agonism,
glycosylated or PEGylated forms, and small-molecule OXTR agonists
that retain at least 50 % maximal activation at the human OXTR at
concentrations ≤ 1 µM.

*"Encenicline"* means the brain-penetrant α7 nicotinic acetylcholine
receptor partial agonist known in clinical development as
EVP-6124. The term includes encenicline free base, salts,
polymorphs, solvates, hydrates, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent α7 nicotinic partial
agonists exhibiting at least 50 % maximal response at the human α7
receptor at concentrations ≤ 1 µM in a standard electrophysiologic
or calcium-flux assay.

*"Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder"* or *"DSED"* means the
trauma- and stressor-related disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and
ICD-11, characterized by a pattern of behavior in which a child
actively approaches and interacts with unfamiliar adults and
demonstrates at least two of: reduced or absent reticence in
approaching unfamiliar adults, overly familiar verbal or physical
behavior, diminished or absent checking back with an adult
caregiver after venturing away, or willingness to go off with an
unfamiliar adult with minimal or no hesitation, arising in the
context of extremes of insufficient care.

*"Selective attachment behavior"* means behavior in which the child
preferentially seeks proximity, reference, or comfort from a stable
caregiver rather than an unfamiliar adult in an unfamiliar or
mildly stressful environment.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a pediatric subject for the
treatment of DSED according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
DAI disinhibited-social-engagement domain score, caregiver
checking-back behavior, structured social-boundary observation
score, caregiver stress index, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Oxytocin

Oxytocin is an endogenous neuropeptide synthesized in the
paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei of the hypothalamus and acts
at the OXTR, a Gαq-coupled receptor that modulates social salience,
attachment, trust, gaze allocation, and caregiver-directed
approach. Synthetic oxytocin is approved in obstetric indications
and intranasal oxytocin has been widely studied in social-cognitive
and neurodevelopmental disorders. In the present invention, the
intranasal route is preferred to maximize central nervous system
exposure relative to peripheral OXTR engagement.

#### 2.2 Encenicline

Encenicline (EVP-6124) is a brain-penetrant α7 nicotinic
acetylcholine receptor partial agonist developed for cognitive
impairment in schizophrenia and Alzheimer's disease. α7 nicotinic
receptor signaling contributes to attentional filtering, novelty
processing, sensory gating, and cue discrimination. Adult clinical
studies generally used once-daily oral doses in the range of about
0.1 mg to 1.0 mg/day. The development program was later
discontinued after mixed efficacy and gastrointestinal safety
signals, but the compound remains mechanistically attractive as a
research asset for salience and attention phenotypes.

#### 2.3 Materials and sourcing posture

Because encenicline is a shelved development asset, material for
the present invention may be obtained by one or more of:
(a) license or transfer from the current rights holder;
(b) GMP custom synthesis of encenicline or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt thereof; (c) use of retained clinical-development
inventory where lawfully available; or (d) substitution with a
pharmacologically equivalent α7 nicotinic partial agonist within
the genus defined herein. The present specification is drafted to
support any of those lawful supply pathways.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise oxytocin and
encenicline, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as an intranasal
oxytocin device and an oral liquid or dispersible tablet containing
encenicline.

The oxytocin component may be formulated as the native peptide, an
acetate or citrate salt, or an analogue retaining OXTR agonism.
The encenicline component may be formulated as free base or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt in oral solution, dispersible
tablet, minitablet, or capsule form. Excipients useful for the
oxytocin composition include buffers (citrate, phosphate),
tonicity agents (sodium chloride, mannitol), preservatives
(chlorobutanol), and permeation enhancers (chitosan,
cyclodextrins). Excipients useful for the encenicline composition
include diluents (microcrystalline cellulose, mannitol), binders
(povidone), disintegrants (crospovidone), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and flavoring agents suitable for pediatric
oral liquid or dispersible dosage forms.

### 4. Dosage forms

Oxytocin is preferably formulated as a metered-dose intranasal
spray delivering about 4 IU per actuation. Encenicline is
preferably formulated as an oral liquid, dispersible tablet, or
minitablet to facilitate pediatric administration and precise
weight-band dosing.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day intranasal
oxytocin device, (b) a 30-day supply of encenicline oral liquid
or dispersible tablets at a weight-appropriate dose, and
(c) written instructions aligned to caregiver-mediated attachment
intervention sessions.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For pediatric subjects with DSED, weight-banded dosing is preferred:

| Body weight | Oxytocin (IU/day, intranasal) | Encenicline (mg/day, oral) |
|---|---|---|
| 10–< 20 kg | 8 (4 IU twice daily) | 0.05–0.10 |
| 20–< 35 kg | 16 (8 IU twice daily) | 0.20–0.30 |
| ≥ 35 kg | 24 (12 IU twice daily) | 0.40–0.60 |

Broad genus ranges include oxytocin 4–48 IU/day and encenicline
0.01–2.0 mg/day. The preferred administration schedule places the
morning intranasal oxytocin dose within 30 minutes of a planned
caregiver-mediated attachment or parent-training session and the
encenicline dose in the morning to align with daytime attentional
demands.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: because pediatric encenicline dosing
in this indication is investigational, severe hepatic or renal
impairment is exclusionary. No standard oxytocin dose adjustment is
required in mild hepatic or renal impairment at the doses
contemplated herein.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration of
encenicline with strong CYP-modulating regimens until definitive
DDI characterization is available. No clinically meaningful
interaction between intranasal oxytocin and encenicline is expected
on current mechanistic understanding.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to pediatric
subjects with DSED. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: developmental age ≥ 9 months; chronological age
about 2 to 10 years; documented history of extremes of insufficient
care; current DSED diagnosis per DSM-5-TR by qualified clinician;
stable caregiving placement for at least 8 weeks; caregiver willing
to participate in attachment-focused intervention; and absence of a
primary autism spectrum disorder diagnosis explaining the social
behavioral phenotype. Exclusion criteria include active psychosis,
uncontrolled seizure disorder, severe gastrointestinal disease,
severe hepatic or renal impairment, and inability to tolerate
intranasal or oral investigational therapy.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

DSED reflects failure of social-selectivity learning. The child
insufficiently differentiates stable caregivers from unfamiliar
adults in assigning trust, approach, and attachment behaviors.

Oxytocin addresses the caregiver-salience dimension by increasing
the behavioral weight assigned to caregiver facial affect, voice,
and proximity cues. Encenicline addresses the attentional-gating
dimension by improving novelty filtering, cue discrimination, and
context-appropriate response selection. The two axes are
non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the same
caregiver-versus-stranger salience computation.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in selective attachment behavior, caregiver
checking-back, and socially appropriate hesitation with unfamiliar
adults than either monotherapy alone.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: OXTR binding and calcium-mobilization assays for
oxytocin; α7 nicotinic receptor binding and electrophysiologic or
calcium-flux assays for encenicline; and combined salience-network
cellular signaling assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include maternal-separation and limited-bedding / nesting models,
social preference tests, caregiver-versus-stranger social-choice
paradigms, 5-choice serial reaction time tasks for attentional
gating, and social novelty preference assays. A supra-additive
effect of the combination on caregiver-directed social preference
and reduction of indiscriminate social approach, relative to either
monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the principal
in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1: intranasal
oxytocin 24 IU/day for 7 days; Period 2: encenicline 0.3 mg/day for
7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK sampling,
exploratory EEG / sensory-gating biomarkers, and gastrointestinal
safety monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal
purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of clinically
meaningful PK interaction prior to pediatric study.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in pediatric DSED (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): intranasal oxytocin + oral encenicline.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): intranasal oxytocin + oral placebo.
* Arm C (placebo): intranasal placebo + oral placebo.

All subjects receive standardized attachment-focused intervention
and caregiver training of equal frequency and intensity. The
primary endpoint is change from baseline in the DAI disinhibited-
social-engagement domain at week 12. Secondary endpoints include
caregiver checking-back behavior, indiscriminate approach
frequency, structured social-boundary observation score, caregiver
stress index, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder in pediatric subjects
comprising co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount
of an oxytocin receptor agonist and a therapeutically effective
amount of the α7 nicotinic partial agonist encenicline are
disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping receptor classes
that converge on caregiver-salience and attentional-gating circuits
implicated in indiscriminate social approach and impaired social
boundaries in Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder, and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in selective
attachment behavior, caregiver checking-back behavior, and
age-appropriate hesitation with unfamiliar adults than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose. The specification
contemplates licensable or custom-synthesis supply of encenicline
or a pharmacologically equivalent α7 nicotinic partial agonist.
