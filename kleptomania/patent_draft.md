## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Kleptomania by co-administering (a) a dopamine
D2 receptor antagonist and (b) a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse
agonist / antagonist, in particular the combination of perospirone
and pimavanserin. The invention is intended for adult subjects with
Kleptomania who are concurrently receiving structured psychotherapy
and behavioral supervision, and addresses tension-driven stealing
urges, reward / relief following theft, and cue-triggered recurrence
in retail and other object-acquisition contexts.

## Background of the invention

Kleptomania is a disruptive, impulse-control, and conduct disorder
defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by recurrent failure to resist
impulses to steal objects that are not needed for personal use or
for their monetary value, increasing sense of tension immediately
before the theft, and pleasure, gratification, or relief at the
time of committing the theft. The stealing is not committed to
express anger or vengeance, is not in response to delusion or
hallucination, and is not better explained by conduct disorder, a
manic episode, or antisocial personality disorder. Although the
market opportunity is small, the disorder is clinically meaningful
because it is associated with shame, secrecy, repeated legal
consequences, loss of employment, and substantial psychosocial
distress.

The prevalence of Kleptomania in the general population is low, but
the condition is enriched in settings where repeated theft behavior
appears inconsistent with economic need. Kleptomania is associated
with high rates of major depressive disorder, obsessive–compulsive
disorder, substance use disorders, eating disorders, and other
impulse-control symptoms.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological. It consists
primarily of cognitive-behavioral therapy, relapse-prevention
planning, cue exposure and response prevention, trigger avoidance,
and in some cases legal or occupational monitoring. No
pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of Kleptomania as a
primary indication. Off-label pharmacotherapy is heterogeneous and
draws from obsessive–compulsive disorder, addiction, and
impulse-control-disorder practice rather than a Kleptomania-
specific evidence base.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, stealing in Kleptomania appears to be maintained
by a reward-salience and tension-relief loop involving dopaminergic
reinforcement, similar in part to addictive and other impulse-
control disorders. Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic
approved in Japan for schizophrenia that functions as a D2 receptor
antagonist with additional serotonergic properties. In the present
invention, the D2-antagonist axis addresses the *reward-salience
and urge-reinforcement* substrate of Kleptomania.

Second, cue-triggered recurrence in Kleptomania is plausibly
mediated by serotonin 5-HT2A-linked salience amplification,
intrusive preoccupation, and compulsive attention to theft-related
cues and object-acquisition opportunities. Pimavanserin
(Nuplazid®) is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. Pimavanserin has no clinically
meaningful dopaminergic receptor affinity. The selective 5-HT2A
axis therefore addresses the *cue-triggered salience and
preoccupation* substrate of Kleptomania without adding additional
D2 blockade burden.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a D2 antagonist in combination with
a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist for Kleptomania
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of dopaminergic
urge reinforcement (a perospirone D2 axis) and attenuation of
5-HT2A-mediated cue salience (a pimavanserin axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of
Kleptomania. The combination is expected to produce a greater
reduction in stealing urges and recurrence than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) perospirone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) pimavanserin or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Kleptomania in an adult subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of perospirone and a therapeutically effective amount of
pimavanserin. Co-administration may be simultaneous or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) perospirone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Kleptomania
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, including
coordination with psychotherapy and behavioral / property-risk
management planning.

The invention contemplates perospirone in a daily dose of about
4 mg to about 48 mg administered orally in one or two divided
doses, with a preferred adult dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day.
The invention contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose of about
17 mg to about 34 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 34 mg/day. The inventive feature is the
concurrent engagement of the D2 and 5-HT2A axes in a subject whose
stealing behavior is driven by tension, reward, and cue salience
rather than by economic need, mania, or psychosis.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in adult
subjects with Kleptomania, in theft incidents, stealing urges, and
object / cue preoccupation, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of perospirone and pimavanserin administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, tartrate or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically
equivalent 5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Kleptomania"* means the impulse-control disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by recurrent failure to resist impulses to
steal objects that are not needed for personal use or for monetary
value, increasing sense of tension immediately before the theft,
pleasure, gratification, or relief at the time of committing the
theft, and absence of theft motivated by gain, vengeance,
delusion, hallucination, mania, or antisocial motive.

*"Stealing urge"* means an experienced internal pressure to take an
object without permission, where the object is not needed for
personal use or monetary value and where the urge is associated
with tension before the act and relief after it.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult subject for the
treatment of Kleptomania according to a dosing regimen disclosed
herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least
one of: monthly theft incidents, stealing-urge score,
cue / object preoccupation score, responder rate, legal /
occupational event count, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is D2 antagonism directed toward reduction of
reward-salience and urge reinforcement. Perospirone is generally
used in adult schizophrenia at oral doses in the range of about
8 mg to 48 mg/day. The compound is not FDA-approved in the United
States.

#### 2.2 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. It has no clinically meaningful
affinity for dopaminergic, muscarinic, histaminergic, adrenergic
or other serotonin receptors outside its primary 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C
profile. The labeled adult dose is 34 mg orally once daily, with
no dose titration required. Pimavanserin remains under active U.S.
patent protection; clinical use of the present invention will
require coordination with the rights holder or a licenseable
development path.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise perospirone and
pimavanserin, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as a perospirone
tablet and a pimavanserin capsule or tablet.

Excipients useful for the perospirone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone), lubricants (magnesium stearate), and coating
polymers. Excipients useful for the pimavanserin composition
include capsule fill excipients, diluents, disintegrants, and
stabilizers suitable for the marketed pimavanserin formulation
profile.

### 4. Dosage forms

Perospirone is preferably formulated as an immediate-release oral
tablet. Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or
tablet suitable for once-daily dosing.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of pimavanserin capsules
or tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to psychotherapy,
risk management, and adherence monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with Kleptomania, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily for 7 days, then 8 mg
  twice daily thereafter, with titration up to 12 mg twice daily
  if clinically needed.
* Pimavanserin 34 mg orally once daily.

Broad genus ranges include perospirone 4–48 mg/day and
pimavanserin 17–34 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses a morning
and evening perospirone regimen and a once-daily pimavanserin
regimen at a consistent clock time.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: severe hepatic or renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers without review of pimavanserin
exposure. Corrected QT interval monitoring should be considered
when other QT-prolonging medications are used.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with Kleptomania. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age ≥ 18 years; clinician-confirmed
Kleptomania; recurrent stealing urges or theft incidents not
motivated by need or monetary value; participation in structured
psychotherapy; and a documented behavioral / property-risk
management plan. Exclusion criteria include theft motivated by
economic need, revenge, mania, psychosis, organized criminal
activity, or antisocial personality disorder without Kleptomania;
active psychosis; uncontrolled substance use disorder; severe
hepatic or renal impairment; and inability to comply with safety
planning. The invention is not a substitute for legal supervision
or store / occupational restrictions where otherwise required.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Kleptomania is maintained by a tension-relief / reward-salience
loop in which object-acquisition cues acquire excessive
motivational weight.

Perospirone addresses dopaminergic reward attribution and impulsive
urge reinforcement via D2 antagonism. Pimavanserin addresses
5-HT2A-mediated cue salience, preoccupation, and compulsive
attention to theft-related stimuli and acquisition contexts without
adding further dopaminergic blockade burden. The two axes are
non-overlapping in their principal mechanistic contribution and
converge on the same stealing recurrence loop.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in stealing urges, cue / object preoccupation,
and recurrent theft incidents than either monotherapy alone.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: D2 receptor binding and functional antagonism
assays for perospirone; 5-HT2A binding and functional inverse-
agonism assays for pimavanserin; and combined salience-network
pharmacology assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include conditioned-place-preference paradigms for cue-reward
association, delay-discounting and go/no-go tasks for impulsive
responding, and object-acquisition salience tasks using non-harmful
reward-associated cues. No animal model is asserted to reproduce
Kleptomania itself; rather, the preclinical program supports
convergent mechanisms of reward salience and cue-driven recurrence.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1:
perospirone titrated to 16 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2:
pimavanserin 34 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is
to establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult Kleptomania (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 80 (approximately 27 per arm) given the
rarity and forensic complexity of the indication.

* Arm A (combination): perospirone + pimavanserin.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pimavanserin + placebo for
  perospirone.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with structured psychotherapy.

All subjects receive standardized psychotherapy emphasizing trigger
identification, relapse prevention, and behavioral accountability.
The primary endpoint is change from baseline in monthly theft
incidents at week 16. Secondary endpoints include stealing-urge
score, cue / object preoccupation score, responder rate, legal /
occupational event count, and CGI-I.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in

(perospirone + pimavanserin), and (iii) the specific indication
(Kleptomania as a tension-relief theft disorder). No prior-art
disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on which the

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Kleptomania in adult subjects comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the D2 antagonist perospirone
and a therapeutically effective amount of the 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist pimavanserin are disclosed. The two agents act
on non-overlapping receptor classes that converge on reward-
salience and cue-preoccupation circuits implicated in recurrent,
tension-driven theft behavior in Kleptomania, and are expected to
produce greater-than-additive improvement in theft incidents,
stealing urges, and cue-triggered recurrence than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose.
