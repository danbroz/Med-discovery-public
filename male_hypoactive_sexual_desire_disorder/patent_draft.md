## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Male Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder (MHSDD)
by co-administering (a) a serotonergic tetracyclic agent having
antagonist activity at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, in particular
mianserin, and (b) a mu-opioid receptor agonist, in particular
sufentanil, in a restricted, session-aligned rescue regimen. The
invention is intended for adult male subjects with persistently
deficient sexual or erotic thoughts, fantasies, or desire causing
clinically meaningful distress, and is not intended for chronic
unsupervised opioid use, for treatment of erectile dysfunction
alone, or for pathologizing sexual orientation or consensual
preference patterns.

## Background of the invention

Male Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder is recognized in modern
clinical sexual-medicine practice as a syndrome in which sexual or
erotic thoughts, fantasies, or desire are persistently deficient or
absent and the subject experiences clinically meaningful personal
distress as a result. The syndrome may be generalized or
situational, lifelong or acquired, and may coexist with mood
disorders, chronic illness, sleep disturbance, medication effects,
relationship strain, endocrine contributors, or idiopathic
incentive-salience blunting.

The prevalence of clinically meaningful low sexual desire in adult
men is substantial, but only a subset meet disorder-level criteria
because distress and impairment are required. The clinical burden
includes reduced intimacy, avoidance of partnered sexual activity,
shame, relationship conflict, lowered quality of life, and
discontinuation of otherwise beneficial psychiatric medications
when desire loss is medication-associated.

The current standard of care is fragmented and etiology-focused. It
consists primarily of history and examination, management of
endocrine or medication contributors, couples or psychosexual
therapy, sleep optimization, and empiric off-label pharmacologic
trials. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States
Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for MHSDD as a
primary indication. A clinically meaningful gap remains for a
mechanism-based regimen that can restore desire and anticipatory
sexual salience in selected adult male subjects without chronic
high-risk sedative or opioid exposure.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, low sexual desire in men may be maintained by
excessive serotonergic inhibition of appetitive sexual interest and
by sleep-related or anxious inhibitory tone that suppresses erotic
initiatory drive. Mianserin is a tetracyclic antidepressant with
antagonist activity at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, alpha2-
adrenergic receptors, and H1 histamine receptors. By reducing
serotonergic braking and altering 5-HT2C-linked inhibitory tone,
mianserin provides a route to disinhibition of sexual-interest
circuits. The mianserin axis therefore addresses the
*serotonergic-desire inhibition substrate* of MHSDD.

Second, a subset of MHSDD appears to involve deficient acute
incentive salience in which sexual cues fail to recruit sufficient
motivational value even when the subject wishes to re-engage in
sexual life. The endogenous opioid system participates in reward
salience, reinforcement, and affective incentive coding. Sufentanil
is a potent mu-opioid receptor agonist approved for analgesic use
in tightly supervised medical settings and available in parenteral
and sublingual / transmucosal formulations. Although chronic or
high-dose opioid exposure is generally associated with sexual
dysfunction, the present invention is based on the recognition that
*very low-dose, intermittent, time-limited, session-aligned*
mu-opioid agonism, administered only on planned sexual-attempt
days, can increase acute erotic incentive salience in selected
subjects when combined with background reduction of serotonergic
inhibitory tone by mianserin.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C-antagonist
antidepressant in combination with low-dose rescue mu-opioid
agonism for MHSDD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that reduction of serotonergic
braking on desire (a mianserin 5-HT2 axis) and tightly limited
enhancement of acute erotic incentive salience (a sufentanil
mu-opioid rescue axis) are complementary and convergent substrates
for treatment of MHSDD in selected adult male subjects. The
combination is expected to produce greater improvement in desire
severity, initiating interest, and distress reduction than either
agent alone at any individually achievable dose, while explicitly
avoiding chronic unsupervised opioid exposure.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) mianserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) sufentanil or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Male Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder in an adult male subject in
need thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of mianserin and a therapeutically
effective amount of sufentanil, wherein the sufentanil component is
used as a session-aligned, as-needed rescue intervention during
planned consensual sexual activity or erotic-engagement attempts
and is not used as chronic daily opioid therapy.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) mianserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) sufentanil or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of MHSDD according
to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, together with explicit
warnings on opioid risk, restricted use frequency, and supervision
requirements.

The invention contemplates mianserin in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 90 mg administered orally once daily or in divided
doses, with a preferred dose of 15 mg to 60 mg/day and a preferred
bedtime initiation dose of 15 mg to 30 mg/day. The invention
contemplates sufentanil in a single rescue dose of about 2.5 mcg to
about 20 mcg by sublingual or transmucosal route administered about
15 to 90 minutes before a planned sexual attempt, with a preferred
maximum daily exposure of about 40 mcg and a preferred maximum use
frequency of about 4 attempt-days per week. The inventive feature
is the combination of chronic background reduction of serotonergic
desire inhibition with a tightly limited, low-dose, session-aligned
rescue intervention for erotic incentive salience.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in adult
male subjects with MHSDD, in desire-deficit severity and sexual-
distress burden, and an increase in erotic thoughts, anticipatory
interest, and successful sexual engagement, that is greater than
the sum of the individual effects of mianserin and sufentanil
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Mianserin"* means the tetracyclic antidepressant
2-methyl-1,2,3,4,10,14b-hexahydrodibenzo[c,f]pyrazino[1,2-a]
azepine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof. The term
includes the hydrochloride and other pharmaceutically acceptable
salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically
labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Sufentanil"* means the potent mu-opioid receptor agonist
N-[4-(methoxymethyl)-1-[2-(2-thienyl)ethyl]-4-piperidyl]-N-
phenylpropanamide or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof.
The term includes parenteral, sublingual, and transmucosal
formulations and clinically equivalent low-dose rescue formulations
of sufentanil used in tightly controlled settings.

*"Male Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder"* or *"MHSDD"* means a
male sexual-interest disorder characterized by persistently
deficient or absent erotic thoughts, fantasies, or desire causing
clinically meaningful distress or impairment.

*"Desire-severity score"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
reduced sexual interest, reduced erotic thoughts, reduced
anticipatory motivation, or associated distress quantified by
structured clinician assessment or validated sexual-interest
instruments.

*"Session-aligned rescue dosing"* means administration of a rescue
medication within a defined interval, preferably about 15 to
90 minutes, before a planned sexual attempt or erotic-engagement
context, with explicit limits on frequency, total dose, and
supervision.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult male subject for the
treatment of MHSDD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
desire-severity score, erotic-thought frequency, sexual-distress
score, satisfying sexual events, subjective desire intensity, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Mianserin

Mianserin is a tetracyclic antidepressant with antagonist activity
at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and H1
histamine receptors. In the present invention, the principal
mechanistic contribution of mianserin is reduction of serotonergic
sexual-interest inhibition and restoration of a more permissive
background state for desire.

#### 2.2 Sufentanil

Sufentanil is a potent mu-opioid receptor agonist approved for
analgesic use in tightly supervised settings. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of sufentanil is
acute, time-limited enhancement of erotic incentive salience during
planned sexual opportunities. The preferred embodiment is a
restricted rescue regimen, not chronic daily opioid therapy.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise mianserin and
sufentanil, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a mianserin tablet and a low-
dose transmucosal sufentanil rescue unit.

Excipients useful for the mianserin composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and film-coating polymers
suitable for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the
sufentanil rescue component include mucoadhesive carriers,
stabilizers, permeation-support excipients, and low-dose unit
delivery matrices appropriate for sublingual or buccal use.

### 4. Dosage forms

Mianserin is preferably formulated as an oral tablet administered
once daily, preferably at bedtime. Sufentanil is preferably
formulated as a low-dose sublingual or buccal dosage form suitable
for restricted rescue use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
mianserin tablets, (b) a limited number of low-dose sufentanil
rescue units consistent with a maximum use frequency of about
4 attempt-days per week, and (c) written instructions aligned to
session-aligned rescue dosing, safety monitoring, endocrine review,
and opioid-risk warnings.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult male subjects with MHSDD, the preferred dosing regimen
is:

* Mianserin 15 mg orally at bedtime for about 7 days, titratable to
  30 mg and then to 60 mg nightly if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.
* Sufentanil about 2.5 mcg to about 20 mcg by sublingual or buccal
  route about 15 to 90 minutes before a planned consensual sexual
  activity or erotic-engagement attempt, with preferred maximum use
  frequency of about 4 attempt-days per week and a preferred
  maximum daily exposure of about 40 mcg.

The preferred embodiment expressly excludes chronic daily opioid
therapy. Rescue doses are aligned to planned attempt days and
should be accompanied by counseling on sedation risk, respiratory
risk, secure storage, and abuse potential.

Medication-medication interactions: concomitant use of other CNS
depressants, benzodiazepines, alcohol misuse, or other opioids is
disfavored or exclusionary because of additive sedation and
respiratory-depression risk. The preferred clinical embodiment
requires review of endocrine, medication-induced, and relationship-
context contributors before initiation.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult male
subjects with MHSDD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age >= 18 years; persistent low sexual desire
causing clinically meaningful distress; willingness to track
planned sexual-attempt outcomes or erotic-interest measures; and
ability to follow restricted rescue-use instructions. Exclusion
criteria include untreated clinically significant hypogonadism or
other endocrine disorder as the primary driver of symptoms, opioid
use disorder or high misuse risk, clinically meaningful respiratory
instability, severe hepatic impairment, uncontrolled major
psychiatric decompensation, and known hypersensitivity to either
active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

MHSDD in selected adult male subjects often includes two
interacting layers: persistent serotonergic inhibition of sexual
interest and deficient acute incentive salience in response to
sexual cues or opportunities.

Mianserin addresses the first layer through 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C
antagonism and related serotonergic disinhibition. Sufentanil
addresses the second through a tightly limited, session-aligned
mu-opioid rescue intervention that enhances acute erotic incentive
salience. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on desire restoration.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in sexual desire and distress reduction than
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 27,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C binding and antagonism assays
for mianserin; mu-opioid receptor binding and signaling assays for
sufentanil; and combined reward-salience / motivational assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include sexual-motivation paradigms, incentive-salience tasks,
reward-seeking models, and serotonergic sexual inhibition models. A
supra-additive effect of the combination on restoration of sexual-
interest signals, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult male volunteers (age 21-55 years). Period 1:
mianserin 15 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: single low-dose
sublingual sufentanil rescue administration on two supervised study
days; Period 3: the combination. Full PK sampling, sedation
monitoring, pulse oximetry, adverse-event monitoring, and
abuse-liability assessments are obtained. The principal purpose is
to establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult MHSDD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): mianserin + session-aligned low-dose
  sufentanil rescue.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): mianserin + placebo rescue.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo background + placebo rescue.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in desire-severity
score across weeks 5-8. Secondary endpoints include erotic-thought
frequency, sexual-distress score, satisfying sexual events,
subjective desire intensity, and CGI-I.

specifically. The closest hits are:

* Sexual-desire-disorder filings involving flibanserin, testosterone,

  the present actives or the present session-aligned rescue

* Sufentanil dosage-form patents disclose pain-management rescue

* Generic sexual dysfunction patents do not disclose the present

  with tightly limited mu-opioid incentive-salience rescue.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (5-HT2A / 5-HT2C
antagonism + mu-opioid agonism), (ii) the specific actives
(mianserin + sufentanil), and (iii) the specific session-aligned
use for MHSDD. No prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-
circuit rationale on which the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Male
Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of mianserin and a therapeutically
effective amount of sufentanil are disclosed, wherein the
mianserin provides background reduction of serotonergic desire
inhibition and the sufentanil provides a low-dose, session-aligned
rescue intervention on planned sexual-attempt days. The two agents
act on non-overlapping desire-inhibition and incentive-salience
circuits and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in desire severity and associated distress than either
agent alone at any individually achievable dose while avoiding
chronic unsupervised opioid exposure.
