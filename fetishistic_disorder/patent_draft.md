## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Fetishistic Disorder by co-administering (a) a
serotonin reuptake inhibitor that is also a 5-HT1A receptor partial
agonist and (b) a non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist, in
particular the combination of vilazodone and bicalutamide. The
invention is intended only for adults with a current diagnosis of
Fetishistic Disorder per the *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of
Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision* (DSM-5-TR), in
whom the fetishistic urges or behaviors are accompanied by
clinically significant distress or impairment in social,
occupational, or other important areas of functioning, and who
voluntarily seek pharmacotherapy with full informed consent.

## Background of the invention

Fetishistic Disorder is defined in DSM-5-TR (302.81) as recurrent
and intense sexual arousal from either the use of nonliving objects
or a highly specific focus on non-genital body part(s), as
manifested by fantasies, urges, or behaviors, that has persisted
for at least six months and that causes clinically significant
distress or impairment in social, occupational, or other important
areas of functioning. The diagnosis specifies "with body part(s),"
"with nonliving object(s)," or "with other," and explicitly
excludes objects specifically designed for genital stimulation
(such as vibrators) and the use of nonliving objects in
cross-dressing (which is classified separately as Transvestic
Disorder under DSM-5-TR § 302.3). In the International
Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11), the disorder
is recognized at code 6D32 when accompanied by distress, impairment,
or risk to others. The present invention is limited to adult
subjects who meet the DSM-5-TR criterion of clinically significant
distress or impairment and who, with full informed consent,
voluntarily seek pharmacotherapy.

The estimated population prevalence of Fetishistic Disorder, as
distinct from non-pathological fetishistic interests, is below 1 %
of adults in epidemiologic study, and the disorder is reported
substantially more frequently in males than in females. The
disorder is associated with subjective distress, with disruption
of partnered relationships, with intrusive sexual ideation that
interferes with occupational and academic function, and (in a
small subset) with impulsive financial expenditure on the focal
object class. Comorbid major depressive disorder, generalized
anxiety disorder, social anxiety disorder, obsessive–compulsive
disorder, and substance use disorders are common.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological in the first
instance and consists of cognitive-behavioral therapy directed to
distress reduction, harm reduction, relationship counseling, and
relapse prevention. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment
of Fetishistic Disorder as a primary indication. Off-label
pharmacotherapy reported in the medical literature for paraphilic
disorders generally has used (i) selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitors (SSRIs) for compulsive sexual behavior, distress, and
intrusive sexual ideation; and (ii) hormonal anti-androgen agents
(medroxyprogesterone acetate, cyproterone acetate, leuprolide,
triptorelin) for reduction of paraphilic sexual urge intensity in
the most severe and treatment-resistant cases. The published
controlled evidence base in Fetishistic Disorder specifically is
sparse and is largely extrapolated from the broader paraphilic-
disorders literature.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, the compulsive- and intrusive-ideation
component of fetishistic urge is mediated by serotonergic and
frontolimbic circuits, with substantial mechanistic overlap with
obsessive–compulsive and addictive phenotypes; this component is
addressable by serotonergic agents but not by hormonal
anti-androgens. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available,
FDA-approved antidepressant that combines selective serotonin
reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor;
the dual mechanism is reported to support a more rapid attenuation
of anxious-compulsive symptomatology than a pure SSRI.

Second, the androgen-mediated component of fetishistic sexual
urge intensity is mediated by androgen receptor signaling in the
central nervous system and in peripheral target tissues. Hormonal
anti-androgen agents substantially reduce circulating testosterone
and produce significant reductions in paraphilic urge in the
published literature, but their adverse-effect burden (decreased
bone mineral density, hot flashes, gynecomastia, mood disturbance,
hepatic effects, cardiovascular effects, complete loss of any
consensual sexual function) has limited their acceptability
outside the most severe forms of paraphilic disorder. The
non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist bicalutamide (Casodex®)
is FDA-approved for prostate carcinoma and competes with
endogenous testosterone and dihydrotestosterone at the androgen
receptor *without* lowering circulating gonadotropin or
testosterone. Bicalutamide therefore offers a *partial* and
*titratable* attenuation of androgen-receptor-mediated drive
without the total-hypogonadism profile of GnRH agonist therapy.
No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a 5-HT1A-partial-agonist SSRI in
combination with a non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist for
any paraphilic indication, and none discloses such a combination
for Fetishistic Disorder specifically.

The present invention recognizes that serotonergic attenuation of
the compulsive-ideation component (a vilazodone HTR1A axis) and
non-castrating titratable reduction of androgen-receptor-mediated
urge (a bicalutamide AR axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of Fetishistic Disorder in adults with
clinically significant distress or impairment who voluntarily
seek pharmacotherapy. The combination is expected to produce a
greater reduction in fetishistic-distress and fetishistic-urge
measures than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose, with a substantially better tolerability profile than legacy
hormonal anti-androgen regimens.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) bicalutamide or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Fetishistic Disorder in an adult subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective
amount of bicalutamide. Co-administration may be simultaneous, in
a unitary dosage form or in separate dosage forms, or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising (a)
vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) bicalutamide or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage
form, and (c) written instructions for the treatment of
Fetishistic Disorder in an adult subject according to a dosing
regimen disclosed herein, together with a written informed-consent
and shared-decision-making module that addresses partner
involvement, the focal-object specifier ("with body part(s)" /
"with nonliving object(s)"), fertility implications of
bicalutamide, and the option of discontinuation.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally with food once daily,
with a preferred adult dose of 20 to 40 mg/day after a 7-day
titration from 10 mg/day. The invention contemplates bicalutamide
in a daily dose of about 25 mg to about 150 mg administered orally
once daily, with a preferred adult dose of 50 mg/day. The mass
ratio of vilazodone to bicalutamide (calculated on the free-base
equivalents) ranges broadly from about 1:15 to about 4:1, and is
preferably from about 1:5 to about 1:1. The dose-equivalent molar
relationship and the partner- and clinician-engaged dosing
schedule are the inventive features, not the absolute mass ratio.

The technical effect of the combination is (a) a reduction, in
adults with Fetishistic Disorder, in the frequency and intensity
of fetishistic urge directed to the specifier-identified focal
class (body part or nonliving object), as measured by the
Multiphasic Sex Inventory-II (MSI-II) Paraphilic Sexual Outlet
sub-scale and by a focal-class-specific behavioral count;
(b) a reduction in disorder-specific distress as measured by the
Sexual Distress Scale; and (c) preservation of consensual sexual
function (relative to GnRH-agonist therapy), each greater than the
sum of the individual effects of vilazodone and bicalutamide
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose,
and with substantially better tolerability than hormonal
anti-androgen monotherapy.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Bicalutamide"* means the compound (R,S)-N-[4-cyano-3-
(trifluoromethyl)phenyl]-3-[(4-fluorophenyl)sulfonyl]-2-hydroxy-2-
methylpropanamide, marketed in the United States as Casodex®.
Bicalutamide is a non-steroidal competitive androgen receptor
antagonist with no agonist activity. The term as used herein
includes the racemate, the active R-enantiomer, and any
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph,
prodrug, isotopically labeled form, or metabolically equivalent
analogue.

*"Fetishistic Disorder"* means the disorder defined in DSM-5-TR
(302.81) and the corresponding ICD-11 construct (6D32):
recurrent and intense sexual arousal from either the use of
nonliving objects or a highly specific focus on non-genital body
part(s), as manifested by fantasies, urges, or behaviors, that
has persisted for at least six months and that causes *clinically
significant distress or impairment* in social, occupational, or
other important areas of functioning. The term *expressly
excludes* the use of objects specifically designed for genital
stimulation, *expressly excludes* the use of nonliving objects in
cross-dressing (Transvestic Disorder, DSM-5-TR § 302.3, separately
addressed in a co-pending provisional application directed to
Transvestic Disorder), and *expressly excludes* non-distressing
fetishistic interests in any context. The DSM-5-TR specifiers
"with body part(s)," "with nonliving object(s)," and "with other"
are recognized; the present invention is applicable to any of the
specifiers.

*"Focal class"* means the specific class of nonliving object(s) or
non-genital body part(s) that is the focus of the subject's
fetishistic urges, as identified at intake.

*"Paraphilic urge"* means an experienced internal pressure to
engage in fetishistic behavior toward the focal class that is
associated, in the subject, with the recurrent and intense
sexual arousal characteristic of Fetishistic Disorder.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult subject for the
treatment of Fetishistic Disorder according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in
at least one of: MSI-II Paraphilic Sexual Outlet sub-scale,
focal-class behavioral count, Sexual Distress Scale,
partner-reported relationship-functioning, or investigator-rated
CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

*"Adult subject"* means a human subject of age ≥ 18 years at the
time of consent.

### 2. The two active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin
across multiple central serotonergic targets; by partially
agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, vilazodone is reported to support
a more rapid attenuation of anxious-compulsive symptomatology
than a pure SSRI and may attenuate the SSRI-typical "switch"
agitation in the first weeks of treatment. Vilazodone is FDA-
approved for the treatment of major depressive disorder in adults
at oral doses of 10 mg/day (initial), titrated to 20 mg/day after
one week and to 40 mg/day thereafter, taken once daily with food
to support oral bioavailability. Vilazodone is metabolized
primarily by CYP3A4 and is a substrate (and weak inhibitor) of
CYP2C19 and CYP2D6. Vilazodone is generic in the United States.

#### 2.2 Bicalutamide

Bicalutamide is a non-steroidal competitive antagonist at the
androgen receptor with no agonist activity. By competitively
displacing testosterone and dihydrotestosterone from the AR
ligand-binding domain, bicalutamide attenuates AR-mediated
transcriptional activity in central and peripheral target tissues
*without suppressing endogenous gonadotropin or testosterone
secretion*. Bicalutamide is FDA-approved for the treatment of
advanced prostate carcinoma at oral doses of 50 mg/day (in
combination with a luteinizing-hormone-releasing-hormone analog).
Bicalutamide is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4. Bicalutamide is
generic in the United States. The present invention adopts the
50 mg/day dosing as the starting dose for adult subjects with
Fetishistic Disorder, with optional titration up to 100 mg/day in
non-responders.

The mass ratio of vilazodone (calculated as the free base) to

Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of vilazodone include the

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-
release oral tablet at strengths of 10 mg, 20 mg, and 40 mg
(free-base equivalent), matching the marketed product. The tablet
should be taken with food to support oral bioavailability.

Bicalutamide is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-
release oral tablet at strengths of 25 mg, 50 mg, and 100 mg.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-tablet bottle of
vilazodone tablets at the appropriate dose-titration strength,
(b) a 30-tablet bottle of bicalutamide 50 mg tablets, and (c) a
printed insert with administration timing, titration schedule, an
informed-consent and shared-decision-making module, a
focal-class identification worksheet for use by the prescribing
clinician, and a list of warning signs (rash, hepatic dysfunction,
mood change) requiring clinical re-evaluation.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adults (subjects ≥ 18 years and ≥ 50 kg) with Fetishistic
Disorder, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily with food on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily
  with food thereafter, matching the FDA-approved titration for
  major depressive disorder.
* Bicalutamide 50 mg orally once daily, with no titration. In
  non-responders at 12 weeks, the dose may be increased to
  100 mg/day with monitoring for hepatic adverse effects.

There is no defined pediatric population; the present invention
is limited to adults.

Hepatic adjustment: vilazodone should be reduced or avoided in
moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment per the prescribing
information. Bicalutamide should be used with caution in moderate
hepatic impairment and avoided in severe hepatic impairment;
serum aminotransferases should be measured at baseline, monthly
for the first 4 months, and periodically thereafter.

Renal adjustment: no specific adjustment is required for either
agent in mild-to-moderate renal impairment; in severe renal
impairment, dose review is recommended.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (clarithromycin, ketoconazole,
ritonavir, itraconazole) without halving the vilazodone dose.
Avoid co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inducers.
Co-administration of bicalutamide with warfarin requires INR
monitoring.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult
subjects who meet DSM-5-TR criteria for Fetishistic Disorder
(302.81), including the criterion of clinically significant
distress or impairment, and who voluntarily seek pharmacotherapy
with full informed consent. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: age ≥ 18 years; current diagnosis
of Fetishistic Disorder per DSM-5-TR for at least 6 months,
including identification of the focal class per the DSM-5-TR
specifier ("with body part(s)" or "with nonliving object(s)" or
"with other"); baseline MSI-II Paraphilic Sexual Outlet sub-scale
≥ a clinically meaningful threshold (to be confirmed in a
feasibility cohort); intact hepatic function (ALT and AST ≤ 1.5 ×

sexual partner. Exclusion criteria include known hypersensitivity
to either active, current treatment with a prohibited concomitant
medication (see § 5), active major depressive episode with
suicidal ideation, current substance use disorder other than
nicotine, current diagnosis of Transvestic Disorder where
cross-dressing is the dominant presentation (in which case the
co-pending Transvestic Disorder application applies), and any
focal-class fixation that involves a non-consenting third party
(in which case forensic clinical management supersedes the
present invention).

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Paraphilic urge in Fetishistic Disorder is mediated by (a) a
compulsive-ideation component supported by serotonergic and
frontolimbic circuits, with mechanistic overlap with
obsessive–compulsive and addictive phenotypes; (b) an
androgen-receptor-mediated central and peripheral drive
component, which in published literature responds substantially to
anti-androgen therapy; and (c) a learned and habit-mediated
behavioral component (focal-class conditioning) addressed by
cognitive-behavioral therapy.

Vilazodone, by combining selective serotonin reuptake inhibition
with 5-HT1A partial agonism, addresses (a) by reducing the
intensity and intrusiveness of paraphilic ideation directed to
the focal class and the associated distress, with the empirically
validated antidepressant mechanism of vilazodone in major
depressive disorder providing the written-description support for
serotonergic central effect. Bicalutamide, by competitively
antagonizing the androgen receptor *without castrating* the
subject, addresses (b) in a titratable manner that preserves a
degree of consensual sexual function and avoids the metabolic,
bone-density, cardiovascular, and gonadotropin-suppression burden
of GnRH-agonist therapy. The two axes are non-overlapping at the
receptor level (serotonergic + 5-HT1A vs. androgen receptor) and
converge on the prefrontal–striatal–hypothalamic paraphilic-urge
and compulsive-ideation circuit.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce
greater-than-additive improvement in paraphilic-distress and
focal-class urge measures, with a tolerability profile
substantially better than legacy hormonal anti-androgen
monotherapy.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of
the invention include: SERT binding (³H-citalopram displacement
on rat brain membranes; Ki for vilazodone ≈ 0.5 nM); 5-HT1A
binding (³H-8-OH-DPAT displacement; Ki for vilazodone ≈ 0.2 nM);
5-HT1A functional partial agonism (cAMP / GTPγS in 5-HT1A-CHO
cells); androgen receptor binding (³H-mibolerone or ³H-R1881
displacement on LNCaP or AR-CHO cells; Ki for bicalutamide
≈ 100 nM); androgen receptor functional antagonism
(transactivation in MMTV-luciferase LNCaP reporter line).

Pre-clinical fetishistic behavior is not directly modelable in
animals, but in-vivo paradigms in rodent that probe the underlying
substrates include: marble-burying for compulsive behavior;
novel-object recognition for object-class behavioral
discrimination; testosterone-administration paradigms for
AR-mediated copulatory behavior; and operant
conditioned-place-preference paradigms in which the reinforcer is
a sex-relevant olfactory stimulus. A supra-additive effect of
the combination on (i) marble-burying suppression, (ii) AR-mediated
copulatory paradigm intensity, and (iii) extinction rate of
sex-stimulus conditioned-place-preference, relative to either
monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the principal
in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult male volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titration to 40 mg/day for 7 days; full PK sampling on
days 1 and 7 with steady-state. Period 2 (after 7-day washout):
bicalutamide 50 mg/day for 28 days (to reach steady state given
the long bicalutamide half-life); full PK sampling on days 1, 14,
and 28. Period 3 (after appropriate washout): combination at the
same doses; PK sampling for both agents on days 1 and 14. The
primary endpoint is the geometric mean ratio for AUC₀–τ and Cmax
of vilazodone and of bicalutamide in combination versus
monotherapy, with 90 % confidence intervals contained within
0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult Fetishistic Disorder (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 90 (30 per arm) given the
relative rarity of the indication, with descriptive analyses and
90 % confidence intervals around the principal effect estimate.

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone titrated to 40 mg/day +
  bicalutamide 50 mg/day.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone titrated to 40 mg/day +
  matching placebo for bicalutamide.
* Arm C (placebo): matching placebo for both, with cognitive-
  behavioral therapy as the standard-of-care comparator.

All subjects receive a standardized, manualized cognitive-
behavioral therapy program of equal frequency, intensity, and
duration (weekly 60-minute sessions over 16 weeks) directed to
focal-class urge management. The primary endpoint is change from
baseline in MSI-II Paraphilic Sexual Outlet sub-scale at week 16.
Secondary endpoints include focal-class behavioral count, Sexual
Distress Scale, partner-reported relationship-functioning,
responder rate, and CGI-I. Safety endpoints include hepatic
enzyme monitoring, gynecomastia assessment, sexual function
inventory, and bone-mineral-density DEXA at baseline and week 24.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Fetishistic Disorder in adult subjects with clinically significant
distress or impairment per DSM-5-TR (302.81) comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
SSRI / 5-HT1A partial agonist vilazodone and a therapeutically
effective amount of the non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist
bicalutamide are disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping
receptor classes that converge on the prefrontal–striatal–
hypothalamic paraphilic-urge and compulsive-ideation circuits
implicated in Fetishistic Disorder, are administered in a
non-castrating dose regimen that preserves endogenous gonadotropin
and testosterone secretion, and are expected to produce greater-
than-additive improvement in fetishistic-distress and focal-class
urge measures with substantially better tolerability than legacy
hormonal anti-androgen monotherapy. The invention applies to all
DSM-5-TR specifiers ("with body part(s)," "with nonliving
object(s)," "with other") and excludes cross-dressing as the
dominant presentation (separately addressed in a co-pending
provisional directed to Transvestic Disorder).
