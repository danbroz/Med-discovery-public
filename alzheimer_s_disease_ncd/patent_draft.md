## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Alzheimer's Disease Neurocognitive Disorder
(AD-NCD) by co-administering (a) a histamine H3 receptor antagonist or
inverse agonist and (b) a muscarinic receptor agonist, in particular
the combination of pitolisant and xanomeline, optionally together with
trospium or another peripherally restricted anticholinergic to limit
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects. The invention is directed to
symptomatic improvement in cognition, alertness, and functional
participation in adults with Alzheimer's-type major neurocognitive
disorder and is not intended to assert cure or reversal of underlying
neurodegeneration.

## Background of the invention

Alzheimer's Disease Neurocognitive Disorder is a progressive
neurodegenerative syndrome characterized by decline in episodic
memory, attention, executive function, language, and daily
functioning, with corresponding caregiver burden and loss of
independence. The condition often includes reduced daytime engagement,
slowed processing, fluctuating behavioral availability, apathy, and
progressive functional dependence long before complete loss of
communication or motor ability.

The clinical and commercial burden of Alzheimer's disease is large and
growing. Cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine remain commonly used
for symptomatic support, while anti-amyloid therapies are beginning to
expand disease-stage-specific background treatment in selected
patients. Nevertheless, substantial unmet need remains for a
well-tolerated symptomatic regimen that improves day-to-day cognition,
alertness, and caregiver-observable function. Many patients continue
to experience attention loss, daytime somnolence, slowed task
initiation, and limited benefit from existing cholinergic strategies.

The mechanistic gap addressed by the present invention has two layers.
First, Alzheimer's disease frequently includes reduced cortical arousal
support and impaired behavioral availability for cognitive effort.
Pitolisant increases histaminergic tone through H3 inverse agonism /
antagonism and supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine,
dopamine, and norepinephrine, thereby improving wakefulness,
attentional set maintenance, and daytime engagement. Second,
xanomeline is a centrally active muscarinic agonist with M1/M4-
preferring clinical activity that can strengthen cortical cholinergic
signal quality relevant to encoding, retrieval support, and executive
processing.

The inventors recognized that these two mechanisms are complementary
and non-redundant in AD-NCD. Pitolisant improves the waking state in
which cognitive work occurs, while xanomeline improves the quality of
cortical cholinergic processing during that state. The combination is
therefore expected to produce greater symptomatic benefit in memory-
adjacent function, attention, and caregiver-observable daily
performance than either monotherapy at any individually achievable
dose.

Prior-art searching identified various Alzheimer's treatment
references, dementia-treatment references, muscarinic receptor
activation references, and histamine H3 antagonist references. Those
references do not teach the specific combination of pitolisant and
xanomeline for Alzheimer's Disease Neurocognitive Disorder, nor the
symptom-layered vigilance-support plus cholinergic-processing
framework disclosed herein.

The present invention is supportive and adjunctive. It is not intended
to replace cholinesterase inhibitors, memantine, anti-amyloid therapy
when otherwise appropriate, caregiver structuring, environmental
supports, or management of agitation, delirium, and safety risk by
standard methods.

burden, Clinical Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I), and caregiver

achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Alzheimer's Disease Neurocognitive Disorder"* or *"AD-NCD"* means
major neurocognitive disorder due to Alzheimer's disease or another
clinically recognized Alzheimer's-type neurocognitive syndrome with
dominant Alzheimer's pathology features.

*"Pitolisant"* means the H3 inverse agonist / antagonist and its
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues that
retain clinically relevant H3 activity.

*"Xanomeline"* means xanomeline and pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, and metabolically equivalent
forms that retain clinically relevant muscarinic receptor agonist
activity.

*"Peripheral anticholinergic mitigator"* means trospium chloride or
another antimuscarinic agent with limited central nervous system
penetration used to reduce peripheral cholinergic adverse effects
associated with xanomeline administration.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount that produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in one or more AD-NCD endpoints when
used according to a regimen disclosed herein.

### 2. Active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant increases histaminergic tone and supports wakefulness,
attention, and state-dependent cortical neurotransmitter release. In
the present invention, its principal contribution is improvement in
vigilance, daytime alertness, and behavioral availability for
cognitive effort.

#### 2.2 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is a centrally active muscarinic receptor agonist with
M1/M4-preferring clinical activity. In the present invention, its
principal contribution is enhancement of cortical cholinergic signal
quality relevant to attention, memory support, and executive
processing.

#### 2.3 Optional trospium chloride

Trospium chloride may optionally be administered with xanomeline to
reduce peripheral cholinergic adverse effects such as nausea,
diarrhea, abdominal cramping, sweating, or urinary urgency, while
preserving central cholinergic efficacy due to limited
blood-brain-barrier penetration.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
xanomeline, optionally together with trospium chloride, plus one or
more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients. The actives
may be administered as separate oral dosage forms, as a co-packaged
kit, or as a synchronized blister-card system aligned to morning and
evening dosing.

Suitable excipients include common diluents, binders, disintegrants,
lubricants, capsule-fill agents, coating systems, and release-control
polymers appropriate for oral solid dosage forms. In preferred
embodiments pitolisant is given in the morning and xanomeline in
divided doses to balance central efficacy and tolerability.

### 4. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with AD-NCD, a preferred regimen comprises:

* Pitolisant 4.5 mg to 35.6 mg orally once daily, preferably in the
  morning.
* Xanomeline 50 mg to 250 mg daily, preferably in divided doses.
* Optional trospium chloride 20 mg to 60 mg daily in divided doses.

In certain embodiments pitolisant is titrated first to establish
tolerability and alertness benefit, after which xanomeline is added at
a low dose and increased gradually. In certain embodiments xanomeline
and trospium are co-initiated.

In preferred embodiments any background dementia regimen such as
donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine, memantine, anti-amyloid
antibody therapy, or structured caregiver-supported routine management
is stable at baseline so that change can be attributed to the present
invention rather than to a major concurrent treatment change.

### 5. Subject populations

Preferred subjects are adults with clinician-confirmed Alzheimer's
Disease Neurocognitive Disorder and persistent impairment in memory-
adjacent function, attention, executive efficiency, daytime
engagement, or caregiver-observed daily task completion. Certain
embodiments focus on subjects with apathy, somnolence, slowed task
initiation, or fluctuating behavioral availability.

Exclusion criteria for preferred embodiments include acute delirium,
uncontrolled psychosis or agitation requiring alternate management,
unstable severe orthostatic hypotension, severe gastrointestinal
intolerance to cholinergic therapy, uncontrolled narrow-angle
glaucoma, or inability to participate in monitoring.

### 6. Mechanistic rationale

AD-NCD often combines a cortical cholinergic deficit with reduced
daytime arousal support. A subject who is insufficiently alert may
perform poorly even if cholinergic tone is partly improved, while a
subject with improved wakefulness but no cholinergic support may
remain limited by poor encoding, attention, and cognitive
organization.

Pitolisant addresses the arousal layer through H3 antagonism / inverse
agonism. Xanomeline addresses the cholinergic-processing layer through
central muscarinic agonism. The combination is therefore expected to
produce larger improvements in attention, cognitive participation, and
caregiver-observable daily function than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.

### 7. Administration and monitoring

Monitoring may include cognitive composite testing, bedside attention
or alertness measures, caregiver diary, daily-function scales,
orthostatic vital signs, bowel tolerance, urinary symptoms, CGI-I, and
engagement or apathy ratings. In selected embodiments, concurrent
use with stable cholinesterase-inhibitor or anti-amyloid therapy is
documented and tracked.

The regimen is not intended to replace cholinesterase inhibitors,
memantine, anti-amyloid therapy, caregiver support, environmental
adaptation, safety planning, or management of acute delirium or severe
behavioral disturbance when present.

### 8. Prophetic clinical study

A prophetic Phase 2 study enrolls adults with AD-NCD, baseline
cognitive and functional impairment, and stable background dementia
care. Subjects are randomized to: (a) pitolisant plus xanomeline,
optionally with trospium; (b) pitolisant plus placebo; or
(c) placebo plus standard care, for 16 weeks after a titration phase.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in a cognitive composite
score emphasizing attention, executive function, and day-to-day
functional cognition. Key secondary endpoints include caregiver-rated
daily-function efficiency, alertness, engagement burden, caregiver
burden, and CGI-I. The combination arm is expected to show the largest
improvement in cognition and behavioral availability with acceptable
tolerability. FIGS. 3-5 are prophetic illustrations of those expected
outcomes.

### 9. Prior-art distinction

The closest prior-art references identified in the prior-art
landscape include:

* Alzheimer's and dementia treatment references - disclose different
  Alzheimer's pharmacologies and do not teach the specific
  pitolisant + xanomeline combination for AD-NCD.
* *Methods and compositions for treatment of disorders ameliorated by
  muscarinic receptor activation* - disclose muscarinic activation but
  do not teach co-administration with pitolisant in AD-NCD.
* Histamine H3 receptor antagonist references - disclose H3-axis
  chemistry without pairing it with xanomeline for Alzheimer's-type
  neurocognitive disorder.

The present invention is distinct in the specific receptor classes
combined, the specific actives chosen, and the symptom-layered
strategy of coupling vigilance support with cortical cholinergic
enhancement in AD-NCD.

A 76-year-old subject with AD-NCD, persistent apathy, slowed daytime

subject demonstrates better caregiver-observed participation, improved
alertness during the daytime, and modest improvement in cognitive
testing without unacceptable peripheral cholinergic adverse effects;
CGI-I is rated 2 ("much improved"). PROPHETIC.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Alzheimer's Disease Neurocognitive Disorder comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
histamine H3 receptor inverse agonist / antagonist pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of the muscarinic receptor agonist
xanomeline are disclosed, optionally together with trospium chloride
or another peripherally restricted anticholinergic to reduce
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects. The two agents act on non-
overlapping vigilance and cholinergic-processing pathways relevant to
AD-NCD and are expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement
in cognition, daytime alertness, and caregiver-observed daily
function compared with either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose within the context of stable background dementia care.
