## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Frontotemporal Neurocognitive Disorder (FTD-NCD)
by co-administering (a) a histamine H3 receptor antagonist or
inverse agonist and (b) a muscarinic M1/M4 receptor agonist, in
particular the combination of pitolisant and xanomeline,
optionally further with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic
such as trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse
effects. The invention addresses executive dysfunction, apathy,
social-cognitive impairment, language-associated inefficiency, and
caregiver burden in frontotemporal-spectrum neurodegeneration; it
does not assert disease-modification of tau, TDP-43, FUS, or other
underlying proteinopathic pathology.

## Background of the invention

Frontotemporal Neurocognitive Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR
and ICD-11 as a syndrome of progressive cognitive and behavioral
decline associated with frontotemporal lobar degeneration. Clinical
phenotypes include behavioral-variant frontotemporal dementia as
well as language-led syndromes such as semantic and nonfluent /
agrammatic variants of primary progressive aphasia. The most
prominent impairments often involve executive control, behavioral
regulation, social cognition, initiation, language production or
comprehension, and caregiver burden rather than primarily
amnestic deficits.

The prevalence of FTD-NCD is lower than that of Alzheimer-type
disorders, but the burden is substantial because onset frequently
occurs in midlife, disrupts employment and relationships, and
places intense strain on caregivers. Behavioral disinhibition,
apathy, compulsive behavior, dietary change, loss of empathy,
rigidity, and language failure are common. Current symptomatic
pharmacology is limited, and cholinesterase inhibitors often show
inconsistent or disappointing benefit in FTD populations.

The current standard of care is supportive. It consists primarily
of behavioral management, speech and language therapy where
appropriate, caregiver support, and treatment of comorbid symptoms.
No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for the treatment of FTD-NCD
as a primary indication. A clinically meaningful gap remains for a
regimen that supports executive and arousal function without
depending on disease-modifying claims.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses is the
absence of a defensible, mechanism-based symptomatic regimen for
the mixed executive-behavioral phenotype of FTD-NCD. A single-agent
approach often leaves one dimension untreated: a wake-promoting
intervention may improve activation without restoring cortical
signal quality, while a cholinergic intervention alone may not
adequately address apathy, daytime sluggishness, or task-initiation
failure.

The present invention recognizes that two non-overlapping CNS axes
can be combined to support cognition and daytime functioning in
FTD-NCD. First, the histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic auto-
and heteroreceptor that suppresses histamine release and modulates
acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine transmission. Selective
inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant elevates extracellular
histamine and supports wakefulness, attention, and task-initiation.
The pitolisant axis therefore addresses the *apathy, vigilance, and
daytime activation* substrate of FTD-NCD.

Second, the muscarinic M1 receptor facilitates cortical and
hippocampal cognition, while M4 signaling contributes to behavioral
stabilization. Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist
developed in the 1990s and more recently formulated with the
peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium as the FDA-approved
xanomeline-trospium combination (Cobenfy®) for schizophrenia in
adults. The xanomeline axis therefore addresses the *executive,
social-cognitive, and cortical signal-quality substrate* of
FTD-NCD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an H3 antagonist or inverse agonist
in combination with a muscarinic M1/M4 agonist for FTD-NCD
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance- and initiation-
support (a pitolisant H3 axis) and post-synaptic muscarinic
facilitation of cortical cognition (a xanomeline M1/M4 axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of FTD-NCD.
The combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic
executive, behavioral, and caregiver benefit than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose, without asserting
neurodegeneration arrest or reversal.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, (b) xanomeline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, and (c) optionally a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic agent such as trospium chloride in an amount
effective to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Frontotemporal Neurocognitive Disorder in a human subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline, optionally further
with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) xanomeline or a salt thereof, optionally co-formulated with
trospium, in a second unitary dosage form, and (c) written
instructions for the symptomatic treatment of FTD-NCD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily. The
invention contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about 50 mg to
about 250 mg administered orally as the free base or as the tartrate
salt in two divided doses; when xanomeline is co-administered with
trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is about 40 mg to about
60 mg administered orally in two divided doses.

The technical effect of the combination is improvement, in subjects
with FTD-NCD, in executive function, initiation, social-cognitive
performance, and caregiver burden, greater than would be obtained
with either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse
agonist marketed as Wakix®. The term includes pitolisant free base,
hydrochloride or other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent H3 inverse agonists.

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-
yl]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine, an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist. The term as used herein includes the free base,
the tartrate salt and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Trospium chloride"* or *"trospium"* means the quaternary ammonium
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist that does not
appreciably cross the blood-brain barrier and may be used to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline.

*"Frontotemporal Neurocognitive Disorder"* or *"FTD-NCD"* means the
frontotemporal-spectrum neurocognitive syndrome characterized by
progressive executive, behavioral, social-cognitive, or language-
predominant decline associated with frontotemporal degeneration.

*"Executive / social-function composite"* means a clinically
meaningful burden of impairment in executive control, initiation,
behavioral regulation, social cognition, or language-supported
daily functioning quantified by structured assessment.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of FTD-NCD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
executive / social-function composite, apathy score, caregiver
burden, behavioral-friction index, language-support score, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two or more active agents to a
subject so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted
on the subject during the period in which the pharmacological
effect of the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether
the two agents are administered in the same dosage form, in
separate dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times
within a window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse agonist
that increases histaminergic wake drive and supports state-
dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of pitolisant is
attenuation of apathy, slowed initiation, and vigilance failure.

#### 2.2 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
xanomeline is support of executive control, social-cognitive
processing, and cortical signal quality.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
xanomeline, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of a pitolisant tablet and a xanomeline or xanomeline-trospium
dosage form.

Excipients useful for the pitolisant composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the xanomeline
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and stabilizing excipients appropriate for muscarinic agonist oral
delivery.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
morning administration. Xanomeline is preferably formulated as an
oral immediate-release or modified-release dosage form administered
in divided doses. The preferred commercial-development embodiment
contemplates xanomeline co-administered with trospium to mitigate
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pitolisant tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of xanomeline or
xanomeline-trospium units, and (c) written instructions aligned to
caregiver support, behavioral management, and functional tracking.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with FTD-NCD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pitolisant 4.5 mg orally once daily, titratable to 17.8 mg and
  then to 35.6 mg once daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.
* Xanomeline 50 mg orally twice daily, titratable to 100 mg or
  125 mg twice daily if clinically indicated and tolerated; when
  paired with trospium, trospium is administered at about 20 mg to
  30 mg twice daily.

The regimen is not intended to replace behavioral management of
unsafe conduct, speech-language therapy, or disease-specific family
support, nor does it assert slowing of underlying proteinopathy.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
FTD-NCD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 18 years; frontotemporal-pattern neurocognitive
decline; clinically meaningful executive, behavioral, social-
cognitive, or language-supported functional impairment; and
willingness of caregivers and clinicians to participate in
structured management. Exclusion criteria include unstable acute
encephalopathy, severe cholinergic intolerance, unsafe cardiac
conduction abnormality, and known hypersensitivity to either
active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

FTD-NCD often includes two interacting layers: reduced vigilance /
initiation support and impaired executive or social-cognitive
cortical signal quality.

Pitolisant addresses the vigilance-initiation layer through H3
antagonism / inverse agonism. Xanomeline addresses the executive-
cognition layer through M1/M4 agonism. The two axes are non-
overlapping at the receptor level and converge on improved
behavioral and cognitive function.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in FTD-NCD burden than either monotherapy alone.
the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 28,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding and inverse-agonism assays
for pitolisant; M1 and M4 receptor binding and signaling assays for
xanomeline; and combined executive / social-cognitive network
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include frontotemporal-degeneration models, apathy paradigms,
social-cognition tasks, executive-control tests, and wake-
maintenance paradigms. A supra-additive effect of the combination
on restoration of initiation and executive / social-cognitive
function, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
pitolisant 17.8 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: xanomeline or
xanomeline-trospium for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, orthostatic vital signs,
and tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult FTD-NCD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant + xanomeline.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pitolisant + placebo for xanomeline.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with supportive standard care.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in executive / social-
function composite at week 16. Secondary endpoints include apathy
score, caregiver burden, behavioral-friction index, language-
support score, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of an H3 antagonist or inverse
agonist with a muscarinic M1/M4 agonist for FTD-NCD specifically.
The closest hits are:

* Dementia and muscarinic-activation patents disclose cholinergic
  strategies but not the present arousal-plus-executive-function
  combination.
* Generic H3 antagonist patents disclose wake-promoting agents but
  not the present FTD-specific indication.
* Broader neuropsychiatric patents do not disclose the present
  convergent-circuit rationale in frontotemporal degeneration.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (H3 antagonism /
inverse agonism + M1/M4 agonism), (ii) the specific actives
(pitolisant + xanomeline), and (iii) the specific indication
(FTD-NCD). No prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit
rationale on which the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Frontotemporal Neurocognitive Disorder comprising co-administration
of a therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline are disclosed,
optionally with trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse
effects. The two agents act on non-overlapping arousal-support and
executive / social-cognitive circuits and are expected to produce
greater-than-additive improvement in behavioral and cognitive
function than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.
