## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Language Disorder (LD) by co-administering (a) a
histamine H3 receptor antagonist or inverse agonist and (b) an
oxytocin receptor agonist, in particular the combination of
pitolisant and oxytocin, in dosage forms and on dosing schedules
timed to scheduled speech-language pathology language-intervention
sessions.

## Background of the invention

Language Disorder is a neurodevelopmental communication condition
defined in the *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders,
Fifth Edition, Text Revision* (DSM-5-TR) and in the International
Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) at code 6A01.2.
The disorder is characterized by persistent difficulties in the
acquisition and use of language across modalities (spoken, written,
sign language, or other), due to deficits in comprehension or
production. The deficits include reduced vocabulary (word knowledge
and use), limited sentence structure (the ability to put words and
word endings together to form sentences based on the rules of grammar
and morphology), and impairments in discourse (the ability to use
vocabulary and connect sentences to explain or describe a topic or
series of events or have a conversation). Onset is in the early
developmental period and the deficits are not attributable to hearing
or other sensory impairment, motor dysfunction, or another medical or
neurological condition, and are not better explained by intellectual
developmental disorder or global developmental delay.

The estimated population prevalence of LD is approximately 7 % of
school-age children, and approximately 50–75 % of children with a
preschool diagnosis continue to demonstrate residual language
deficits into adolescence and adulthood. LD is associated with
elevated rates of later reading-comprehension difficulty, written
language disorder, social-communication difficulty, anxiety,
depression, and reduced occupational attainment. Comorbid
Attention-Deficit / Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), Speech Sound
Disorder, specific learning disorder, developmental coordination
disorder, and Social (Pragmatic) Communication Disorder are common.

The current standard of care for LD is non-pharmacological. It
consists primarily of speech-language pathology directed to
expressive and receptive vocabulary, morphosyntax, and narrative
discourse, delivered as individual or small-group sessions, with
adjunctive parent- and teacher-mediated language facilitation. No
pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of LD as a primary
indication. Off-label use of stimulants in subjects with comorbid
ADHD is reported, but stimulants do not target the working-memory or
social-feedback substrates that mediate language acquisition, and
the published controlled evidence of language benefit from these
agents is sparse.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, language acquisition in LD is impaired at the
level of (i) verbal working memory for the on-line maintenance of a
sentence template across phrase production, (ii) sustained attention
to a clinician's modeled exemplar across repeated trials, and
(iii) on-line comparison of the subject's produced utterance to the
target. Each of these subprocesses depends on state-appropriate
cortical arousal and on cholinergic and dopaminergic signaling in
left perisylvian cortex (left inferior frontal gyrus, posterior
superior temporal gyrus, supramarginal gyrus) and dorsal striatum.
The histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic auto- and heteroreceptor
that, when constitutively active, suppresses the release of
histamine and of acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine in
cortex and striatum. Selective inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant
(Wakix®, FDA-approved for excessive daytime sleepiness in adults
with narcolepsy) elevates extracellular histamine and supports
state-dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine, without the
abuse liability of stimulant scheduling. The H3 axis therefore
addresses the *vigilance, working-memory, and self-monitoring
substrate* of language learning, but does not address the
social-motivational substrate that determines whether a child
engages with the clinician's model and attends to corrective
feedback.

Second, oxytocin receptor (OXTR) signaling in the prefrontal cortex,
amygdala, ventral tegmental area, and superior temporal sulcus
modulates social motivation, eye-gaze allocation, the salience of
socially contingent feedback, and the affective valence of
caregiver-mediated practice. Intranasal oxytocin has been
investigated in autism spectrum disorder, Prader–Willi syndrome,
schizophrenia, and post-traumatic stress disorder, with
mechanism-consistent signals on social attention and feedback
processing. No issued patent or published application known to the
present inventors discloses the use of an oxytocin receptor agonist
in combination with an H3 receptor antagonist or inverse agonist for
any indication, and none discloses such a combination for LD
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance- and working-memory-
enabled language practice (an H3 antagonist axis) and socially
scaffolded clinician-modeled practice (an OXTR agonist axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for language consolidation
in LD. The combination is expected to produce a greater language
outcome than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose,
by acting on two non-overlapping CNS axes that converge on the same
task-relevant left perisylvian–striatal–limbic circuit.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) oxytocin or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, analogue, fragment, or
prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Language Disorder in a subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount of oxytocin.
Co-administration may be simultaneous, in a unitary dosage form or
in separate dosage forms, or sequential, with each agent
administered within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising (a)
pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) oxytocin or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form, and
(c) written instructions for the treatment of LD in a pediatric or
adult subject according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, with
the oxytocin actuation explicitly timed to within 30 minutes of a
scheduled language-intervention session.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 8.9 to 17.8 mg/day and a preferred
pediatric dose weight-banded as set forth herein. The invention
contemplates oxytocin in a daily dose of about 8 IU to about 80 IU
administered intranasally in divided doses, with a preferred dose
of 24 IU twice daily timed to practice sessions. The mass ratio of
pitolisant to oxytocin (calculated on the free-base equivalents)
ranges broadly from about 1:200,000 to about 1:5,000, and is
preferably from about 1:50,000 to about 1:20,000; the
dose-equivalent molar relationship and the session-aligned timing
relationship are the inventive features, not the absolute mass
ratio.

The technical effect of the combination is an increase, in subjects
with LD, in language outcomes (Clinical Evaluation of Language
Fundamentals, 5th ed. (CELF-5) Core Language Score; Peabody Picture
Vocabulary Test, 5th ed. (PPVT-5) Standard Score; Expressive
One-Word Picture Vocabulary Test, 4th ed. (EOWPVT-4) Standard
Score; Mean Length of Utterance in morphemes (MLU-m) from a
language sample), that is greater than the sum of the individual
effects of pitolisant and oxytocin administered as monotherapies at
any individually achievable dose. The combination is opioid-free,
stimulant-free, and has no known dependence liability.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the compound 1-{3-[3-(4-chlorophenyl)propoxy]
propyl}piperidine, also known as BF2.649, marketed in the United
States as Wakix® and in the European Union as Wakix®. Pitolisant is
a selective inverse agonist of the human histamine H3 receptor with
a binding affinity (Ki) of approximately 0.16 nM. The term as used
herein includes the free base, the hydrochloride salt and other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Oxytocin"* means the cyclic nonapeptide of sequence
H-Cys-Tyr-Ile-Gln-Asn-Cys-Pro-Leu-Gly-NH₂ with a disulfide bond
between Cys¹ and Cys⁶. The term as used herein includes the free
peptide and its pharmaceutically acceptable acetate or citrate salts,
glycosylated or PEGylated derivatives that retain agonism at the
human OXTR with EC₅₀ within ten-fold of native oxytocin, peptidic
agonists such as carbetocin, and small-molecule OXTR agonists such
as TC OT 39, WAY 267,464, LIT-001, or such other agents that produce
≥ 50 % maximal calcium mobilization at the human OXTR at
concentrations ≤ 1 µM in a standard fluorometric calcium-flux assay.

*"Language Disorder"* or *"LD"* means the disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and in ICD-11 (6A01.2), characterized by persistent
difficulties in the acquisition and use of language across modalities
(spoken, written, sign language, or other) due to deficits in
comprehension or production, with reduced vocabulary, limited
sentence structure, and impaired discourse, with deficits resulting
in functional limitations in effective communication, social
participation, social relationships, academic achievement, or
occupational performance, and which is not attributable to hearing
or other sensory impairment, motor dysfunction, or another medical
or neurological condition.

*"Language outcome"* means a subject's measured performance on at
least one of: CELF-5 Core Language Score; PPVT-5 Standard Score;
EOWPVT-4 Standard Score; Mean Length of Utterance in morphemes
(MLU-m) from a 100-utterance conversational language sample; or a
comparable validated language instrument.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a subject for the treatment of LD
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: CELF-5 Core
Language Score, PPVT-5 Standard Score, EOWPVT-4 Standard Score,
MLU-m, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject so
that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate dosage
forms, at the same time, or at different times within a window of
about 24 hours.

*"Session-aligned dosing"* means administration of intranasal
oxytocin within a window of about 30 minutes prior to or during a
scheduled language-intervention session, with the purpose of
aligning the central oxytocin pharmacodynamic window with the
period of clinician-modeled and socially scaffolded language
practice.

### 2. The two active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a piperidine derivative and a high-affinity, selective
inverse agonist at the human histamine H3 receptor. The H3 receptor
is a presynaptic autoreceptor and heteroreceptor that, when
constitutively active, suppresses the release of histamine from
histaminergic neurons and the release of acetylcholine, dopamine,
and norepinephrine from cortical, striatal, and hippocampal neurons.
Inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant elevates extracellular histamine
and supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine
in left inferior frontal gyrus, posterior superior temporal gyrus,
supramarginal gyrus, and dorsal striatum — circuits implicated in
phonological working memory, lexical retrieval, syntactic
sequencing, and self-monitoring of speech. Pitolisant is approved
by the FDA for the treatment of excessive daytime sleepiness in
adults with narcolepsy, and is approved in the European Union for
excessive daytime sleepiness and cataplexy in adults with
narcolepsy. Pitolisant is metabolized primarily by CYP2D6 and
CYP3A4. The medication is a substrate and weak inhibitor of CYP3A4
and is not a significant inducer of common cytochrome P450 enzymes.
Pitolisant is under U.S. brand-patent protection through the late
2020s; clinical deployment of the present invention will require
coordination with the marketing authorization holder. The present
invention does not require any specific salt, polymorph, or
excipient combination of pitolisant and is not asserted to be a
composition-of-matter invention as to pitolisant.

#### 2.2 Oxytocin

Oxytocin is the endogenous cyclic nonapeptide synthesized in the
paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei of the hypothalamus. Oxytocin
acts at the OXTR, a Gαq-coupled G protein-coupled receptor that
mobilizes intracellular calcium and modulates social cognition,
attachment, trust, eye-gaze allocation, and the recognition of
socially relevant affect. Synthetic oxytocin (Pitocin®, Syntocinon®)
is approved in many jurisdictions for the induction of labor and the
prevention or treatment of postpartum hemorrhage. Investigational
use of intranasal oxytocin in CNS indications is supported by a
substantial peer-reviewed literature. Native oxytocin is freely
available from multiple suppliers and has no live U.S.
composition-of-matter patent restriction; the present invention does
not require any proprietary OXTR agonist. The intranasal route is
preferred to maximize central nervous system exposure relative to
peripheral OXTR engagement.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
oxytocin, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a unitary fixed-dose
combination dosage form (for example, a single oral tablet plus a
co-packaged intranasal device) or a co-packaged kit of two separate
dosage forms.

The mass ratio of pitolisant (calculated as the free base) to
oxytocin (calculated as the unmodified peptide free base) is in the
range of about 1:200,000 to about 1:5,000, preferably about
1:100,000 to about 1:10,000, and most preferably about 1:50,000 to
about 1:20,000, reflecting that the molar potency of oxytocin at
OXTR is several orders of magnitude greater than the per-milligram
potency of pitolisant at H3.

Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of pitolisant include the
hydrochloride, fumarate, succinate, citrate, and tartrate salts.
Pharmaceutically acceptable polymorphic forms include all
crystalline and amorphous forms whose pharmacokinetic profile in
humans is bioequivalent to the marketed pitolisant hydrochloride.
Solvates and hydrates are included. Pharmaceutically acceptable
forms of oxytocin include the free peptide acetate or citrate salt,
the carbetocin analogue, glycosylated and PEGylated derivatives that
retain agonism at OXTR within ten-fold of native, and small-molecule
OXTR agonists as defined in § 1.

Excipients useful in the compositions include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate, mannitol),
binders (povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate, croscarmellose sodium),
lubricants (magnesium stearate, sodium stearyl fumarate), glidants
(colloidal silicon dioxide), film-coating polymers (hypromellose,

preservatives (benzyl alcohol, chlorobutanol), tonicity agents
(sodium chloride, mannitol), and buffers (citrate, phosphate,
acetate).

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-release
oral tablet at strengths of 4.5 mg and 17.8 mg (free-base
equivalent), matching the marketed product. Modified-release oral
tablets at strengths up to 35.6 mg per unit are within the scope of
the invention.

Oxytocin is preferably formulated as a metered-dose intranasal
spray delivering 4 IU per actuation, with permeation enhancement to
maximize central nervous system bioavailability. Sublingual films,
buccal tablets, and depot subcutaneous formulations are within the
scope of the invention. Intravenous and intramuscular dosage forms
are appropriate for inpatient initiation but not for chronic
ambulatory use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-tablet bottle of
pitolisant immediate-release oral tablets at the appropriate
weight-band strength, (b) a 30-day supply of an intranasal oxytocin
spray device, and (c) a printed insert with administration timing
aligned to scheduled language-intervention sessions.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adults (subjects ≥ 18 years and ≥ 50 kg) with persistent LD,
the preferred dosing regimen is pitolisant 8.9 mg once daily for one
week followed by 17.8 mg once daily, taken on awakening, plus
intranasal oxytocin 24 IU twice daily, timed to within 30 minutes
of scheduled speech-language pathology language sessions. Maximum
recommended pitolisant dose is 35.6 mg/day; maximum recommended
oxytocin dose is 80 IU/day in three or four divided actuations.

For pediatric subjects ≥ 6 years and < 18 years (the principal
treatment population), weight-banded dosing is preferred:

| Body weight | Pitolisant (mg/day, oral) | Oxytocin (IU/day, intranasal) |
|---|---|---|
| 20–< 30 kg | 4.5 | 16 (8 IU twice daily) |
| 30–< 50 kg | 8.9 | 24 (12 IU twice daily) |
| ≥ 50 kg | 8.9 → 17.8 (titration) | 24–48 |

Hepatic adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in
subjects with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B) and is
not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C). No
oxytocin dose adjustment is required for hepatic impairment.

Renal adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²). No
oxytocin adjustment is required.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP2D6 inhibitors (paroxetine, fluoxetine, bupropion) without
halving the pitolisant dose. Avoid co-administration with strong
CYP3A4 inducers. No clinically significant medication–medication
interactions are expected between pitolisant and intranasal
oxytocin.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects who
meet DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 criteria for LD. Inclusion criteria for the
preferred treatment population include: age ≥ 6 years and ≤ 18
years; CELF-5 Core Language Score ≤ 78 (≤ 1.5 SD below age mean);
intact peripheral hearing (pure-tone average ≤ 25 dB HL
bilaterally); intelligence quotient (non-verbal) ≥ 80; absence of a
diagnosed neurologic condition that better accounts for the language
impairment (no acquired aphasia, no progressive neurological
disease, no global developmental delay); absence of an active
diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder. Comorbid ADHD, Speech Sound
Disorder, specific learning disorder, developmental coordination
disorder, and Social (Pragmatic) Communication Disorder are not
exclusionary; LD frequently co-occurs with these conditions and the
present invention is expected to address language symptoms in those
subjects without exacerbating the co-occurring condition.
Exclusion criteria include known hypersensitivity to either active,
current treatment with a prohibited concomitant medication
(see § 5), pregnancy or breastfeeding, and inability to comply with
intranasal administration.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Language acquisition in LD is impaired at the level of (a) the
maintenance of phonological and lexical material in verbal working
memory across phrase production, (b) the on-line monitoring of the
subject's own utterance against the clinician's modeled exemplar
and contingent updating of lexical and morphosyntactic selection
based on auditory feedback, and (c) the affective and motivational
scaffolding by which a child engages with the clinician and attends
to corrective feedback. Selective H3 inverse agonism by pitolisant
addresses (a) and (b) by elevating central histamine and supporting
state-dependent acetylcholine and dopamine release in left
perisylvian cortex and dorsal striatum, with documented clinical
effect on psychomotor vigilance and discourse-relevant attention in
narcolepsy populations. OXTR agonism by intranasal oxytocin
addresses (c) by enhancing clinician-directed eye gaze, recognition
of clinician affect, and the salience of socially contingent
feedback, all of which mediate the quality of language practice
during pediatric speech-language intervention. The two axes are
non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the left
perisylvian–striatal–limbic language circuit.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in language outcomes that depend on both
vigilance- and working-memory-enabled language practice and
socially scaffolded clinician-modeled practice.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding (³H-N-α-methylhistamine
displacement on rat cortical membranes; Ki for pitolisant
≈ 1.5 nM); H3 functional inverse agonism (cAMP accumulation in
CHO-K1 cells expressing human H3); OXTR binding (³H-oxytocin
displacement on human OXTR-CHO membranes; Kd for oxytocin ≈ 1 nM);
OXTR functional agonism (calcium mobilization in OXTR-CHO cells;
EC₅₀ for oxytocin ≈ 5–20 nM).

Although LD is a uniquely human condition (no animal model
recapitulates linguistic syntax), in-vivo behavioral paradigms in
rodent and non-human primate that probe the underlying substrates
are appropriate. These include: five-choice serial reaction time
task (5-CSRTT) for sustained attention; delayed-non-match-to-sample
for short-term and working memory; reversal-learning paradigms for
behavioral updating under feedback; ultrasonic vocalization (USV)
emission and partner-USV contingency in juvenile rat dyads as a
proxy for socially contingent vocal output; and three-chamber
social-novelty paradigms for clinician-analog social motivation. A
supra-additive effect of the combination on (i) 5-CSRTT
discrimination accuracy, (ii) delayed-non-match-to-sample accuracy,
and (iii) socially contingent USV emission, relative to either
monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the principal
in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years; equal sex
distribution). Period 1: pitolisant 17.8 mg orally once daily for
7 days; full PK sampling on days 1 and 7. Period 2 (after 7-day
washout): intranasal oxytocin 24 IU twice daily for 7 days; full
PK sampling on days 1 and 7. Period 3 (after 7-day washout):
combination at the same doses for 7 days; PK sampling for both
agents on days 1 and 7. The primary endpoint is the geometric mean
ratio for AUC₀–τ and Cmax of pitolisant in combination versus
monotherapy, with 90 % confidence intervals contained within
0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in pediatric LD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm) with 80 % power to
detect a 6-standard-score-point difference in CELF-5 Core Language
Score between the combination arm and placebo at week 12,
assuming a standard deviation of 12 standard-score points and a
two-sided α of 0.05.

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant 8.9 mg/day oral + intranasal
  oxytocin 24 IU twice daily.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pitolisant 8.9 mg/day oral + matching
  intranasal placebo.
* Arm C (placebo): matching oral placebo + matching intranasal
  placebo.

All subjects receive a standardized, manualized language
intervention of equal frequency, intensity, and duration
(twice-weekly 45-minute sessions over 12 weeks) delivered by a
certified speech-language pathologist and aligned to the oxytocin
actuation window. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in
CELF-5 Core Language Score at week 12. Secondary endpoints include
PPVT-5 Standard Score, EOWPVT-4 Standard Score, MLU-m from a
100-utterance language sample, responder rate (≥ 1 standard-
deviation CELF-5 Core Language Score improvement), and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Language Disorder comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of the histamine H3 receptor inverse agonist
pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount of the oxytocin
receptor agonist oxytocin, in dosage forms and on dosing schedules
timed to scheduled language-intervention sessions, are disclosed.
The two agents act on non-overlapping receptor classes that
converge on the left perisylvian–striatal–limbic language-learning
circuit implicated in Language Disorder, and are expected to
produce greater-than-additive improvement in language outcomes
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
