## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Avoidant Personality Disorder (AvPD) by
co-administering (a) a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse agonist /
antagonist and (b) a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist, in
particular the combination of pimavanserin and perospirone. The
invention is intended for adult subjects with AvPD who are
concurrently receiving structured psychotherapy or supportive
behavioral treatment, and addresses rejection-salience,
interpersonal avoidance, social inhibition, and threat-weighted
self-appraisal.

## Background of the invention

Avoidant Personality Disorder is a personality disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by a pervasive pattern of social inhibition,
feelings of inadequacy, and hypersensitivity to negative
evaluation, beginning by early adulthood and present across
contexts. Characteristic features include avoidance of occupational
or social activities involving significant interpersonal contact
because of fears of criticism, disapproval, or rejection;
unwillingness to get involved with people unless certain of being
liked; restraint in intimate relationships because of fear of
shame; preoccupation with criticism or rejection; inhibition in new
interpersonal situations because of feelings of inadequacy; and
viewing oneself as socially inept, personally unappealing, or
inferior.

The prevalence of AvPD in the general population is modest but the
clinical burden is substantial. The disorder is associated with
chronic social isolation, underemployment, educational
underachievement, low treatment persistence, and high comorbidity
with social anxiety disorder, major depressive disorder, trauma-
spectrum symptoms, and substance use disorders. Although it shares
surface features with social anxiety disorder, the enduring
personality pattern of interpersonal avoidance and defectiveness
expectancy often proves more impairing and less responsive to
symptom-only anxiolysis.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological in the first
instance and consists of psychotherapy, supportive treatment, and
management of comorbid disorders. No pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
for the treatment of AvPD as a primary indication. Where medication
is used, clinicians generally borrow from anxiety and depressive-
spectrum practice, but those approaches are inconsistently
effective and may not directly address the maladaptive salience of
rejection cues and the rigid avoidance response that defines the
disorder.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, AvPD includes pathologically amplified salience
of criticism, shame, and rejection, together with rigid
interpretation of ambiguous social cues as negative. Pimavanserin
(Nuplazid®) is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. Although developed for another
indication, its selective 5-HT2A mechanism provides a way to
attenuate maladaptive social-threat salience and rigid negative
cue weighting without adding direct D2 blockade burden.

Second, avoidant behavior is maintained by dopaminergically
reinforced withdrawal and by action-selection biases that favor
avoidance over exploratory or affiliative behavior. Perospirone is
an atypical antipsychotic approved in Japan for schizophrenia that
acts as a D2 antagonist with additional serotonergic properties.
In the present invention, the D2 axis addresses the
*withdrawal-reinforcement and avoidant action-selection substrate*
of AvPD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist /
antagonist in combination with a D2 antagonist for AvPD
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of rejection-
salience and catastrophic social-cue weighting (a pimavanserin
5-HT2A axis) and attenuation of avoidant reinforcement
(a perospirone D2 axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of AvPD. The combination is expected to
produce greater symptomatic improvement in avoidance severity and
social functioning than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pimavanserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) perospirone or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Avoidant Personality Disorder in an adult subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of pimavanserin and a therapeutically effective
amount of perospirone. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) perospirone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of AvPD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with psychotherapy
or supportive treatment.

The invention contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose of about
17 mg to about 34 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 34 mg/day. The invention contemplates
perospirone in a daily dose of about 4 mg to about 48 mg
administered orally in one or two divided doses, with a preferred
dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in adult
subjects with AvPD, in social-avoidance severity, rejection
salience, interpersonal inhibition, and partner / caregiver burden,
that is greater than the sum of the individual effects of
pimavanserin and perospirone administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, tartrate or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically
equivalent 5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Avoidant Personality Disorder"* or *"AvPD"* means the personality
disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by a pervasive pattern of
social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy, and hypersensitivity to
negative evaluation.

*"Social-avoidance / rejection-sensitivity score"* means a
clinically meaningful burden of social inhibition, rejection
sensitivity, shame-based avoidance, or interpersonal fear
quantified by structured clinician assessment or validated
personality / symptom instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult subject for the
treatment of AvPD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
social-avoidance / rejection-sensitivity score, interpersonal-
function score, social-risk-taking measure, quality of life, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. It has no clinically meaningful
affinity for dopamine D2 receptors. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of pimavanserin is attenuation
of maladaptive social-threat salience and rigid rejection
expectancy.

#### 2.2 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is attenuation of withdrawal-maintaining avoidant
reinforcement and action-selection bias toward social retreat.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pimavanserin and
perospirone, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as a
pimavanserin capsule and a perospirone tablet.

Excipients useful for the pimavanserin composition include capsule
fill excipients, diluents, disintegrants, and stabilizers suitable
for the marketed pimavanserin formulation profile. Excipients useful
for the perospirone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone), lubricants (magnesium stearate), and coating
polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or tablet
suitable for once-daily dosing. Perospirone is preferably
formulated as an immediate-release oral tablet.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pimavanserin capsules or tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to
psychotherapy and symptom monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with AvPD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pimavanserin 34 mg orally once daily.
* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily for 7 days, then 8 mg twice
  daily thereafter, with titration up to 12 mg twice daily if
  clinically needed.

Broad genus ranges include pimavanserin 17–34 mg/day and
perospirone 4–48 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses once-daily
pimavanserin with one or two divided perospirone doses.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: severe hepatic or renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers without review of pimavanserin
exposure. Corrected QT interval monitoring should be considered
when other QT-prolonging medications are used.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with AvPD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age ≥ 18 years; clinician-confirmed AvPD;
clinically meaningful avoidance, rejection sensitivity, or social
inhibition at baseline; and capacity to participate in
psychotherapy or supportive behavioral treatment. Exclusion
criteria include schizophrenia, active psychotic disorder, active
mania, uncontrolled substance use disorder, severe hepatic or renal
impairment, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

AvPD is characterized by two interacting layers: maladaptive
rejection salience and reinforcement of avoidant behavior. These
layers produce chronic social withdrawal, shame, and low treatment
engagement.

Pimavanserin addresses the salience / rejection-expectancy layer
through 5-HT2A inverse agonism. Perospirone addresses the avoidant
reinforcement / action-selection layer through D2 antagonism. The
two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on
the social-avoidance phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in avoidance severity and greater improvement in
social participation than either monotherapy alone. The taxonomy-
receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 32,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A binding and inverse-agonism assays for
pimavanserin; D2 receptor binding and functional antagonism assays
for perospirone; and combined social-threat / salience network
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include social-avoidance tasks, rejection-like social-defeat
paradigms, risk-taking / approach-avoidance conflict tests, and
social salience attribution paradigms. A supra-additive effect of
the combination on reduction of social avoidance and maladaptive
threat weighting, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
pimavanserin 34 mg once daily for 7 days; Period 2: perospirone
titrated to 16 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult AvPD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pimavanserin + perospirone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pimavanserin + placebo for
  perospirone.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with structured psychotherapy.

All subjects receive standardized psychotherapy or supportive
behavioral treatment focused on exposure, rejection tolerance, and
social participation. The primary endpoint is change from baseline
in social-avoidance / rejection-sensitivity score at week 12.
Secondary endpoints include interpersonal-function score,
social-risk-taking measure, quality of life, and CGI-I.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in

(pimavanserin + perospirone), and (iii) the specific indication
(Avoidant Personality Disorder). No prior-art disclosure suggests
the convergent-circuit rationale on which the present invention is
based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Avoidant Personality Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the selective 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist pimavanserin and a therapeutically effective
amount of the D2 antagonist perospirone are disclosed. The two
agents act on non-overlapping rejection-salience and avoidance-
reinforcement circuits implicated in persistent social inhibition
and are expected to produce greater-than-additive symptomatic
improvement than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.
