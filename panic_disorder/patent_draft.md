## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Panic Disorder by co-administering
(a) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with 5-HT1A partial agonist
activity, in particular vilazodone, and (b) a beta-adrenoceptor
antagonist, in particular betaxolol. The invention is intended for
subjects with recurrent unexpected panic attacks, anticipatory
anxiety, and fear of future attacks, and addresses panic frequency,
interoceptive alarm, autonomic surge, and avoidance behavior while
continuing psychotherapy where indicated.

## Background of the invention

Panic Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as an anxiety
disorder characterized by recurrent unexpected panic attacks and
persistent concern or maladaptive behavioral change related to the
attacks. Panic episodes may include palpitations, sweating,
trembling, shortness of breath, chest discomfort, dizziness,
paresthesias, derealization, and fear of losing control or dying.

The prevalence of Panic Disorder is clinically meaningful and the
burden is substantial. The disorder is associated with anticipatory
anxiety, emergency-care utilization, agoraphobic avoidance, work
impairment, reduced independence, sleep disruption, and high
comorbidity with depression and substance use. Many subjects become
caught in a self-reinforcing cycle in which interoceptive cues such
as tachycardia are misread as evidence that another attack is
starting.

The current standard of care is psychotherapeutic and serotonergic.
It consists primarily of cognitive-behavioral therapy, interoceptive
exposure, SSRIs or related antidepressants, and in selected cases
benzodiazepines. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for a
combination strategy that simultaneously reduces panic-threat
cognition and beta-adrenergic body-signal amplification. A
clinically meaningful gap remains for a mechanism-based regimen
targeting both the cognitive and physiologic layers of panic.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, Panic Disorder includes anticipatory anxiety,
catastrophic interpretation of bodily sensations, and serotonergic
vulnerability to recurrent panic. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an
orally available antidepressant that combines selective serotonin
reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor.
This dual mechanism supports attenuation of anxious distress and
panic expectancy. The vilazodone axis therefore addresses the
*panic-expectancy and anxious-serotonergic* substrate of Panic
Disorder.

Second, Panic Disorder also includes intense sympathetic
overactivation with tachycardia, tremor, sweating, and chest
tightness that reinforce the subjective sense of danger. Betaxolol
is a beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist approved for
hypertension and glaucoma that reduces heart rate and adrenergic
surge. Relative to nonselective beta blockers, the beta1-selective
profile may offer a more targeted autonomic intervention where
bronchospasm risk or broad nonselective blockade is undesirable.
The betaxolol axis therefore addresses the *autonomic-interoceptive
alarm substrate* of Panic Disorder.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist in combination with a beta1-selective
antagonist for Panic Disorder specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of panic
expectancy (a vilazodone axis) and attenuation of sympathetic body-
signal amplification (a betaxolol axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for treatment of Panic Disorder. The
combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement
in panic frequency, anticipatory anxiety, and interoceptive alarm
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) betaxolol or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, or prodrug thereof,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Panic Disorder in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective amount of betaxolol.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) betaxolol or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Panic Disorder
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with
panic-focused psychotherapy or interoceptive exposure.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with
a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from
10 mg/day. The invention contemplates betaxolol in a daily dose of
about 2.5 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once or twice
daily, with a preferred dose of 5 mg to 20 mg/day. The inventive
feature is the concurrent engagement of serotonergic panic-
expectancy reduction and beta1-selective autonomic stabilization.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with Panic Disorder, in panic-attack frequency, anticipatory
anxiety, autonomic-distress intensity, and functional avoidance,
that is greater than the sum of the individual effects of
vilazodone and betaxolol administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Betaxolol"* means the beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor
antagonist approved in oral and ophthalmic formulations. The term
includes betaxolol free base, hydrochloride or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
and prodrugs.

*"Panic Disorder"* means the disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and
ICD-11 by recurrent unexpected panic attacks and persistent concern
or maladaptive behavioral change related to the attacks.

*"Panic-frequency score"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
recurrent panic attacks, anticipatory anxiety, or panic-related
functional change quantified by structured clinician assessment or
validated panic instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of Panic Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
panic-frequency score, anticipatory-anxiety score, interoceptive
alarm score, avoidance burden, responder rate, or investigator-
rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
panic expectancy and anxious distress.

#### 2.2 Betaxolol

Betaxolol is a beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist with
oral formulations suitable for autonomic stabilization. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
betaxolol is damping of tachycardia and interoceptive adrenergic
alarm that reinforce panic.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
betaxolol, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of a vilazodone tablet and a betaxolol tablet.

Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers. Excipients useful for the betaxolol
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and coating polymers suitable for oral tablet manufacture.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an oral tablet taken with
food. Betaxolol is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable
for once-daily or twice-daily use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of betaxolol tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to panic tracking, interoceptive
exposure, and symptom monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with Panic Disorder, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.
* Betaxolol 5 mg orally once daily, titratable to 10 mg and then to
  20 mg daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.

The regimen is not intended to replace panic-focused psychotherapy
or interoceptive exposure. It is also not intended to replace acute
medical evaluation of chest pain or other symptoms when true medical
emergencies cannot be excluded.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
Panic Disorder. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age >= 12 years; recurrent unexpected panic
attacks; anticipatory anxiety or panic-linked avoidance; and
willingness to engage in behavioral treatment. Exclusion criteria
include untreated severe cardiac conduction abnormality, severe
hepatic disease, psychotic disorders driving panic-like symptoms,
and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Panic Disorder often includes two interacting layers: catastrophic
panic expectancy and sympathetic body-signal amplification.

Vilazodone addresses the panic-expectancy layer through SERT
inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Betaxolol addresses the
autonomic-interoceptive layer through beta1-selective antagonism.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on reduction of panic frequency and severity.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; beta1-adrenoceptor binding and antagonism assays for
betaxolol; and combined interoceptive-threat modulation assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include panic-vulnerability paradigms, interoceptive challenge
models, autonomic-escalation tasks, and threat-learning assays. A
supra-additive effect of the combination on reduction of panic-like
reactivity, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in Section 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: betaxolol
10 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full
PK sampling, ECG monitoring, orthostatic vital signs, and
tolerability assessments are obtained.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult Panic Disorder (FIG. 4).
Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + betaxolol.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for betaxolol.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with panic-focused
  psychotherapy.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in panic-frequency
score at week 12. Secondary endpoints include anticipatory-anxiety
score, interoceptive alarm score, avoidance burden, responder rate,
and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Panic
Disorder comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective
amount of betaxolol are disclosed. The two agents act on non-
overlapping panic-expectancy and autonomic-alarm circuits and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in panic
frequency, anticipatory anxiety, and interoceptive distress than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
