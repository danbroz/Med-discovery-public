## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Sleep Related Hypoventilation (SRH) by
co-administering (a) an orexin receptor antagonist, in particular
suvorexant, and (b) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with
5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine. The
invention is intended for carefully selected adult subjects with
stable sleep-related hypoventilation who remain on standard
ventilatory or respiratory-support management and who experience
sleep-fragmentation, circadian dysregulation, or maladaptive
arousal patterns that worsen adherence or quality of sleep. The
invention is not intended to replace ventilatory support or to
treat unstable hypercapnic respiratory failure.

## Background of the invention

Sleep Related Hypoventilation comprises sleep-associated alveolar
hypoventilation leading to increased carbon dioxide levels during
sleep and may arise from obesity hypoventilation syndrome,
neuromuscular disease, chest-wall disorders, central hypoventilation
syndromes, medication-related respiratory suppression, or other
recognized causes. The syndrome is associated with poor sleep
quality, frequent arousals, non-restorative sleep, daytime fatigue,
morning headache, cardiovascular burden, and reduced adherence to
positive airway pressure or other nocturnal ventilatory support.

The prevalence of clinically meaningful SRH is lower than that of
obstructive sleep apnea, but the medical burden is substantial.
Unlike simple insomnia, the sleep complaint exists in a setting of
physiologic respiratory vulnerability. Management therefore centers
on ventilatory support, optimization of the underlying medical
cause, and avoidance of respiratory depressants. No pharmaceutical
product is approved by the United States Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) specifically for the treatment of SRH as a
primary indication.

The current standard of care is supportive and respiratory-
directed. It consists primarily of positive airway pressure or
non-invasive ventilation, treatment of the underlying respiratory
or neuromuscular condition, avoidance of respiratory depressants,
and optimization of sleep timing and adherence. However, many
subjects continue to experience sleep-fragmentation, difficulty
initiating sleep with ventilatory equipment, circadian
misalignment, and hyperarousal that undermine treatment adherence.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses is not a
failure of ventilation-support technology itself, but the lack of a
carefully selected pharmacologic regimen that improves sleep
initiation, circadian entrainment, and nocturnal arousal regulation
without being framed as a primary respiratory treatment.

First, orexin signaling contributes to wake-maintenance, nocturnal
arousal, and sleep fragmentation. Suvorexant is a dual orexin
receptor antagonist approved for insomnia that facilitates sleep
onset and maintenance by reducing orexin-driven wakefulness.
Formal respiratory-safety studies have been conducted in selected
sleep-disordered-breathing and chronic respiratory populations,
indicating that the main clinical question is not whether the
mechanism exists, but in which stable and monitored subpopulation
it can be used safely. In the present invention, the suvorexant
axis addresses the *maladaptive nocturnal arousal and sleep-
initiation failure* substrate of SRH in carefully selected,
respiratorily stable subjects.

Second, SRH is frequently accompanied by circadian disorganization,
fragmented sleep timing, and poor alignment between the desired
sleep window and the onset of restorative sleep. Agomelatine is a
melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent agonist at MT1
and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors. This
combination supports circadian resynchronization, improves
subjective sleep quality, and can reduce hyperarousal without the
direct respiratory-depressant framing associated with sedative-
hypnotics. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses the
*circadian-disorganization and sleep-quality substrate* of SRH.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an orexin receptor antagonist in
combination with a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist with 5-HT2C
antagonism for SRH specifically in selected, ventilatorily stable
subjects receiving standard respiratory management.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of orexin-driven
sleep-fragmenting wakefulness (a suvorexant axis) and restoration
of circadian alignment (an agomelatine axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for improving sleep continuity and treatment
adherence in SRH. The combination is expected to produce greater
improvement in nocturnal sleep continuity and adherence-supporting
sleep quality than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose, without asserting replacement of ventilatory
support or direct correction of the underlying respiratory defect.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) suvorexant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) agomelatine or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, co-crystal,
or prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Sleep Related Hypoventilation in a selected adult subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of suvorexant and a
therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine, while the subject
continues standard ventilatory or respiratory-support treatment.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) suvorexant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of SRH according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with continued
ventilatory support and respiratory monitoring.

The invention contemplates suvorexant in a daily dose of about
5 mg to about 20 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a
preferred adult dose of 10 mg to 20 mg nightly. The invention
contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about 25 mg to about
50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a preferred dose of
25 mg nightly and optional escalation to 50 mg nightly after about
2 weeks if clinically indicated. The inventive feature is the
concurrent engagement of orexin-antagonist sleep-initiation support
and melatonergic circadian resynchronization in a stable,
monitored SRH population.

The technical effect of the combination is improvement, in selected
subjects with SRH, in sleep continuity, subjective sleep quality,
sleep-onset latency with ventilatory support in place, and
respiratory-treatment adherence, that is greater than the sum of
the individual effects of suvorexant and agomelatine administered
as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Suvorexant"* means the dual orexin receptor antagonist approved
for insomnia. The term includes suvorexant free base, salts,
polymorphs, solvates, hydrates, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent orexin receptor antagonists
with a clinically comparable sleep-promoting profile.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Sleep Related Hypoventilation"* or *"SRH"* means a sleep-related
breathing disorder characterized by alveolar hypoventilation during
sleep, with elevated nocturnal carbon dioxide levels attributable
to recognized causes such as obesity hypoventilation syndrome,
neuromuscular disease, chest-wall disorders, central
hypoventilation, or medication-related respiratory suppression.

*"Stable SRH subject"* means a subject whose underlying
hypoventilation syndrome is medically characterized, whose
ventilatory support is in place or prescribed as indicated, and who
does not have unstable hypercapnic respiratory failure or other
acute respiratory decompensation.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a stable adult subject for the
treatment of SRH according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
sleep-continuity score, ventilatory-support adherence,
sleep-onset latency, daytime fatigue, quality of life, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Suvorexant

Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia. By reducing orexin-mediated wake drive, it facilitates
sleep onset and maintenance. In the present invention, suvorexant
is used only in selected, stable SRH subjects under close
monitoring and as an adjunct to ongoing respiratory support rather
than as a stand-alone respiratory treatment.

#### 2.2 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant acting as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and a neutral antagonist at
5-HT2C receptors. This profile supports circadian resynchronization
and subjective sleep-quality improvement. In the present invention,
the principal mechanistic contribution of agomelatine is
restoration of stable sleep timing and reduction of circadian
misalignment that undermines respiratory-treatment adherence.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise suvorexant and
agomelatine, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a suvorexant tablet and an
agomelatine tablet.

Excipients useful for the suvorexant composition include tablet
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and film-coating polymers
appropriate for low-dose bedtime administration. Excipients useful
for the agomelatine composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Suvorexant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime dosing. Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral
tablet suitable for bedtime administration.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
suvorexant tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of agomelatine tablets,
and (c) written instructions aligned to continued ventilatory
support and symptom monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For selected stable adult subjects with SRH, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Suvorexant 5 mg orally at bedtime for about 3 nights, titratable
  to 10 mg and then to 20 mg nightly if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.
* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days,
  titratable to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.

The preferred embodiment requires that the subject continue
positive airway pressure, non-invasive ventilation, or other
standard respiratory support as indicated. The regimen is not
preferred for unstable hypercapnic respiratory failure or for
subjects in whom even mild additional sleep-drive manipulation is
unsafe.

Hepatic adjustment: clinically meaningful hepatic impairment is
exclusionary or requires careful monitoring because agomelatine is
associated with hepatotoxicity risk in susceptible patients.
Severe respiratory instability, severe hypercapnia, or inability to
maintain prescribed ventilatory support is exclusionary.

Medication–medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors. Suvorexant should be used with
caution alongside other CNS depressants. The regimen is not
intended to replace ventilatory support or to treat acute
respiratory decompensation.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to stable adult
subjects with SRH. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age ≥ 18 years; clinician-confirmed SRH;
stable respiratory-management plan (e.g., PAP or non-invasive
ventilation) in place; clinically meaningful sleep-fragmentation or
sleep-initiation difficulty interfering with adherence or quality of
sleep; and ability to participate in monitoring. Exclusion criteria
include unstable hypercapnic respiratory failure, severe
decompensated pulmonary disease, inability to use prescribed
ventilatory support, severe hepatic disease, and known
hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

SRH in appropriately selected subjects often includes two
clinically linked but distinct problems: maladaptive nocturnal
arousal that interferes with sleep initiation / continuity, and
circadian misalignment that worsens adherence to supportive
ventilatory therapy.

Suvorexant addresses the first layer by attenuating orexin-driven
wake maintenance. Agomelatine addresses the second by improving
circadian entrainment and subjective sleep timing. The two axes are
non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the
adherence-relevant sleep phenotype rather than on ventilation per
se.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in sleep continuity and treatment adherence
this combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: orexin receptor binding and antagonism assays
for suvorexant; MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays for
agomelatine; and combined sleep / circadian network assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include sleep-fragmentation models, circadian-shift paradigms, and
respiratory-monitoring paradigms in stable respiratory-challenge
models. Because respiratory safety is central to the indication,
the preclinical program should explicitly compare stable monitored
respiratory settings against decompensated settings and exclude the
latter from the preferred clinical embodiment.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
suvorexant 10 mg nightly for 3 days; Period 2: agomelatine 25 mg
nightly for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, liver-function monitoring, nocturnal oximetry, capnography,
and tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in stable adult SRH (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 90 (30 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): suvorexant + agomelatine.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): suvorexant + placebo for agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard respiratory
  management.

All subjects continue prescribed ventilatory support. The primary
endpoint is change from baseline in sleep-continuity score at week
6. Secondary endpoints include adherence to ventilatory support,
sleep-onset latency, daytime fatigue, quality of life, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the adjunctive
treatment of Sleep Related Hypoventilation in stable adult subjects
receiving ventilatory support comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the orexin antagonist
suvorexant and a therapeutically effective amount of the
melatonergic antidepressant agomelatine are disclosed. The two
agents act on non-overlapping arousal and circadian-entrainment
circuits implicated in sleep fragmentation and poor respiratory-
treatment adherence, and are expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in sleep continuity and treatment tolerance
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
