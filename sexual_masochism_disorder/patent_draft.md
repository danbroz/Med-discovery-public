## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Sexual Masochism Disorder by co-administering
(a) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor that is also a 5-HT1A receptor
partial agonist and (b) a non-steroidal androgen receptor
antagonist, in particular the combination of vilazodone and
bicalutamide. The invention is intended only for adults with a
current diagnosis of Sexual Masochism Disorder per the *Diagnostic
and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text
Revision* (DSM-5-TR), in whom masochistic urges or behaviors are
accompanied by clinically significant distress or impairment or by
clinically meaningful self-endangering behavior, and who
voluntarily seek pharmacotherapy within a structured psychiatric
treatment setting. The invention is not intended to pathologize
consensual, non-distressing sexual interests or BDSM practices in
the absence of disorder-level distress, impairment, or safety risk.

## Background of the invention

Sexual Masochism Disorder is defined in DSM-5-TR as recurrent and
intense sexual arousal from the act of being humiliated, beaten,
bound, or otherwise made to suffer, as manifested by fantasies,
urges, or behaviors, that has persisted for at least six months and
causes clinically significant distress or impairment in social,
occupational, or other important areas of functioning. DSM-5-TR
also recognizes the clinically important specifier "with
asphyxiophilia," in which the subject is aroused by oxygen
deprivation. The diagnosis is limited to subjects who are at least
18 years of age. The corresponding International Classification of
Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) construct similarly requires
persistence of the arousal pattern together with distress,
impairment, or clinically meaningful safety risk. The present
invention is limited to adult subjects who meet the DSM-5-TR
diagnostic construct and who receive treatment in a clinical
setting with safety planning.

The estimated population prevalence of Sexual Masochism Disorder,
as distinct from consensual and non-distressing masochistic sexual
interests, is substantially below 1 % in community study. The
condition is likely under-reported because of shame, avoidance of
clinical contact, and overlap with consensual BDSM communities in
which disorder-level distress is absent. Sexual Masochism Disorder
is associated with substantial subjective distress, relationship
disruption, depressive and anxious symptoms, and, in the
asphyxiophilia subset, material risk of serious injury or death.
Comorbid major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder,
obsessive–compulsive disorder, trauma-related disorders, and
substance use disorders may co-occur.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological in the first
instance and consists of cognitive-behavioral therapy, relapse-
prevention planning, distress-tolerance work, safety planning, and,
where indicated, harm-reduction counseling. No pharmaceutical
product is approved by the United States Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) for the treatment of Sexual Masochism
Disorder as a primary indication. Off-label pharmacotherapy
reported in the medical literature for paraphilic disorders
generally has used (i) selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors
(SSRIs) for compulsive sexual ideation, distress, and urge
attenuation; and (ii) hormonal anti-androgen agents
(medroxyprogesterone acetate, cyproterone acetate, leuprolide,
triptorelin) for marked reduction of sexual urge intensity in the
most severe and treatment-resistant cases. The published controlled
evidence base in Sexual Masochism Disorder specifically is sparse
and is largely extrapolated from the broader paraphilic-disorders
literature.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, the compulsive- and intrusive-ideation
component of masochistic urge is mediated by serotonergic and
frontolimbic circuits, with mechanistic overlap with obsessive–
compulsive and addictive phenotypes. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an
orally available, FDA-approved antidepressant that combines
selective serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the
5-HT1A receptor. The dual mechanism is reported to support a more
rapid attenuation of anxious-compulsive symptomatology than a pure
SSRI. The serotonergic axis therefore addresses the *intrusive
ideation, compulsivity, and distress* substrate of Sexual
Masochism Disorder, but does not directly attenuate androgen-
receptor-mediated sexual drive.

Second, the androgen-mediated component of masochistic sexual urge
intensity is mediated by androgen receptor signaling in the central
and peripheral nervous systems. Hormonal anti-androgen agents
substantially reduce circulating testosterone and can produce
significant reductions in paraphilic urge in the published
literature, but their adverse-effect burden (decreased bone mineral
density, hot flashes, gynecomastia, mood disturbance, hepatic
effects, cardiovascular effects, and complete loss of any
consensual sexual function) has limited their acceptability
outside the most severe forms of paraphilic disorder. The non-
steroidal androgen receptor antagonist bicalutamide (Casodex®) is
FDA-approved for prostate carcinoma and competes with endogenous
testosterone and dihydrotestosterone at the androgen receptor
*without* lowering circulating gonadotropin or testosterone.
Bicalutamide therefore offers a *partial* and *titratable*
attenuation of androgen-receptor-mediated drive without the
total-hypogonadism profile of GnRH agonist therapy.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a 5-HT1A-partial-agonist SSRI in
combination with a non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist for
any paraphilic indication, and none discloses such a combination
for Sexual Masochism Disorder specifically.

The present invention recognizes that serotonergic attenuation of
the compulsive / distress component (a vilazodone HTR1A axis) and
non-castrating titratable reduction of androgen-receptor-mediated
urge (a bicalutamide AR axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of Sexual Masochism Disorder in adults
with clinically significant distress, impairment, or self-
endangering behavior. The combination is expected to produce a
greater reduction in masochistic-distress and masochistic-urge
measures than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose, with a substantially better tolerability profile than legacy
hormonal anti-androgen regimens.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) bicalutamide or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Sexual Masochism Disorder in an adult subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective
amount of bicalutamide. Co-administration may be simultaneous, in a
unitary dosage form or in separate dosage forms, or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising (a)
vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) bicalutamide or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Sexual
Masochism Disorder in an adult subject according to a dosing
regimen disclosed herein, together with a written informed-consent
and shared-decision-making module that addresses safety planning,
fertility implications of bicalutamide, and the option of
discontinuation.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally with food once daily, with
a preferred adult dose of 20 to 40 mg/day after a 7-day titration
from 10 mg/day. The invention contemplates bicalutamide in a daily
dose of about 25 mg to about 150 mg administered orally once daily,
with a preferred adult dose of 50 mg/day. The mass ratio of
vilazodone to bicalutamide (calculated on the free-base
equivalents) ranges broadly from about 1:15 to about 4:1, and is
preferably from about 1:5 to about 1:1.

The technical effect of the combination is (a) a reduction, in
adults with Sexual Masochism Disorder, in the frequency and
intensity of masochistic urge, as measured by the Multiphasic Sex
Inventory-II (MSI-II) Paraphilic Sexual Outlet sub-scale and a
masochistic-urge frequency diary; (b) a reduction in disorder-
specific distress as measured by the Sexual Distress Scale; and
(c) a reduction in high-risk self-endangering sexual behaviors,
each greater than the sum of the individual effects of vilazodone
and bicalutamide administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

Bicalutamide is a non-steroidal competitive androgen receptor

pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph,

*"Sexual Masochism Disorder"* means the disorder defined in DSM-5-TR:
recurrent and intense sexual arousal from the act of being

subjects who are at least 18 years of age.

*"Masochistic sexual urge"* means an experienced internal pressure
to engage in masochistic fantasy or behavior that is associated, in

*"High-risk self-endangering behavior"* means a behavior associated
with the subject's Sexual Masochism Disorder that creates a

specifier "with asphyxiophilia" and other clinician-documented

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult subject for the

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject

agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate

window of about 24 hours.

*"Adult subject"* means a human subject of age ≥ 18 years at the
time of consent.

### 2. The two active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin
across multiple central serotonergic targets; by partially
agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, vilazodone is reported to support a
more rapid attenuation of anxious-compulsive symptomatology than a
pure SSRI. Vilazodone is FDA-approved for the treatment of major
depressive disorder in adults at oral doses of 10 mg/day
(initial), titrated to 20 mg/day after one week and to 40 mg/day
thereafter, taken once daily with food to support oral
bioavailability. Vilazodone is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4 and
is a substrate (and weak inhibitor) of CYP2C19 and CYP2D6.
Vilazodone is generic in the United States.

#### 2.2 Bicalutamide

Bicalutamide is a non-steroidal competitive antagonist at the
androgen receptor with no agonist activity. By competitively
displacing testosterone and dihydrotestosterone from the AR
ligand-binding domain, bicalutamide attenuates AR-mediated
transcriptional activity in central and peripheral target tissues
*without suppressing endogenous gonadotropin or testosterone
secretion*. Bicalutamide is FDA-approved for the treatment of
advanced prostate carcinoma at oral doses of 50 mg/day. Bicalutamide
is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4. Bicalutamide is generic in the
United States. The present invention adopts the 50 mg/day dosing as
the starting dose for adult subjects with Sexual Masochism
Disorder, with optional titration up to 100 mg/day in non-
responders.

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-

Bicalutamide is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-tablet bottle of

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adults (subjects ≥ 18 years and ≥ 50 kg) with Sexual
Masochism Disorder, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily with food on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily
  with food thereafter.
* Bicalutamide 50 mg orally once daily, with no titration. In
  non-responders at 12 weeks, the dose may be increased to
  100 mg/day with monitoring for hepatic adverse effects.

There is no defined pediatric population; the present invention
is limited to adults.

Hepatic adjustment: vilazodone should be reduced or avoided in
moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment per the prescribing
information. Bicalutamide should be used with caution in moderate
hepatic impairment and avoided in severe hepatic impairment; serum
aminotransferases should be measured at baseline, monthly for the
first 4 months, and periodically thereafter.

Renal adjustment: no specific adjustment is required for either
agent in mild-to-moderate renal impairment; in severe renal
impairment, dose review is recommended.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (clarithromycin, ketoconazole,
ritonavir, itraconazole) without halving the vilazodone dose.
Avoid co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inducers.
Co-administration of bicalutamide with warfarin requires INR
monitoring.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Sexual Masochism Disorder is mediated by (a) a compulsive-ideation
component supported by serotonergic and frontolimbic circuits,
with mechanistic overlap with obsessive–compulsive and addictive
phenotypes; (b) an androgen-receptor-mediated sexual-drive
component; and (c) in a clinically important subset, a high-risk
self-endangering behavioral component. Structured psychotherapy
primarily addresses cognitive distortions, distress tolerance, and
safety planning, but often leaves the biologic urge substrate
insufficiently attenuated.

Vilazodone, by combining selective serotonin reuptake inhibition
with 5-HT1A partial agonism, addresses (a) by reducing the
intensity and intrusiveness of masochistic ideation and the
associated distress. Bicalutamide, by competitively antagonizing
the androgen receptor *without castrating* the subject, addresses
(b) in a titratable manner that preserves a degree of consensual
sexual function and avoids the metabolic, bone-density,
cardiovascular, and gonadotropin-suppression burden of GnRH-agonist
therapy. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
(serotonergic + 5-HT1A vs. androgen receptor) and converge on the
prefrontal–striatal–hypothalamic paraphilic-urge and compulsive-
distress circuit.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in masochistic-distress and masochistic-urge
measures, with a tolerability profile substantially better than
legacy hormonal anti-androgen monotherapy. In the self-endangering
subset, reduction of urge intensity is also expected to reduce
frequency of high-risk behavior as part of a broader safety-plan
framework.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of
the invention include: SERT binding (³H-citalopram displacement
on rat brain membranes; Ki for vilazodone ≈ 0.5 nM); 5-HT1A
binding (³H-8-OH-DPAT displacement; Ki for vilazodone ≈ 0.2 nM);
5-HT1A functional partial agonism (cAMP / GTPγS in 5-HT1A-CHO
cells); androgen receptor binding (³H-mibolerone or ³H-R1881
displacement on LNCaP or AR-CHO cells; Ki for bicalutamide
≈ 100 nM); androgen receptor functional antagonism
(transactivation in MMTV-luciferase LNCaP reporter line).

Pre-clinical sexual masochism is not directly modelable in
animals, and no animal assay is asserted to reproduce the human
disorder. However, in-vivo paradigms in rodent that probe the
underlying substrates include: marble-burying for compulsive
behavior; conditioned-place-preference paradigms for aversive /
reward coupling; testosterone-administration paradigms for
AR-mediated sexual drive; and risk-preference tasks in which
animals choose between safe and punishing rewards. A supra-
additive effect of the combination on (i) compulsive-behavior
suppression, (ii) reduction of maladaptive aversive / reward
coupling, and (iii) AR-mediated sexual-drive attenuation,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable
dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the
Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult male volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titration to 40 mg/day for 7 days; full PK sampling on
days 1 and 7 with steady-state. Period 2 (after 7-day washout):
bicalutamide 50 mg/day for 28 days; full PK sampling on days 1,
14, and 28. Period 3 (after appropriate washout): combination at
the same doses; PK sampling for both agents on days 1 and 14. The
primary endpoint is the geometric mean ratio for AUC₀–τ and Cmax
of vilazodone and of bicalutamide in combination versus
monotherapy, with 90 % confidence intervals contained within
0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult Sexual Masochism Disorder (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 90 (30 per arm) with
descriptive analyses and 90 % confidence intervals around the
principal effect estimate.

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone titrated to 40 mg/day +
  bicalutamide 50 mg/day.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone titrated to 40 mg/day +
  matching placebo for bicalutamide.
* Arm C (placebo): matching placebo for both, with structured
  psychotherapy as the standard-of-care comparator.

All subjects receive standardized, manualized psychotherapy of
equal frequency, intensity, and duration (weekly 60-minute
sessions over 16 weeks) directed to distress tolerance, cognitive
restructuring, and safety planning. The primary endpoint is change
from baseline in MSI-II Paraphilic Sexual Outlet sub-scale at
week 16. Secondary endpoints include masochistic-urge frequency
diary, Sexual Distress Scale, high-risk self-endangering behavior
count, responder rate, and CGI-I. Safety endpoints include hepatic
enzyme monitoring, gynecomastia assessment, sexual function
inventory, and bone-mineral-density DEXA at baseline and week 24.

### Example 3 — Prophetic Phase 2 outcome

A Phase 2 study conducted in accordance with § 9.2 is expected to
demonstrate, at week 16, a least-squares mean reduction in MSI-II
Paraphilic Sexual Outlet sub-scale of approximately 31 % from
baseline in the combination arm, 15 % in the vilazodone
monotherapy arm, and 5 % in the placebo arm, with combination-
versus-placebo p < 0.05 (descriptive). Masochistic-urge frequency
diary counts are expected to decrease by approximately 45 % in the
combination arm versus 18 % in the monotherapy arm and 5 % in
placebo. High-risk self-endangering behavior count is expected to
decrease by approximately 70 % in the combination arm as part of a
structured safety-plan framework. The forest plot of prophetic

### Example 4 — Worked patient case

A 34-year-old male with a 10-year history of recurrent and intense
masochistic sexual ideation associated with marked distress,
relationship disruption, and clinician-documented high-risk self-
endangering behavior, with no history of hepatic disease, normal
baseline ALT/AST, and a stable weekly structured psychotherapy
regimen begins vilazodone 10 mg orally with food for 7 days,
titrated to 20 mg for 7 days and to 40 mg thereafter, plus
bicalutamide 50 mg orally once daily, with informed-consent

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Sexual Masochism Disorder in adult subjects comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
SSRI / 5-HT1A partial agonist vilazodone and a therapeutically
effective amount of the non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist
bicalutamide are disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping
receptor classes that converge on the prefrontal–striatal–
hypothalamic paraphilic-urge and compulsive-distress circuits
implicated in Sexual Masochism Disorder, are administered in a
non-castrating dose regimen that preserves endogenous gonadotropin
and testosterone secretion, and are expected to produce greater-
than-additive improvement in masochistic-distress and
masochistic-urge measures, including reduction of high-risk self-
endangering behavior, with substantially better tolerability than
legacy hormonal anti-androgen monotherapy. The invention is limited
to adult subjects with disorder-level distress, impairment, or
self-endangering risk and is not intended to pathologize
consensual, non-distressing BDSM practice.
