## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Female Orgasmic Disorder by co-administering
(a) a mu-opioid receptor agonist, in particular sufentanil, in a
restricted, session-aligned rescue regimen, and (b) a serotonergic
tetracyclic agent having antagonist activity at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C
receptors, in particular mianserin. The invention is intended for
adult female subjects with acquired or lifelong orgasmic delay,
marked infrequency, absent orgasm, or markedly reduced orgasmic
intensity causing clinically meaningful distress, and is not
intended for chronic unsupervised opioid use.

## Background of the invention

Female Orgasmic Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
sexual dysfunction characterized by marked delay in, marked
infrequency of, marked absence of, or marked reduction in the
intensity of orgasm after a normal sexual excitement phase, on
nearly all or all sexual occasions and accompanied by clinically
meaningful distress. The syndrome may occur as a lifelong
presentation or may be acquired after a period of satisfactory
sexual functioning.

The prevalence of orgasmic difficulty is meaningful across the
adult female population, but only a subset meet the threshold for
clinical disorder because distress, persistence, and impairment are
required. Common contributors include serotonergic antidepressant
exposure, menopausal transition, chronic pain, pelvic-floor
dysfunction, mood and anxiety disorders, trauma-related symptoms,
relationship stress, endocrine change, and idiopathic high
orgasmic threshold. The clinical burden includes reduced sexual
satisfaction, shame, avoidance of intimacy, partnership strain, and
discontinuation of otherwise beneficial medications when sexual
side effects are implicated.

The current standard of care is fragmented and unsatisfactory. It
consists primarily of education, psychosexual therapy, pelvic-floor
or behavioral interventions, management of contributing medical or
relationship factors, and empiric off-label medication trials. No
pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for Female Orgasmic Disorder
as a primary indication. A clinically meaningful gap remains for a
mechanism-based regimen that can restore orgasmic completion in
selected patients without resorting to chronic high-risk sedative
or opioid exposure.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, female orgasmic delay or anorgasmia is often
maintained by excessive serotonergic inhibition of orgasmic
completion, especially where the syndrome is associated with
serotonergic antidepressant exposure or central inhibitory tone.
Mianserin is a tetracyclic antidepressant with antagonist activity
at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and H1
histamine receptors. Clinical literature has shown that mianserin
can improve serotonin-reuptake-inhibitor-associated sexual
dysfunction, especially anorgasmia. The mianserin axis therefore
addresses the *serotonergic orgasm-inhibition substrate* of Female
Orgasmic Disorder.

Second, a subset of female orgasmic disorder appears to involve a
high acute orgasmic threshold in which genital stimulation or
sexual excitement does not translate into orgasmic completion even
when desire and consensual interest are present. The endogenous
opioid system participates in reward salience, sensory integration,
and orgasmic reinforcement. Sufentanil is a potent mu-opioid
receptor agonist approved for analgesic use in tightly supervised
medical settings and available in parenteral and sublingual /
transmucosal formulations. Although chronic or high-dose opioid
exposure is generally associated with sexual dysfunction, the
present invention is based on the counter-intuitive recognition
that *very low-dose, intermittent, time-limited, session-aligned*
mu-opioid agonism, administered only as a rescue adjunct on planned
sexual-attempt days, can lower the acute orgasmic threshold in
selected subjects when combined with background reduction of
serotonergic inhibitory tone by mianserin.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a low-dose rescue mu-opioid agonist
in combination with a 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C-antagonist antidepressant
for Female Orgasmic Disorder specifically.

The present invention recognizes that reduction of serotonergic
braking on orgasmic completion (a mianserin 5-HT2 axis) and
tightly limited enhancement of acute orgasmic salience
(a sufentanil mu-opioid rescue axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for treatment of Female Orgasmic Disorder in
selected adult female subjects. The combination is expected to
produce greater improvement in orgasmic completion rate, orgasmic
latency, and orgasmic intensity than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose, while explicitly avoiding chronic
unsupervised opioid exposure.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) sufentanil or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) mianserin or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Female Orgasmic Disorder in an adult female subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of mianserin and a
therapeutically effective amount of sufentanil, wherein the
sufentanil component is used as a session-aligned, as-needed rescue
intervention during planned consensual sexual activity or self-
stimulation attempts and is not used as chronic daily opioid
therapy.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) mianserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) sufentanil or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Female Orgasmic
Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, together
with explicit warnings on opioid risk, restricted use frequency,
and supervision requirements.

The invention contemplates mianserin in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 90 mg administered orally once daily or in divided
doses, with a preferred dose of 15 mg to 60 mg/day and a preferred
bedtime initiation dose of 15 mg to 30 mg/day. The invention
contemplates sufentanil in a single rescue dose of about 2.5 mcg to
about 20 mcg by sublingual or transmucosal route administered about
15 to 90 minutes before a planned sexual attempt, with a preferred
maximum daily exposure of about 40 mcg and a preferred maximum use
frequency of about 4 attempt-days per week. The inventive feature
is the combination of chronic background reduction of serotonergic
inhibitory tone with a tightly limited, low-dose, session-aligned
rescue intervention for orgasmic completion.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in adult
female subjects with Female Orgasmic Disorder, in orgasmic latency,
failure-to-orgasm rate, and sexual-distress burden, and an increase
in successful orgasmic completion and subjective orgasmic intensity
during planned consensual sexual activity or self-stimulation
attempts, that is greater than the sum of the individual effects of
mianserin and sufentanil administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Mianserin"* means the tetracyclic antidepressant
2-methyl-1,2,3,4,10,14b-hexahydrodibenzo[c,f]pyrazino[1,2-a]
azepine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof. The term
includes the hydrochloride and other pharmaceutically acceptable
salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically
labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Sufentanil"* means the potent mu-opioid receptor agonist
N-[4-(methoxymethyl)-1-[2-(2-thienyl)ethyl]-4-piperidyl]-N-
phenylpropanamide or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof.
The term includes parenteral, sublingual, and transmucosal
formulations and clinically equivalent low-dose rescue formulations
of sufentanil used in tightly controlled settings.

*"Female Orgasmic Disorder"* means the female sexual dysfunction
defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by marked delay in, marked
infrequency of, marked absence of, or markedly reduced intensity of
orgasm, persisting for at least 6 months and causing clinically
meaningful distress.

*"Successful orgasmic completion"* means orgasm occurring during a
planned consensual sexual activity or self-stimulation attempt
within a clinically acceptable timeframe and accompanied by
subjectively meaningful completion.

*"Session-aligned rescue dosing"* means administration of a rescue
medication within a defined interval, preferably about 15 to
90 minutes, before a planned sexual attempt, with explicit limits
on frequency, total dose, and supervision.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult female subject for the
treatment of Female Orgasmic Disorder according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in
at least one of: successful orgasmic completion rate, orgasmic
latency, Female Sexual Function Index orgasm-domain score, sexual
distress score, subjective orgasmic intensity, or investigator-
rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Mianserin

Mianserin is a tetracyclic antidepressant with antagonist activity
at 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C receptors, alpha2-adrenergic receptors, and H1
histamine receptors. In the present invention, the principal
mechanistic contribution of mianserin is reduction of serotonergic
orgasm-inhibition and restoration of a more permissive background
state for orgasmic completion.

#### 2.2 Sufentanil

Sufentanil is a potent mu-opioid receptor agonist approved for
analgesic use in tightly supervised settings. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of sufentanil is
acute, time-limited lowering of orgasmic threshold during planned
sexual attempts. The preferred embodiment is a restricted rescue
regimen, not chronic daily opioid therapy.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise mianserin and
sufentanil, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a mianserin tablet and a low-
dose transmucosal sufentanil rescue unit.

Excipients useful for the mianserin composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and film-coating polymers
suitable for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the
sufentanil rescue component include mucoadhesive carriers,
stabilizers, permeation-support excipients, and low-dose unit
delivery matrices appropriate for sublingual or buccal use.

### 4. Dosage forms

Mianserin is preferably formulated as an oral tablet administered
once daily, preferably at bedtime. Sufentanil is preferably
formulated as a low-dose sublingual or buccal dosage form suitable
for restricted rescue use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
mianserin tablets, (b) a limited number of low-dose sufentanil
rescue units consistent with a maximum use frequency of about
4 attempt-days per week, and (c) written instructions aligned to
session-aligned rescue dosing, safety monitoring, and opioid-risk
warnings.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult female subjects with Female Orgasmic Disorder, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Mianserin 15 mg orally at bedtime for about 7 days, titratable to
  30 mg and then to 60 mg nightly if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.
* Sufentanil about 2.5 mcg to about 20 mcg by sublingual or buccal
  route about 15 to 90 minutes before a planned consensual sexual
  activity or self-stimulation attempt, with preferred maximum use
  frequency of about 4 attempt-days per week and a preferred
  maximum daily exposure of about 40 mcg.

The preferred embodiment expressly excludes chronic daily opioid
therapy. Rescue doses are aligned to planned attempt days and
should be accompanied by counseling on sedation risk, respiratory
risk, and secure storage.

Medication–medication interactions: concomitant use of other CNS
depressants, benzodiazepines, alcohol misuse, or other opioids is
disfavored or exclusionary because of additive sedation and
respiratory-depression risk. The preferred clinical embodiment
requires screening for opioid use disorder and respiratory risk
before initiation.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult female
subjects with Female Orgasmic Disorder. Inclusion criteria for the
preferred treatment population include: age ≥ 18 years; acquired or
lifelong orgasmic delay / absence causing clinically meaningful
distress; willingness to track planned sexual-attempt outcomes; and
ability to follow restricted rescue-use instructions. Exclusion
criteria include pregnancy or breastfeeding, opioid use disorder or
high misuse risk, clinically meaningful respiratory instability,
severe hepatic impairment, uncontrolled major psychiatric
decompensation, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Female Orgasmic Disorder in selected adult subjects often includes
two interacting layers: persistent serotonergic inhibition of
orgasmic completion and an acutely elevated orgasmic threshold that
prevents completion even when desire and consensual engagement are
present.

Mianserin addresses the first layer through 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C
antagonism and related serotonergic disinhibition. Sufentanil
addresses the second through a tightly limited, session-aligned
mu-opioid rescue intervention that enhances acute orgasmic salience.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge
on successful orgasmic completion.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in orgasmic completion and orgasmic intensity
this combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 28,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C binding and antagonism assays for
mianserin; mu-opioid receptor binding and signaling assays for
sufentanil; and combined reward-salience / sensory-integration
paradigms where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include orgasm-threshold or consummatory-behavior models, reward-
salience paradigms, and models of serotonergic sexual inhibition. A
supra-additive effect of the combination on restoration of
orgasm-like completion signals, relative to either monotherapy at
any individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof
point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult female volunteers (age 21–55 years, non-pregnant).
Period 1: mianserin 15 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: single low-
dose sublingual sufentanil rescue administration on two supervised
study days; Period 3: the combination. Full PK sampling,
sedation monitoring, pulse oximetry, adverse-event monitoring, and
abuse-liability assessments are obtained. The principal purpose is
to establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult Female Orgasmic Disorder (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): mianserin + session-aligned low-dose
  sufentanil rescue.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): mianserin + placebo rescue.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo background + placebo rescue.

The primary endpoint is successful orgasmic completion rate across
planned consensual sexual activity or self-stimulation attempts
during weeks 5–8. Secondary endpoints include orgasmic latency,
Female Sexual Function Index orgasm-domain score, sexual distress
score, subjective orgasmic intensity, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of a low-dose rescue mu-opioid
agonist with a 5-HT2A / 5-HT2C-antagonist antidepressant for
Female Orgasmic Disorder specifically. The closest hits are:

* Female sexual dysfunction patents such as *Use of misoprostol
  and/or metabolites of misoprostol for treating sexual dysfunction
  in women* and related filings disclose other mechanisms and do
  not teach the present actives or the present session-aligned
  rescue architecture.
* Sufentanil dosage-form patents disclose pain-management rescue
  formulations and do not teach sexual-dysfunction use.
* Generic female sexual dysfunction filings do not disclose the
  present serotonergic-disinhibition plus low-dose rescue mu-opioid
  framework.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (mu-opioid agonism +
5-HT2A / 5-HT2C antagonism), (ii) the specific actives (sufentanil
+ mianserin), and (iii) the specific session-aligned rescue use for
Female Orgasmic Disorder. No prior-art disclosure suggests the
convergent-circuit rationale on which the present invention is
based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Female
Orgasmic Disorder comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of mianserin and a therapeutically effective
amount of sufentanil are disclosed, wherein the mianserin provides
background reduction of serotonergic orgasm-inhibition and the
sufentanil provides a low-dose, session-aligned rescue intervention
on planned sexual-attempt days. The two agents act on non-
overlapping orgasmic-threshold and serotonergic-inhibition circuits
and are expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in
successful orgasmic completion, orgasmic latency, and orgasmic
intensity than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose while avoiding chronic unsupervised opioid exposure.
