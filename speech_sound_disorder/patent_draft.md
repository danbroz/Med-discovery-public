## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Speech Sound Disorder (SSD) by co-administering
(a) a histamine H3 receptor antagonist or inverse agonist and (b) an
oxytocin receptor agonist, in particular the combination of
pitolisant and oxytocin, in dosage forms and on dosing schedules
timed to scheduled speech-language pathology articulation /
phonological intervention sessions.

## Background of the invention

Speech Sound Disorder is a neurodevelopmental communication condition
defined in the *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders,
Fifth Edition, Text Revision* (DSM-5-TR) and in the International
Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) at code 6A01.0.
The disorder is characterized by persistent difficulty with speech
sound production that interferes with speech intelligibility or
prevents verbal communication of messages. The deficits cause
limitations in effective communication that interfere with social
participation, academic achievement, or occupational performance.
Onset is in the early developmental period, and the deficits are not
attributable to a congenital or acquired condition such as cerebral
palsy, cleft palate, deafness or hearing loss, traumatic brain injury,
or other medical or neurological conditions. Within SSD, two broad
phenotypes are recognized clinically: (i) phonological-disorder
phenotype, in which a child's substitution and omission errors follow
a rule-governed pattern that suggests immature phonological
representation; and (ii) articulation-disorder phenotype, in which
errors are sound-specific and motoric. A small subset of children
with SSD meet criteria for childhood apraxia of speech (CAS), in
which error patterns suggest a deficit of motor planning and
programming.

The estimated population prevalence of SSD is between 2 % and 13 % of
school-age children, with a typical figure of 8 % at age 6 years. A
substantial subset (15–40 %, depending on the cohort) continues to
demonstrate residual articulation or intelligibility deficits into
adolescence and adulthood. SSD is associated with elevated rates of
later reading-decoding difficulty, language disorder, social
withdrawal, and bullying victimization. Comorbid Attention-Deficit /
Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), specific learning disorder,
developmental coordination disorder, language disorder, and Social
(Pragmatic) Communication Disorder are common.

The current standard of care for SSD is non-pharmacological. It
consists primarily of speech-language pathology directed to
articulation training (motor-based approaches such as PROMPT or
Dynamic Temporal and Tactile Cueing) or phonological-pattern
intervention (cycles approach, multiple-oppositions approach), with
adjunctive instrumental biofeedback in residual / persistent cases.
No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of SSD as a primary
indication. Off-label use of stimulants in subjects with comorbid
ADHD is reported, but stimulants do not target the motor-planning or
self-monitoring substrates that mediate phonological and articulatory
acquisition, and the published controlled evidence of articulation
benefit from these agents is sparse.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, articulation and phonological practice in SSD
require sustained attention to the clinician's modeled target,
on-line monitoring of the subject's own articulatory output against
that target, and rapid motor-program adjustment based on auditory and
proprioceptive feedback. Each of these subprocesses depends on
state-appropriate cortical arousal and on cholinergic and dopaminergic
signaling in left inferior frontal cortex, supplementary motor area,
and dorsal striatum. The histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic
auto- and heteroreceptor that, when constitutively active, suppresses
the release of histamine and of acetylcholine, dopamine, and
norepinephrine in cortex and striatum. Selective inverse agonism at
H3 by pitolisant (Wakix®, FDA-approved for excessive daytime
sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy) elevates extracellular histamine
and supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine,
without the abuse liability of stimulant scheduling. The H3 axis
therefore addresses the *vigilance, motor-attention, and
self-monitoring substrate* of articulation and phonological learning,
but does not address the social-motivational substrate that
determines whether a child engages with the clinician's model and
attends to corrective feedback.

Second, oxytocin receptor (OXTR) signaling in the prefrontal cortex,
amygdala, ventral tegmental area, and superior temporal sulcus
modulates social motivation, eye-gaze allocation, the salience of
socially contingent feedback, and the affective valence of
caregiver-mediated practice. Intranasal oxytocin has been
investigated in autism spectrum disorder, Prader–Willi syndrome,
schizophrenia, and post-traumatic stress disorder, with
mechanism-consistent signals on social attention and feedback
processing. No issued patent or published application known to the
present inventors discloses the use of an oxytocin receptor agonist
in combination with an H3 receptor antagonist or inverse agonist for
any indication, and none discloses such a combination for SSD
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance- and
self-monitoring-enabled articulation practice (an H3 antagonist
axis) and socially scaffolded clinician-modeled practice (an OXTR
agonist axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for
phonological and articulatory consolidation in SSD. The combination
is expected to produce a greater speech-intelligibility outcome than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose, by acting on
two non-overlapping CNS axes that converge on the same task-relevant
fronto-striatal–temporal circuit.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) oxytocin or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, analogue, fragment, or
prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Speech Sound Disorder in a subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount of oxytocin.
Co-administration may be simultaneous, in a unitary dosage form or
in separate dosage forms, or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising (a)
pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) oxytocin or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form, and
(c) written instructions for the treatment of SSD in a pediatric
subject according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, with the
oxytocin actuation explicitly timed to within 30 minutes of a
scheduled articulation- or phonological-intervention session.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about 4.5 mg
to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily, with a preferred
adult dose of 8.9 to 17.8 mg/day and a preferred pediatric dose
weight-banded as set forth herein. The invention contemplates
oxytocin in a daily dose of about 8 IU to about 80 IU administered
intranasally in divided doses, with a preferred dose of 24 IU twice
daily timed to practice sessions. The mass ratio of pitolisant to
oxytocin (calculated on the free-base equivalents) ranges broadly
from about 1:200,000 to about 1:5,000, and is preferably from about
1:50,000 to about 1:20,000; the dose-equivalent molar relationship
and the session-aligned timing relationship are the inventive
features, not the absolute mass ratio.

The technical effect of the combination is an increase, in subjects
with SSD, in speech-intelligibility outcomes (Percentage of
Consonants Correct (PCC) in conversational speech; Goldman-Fristoe
Test of Articulation, 3rd ed. (GFTA-3) Sounds-in-Words Standard
Score; Intelligibility in Context Scale (ICS) total), that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of pitolisant and
oxytocin administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable
dose. The combination is opioid-free, stimulant-free, and has no
known dependence liability.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the compound 1-{3-[3-(4-chlorophenyl)propoxy]
propyl}piperidine, also known as BF2.649, marketed in the United
States as Wakix® and in the European Union as Wakix®. Pitolisant is
a selective inverse agonist of the human histamine H3 receptor with
a binding affinity (Ki) of approximately 0.16 nM. The term as used
herein includes the free base, the hydrochloride salt and other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Oxytocin"* means the cyclic nonapeptide of sequence
H-Cys-Tyr-Ile-Gln-Asn-Cys-Pro-Leu-Gly-NH₂ with a disulfide bond
between Cys¹ and Cys⁶. The term as used herein includes the free
peptide and its pharmaceutically acceptable acetate or citrate salts,
glycosylated or PEGylated derivatives that retain agonism at the
human OXTR with EC₅₀ within ten-fold of native oxytocin, peptidic
agonists such as carbetocin, and small-molecule OXTR agonists such as
TC OT 39, WAY 267,464, LIT-001, or such other agents that produce
≥ 50 % maximal calcium mobilization at the human OXTR at
concentrations ≤ 1 µM in a standard fluorometric calcium-flux assay.

*"Speech Sound Disorder"* or *"SSD"* means the disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and in ICD-11 (6A01.0), characterized by persistent
difficulty with speech sound production that interferes with speech
intelligibility or prevents verbal communication of messages, with
deficits resulting in functional limitations in effective
communication, social participation, academic achievement, or
occupational performance, and which is not attributable to a
congenital or acquired condition such as cerebral palsy, cleft
palate, deafness or hearing loss, traumatic brain injury, or other
medical or neurological condition. The term as used herein
encompasses the phonological-disorder phenotype, the
articulation-disorder phenotype, and the childhood apraxia of speech
phenotype.

*"Speech-intelligibility outcome"* means a subject's measured
performance on at least one of: Percentage of Consonants Correct
(PCC) computed from a 100-utterance conversational sample;
Goldman-Fristoe Test of Articulation, 3rd ed. (GFTA-3) Sounds-in-
Words Standard Score; Intelligibility in Context Scale (ICS) total;
or a comparable validated speech-intelligibility instrument.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a subject for the treatment of
SSD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: PCC, GFTA-3
Sounds-in-Words Standard Score, ICS total, or investigator-rated
CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject so
that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of the
other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two agents
are administered in the same dosage form, in separate dosage forms,
at the same time, or at different times within a window of about 24
hours.

*"Session-aligned dosing"* means administration of intranasal
oxytocin within a window of about 30 minutes prior to or during a
scheduled articulation- or phonological-intervention session, with
the purpose of aligning the central oxytocin pharmacodynamic window
with the period of clinician-modeled and socially scaffolded
articulation practice.

### 2. The two active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a piperidine derivative and a high-affinity, selective
inverse agonist at the human histamine H3 receptor. The H3 receptor
is a presynaptic autoreceptor and heteroreceptor that, when
constitutively active, suppresses the release of histamine from
histaminergic neurons and the release of acetylcholine, dopamine,
and norepinephrine from cortical, striatal, and hippocampal neurons.
Inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant elevates extracellular histamine
and supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine
in left inferior frontal cortex, supplementary motor area, and
dorsal striatum — circuits implicated in articulatory motor
planning, phonological working memory, and self-monitoring.
Pitolisant is approved by the FDA for the treatment of excessive
daytime sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy, and is approved in the
European Union for excessive daytime sleepiness and cataplexy in
adults with narcolepsy. Pitolisant is metabolized primarily by
CYP2D6 and CYP3A4. The medication is a substrate and weak inhibitor
of CYP3A4 and is not a significant inducer of common cytochrome
P450 enzymes. Pitolisant is under U.S. brand-patent protection
through the late 2020s; clinical deployment of the present invention
will require coordination with the marketing authorization holder.
The present invention does not require any specific salt, polymorph,
or excipient combination of pitolisant and is not asserted to be a
composition-of-matter invention as to pitolisant.

#### 2.2 Oxytocin

Oxytocin is the endogenous cyclic nonapeptide synthesized in the
paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei of the hypothalamus. Oxytocin
acts at the OXTR, a Gαq-coupled G protein-coupled receptor that
mobilizes intracellular calcium and modulates social cognition,
attachment, trust, eye-gaze allocation, and the recognition of
socially relevant affect. Synthetic oxytocin (Pitocin®, Syntocinon®)
is approved in many jurisdictions for the induction of labor and the
prevention or treatment of postpartum hemorrhage. Investigational
use of intranasal oxytocin in CNS indications is supported by a
substantial peer-reviewed literature. Native oxytocin is freely
available from multiple suppliers and has no live U.S.
composition-of-matter patent restriction; the present invention does
not require any proprietary OXTR agonist. The intranasal route is
preferred to maximize central nervous system exposure relative to
peripheral OXTR engagement.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
oxytocin, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a unitary
fixed-dose combination dosage form (for example, a single oral
tablet plus a co-packaged intranasal device) or a co-packaged kit of
two separate dosage forms.

The mass ratio of pitolisant (calculated as the free base) to

Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of pitolisant include the

Pharmaceutically acceptable polymorphic forms include all

Solvates and hydrates are included. Pharmaceutically acceptable
forms of oxytocin include the free peptide acetate or citrate salt,
the carbetocin analogue, glycosylated and PEGylated derivatives that
retain agonism at OXTR within ten-fold of native, and small-molecule

Excipients useful in the compositions include diluents

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-release
oral tablet at strengths of 4.5 mg and 17.8 mg (free-base
equivalent), matching the marketed product. Modified-release oral
tablets at strengths up to 35.6 mg per unit are within the scope of
the invention.

Oxytocin is preferably formulated as a metered-dose intranasal spray
delivering 4 IU per actuation, with permeation enhancement to
maximize central nervous system bioavailability. Sublingual films,
buccal tablets, and depot subcutaneous formulations are within the
scope of the invention. Intravenous and intramuscular dosage forms
are appropriate for inpatient initiation but not for chronic
ambulatory use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-tablet bottle of
pitolisant immediate-release oral tablets at the appropriate
weight-band strength, (b) a 30-day supply of an intranasal oxytocin
spray device, and (c) a printed insert with administration timing
aligned to scheduled articulation- or phonological-intervention
sessions.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adults (subjects ≥ 18 years and ≥ 50 kg) with persistent SSD,
the preferred dosing regimen is pitolisant 8.9 mg once daily for one
week followed by 17.8 mg once daily, taken on awakening, plus
intranasal oxytocin 24 IU twice daily, timed to within 30 minutes of
scheduled speech-language pathology sessions. Maximum recommended
pitolisant dose is 35.6 mg/day; maximum recommended oxytocin dose is
80 IU/day in three or four divided actuations.

For pediatric subjects ≥ 6 years and < 18 years (the principal
treatment population), weight-banded dosing is preferred:

| Body weight | Pitolisant (mg/day, oral) | Oxytocin (IU/day, intranasal) |
|---|---|---|
| 20–< 30 kg | 4.5 | 16 (8 IU twice daily) |
| 30–< 50 kg | 8.9 | 24 (12 IU twice daily) |
| ≥ 50 kg | 8.9 → 17.8 (titration) | 24–48 |

Hepatic adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B) and is not
recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C). No oxytocin
dose adjustment is required for hepatic impairment.

Renal adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²). No
oxytocin adjustment is required.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP2D6 inhibitors (paroxetine, fluoxetine, bupropion) without
halving the pitolisant dose. Avoid co-administration with strong
CYP3A4 inducers. No clinically significant medication–medication
interactions are expected between pitolisant and intranasal oxytocin.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects who
meet DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 criteria for SSD. Inclusion criteria for the
preferred treatment population include: age ≥ 6 years and ≤ 18 years;
PCC ≤ 80 % in conversational speech sample, or GFTA-3 Sounds-in-
Words Standard Score ≤ 78 (≤ 1.5 SD below age mean); intact peripheral
hearing (pure-tone average ≤ 25 dB HL bilaterally); absence of overt
oral-structural anomaly (no cleft palate, no submucous cleft, no
fistula); absence of a diagnosed neurologic condition that better
accounts for the speech impairment (no cerebral palsy, no acquired
brain injury, no progressive neurological disease); intelligence
quotient ≥ 70. Comorbid ADHD, language disorder, specific learning
disorder, developmental coordination disorder, and Social
(Pragmatic) Communication Disorder are not exclusionary; SSD
frequently co-occurs with these conditions and the present
invention is expected to address articulation symptoms in those
subjects without exacerbating the co-occurring condition.
Exclusion criteria include known hypersensitivity to either active,
current treatment with a prohibited concomitant medication
(see § 5), pregnancy or breastfeeding, and inability to comply with
intranasal administration.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Phonological and articulation acquisition in SSD is impaired at the
level of (a) the maintenance of attention to a clinician's modeled
target across repeated trials, (b) the on-line monitoring of the
subject's own articulatory output against the target and the
contingent updating of motor programs based on auditory and
proprioceptive feedback, and (c) the affective and motivational
scaffolding by which a child engages with the clinician and attends
to corrective feedback. Selective H3 inverse agonism by pitolisant
addresses (a) and (b) by elevating central histamine and supporting
state-dependent acetylcholine and dopamine release in left inferior
frontal cortex, supplementary motor area, and dorsal striatum, with
documented clinical effect on psychomotor vigilance and
discourse-relevant attention in narcolepsy populations. OXTR
agonism by intranasal oxytocin addresses (c) by enhancing
clinician-directed eye gaze, recognition of clinician affect, and
the salience of socially contingent feedback, all of which mediate
the quality of articulation practice during pediatric speech-language
intervention. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on the fronto-striatal–temporal articulation circuit.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in speech-intelligibility outcomes that depend
on both vigilance- and self-monitoring-enabled articulation practice
and socially scaffolded clinician-modeled practice.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding (³H-N-α-methylhistamine
displacement on rat cortical membranes; Ki for pitolisant ≈ 1.5 nM);
H3 functional inverse agonism (cAMP accumulation in CHO-K1 cells
expressing human H3); OXTR binding (³H-oxytocin displacement on
human OXTR-CHO membranes; Kd for oxytocin ≈ 1 nM); OXTR functional
agonism (calcium mobilization in OXTR-CHO cells; EC₅₀ for oxytocin
≈ 5–20 nM).

Although SSD is a uniquely human condition (no animal model
recapitulates phonological speech), in-vivo behavioral paradigms in
rodent and non-human primate that probe the underlying substrates
are appropriate. These include: five-choice serial reaction time
task (5-CSRTT) for sustained attention; reversal-learning paradigms
for motor-program updating under feedback; complex motor-sequence
learning in rodent forelimb reaching; ultrasonic vocalization (USV)
emission and partner-USV contingency in juvenile rat dyads as a
proxy for socially contingent vocal output; and three-chamber
social-novelty paradigms for clinician-analog social motivation. A
supra-additive effect of the combination on (i) 5-CSRTT
discrimination accuracy, (ii) reversal-learning trials-to-criterion,
and (iii) socially contingent USV emission, relative to either
monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the principal
in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years; equal sex
distribution). Period 1: pitolisant 17.8 mg orally once daily for
7 days; full PK sampling on days 1 and 7. Period 2 (after 7-day
washout): intranasal oxytocin 24 IU twice daily for 7 days; full PK
sampling on days 1 and 7 (oxytocin plasma concentrations and a
salivary or central biomarker if validated). Period 3 (after 7-day
washout): combination at the same doses for 7 days; PK sampling for
both agents on days 1 and 7. The primary endpoint is the geometric
mean ratio for AUC₀–τ and Cmax of pitolisant in combination versus
monotherapy, with 90 % confidence intervals contained within
0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in pediatric SSD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm) with 80 % power to
detect a 7-percentage-point difference in PCC change from baseline
between the combination arm and placebo at week 12, assuming a
standard deviation of 13 and a two-sided α of 0.05.

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant 8.9 mg/day oral + intranasal
  oxytocin 24 IU twice daily.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pitolisant 8.9 mg/day oral + matching
  intranasal placebo.
* Arm C (placebo): matching oral placebo + matching intranasal
  placebo.

All subjects receive a standardized, manualized articulation /
phonological intervention of equal frequency, intensity, and
duration (twice-weekly 45-minute sessions over 12 weeks) delivered
by a certified speech-language pathologist and aligned to the
oxytocin actuation window. The primary endpoint is change from
baseline in PCC at week 12. Secondary endpoints include GFTA-3
Sounds-in-Words Standard Score, ICS total, responder rate (≥ 10
percentage-point PCC improvement), parent-reported communicative
participation, and CGI-I.

### Example 4 — Worked patient case

A 7-year-old female with a confirmed SSD diagnosis (PCC = 62 % in

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Speech
Sound Disorder comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of the histamine H3 receptor inverse agonist
pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount of the oxytocin
receptor agonist oxytocin, in dosage forms and on dosing schedules
timed to scheduled articulation- and phonological-intervention
sessions, are disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping
receptor classes that converge on the fronto-striatal–temporal
articulation-and-self-monitoring circuit implicated in Speech Sound
Disorder, and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in speech-intelligibility outcomes than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose.
