## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Rumination Disorder by co-administering (a) a
serotonin reuptake inhibitor that is also a 5-HT1A receptor partial
agonist and (b) a corticotropin-releasing hormone receptor type 1
(CRHR1) antagonist, in particular the combination of vilazodone and
crinecerfont. The invention is intended for adolescent and adult
subjects with persistent Rumination Disorder who are receiving
behavioral therapy and nutritional support, and addresses stress-
triggered regurgitation, interoceptive distress, and the learned
habit loop that maintains repeated regurgitation.

## Background of the invention

Rumination Disorder is a feeding and eating disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by repeated regurgitation of food after feeding
or eating over a period of at least one month, where the
regurgitated food may be rechewed, reswallowed, or spit out, and
the behavior is not attributable to a gastrointestinal or other
medical condition and does not occur exclusively during anorexia
nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge-eating disorder, or avoidant /
restrictive food intake disorder. In some subjects, the behavior is
accompanied by a characteristic premonitory sensation and by
reinforcement through transient relief of visceral discomfort or
stress. Although the disorder is classically described in infants
and individuals with developmental disability, clinically
meaningful persistent rumination also occurs in adolescents and
adults with otherwise intact cognition.

The prevalence of Rumination Disorder in the general population is
low and the condition is substantially under-diagnosed. The
disorder is associated with dental erosion, weight loss,
malnutrition, electrolyte disturbances, social embarrassment, and
avoidance of meals in public. Comorbid anxiety disorders,
obsessive–compulsive symptoms, trauma-related symptoms, and
functional gastrointestinal disorders are common.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological. It consists
primarily of diaphragmatic breathing, habit-reversal methods,
meal-structure interventions, nutritional rehabilitation, and
psychological treatment directed toward interoceptive awareness and
stress regulation. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment
of Rumination Disorder as a primary indication. Off-label
medication use in this population is heterogeneous and usually
targets anxiety, dyspepsia, or nausea rather than the core
regurgitation phenotype.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, persistent rumination is maintained by a learned
visceral–habit loop that has substantial mechanistic overlap with
obsessive–compulsive and interoceptive-anxiety phenotypes.
Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available, FDA-approved
antidepressant that combines selective serotonin reuptake
inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual
mechanism supports attenuation of anxious-compulsive
symptomatology, interoceptive distress, and habit-driven urge
states. The serotonergic axis therefore addresses the *intrusive
urge and interoceptive-anxiety* substrate of Rumination Disorder,
but does not directly attenuate hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal
(HPA) axis hyper-reactivity.

Second, stress physiology strongly modulates upper gastrointestinal
motor patterning, visceral hypersensitivity, and vomiting /
regurgitation-like behaviors. Corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF)
acting at CRHR1 contributes to stress-induced gastrointestinal
dysmotility and amplification of visceral distress. Crinecerfont
(CRENESSITY®) is a selective CRHR1 antagonist approved by the FDA
in 2024 as adjunctive treatment to glucocorticoid replacement in
classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, where it reduces CRF-driven
ACTH secretion. More broadly, CRHR1 antagonism has long been a
mechanistically attractive approach for stress-linked psychiatric
and visceral conditions. In the present invention, crinecerfont
addresses the *stress-axis and visceral-reactivity* substrate of
Rumination Disorder.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a 5-HT1A-partial-agonist SSRI in
combination with a CRHR1 antagonist for Rumination Disorder
specifically, and none discloses such a combination for repeated
human post-prandial regurgitation as a feeding-and-eating disorder.

The present invention recognizes that serotonergic attenuation of
the learned interoceptive-habit loop (a vilazodone HTR1A axis) and
CRHR1-mediated attenuation of stress-reactive visceral physiology
(a crinecerfont axis) are complementary and convergent substrates
for treatment of Rumination Disorder. The combination is expected
to produce a greater reduction in regurgitation frequency and meal-
associated distress than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) crinecerfont or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Rumination Disorder in an adolescent or adult subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone and a
therapeutically effective amount of crinecerfont. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) crinecerfont or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Rumination
Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, including
administration with meals and coordination with diaphragmatic
breathing / habit-reversal therapy.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally with food once daily, with
a preferred adult and adolescent dose of 20 to 40 mg/day after a
7-day titration from 10 mg/day. The invention contemplates
crinecerfont in a daily dose of about 50 mg to about 200 mg
administered orally in two divided doses with meals, with a
preferred dose of 50 mg twice daily for subjects < 55 kg and
100 mg twice daily for subjects ≥ 55 kg, aligned to the currently
approved adult and pediatric classic-CAH dose bands. The
inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of the HTR1A /
serotonergic and CRHR1 stress-axis pathways during meal-related
habit-reversal windows.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in
adolescent and adult subjects with Rumination Disorder, in
post-prandial regurgitation frequency, meal-associated distress,
and reinforcement of the rumination habit loop, that is greater
than the sum of the individual effects of vilazodone and
crinecerfont administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Crinecerfont"* means the selective corticotropin-releasing
hormone receptor type 1 (CRHR1) antagonist approved in the United
States as CRENESSITY®. The term includes crinecerfont free base,
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically
equivalent CRHR1 antagonists within the same activity genus.

*"Rumination Disorder"* means the feeding and eating disorder
defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by repeated regurgitation of food
after feeding or eating, with the regurgitated food rechewed,
reswallowed, or spit out, over a period of at least one month,
where the behavior is not attributable to a gastrointestinal or
other medical condition and does not occur exclusively during
another eating disorder.

*"Regurgitation episode"* means a discrete event in which recently
ingested food is brought back into the mouth after eating and is
rechewed, reswallowed, or expelled.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adolescent or adult subject
for the treatment of Rumination Disorder according to a dosing
regimen disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful
improvement in at least one of: weekly regurgitation episodes,
meal-associated distress score, percentage of meals followed by
regurgitation, body-weight stability or gain, burden index, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin
across multiple central serotonergic targets; by partially
agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, vilazodone supports attenuation of
anxious-compulsive symptomatology and interoceptive distress.
Vilazodone is FDA-approved for the treatment of major depressive
disorder in adults at oral doses of 10 mg/day (initial), titrated
to 20 mg/day after one week and to 40 mg/day thereafter, taken
once daily with food to support oral bioavailability. Vilazodone
is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4 and is a substrate (and weak
inhibitor) of CYP2C19 and CYP2D6. Vilazodone is generic in the
United States.

#### 2.2 Crinecerfont

Crinecerfont is a selective CRHR1 antagonist approved by the FDA
in 2024 as adjunctive treatment to glucocorticoid replacement in
classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia in adults and pediatric
patients 4 years of age and older. It suppresses CRF-driven ACTH
signaling and is available in capsule and oral-solution
formulations. Approved dosing is 100 mg twice daily with meals in
adults, and weight-based twice-daily dosing in pediatric patients
(e.g., 25 mg BID for 10–<20 kg, 50 mg BID for 20–<55 kg, 100 mg
BID for ≥55 kg). The compound is under active U.S. patent
protection into the 2040s; clinical use of the present invention
will require coordination with the rights holder or a licenseable
development path.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
crinecerfont, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as a vilazodone
tablet and a crinecerfont capsule or oral solution.

The vilazodone component may be formulated as the hydrochloride
salt in immediate-release tablet form. The crinecerfont component
may be formulated as capsule or oral solution for administration
with meals. Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition
include diluents (lactose monohydrate, microcrystalline cellulose),
binders (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers. Excipients useful for
the crinecerfont composition include capsule fill excipients,
suspending agents, flavoring agents for oral solution, buffers, and
vehicles suitable for meal-associated dosing.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-
release oral tablet at strengths of 10 mg, 20 mg, and 40 mg.
Crinecerfont is preferably formulated as oral capsules and/or oral
solution suitable for twice-daily dosing with meals.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of crinecerfont capsules
or oral solution, and (c) written instructions aligned to meals,
diaphragmatic breathing practice, and habit-reversal sessions.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with Rumination Disorder, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter.
* Crinecerfont 50 mg orally twice daily with meals for subjects
  < 55 kg, and 100 mg orally twice daily with meals for subjects
  ≥ 55 kg.

Broad genus ranges include vilazodone 10–40 mg/day and crinecerfont
50–200 mg/day in two divided doses. The preferred schedule aligns
morning crinecerfont with breakfast and behavioral practice, and
evening crinecerfont with the evening meal.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: severe hepatic or renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication. Standard meal-associated crinecerfont dosing is
maintained in mild-to-moderate impairment only if clinically
appropriate.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers until definitive DDI
characterization is available for the combination. Vilazodone dose
adjustment may be required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adolescent and
adult subjects with persistent Rumination Disorder. Inclusion
criteria for the preferred treatment population include:
chronological age ≥ 12 years; persistent Rumination Disorder for at
least 3 months; baseline weekly regurgitation frequency above a
clinically meaningful threshold; participation in diaphragmatic
breathing or habit-reversal therapy; absence of a gastrointestinal
structural disorder explaining the regurgitation phenotype; and
body weight stable enough to safely participate in outpatient
treatment. Exclusion criteria include active bulimia nervosa or
anorexia nervosa, uncontrolled gastrointestinal disease, active
psychosis, severe hepatic or renal impairment, pregnancy, and
inability to comply with meal-linked dosing.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Rumination Disorder is maintained by interaction between a learned
regurgitation habit loop and stress-reactive visceral physiology.

Vilazodone addresses the learned interoceptive-habit loop by
reducing intrusive urge, interoceptive anxiety, and compulsive
repetition. Crinecerfont addresses the stress-reactive visceral
component by attenuating CRHR1-mediated amplification of HPA-axis
stress signaling and stress-associated gastrointestinal dysmotility.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on the same post-prandial regurgitation-maintenance loop.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in post-prandial regurgitation frequency and
meal-associated distress than either monotherapy alone.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; CRHR1 binding and ACTH-suppression cellular assays for
crinecerfont; and combined stress-hormone / visceral-signaling
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include stress-induced gastric dysmotility models, conditioned
interoceptive discomfort paradigms, meal-triggered regurgitation-
like behavior models where ethically appropriate, and anxiety /
compulsivity assays such as marble-burying or avoidance-based
conflict tasks. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
reduction of stress-triggered upper-GI motor disruption and
compulsive repetition, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 40 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2:
crinecerfont 100 mg twice daily with meals for 7 days; Period 3:
the combination for 7 days. Full PK sampling, cortisol / ACTH
biomarkers, and gastrointestinal tolerability monitoring are
obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to establish
tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult Rumination Disorder
(FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120
(40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + crinecerfont.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for
  crinecerfont.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard-of-care
  behavioral treatment.

All subjects receive standardized behavioral treatment directed to
diaphragmatic breathing and habit reversal. The primary endpoint is
change from baseline in weekly regurgitation episodes at week 12.
Secondary endpoints include meal-associated distress score,
percentage of meals followed by regurgitation, body-weight change,
burden index, and CGI-I.

normal upper endoscopy, repeated post-prandial regurgitation for

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Rumination Disorder in adolescent and adult subjects comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
SSRI / 5-HT1A partial agonist vilazodone and a therapeutically
effective amount of the CRHR1 antagonist crinecerfont are
disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping receptor classes
that converge on stress-reactive visceral and habit-maintenance
circuits implicated in persistent post-prandial regurgitation in
Rumination Disorder, and are expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in regurgitation frequency, meal-associated
distress, and behavioral reinforcement of rumination than either
agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
