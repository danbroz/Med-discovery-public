## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Adjustment Disorder by co-administering
(a) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with 5-HT2C
antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine, and
(b) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with 5-HT1A partial agonist
activity, in particular vilazodone. The invention is intended for
subjects with clinically significant maladaptive emotional or
behavioral response to an identifiable stressor and addresses
stress-linked anxious distress, rumination, insomnia, and impaired
functional adaptation. The invention is not intended to pathologize
ordinary transient distress that remains proportionate and
self-limited.

## Background of the invention

Adjustment Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
maladaptive emotional or behavioral response to an identifiable
stressor occurring within the months following that stressor, with
distress out of proportion to the severity or intensity of the
stressor and / or significant impairment in social, occupational, or
other important functioning. Clinical presentations may include
anxiety, depressed mood, mixed emotional disturbance, conduct
features, or mixed disturbance of emotions and conduct.

The prevalence of Adjustment Disorder is clinically meaningful in
primary care, consultation-liaison, oncology, rehabilitation,
military, disaster, grief, employment, and legal-stress contexts.
The burden includes insomnia, rumination, social withdrawal,
reduced adherence to medical treatment, occupational disruption,
relationship strain, and increased healthcare use. Unlike
major depressive disorder or primary anxiety disorders, the syndrome
is defined by a temporally linked stressor and maladaptive response
profile.

The current standard of care is primarily psychotherapeutic and
supportive. It consists of psychoeducation, problem-solving,
supportive therapy, CBT-informed coping strategies, and treatment of
co-occurring symptoms when clinically indicated. No pharmaceutical
product is approved by the United States Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) specifically for the treatment of Adjustment
Disorder as a primary indication. Existing borrowed antidepressant
or anxiolytic approaches may reduce broad distress but often do not
directly address the combination of stress-linked rumination,
nocturnal hyperarousal, and reduced adaptive recovery.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, Adjustment Disorder frequently includes circadian
disorganization, poor sleep continuity, early-morning stress
reactivity, and reduced physiologic recovery after stressor-linked
activation. Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts
as a potent agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at
5-HT2C receptors. This combination supports circadian
resynchronization, improvement in sleep architecture, and reduction
of nocturnal hyperarousal. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses
the *sleep-recovery and circadian destabilization* substrate of
Adjustment Disorder.

Second, Adjustment Disorder also includes intrusive stressor-linked
rumination, anxious anticipatory thinking, and reduced serotonergic
resilience under situational pressure. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an
orally available antidepressant that combines selective serotonin
reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor.
This dual mechanism supports attenuation of anxious distress and
threat-related cognition while maintaining an anxiety-relevant
pharmacology profile. The vilazodone axis therefore addresses the
*anxious-serotonergic and stressor-rumination* substrate of
Adjustment Disorder.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
with 5-HT2C antagonism in combination with a serotonin reuptake
inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial agonist for Adjustment Disorder
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that restoration of sleep-linked
recovery (an agomelatine axis) and attenuation of stressor-linked
rumination (a vilazodone axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of Adjustment Disorder. The combination is
expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement in distress,
insomnia, and functional adaptation than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Adjustment Disorder in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of agomelatine and a therapeutically effective amount of
vilazodone.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Adjustment
Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in
conjunction with stressor-focused psychotherapy or supportive care.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a
preferred initiation dose of 25 mg nightly and optional escalation
to 50 mg nightly after about 2 weeks if clinically indicated. The
invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about 10 mg to
about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with a
preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from 10 mg/day.
The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of melatonergic
sleep-recovery support and serotonergic stressor-rumination
attenuation in Adjustment Disorder.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with Adjustment Disorder, in distress severity, sleep disruption,
rumination burden, and functional impairment, that is greater than
the sum of the individual effects of agomelatine and vilazodone
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Adjustment Disorder"* means the stressor-linked syndrome defined
in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by disproportionate distress and / or
functional impairment arising in response to an identifiable
stressor.

*"Adjustment-disorder-severity composite"* means a clinically
meaningful burden of stressor-linked distress, rumination, insomnia,
or role dysfunction quantified by structured clinician assessment
or validated symptom instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of Adjustment Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed
herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least
one of: adjustment-disorder-severity composite, sleep-quality
score, rumination score, role-function score, responder rate, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is restoration of sleep continuity and
stress-recovery physiology.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious distress and stressor-focused rumination. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of vilazodone is
reduction of maladaptive cognitive-emotional response to the
stressor.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of an agomelatine tablet and a vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the agomelatine composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the vilazodone
composition include diluents (microcrystalline cellulose, lactose
monohydrate), binders (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone),
disintegrants (crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime administration. Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an
oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
agomelatine tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of vilazodone tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to supportive psychotherapy,
stressor-focused coping, and sleep stabilization.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with Adjustment Disorder, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days, titratable
  to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.

The regimen is not intended to replace psychotherapy, social
intervention, or practical problem solving around the stressor.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
Adjustment Disorder. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: recent identifiable stressor; disproportionate
distress or functional impairment; and willingness to engage in
supportive or stressor-focused treatment. Exclusion criteria
include normative transient distress without clinically meaningful
impairment, untreated primary psychotic disorder, severe hepatic
disease, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Adjustment Disorder often includes two interacting layers:
stressor-linked rumination and anxious distress, and poor nocturnal
recovery that amplifies next-day impairment.

Agomelatine addresses the sleep-recovery layer through MT1 / MT2
agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. Vilazodone addresses the rumination-
distress layer through SERT inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on the same maladaptive stress-response phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in Adjustment Disorder burden than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 31,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays
for agomelatine; SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; and combined stress-recovery assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include acute stress and adaptation models, sleep-fragmentation
paradigms, rumination-like perseveration tasks, and stress-recovery
assays. A supra-additive effect of the combination on reduction of
stress-linked dysfunction, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in Section 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
agomelatine 25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone
titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, liver-function monitoring, and
tolerability assessments are obtained.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult Adjustment Disorder
(FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 150
(50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): agomelatine + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with supportive psychotherapy.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in adjustment-
disorder-severity composite at week 10. Secondary endpoints include
sleep-quality score, rumination score, role-function score,
responder rate, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Adjustment Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping sleep-recovery and stressor-
rumination circuits implicated in maladaptive stress response and
are expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in
distress, insomnia, and functional adaptation than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose.
