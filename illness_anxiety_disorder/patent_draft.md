## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Illness Anxiety Disorder (IAD) by
co-administering (a) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist
having 5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine, and
(b) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with 5-HT1A partial agonist
activity, in particular vilazodone. The invention is intended for
subjects with persistent fear of having or acquiring a serious
illness despite absent or only mild somatic symptoms, and addresses
health-related preoccupation, reassurance seeking, body-checking,
avoidance of medical settings, sleep disruption, and anxious threat
expectancy while preserving appropriate medical evaluation.

## Background of the invention

Illness Anxiety Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
condition in which preoccupation with having or acquiring a serious
illness persists despite minimal or absent somatic symptoms and is
accompanied by high health anxiety, excessive health-related
behaviors, or maladaptive avoidance. The core impairment is not the
severity of somatic symptoms themselves but the persistent
misinterpretation of ordinary bodily sensations or benign health
information as evidence of severe disease.

The prevalence of IAD is modest but clinically meaningful across
primary care, specialty clinics, and psychiatry settings. The
clinical burden includes chronic reassurance seeking, repeated
self-examination, compulsive internet or medical research,
healthcare overutilization in some subjects, healthcare avoidance
in others, impaired work and relationship function, poor sleep, and
high comorbidity with anxiety, depression, obsessive-compulsive
features, and trauma-spectrum symptoms. The disorder often becomes
self-reinforcing because transient physiologic arousal is read as
evidence of illness.

The current standard of care is primarily psychotherapeutic and
longitudinal. It consists of psychoeducation, cognitive-behavioral
therapy, structured primary-care follow-up, limitation of
reassurance cycles, and empiric use of antidepressants borrowed
from broader anxiety practice. No pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
specifically for IAD as a primary indication. A clinically
meaningful gap remains for a regimen that reduces both health-
related threat expectancy and the circadian / sleep disruption that
amplifies nocturnal and early-morning illness vigilance.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, IAD frequently includes sleep disruption,
nighttime symptom monitoring, circadian disorganization, and
inability to disengage from bodily vigilance at the end of the day.
Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. This combination supports resynchronization of circadian
timing, improvement in sleep architecture, and reduction of
nighttime hyperarousal. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses
the *circadian, sleep-linked, and nocturnal hypervigilance*
substrate of IAD.

Second, IAD also includes anxious threat expectancy, catastrophic
misinterpretation of benign sensations, and reduced serotonergic
resilience in the face of health uncertainty. Vilazodone
(Viibryd®) is an orally available antidepressant that combines
selective serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the
5-HT1A receptor. This dual mechanism supports attenuation of
anxious distress and intrusive threat-related cognition while
maintaining a recognizable anxiety-disorder pharmacology profile.
The vilazodone axis therefore addresses the *anxious-serotonergic
and health-threat amplification* substrate of IAD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
with 5-HT2C antagonism in combination with a serotonin reuptake
inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial agonist for IAD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that circadian stabilization and
nocturnal threat reduction (an agomelatine axis) and anxious-
serotonergic attenuation of catastrophic health beliefs (a
vilazodone axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for
treatment of IAD. The combination is expected to produce greater
symptomatic improvement in illness preoccupation, reassurance
seeking, health-related avoidance, and sleep disturbance than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose, while
preserving appropriate medical care and not discouraging evaluation
of clinically significant new symptoms.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Illness Anxiety Disorder in a human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of agomelatine and a therapeutically effective
amount of vilazodone. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of IAD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with structured
follow-up and continued appropriate medical evaluation.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a
preferred initiation dose of 25 mg nightly and a preferred
escalation to 50 mg nightly after about 2 weeks if clinically
indicated. The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of
about 10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with
food, with a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration
from 10 mg/day. The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement
of circadian-melatonergic regulation and serotonergic / 5-HT1A
stabilization in a health-anxiety syndrome.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with IAD, in illness-preoccupation severity, reassurance seeking,
health-related avoidance, and sleep disruption, that is greater
than the sum of the individual effects of agomelatine and
vilazodone administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Illness Anxiety Disorder"* or *"IAD"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11
condition in which preoccupation with having or acquiring a serious
illness persists despite absent or only mild somatic symptoms and
is accompanied by high health anxiety and excessive health-related
behavior or maladaptive avoidance.

*"Illness-preoccupation score"* means a clinically meaningful burden
of health anxiety, illness-related preoccupation, catastrophic
misinterpretation of bodily sensations, or reassurance-seeking,
quantified by structured clinician assessment or validated health-
anxiety instruments.

*"Appropriate medical evaluation"* means medically indicated
assessment, monitoring, or treatment of new red-flag symptoms or
recognized health conditions according to accepted clinical
judgment, rather than withholding evaluation inappropriately.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of IAD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
illness-preoccupation score, reassurance-seeking frequency,
health-anxiety severity, sleep-quality score, healthcare-
utilization burden, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of agomelatine is reduction of circadian instability,
nocturnal hypervigilance, and sleep-linked threat amplification.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious distress and catastrophic health-related cognition. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
vilazodone is reduction of health-threat expectancy and intrusive
illness preoccupation.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as an agomelatine tablet and a
vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the agomelatine composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers. Excipients useful for
the vilazodone composition include diluents (microcrystalline
cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders (hydroxypropyl
methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants (crospovidone, sodium
starch glycolate), lubricants (magnesium stearate), glidants
(colloidal silicon dioxide), and film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime administration. Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an
oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
agomelatine tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of vilazodone tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to structured follow-up,
measurement-based symptom tracking, and continued appropriate
medical care.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with IAD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days, titratable
  to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.

Broad genus ranges include agomelatine 25-50 mg/day and vilazodone
10-40 mg/day. The preferred schedule administers agomelatine at
bedtime and vilazodone once daily with food.

Medication-medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors and requires hepatic monitoring in
appropriate patients. Vilazodone dose adjustment may be required
with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. The regimen is not intended to
replace evaluation of clinically significant new symptoms.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
IAD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 18 years; persistent preoccupation with having or
acquiring serious illness despite absent or mild somatic symptoms;
clinically meaningful distress or impairment; and willingness to
participate in structured follow-up. Exclusion criteria include
delusional disorder with fixed somatic delusions, untreated primary
psychotic disorder, severe hepatic disease, and known
hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

IAD often includes two interacting layers: persistent health-threat
expectancy and catastrophic interpretation of benign sensations,
and nocturnal / circadian amplification of bodily vigilance.

Agomelatine addresses the circadian-hypervigilance layer through
MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. Vilazodone addresses the
health-threat expectancy layer through SERT inhibition and 5-HT1A
partial agonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor
level and converge on the same illness-preoccupation phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in IAD burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 31,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays
for agomelatine; SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; and combined sleep-threat modulation assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include anxiety and uncertainty models, interoceptive threat
paradigms, circadian-shift paradigms, and body-signal vigilance
models. A supra-additive effect of the combination on reduction of
health-threat responses and sleep-linked hyperarousal, relative to
either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is the
principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in
§ 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
agomelatine 25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone
titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, liver-function monitoring, ECG
monitoring, and tolerability assessments are obtained in each
period. The principal purpose is to establish tolerability and
absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult IAD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): agomelatine + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with structured follow-up and
  CBT-informed care.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in an illness-
preoccupation score at week 12. Secondary endpoints include
reassurance-seeking frequency, health-anxiety severity, sleep-
quality score, healthcare-utilization burden, and CGI-I.

* Anxiety-related patents and cannabinoid or other anxiolytic

  teach the present pair or the present illness-anxiety indication.
* Generic agomelatine and vilazodone formulation patents disclose

* Anxiety-disorder combination filings involving other receptor

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (MT1 / MT2 agonism +
5-HT2C antagonism + SERT inhibition + 5-HT1A partial agonism),
(ii) the specific actives (agomelatine + vilazodone), and
(iii) the specific indication (IAD). No prior-art disclosure
suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on which the present
invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Illness Anxiety Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping circadian-hypervigilance and
health-threat expectancy circuits implicated in persistent illness
preoccupation and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in illness preoccupation, reassurance seeking, and
sleep-related health vigilance than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose while preserving appropriate medical
evaluation.
