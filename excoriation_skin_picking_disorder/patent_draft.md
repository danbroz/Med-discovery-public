## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Excoriation (Skin Picking) Disorder by
co-administering (a) a serotonergic antidepressant having serotonin
reuptake inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonist activity, in
particular vilazodone, and (b) a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse
agonist / antagonist, in particular pimavanserin. The invention is
intended for adolescent and adult subjects with recurrent skin
picking causing lesions and addresses urge build-up, maladaptive
salience of cutaneous imperfections, repetitive action release, and
cue-triggered perseveration while continuing behavioral treatment
and appropriate dermatologic care. The invention is not intended as
a primary dermatologic treatment for structural skin disease.

## Background of the invention

Excoriation (Skin Picking) Disorder is an obsessive-compulsive and
related disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by recurrent skin
picking resulting in skin lesions, repeated attempts to decrease or
stop the behavior, and clinically significant distress or
impairment. The picking may involve the face, arms, hands, scalp,
cuticles, trunk, or other body sites and is often preceded by
tension, boredom, aversive affect, or perception of skin
imperfection, and followed by transient relief or gratification.

The prevalence of Excoriation Disorder is generally estimated in the
low single digits but is under-recognized and highly impairing. The
clinical burden includes scarring, infection risk, bleeding,
dermatologic burden, shame, social avoidance, occupational
disruption, and high comorbidity with anxiety, depression,
obsessive-compulsive symptoms, body dysmorphic symptoms, and other
body-focused repetitive behaviors.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological in the first
instance and consists primarily of habit-reversal training,
stimulus control, acceptance- and awareness-based behavioral
interventions, and dermatologic wound care. No pharmaceutical
product is approved by the United States Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) specifically for the treatment of Excoriation
Disorder as a primary indication. Off-label medication approaches
such as SSRIs or other agents are inconsistent and often only
partially effective, leaving a need for a more mechanistically
coherent pharmacologic strategy.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, Excoriation Disorder frequently includes anxious
build-up, intrusive self-monitoring, perfectionistic or aversive
focus on minor cutaneous irregularities, and repetitive urge
amplification. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available
antidepressant that combines selective serotonin reuptake
inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual
mechanism supports attenuation of anxious-compulsive distress and
intrusive urge-related cognition. The vilazodone axis therefore
addresses the *urge-amplification and anxious-compulsive distress*
substrate of Excoriation Disorder.

Second, Excoriation Disorder also includes maladaptive salience
tagging of skin texture, blemishes, scabs, or perceived
imperfections, with rigid attentional capture and repetitive action
release once the picking sequence begins. Pimavanserin
(Nuplazid®) is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. Although developed for another
indication, its selective 5-HT2A mechanism provides a way to
attenuate cue-linked salience and perseverative focus without
adding direct D2 blockade burden. The pimavanserin axis therefore
addresses the *cutaneous-cue salience and repetitive-perseveration*
substrate of Excoriation Disorder.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist in combination with a selective 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist for Excoriation Disorder specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of urge-related
anxious amplification (a vilazodone axis) and attenuation of
maladaptive salience tagging to cutaneous cues (a pimavanserin
axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of
Excoriation Disorder. The combination is expected to produce
greater symptomatic improvement in picking severity, urge burden,
lesion burden, and quality of life than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) pimavanserin or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Excoriation (Skin Picking) Disorder in a human subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone and a
therapeutically effective amount of pimavanserin. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Excoriation
Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in
conjunction with habit-reversal or other behavioral treatment and
appropriate dermatologic care.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with
a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from
10 mg/day. The invention contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose
of about 17 mg to about 34 mg administered orally once daily, with
a preferred adult dose of 34 mg/day. The inventive feature is the
concurrent engagement of serotonergic urge-modulation and 5-HT2A-
linked cutaneous salience modulation in a body-focused repetitive
behavior.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with Excoriation Disorder, in picking frequency, urge severity,
cue-triggered picking, lesion burden, and functional impairment,
that is greater than the sum of the individual effects of
vilazodone and pimavanserin administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically equivalent
5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Excoriation (Skin Picking) Disorder"* or *"Excoriation Disorder"*
means the obsessive-compulsive and related disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by recurrent skin picking resulting in skin
lesions, repeated attempts to decrease or stop the behavior, and
clinically significant distress or impairment.

*"Skin-picking-severity score"* means a clinically meaningful burden
of picking frequency, urge severity, resistance difficulty, lesion
burden, or associated distress quantified by structured clinician
assessment or validated excoriation instruments.

*"Lesion burden"* means the extent, number, severity, healing
status, or distribution of skin lesions attributable to recurrent
picking, as assessed by structured dermatologic or clinician-rated
measurement.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of Excoriation Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed
herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least
one of: skin-picking-severity score, urge severity, lesion burden,
time spent picking, quality of life, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious-compulsive distress and intrusive urge-related cognition.
In the present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
vilazodone is reduction of urge build-up and urge-linked anxious
amplification.

#### 2.2 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. It has no clinically meaningful
affinity for dopamine D2 receptors. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of pimavanserin is attenuation
of maladaptive salience assigned to skin irregularities and
perseverative attentional capture around picking cues.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
pimavanserin, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as a vilazodone
tablet and a pimavanserin capsule or tablet.

Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers. Excipients useful for the pimavanserin
composition include capsule fill excipients, diluents,
disintegrants, and stabilizers suitable for marketed pimavanserin
formulation profiles.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an oral tablet taken with
food. Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or
tablet suitable for once-daily use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of pimavanserin capsules or
tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to habit-reversal
treatment, lesion monitoring, and appropriate wound care.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with Excoriation Disorder, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.
* Pimavanserin 17 mg orally once daily, titratable to 34 mg once
  daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.

Broad genus ranges include vilazodone 10-40 mg/day and pimavanserin
17-34 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses once-daily vilazodone
with food and once-daily pimavanserin.

Medication-medication interactions: vilazodone dose adjustment may
be required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Corrected QT interval
monitoring should be considered when pimavanserin is combined with
other QT-prolonging agents. The regimen is intended to complement,
not replace, dermatologic treatment of infection, wound care, or
other skin disease when present.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
Excoriation Disorder. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: age >= 12 years; recurrent skin
picking causing lesions; repeated attempts to stop; clinically
meaningful distress or impairment; and willingness to participate
in behavioral treatment. Exclusion criteria include untreated
primary psychotic disorder, severe dermatologic disease requiring
active separate medical management before inclusion, severe hepatic
impairment, clinically significant baseline QT prolongation, and
known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Excoriation Disorder often includes two interacting layers:
recurrent anxious urge build-up and intrusive body-focused
attention, and maladaptive salience assigned to skin texture,
blemishes, or healing lesions that drives repetitive action
release.

Vilazodone addresses the urge-amplification layer through SERT
inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Pimavanserin addresses the
cutaneous-cue salience layer through selective 5-HT2A inverse
agonism / antagonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the
receptor level and converge on the same repetitive-picking
phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in Excoriation Disorder burden than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; 5-HT2A binding and inverse-agonism assays for
pimavanserin; and combined salience / repetitive-behavior network
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include grooming and repetitive-touching models, cue-triggered
repetitive-behavior tasks, compulsive grooming paradigms, and
perseverative attentional-capture models. A supra-additive effect
of the combination on reduction of repetitive picking-like behavior
and urge-related salience, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2:
pimavanserin 34 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult Excoriation Disorder
(FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 150
(50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + pimavanserin.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for
  pimavanserin.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with structured behavioral
  treatment.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in skin-picking-
severity total at week 12. Secondary endpoints include urge
severity, lesion burden, time spent picking, quality of life, and
CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Excoriation (Skin Picking) Disorder comprising co-administration of
a therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone and a
therapeutically effective amount of pimavanserin are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping urge-amplification and cutaneous-
cue salience circuits implicated in recurrent skin picking and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in
skin-picking severity, lesion burden, and quality of life than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
