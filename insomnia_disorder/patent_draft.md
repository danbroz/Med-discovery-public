## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Insomnia Disorder by co-administering
(a) an orexin receptor antagonist and (b) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2
receptor agonist with 5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular the
combination of suvorexant and agomelatine. The invention is directed
to adult subjects with difficulty initiating sleep, maintaining sleep,
or achieving restorative sleep despite adequate sleep opportunity, and
addresses nocturnal hyperarousal, unstable sleep timing, sleep
fragmentation, and impaired next-day functioning. The invention is not
intended to replace cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia, to
support dangerous sedative stacking, or to treat untreated severe
sleep-disordered breathing.

## Background of the invention

Insomnia Disorder is a chronic sleep-wake disorder characterized by
difficulty initiating sleep, difficulty maintaining sleep, early-
morning awakening, or nonrestorative sleep associated with clinically
meaningful distress or daytime impairment. The disorder may be acute
or chronic in perception, but chronic insomnia often involves a
self-reinforcing loop of conditioned arousal, irregular sleep timing,
worry about sleep, and next-day functional deterioration.

The burden of insomnia is substantial. Affected subjects commonly
experience fatigue, cognitive inefficiency, irritability, anxiety,
reduced work performance, accident risk, cardiometabolic stress, and
worsening of comorbid psychiatric or medical conditions. Many
patients also experience a mismatch between excessive nighttime wake
drive and inadequate circadian support for consolidated restorative
sleep.

Current therapy remains incomplete. Cognitive behavioral therapy for
insomnia (CBT-I) is first-line and essential, but access and adherence
are variable. Existing hypnotics may improve sleep acutely but can
carry tolerance, next-day impairment, anticholinergic burden, or
GABAergic dependence concerns. Orexin antagonists represent an
important advance, yet many subjects continue to experience unstable
sleep timing, incomplete sleep-quality recovery, or residual insomnia
despite treatment. There remains a need for a rational combination
that addresses both nocturnal wake drive and circadian / sleep-recovery
biology.

The mechanistic gap addressed by the present invention has two layers.
First, suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist that reduces
orexin-mediated wakefulness and thereby facilitates sleep onset and
maintenance without relying on direct GABA-A potentiation. Second,
agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors, supporting circadian alignment, subjective sleep quality,
and sleep-linked recovery.

The inventors recognized that these two mechanisms are complementary.
Suvorexant addresses the hyperaroused wake-maintenance layer that
prevents or interrupts sleep. Agomelatine addresses the circadian and
sleep-recovery layer that determines whether sleep occurs at the right
time and restores function by morning. Together they are expected to
produce greater symptomatic improvement in insomnia severity, sleep
continuity, and next-day functioning than either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose.

Prior-art searching identified insomnia-treatment references,
orexin-modulator references, agomelatine formulation references, and
general sleep-disorder references. Those disclosures do not teach the
specific combination of suvorexant and agomelatine for Insomnia
Disorder, nor the dual wake-drive reduction plus circadian-recovery
framework disclosed herein.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) suvorexant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof and (b) agomelatine or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Insomnia Disorder in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering a therapeutically effective amount of suvorexant and
a therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered within
a window of about 24 hours of the other, and preferably timed so that
both actives support the intended sleep episode.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) suvorexant in a first dosage form, (b) agomelatine in a second
dosage form, and (c) written instructions for use in a subject with
Insomnia Disorder, including guidance regarding bedtime timing,
day-after impairment precautions, and concurrent use of CBT-I or
sleep-hygiene intervention.

The invention contemplates suvorexant in a daily dose of about
5 mg to about 20 mg administered orally in the target sleep window.
The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime or shortly before
intended sleep onset.

The technical effect of the combination is expected to be greater than
additive improvement in one or more endpoints selected from the group
consisting of insomnia-severity score, sleep-onset latency, wake after
sleep onset, total sleep time, sleep quality, next-day functioning,
and Clinical Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I), compared with
either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Insomnia Disorder"* means insomnia disorder as defined by DSM-5-TR,
ICD-11, or an equivalent diagnostic framework, characterized by
difficulty initiating sleep, maintaining sleep, or obtaining
restorative sleep despite adequate sleep opportunity, together with
clinically meaningful distress or impairment.

*"Suvorexant"* means the dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia and includes the free base, salts, polymorphs, solvates,
hydrates, prodrugs, labeled forms, and pharmacologically equivalent
orexin receptor antagonists with a clinically comparable
sleep-promoting profile.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide and pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, co-crystals,
prodrugs, and metabolically equivalent forms that retain clinically
relevant MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount that produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in one or more insomnia endpoints
when used according to a regimen disclosed herein.

### 2. Active agents

#### 2.1 Suvorexant

Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist that reduces
orexin-mediated wake drive and facilitates sleep onset and
maintenance. In the present invention, its principal contribution is
reduction of nocturnal hyperarousal and persistent wake pressure.

#### 2.2 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, its principal contribution is
support of circadian alignment and restoration of sleep-linked
recovery.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise suvorexant and
agomelatine together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients. The actives may be provided as separate oral
tablets in a co-packaged kit or as a synchronized bedtime blister-card
configuration.

Suitable excipients include common oral-tablet diluents, binders,
disintegrants, lubricants, coating systems, and release-control
materials. In preferred embodiments suvorexant is supplied as an
immediate-release bedtime tablet and agomelatine as an immediate-
release tablet given at or shortly before intended sleep onset.

### 4. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with Insomnia Disorder, a preferred regimen
comprises:

* Suvorexant 5 mg to 20 mg orally in the target sleep window.
* Agomelatine 25 mg to 50 mg orally at bedtime or shortly before the
  intended sleep onset.

In certain embodiments agomelatine is initiated first in subjects with
prominent circadian or sleep-timing instability and suvorexant is
added for persistent nocturnal wake drive. In other embodiments
suvorexant is initiated first for sleep-onset or maintenance failure
and agomelatine is layered in when overnight recovery or sleep timing
remains unstable.

In preferred embodiments CBT-I, sleep-hygiene measures, stimulus
control, or other standard behavioral treatment remains in place.

### 5. Subject populations

Preferred subjects are adults with clinician-confirmed Insomnia
Disorder and persistent difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep
despite behavioral interventions, prior hypnotics, or both. Certain
embodiments focus on subjects with nocturnal hyperarousal, irregular
sleep timing, poor next-day recovery, or mixed sleep-onset plus
maintenance insomnia.

Exclusion criteria for preferred embodiments include untreated severe
obstructive sleep apnea or hypoventilation, narcolepsy-spectrum
disorders, unsafe sedative polypharmacy, active parasomnia worsened by
sleep agents, severe hepatic impairment incompatible with agomelatine
use, or a setting in which safety-critical overnight wakefulness is
required without risk management.

### 6. Mechanistic rationale

Insomnia often persists because excessive wake drive and inadequate
sleep-timing support reinforce one another. A subject who cannot
deactivate nocturnal orexin-driven arousal may fail to fall asleep,
while a subject whose circadian timing and overnight recovery remain
misaligned may continue to experience unstable, low-quality sleep even
when a hypnotic effect is present.

Suvorexant addresses the wake-drive layer through orexin antagonism.
Agomelatine addresses the circadian and recovery layer through MT1 /
MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. The combination is therefore
expected to produce larger improvements in insomnia severity, sleep
continuity, and next-day function than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.

### 7. Administration and monitoring

Monitoring may include Insomnia Severity Index, sleep diary,
sleep-onset latency, wake after sleep onset, total sleep time, next-
day functioning, liver-function tests, daytime sedation assessment,
and CGI-I.

The regimen is not intended to replace CBT-I, sleep scheduling,
evaluation for untreated sleep-disordered breathing, or management of
underlying psychiatric or medical contributors to insomnia.

### 8. Prophetic clinical study

A prophetic Phase 2 study enrolls adults with Insomnia Disorder,
baseline elevated insomnia severity, and persistent difficulty with
sleep initiation or maintenance despite standard advice or behavioral
care. Subjects are randomized to: (a) suvorexant plus agomelatine;
(b) suvorexant plus placebo; or (c) placebo plus standard of care, for
8 weeks.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in insomnia-severity
score. Key secondary endpoints include sleep-onset latency, wake after
sleep onset, total sleep time, next-day functioning, and CGI-I. The
combination arm is expected to show the largest improvement in both
nocturnal symptoms and restorative next-day function. FIGS. 3-5 are
prophetic illustrations of those expected outcomes.

### 9. Prior-art distinction

The closest prior-art references identified in the prior-art
landscape include:

* Insomnia-treatment and sleep-disorder references - disclose general
  insomnia pharmacology, but do not teach the specific suvorexant +
  agomelatine combination.
* Orexin-modulator references - disclose orexin antagonists without
  pairing them with agomelatine for Insomnia Disorder.
* Agomelatine formulation references - disclose agomelatine as an
  individual compound, not the present dual wake-drive reduction plus
  circadian-recovery strategy.

The present invention is distinct in the specific receptor classes
combined, the specific actives chosen, and the symptom-layered
strategy of coupling nocturnal wake-drive attenuation with melatonergic
recovery support in Insomnia Disorder.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Insomnia
Disorder comprising co-administration of a therapeutically effective
amount of the dual orexin receptor antagonist suvorexant and a
therapeutically effective amount of the melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
and 5-HT2C antagonist agomelatine are disclosed. The two agents act
on non-overlapping nocturnal wake-drive and circadian-recovery
pathways relevant to insomnia and are expected to produce greater-
than-additive improvement in sleep initiation, sleep continuity, and
next-day functioning compared with either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.
