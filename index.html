It organizes the neuroscience used to frame the patent-pending assets: symptom biology, neurotransmitter systems, brain circuits, genetic and developmental context, treatment mechanisms, and chapter-level references.

Computational Neuroscience Method

The portfolio starts with disease biology, then maps candidate drug mechanisms onto symptom circuitry and FDA-relevant development questions. The goal is not to claim clinical proof today; it is to give a pharma team a clear, testable starting point for diligence.

1Disease biologyEach indication is grounded in disorder-specific neurobiology: symptoms, circuits, neurotransmitters, brain regions, genetics, and treatment gaps.
2Mechanism mappingCandidate agents are mapped to receptors, transporters, neuroendocrine systems, sleep-wake systems, arousal biology, and safety constraints.
3Computational model layerModel outputs connect drug mechanisms to disease phenotypes, latent variables, endpoints, and predicted development risks.
4Evidence labelingManuscripts separate established biology from repository-derived, modeled, prophetic, and data-needed claims so diligence can focus on the next experiments.
5Pharma translationEach asset is framed around formulation feasibility, component contribution, clinical pharmacology, endpoint selection, and FDA review risk.
