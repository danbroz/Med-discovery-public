## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) by co-administering
(a) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with 5-HT2C antagonist
activity and (b) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with 5-HT1A partial
agonist activity, in particular the combination of agomelatine and
vilazodone. The invention is directed to adult subjects with episodic
or recurrent MDD and addresses depressed mood, anhedonia, anxious
rumination, sleep disruption, circadian flattening, fatigue, and
functional impairment. The invention is not intended to replace
evaluation for bipolar-spectrum illness, psychotic depression, or
suicidal crisis requiring higher-acuity intervention.

## Background of the invention

Major Depressive Disorder is a common and disabling psychiatric
disorder characterized by depressed mood and/or loss of interest or
pleasure together with changes in sleep, appetite, energy,
concentration, psychomotor function, guilt, hopelessness, or suicidal
thinking. The disorder can be recurrent, severe, and highly impairing
across occupational, social, and physical-health domains.

The clinical and commercial burden of MDD is extremely high. A large
proportion of patients experience partial response, recurrent
episodes, treatment-emergent sleep problems, or persistent cognitive-
emotional blunting despite antidepressant exposure. Even when core
mood symptoms improve, fatigue, poor sleep recovery, anxious
rumination, and reduced motivation often remain significant barriers
to remission and functional return.

Current pharmacotherapy is dominated by serotonin-based strategies,
with variable tolerability and incomplete remission. Standard SSRIs,
SNRIs, atypical antidepressants, and augmentation approaches are all
used, but no single oral combination product is specifically designed
to engage both the anxious-serotonergic substrate of MDD and the
circadian / sleep-recovery substrate that often perpetuates the
episode. There remains a need for a mechanism-based combination that
addresses both daytime depressive-ruminative burden and nighttime
recovery failure.

The mechanistic gap addressed by the present invention has two layers.
First, vilazodone is an orally available antidepressant that combines
serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A
receptor. This dual mechanism supports attenuation of depressive
rumination, anxious distress, and negative expectancy. Second,
agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. This pharmacology supports circadian resynchronization,
improvement in sleep architecture, and restoration of sleep-linked
recovery and motivational tone.

The inventors recognized that these mechanisms are complementary and
non-redundant in MDD. Vilazodone improves the serotonergic and 5-HT1A-
linked daytime emotional-cognitive substrate of depression, while
agomelatine improves circadian alignment and overnight restoration.
Together, the combination is expected to produce greater improvement
in depressive severity, sleep quality, and return of function than
either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose.

Prior-art searching identified agomelatine formulation references,
vilazodone formulation references, depressive-symptom treatment
references, and broader combination methods involving sleep or mood
therapeutics. Those disclosures do not teach the specific combination
of agomelatine and vilazodone for Major Depressive Disorder, nor the
explicit framework of coupling daytime serotonergic / 5-HT1A support
with nighttime melatonergic recovery support.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof and
(b) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating Major
Depressive Disorder in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering a therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and
a therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone. In preferred
embodiments, vilazodone is administered once daily with food and
agomelatine is administered at bedtime.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine in a first dosage form, (b) vilazodone in a second
dosage form, and (c) written instructions for use in subjects with
MDD, including guidance regarding titration, liver-function
monitoring, sleep regularity, and continued psychotherapy or other
standard supportive care where appropriate.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime. The invention
contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about 10 mg to about 40 mg
administered orally once daily with food after titration.

The technical effect of the combination is expected to be greater than
additive improvement in one or more endpoints selected from the group
consisting of depressive-severity score, remission rate, response
rate, sleep quality, anhedonia burden, role function, and Clinical
Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I), compared with either
monotherapy at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Major Depressive Disorder"* or *"MDD"* means major depressive
disorder as defined by DSM-5-TR, ICD-11, or an equivalent diagnostic
framework, characterized by one or more major depressive episodes with
clinically meaningful distress or impairment.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide and pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, co-crystals,
prodrugs, and metabolically equivalent forms that retain clinically
relevant MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism.

*"Vilazodone"* means the serotonin reuptake inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial
agonist and pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates,
polymorphs, prodrugs, and metabolically equivalent analogues that
retain clinically relevant transporter inhibition and 5-HT1A partial
agonism.

*"Depressive-severity score"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
depressed mood, anhedonia, hopelessness, fatigue, anxious rumination,
sleep disruption, or functional impairment quantified by structured
clinician assessment or validated depressive-symptom instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount that produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in one or more MDD endpoints when
used according to a regimen disclosed herein.

### 2. Active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, its principal contribution is
restoration of sleep-linked recovery, circadian alignment, and
motivational tone.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available serotonin reuptake inhibitor and
5-HT1A partial agonist. In the present invention, its principal
contribution is attenuation of depressive rumination, anxious distress,
and maladaptive negative expectancy.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
vilazodone together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients. The actives may be provided as separate oral
tablets in a co-packaged kit or as a day-night blister configuration
linking vilazodone daytime dosing with agomelatine bedtime dosing.

Suitable excipients include common oral-tablet diluents, binders,
disintegrants, lubricants, coating systems, and release-control
materials. In preferred embodiments agomelatine is supplied as an
immediate-release bedtime tablet and vilazodone as an immediate-
release tablet taken with food.

### 4. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with Major Depressive Disorder, a preferred regimen
comprises:

* Agomelatine 25 mg to 50 mg orally at bedtime.
* Vilazodone 10 mg to 40 mg orally once daily with food.

In certain embodiments vilazodone is titrated from 10 mg to 20 mg and
then to 40 mg as tolerated. In certain embodiments agomelatine is
initiated at 25 mg nightly and escalated to 50 mg nightly after about
2 weeks if clinically indicated and tolerated.

In preferred embodiments psychotherapy, measurement-based care, or
other standard supportive treatment remains in place so that
incremental benefit on depressive and sleep endpoints can be
meaningfully assessed.

### 5. Subject populations

Preferred subjects are adults with clinician-confirmed MDD and
persistent depressed mood, anhedonia, anxious rumination, insomnia,
fatigue, or functional impairment despite prior psychotherapy,
serotonergic treatment, or both. Certain embodiments focus on
subjects with depressive episodes marked by poor sleep recovery,
circadian flattening, or early-morning worsening.

Exclusion criteria for preferred embodiments include active bipolar-
spectrum illness, psychotic depression requiring alternate management,
acute suicidal crisis requiring higher-acuity intervention, severe
hepatic impairment incompatible with agomelatine use, or intolerance
to serotonergic agents.

### 6. Mechanistic rationale

MDD often persists because daytime depressive-ruminative burden and
nighttime recovery failure reinforce one another. A subject who sleeps
poorly enters the next day with worse fatigue, lower resilience, and
greater negative bias, while a subject with persistent rumination may
remain unable to achieve restorative sleep or circadian regularity.

Vilazodone addresses the daytime depressive-ruminative layer through
serotonin reuptake inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Agomelatine
addresses the sleep-recovery and circadian layer through MT1 / MT2
agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. The combination is therefore expected
to produce larger improvements in depressive severity, sleep quality,
motivation, and daily function than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.

### 7. Administration and monitoring

Monitoring may include MADRS, HAM-D, PHQ-9, sleep-quality assessment,
anhedonia tracking, liver-function tests, GI tolerability, sexual-side-
effect assessment, role function, and CGI-I.

The regimen is not intended to replace psychotherapy, evaluation for
bipolar-spectrum illness, safety planning for suicidality, or other
standard psychiatric care that remains essential in MDD treatment.

### 8. Prophetic clinical study

A prophetic Phase 2 study enrolls adults with MDD, baseline elevated
depressive severity, and persistent sleep disruption or anxious
rumination. Subjects are randomized to: (a) agomelatine plus
vilazodone; (b) vilazodone plus placebo; or (c) placebo plus stable
standard of care, for 8 to 12 weeks after titration.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in depressive-severity
score. Key secondary endpoints include remission rate, response rate,
sleep quality, anhedonia burden, role function, and CGI-I. The
combination arm is expected to show the largest improvement in both
depressive symptoms and restorative overnight recovery. FIGS. 3-5 are
prophetic illustrations of those expected outcomes.

### 9. Prior-art distinction

The closest prior-art references identified in the prior-art
landscape include:

* Depressive-symptom treatment references - disclose MDD treatment
  concepts generally, but do not teach the specific agomelatine +
  vilazodone combination.
* Agomelatine and vilazodone formulation references - disclose the
  individual compounds, not the present combination strategy.
* Combination methods including sleep therapeutics for mood - do not
  teach the specific receptor-class pairing and dosing framework
  disclosed herein.

The present invention is distinct in the specific receptor classes
combined, the specific actives chosen, and the flagship MDD framework
of coupling daytime serotonergic / 5-HT1A support with nighttime
melatonergic recovery support.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Major
Depressive Disorder comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of the melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist and 5-HT2C
antagonist agomelatine and a therapeutically effective amount of the
serotonin reuptake inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial agonist vilazodone are
disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping daytime depressive-
ruminative and nighttime sleep-recovery pathways relevant to MDD and
are expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in
depressive severity, remission, and functional recovery compared with
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
