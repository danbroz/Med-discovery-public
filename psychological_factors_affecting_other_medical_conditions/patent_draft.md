## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Psychological Factors Affecting Other Medical
Conditions (PFAMC) by co-administering (a) a serotonin reuptake
inhibitor with 5-HT1A partial agonist activity, in particular
vilazodone, and (b) a beta-adrenoceptor antagonist, in particular
betaxolol. The invention is intended for subjects in whom
psychological or behavioral factors measurably worsen the course,
symptoms, or treatment adherence of another medical condition, and
addresses anxious distress, hypervigilance, autonomic amplification,
and behaviorally mediated exacerbation while continuing standard
care for the underlying medical condition.

## Background of the invention

Psychological Factors Affecting Other Medical Conditions is
recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 when psychological or behavioral
factors adversely affect the course of a medical condition,
interfere with its treatment, constitute additional health risks,
or influence the underlying pathophysiology. The diagnosis is not
limited to any single disease domain. It may occur in asthma,
cardiovascular disease, chronic pain, migraine, inflammatory bowel
disease, diabetes, dermatologic conditions, functional GI
syndromes, and other medical conditions where stress-related or
behavioral mechanisms worsen disease expression or adherence.

The prevalence of PFAMC is difficult to estimate because it is
usually embedded inside the diagnosis of the underlying medical
illness rather than recorded as a separate syndrome. Nonetheless,
its clinical burden is substantial. Subjects may experience
anxious-preoccupied attention to body signals, autonomic arousal,
nonadherence to treatment, avoidance of beneficial medical care,
exaggerated symptom amplification, or recurrent flare patterns that
track with stress and dysregulation. Caregiver burden, healthcare
utilization, and perceived medical severity often increase.

The current standard of care is supportive and interdisciplinary. It
consists primarily of treating the underlying medical condition,
providing psychoeducation, using psychotherapy or behavioral
interventions, and occasionally adding empiric anxiolytics or
antidepressants. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for
PFAMC as a primary indication. A clinically meaningful gap remains
for a medication regimen that simultaneously reduces anxious-
interpretive amplification and dampens stress-related autonomic
surges that worsen another medical condition.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, PFAMC often includes chronic anxious distress,
intrusive symptom monitoring, intolerance of uncertainty, and poor
cognitive control over threat interpretations related to the
medical condition. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available
antidepressant that combines selective serotonin reuptake
inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual
mechanism supports attenuation of anxious symptoms and intrusive
threat-related cognition and may improve tolerability relative to
some conventional SSRIs. The vilazodone axis therefore addresses
the *anxious-serotonergic and cognitive-threat amplification*
substrate of PFAMC.

Second, PFAMC often includes autonomic surges such as tachycardia,
tremor, sweating, chest tightness, stress-linked dyspnea, or
somatic escalation that directly worsens the course of the
underlying medical condition or impairs adherence. Betaxolol is a
beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist approved for
hypertension and glaucoma that reduces heart rate and adrenergic
surge. Relative to nonselective beta blockers, the beta1-selective
profile may offer a more targeted cardiovascular-autonomic
intervention in medically complex patients where bronchospasm risk
or excessive nonselective beta blockade is undesirable. The
betaxolol axis therefore addresses the *autonomic-amplification
and physiologic stress-reactivity* substrate of PFAMC.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist in combination with a beta1-selective
antagonist for PFAMC specifically.

The present invention recognizes that reduction of anxious-
interpretive amplification (a vilazodone axis) and reduction of
stress-linked autonomic surges (a betaxolol axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of PFAMC. The
combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement
and better disease-management adherence than either agent alone at
any individually achievable dose, while continuing standard care
for the underlying medical condition.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) betaxolol or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, or prodrug thereof,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Psychological Factors Affecting Other Medical Conditions in a human
subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject
a therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone and a
therapeutically effective amount of betaxolol. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) betaxolol or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of PFAMC according
to a dosing regimen disclosed herein while continuing standard care
for the underlying medical condition.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with
a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from
10 mg/day. The invention contemplates betaxolol in a daily dose of
about 2.5 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once or twice
daily, with a preferred dose of 5 mg to 20 mg/day. The inventive
feature is the concurrent engagement of serotonergic threat-
modulation and beta1-selective autonomic stabilization in a subject
whose psychological factors worsen a medical condition.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with PFAMC, in psychological-factor-driven exacerbation of the
underlying medical condition, anxious distress, autonomic surges,
and adherence failure, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of vilazodone and betaxolol administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Betaxolol"* means the beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor
antagonist approved in oral and ophthalmic formulations. The term
includes betaxolol free base, hydrochloride or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
and prodrugs.

*"Psychological Factors Affecting Other Medical Conditions"* or
*"PFAMC"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 syndrome in which
psychological or behavioral factors adversely affect the course,
treatment, risk, or pathophysiology of another medical condition.

*"PFAMC impact score"* means a clinically meaningful composite of
psychological-factor-driven symptom amplification, nonadherence,
stress-linked exacerbation, or functional impairment associated
with the underlying medical condition.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the
treatment of PFAMC according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
PFAMC impact score, autonomic-distress score, adherence /
self-management score, healthcare-utilization burden, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious symptoms, intrusive threat-related cognition, and
anticipatory distress. In the present invention, the principal
mechanistic contribution of vilazodone is reduction of anxious-
interpretive amplification that worsens the course of another
medical condition.

#### 2.2 Betaxolol

Betaxolol is a beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist used in
the treatment of hypertension and glaucoma. In oral use, betaxolol
reduces heart rate, adrenergic drive, and somatic distress. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
betaxolol is reduction of stress-linked autonomic surges that
amplify medical symptoms, impair self-management, or trigger
avoidance behavior.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
betaxolol, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a vilazodone tablet and a
betaxolol tablet.

Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers. Excipients useful for the betaxolol
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and coating systems suitable for stable oral administration.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an immediate-release oral
tablet taken with food. Betaxolol is preferably formulated as an
immediate-release oral tablet.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of betaxolol tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to continuing care of the
underlying medical condition and monitoring of symptom triggers.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with PFAMC, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.
* Betaxolol 5 mg orally once daily on day 1, titratable to 10 mg
  or 20 mg/day according to heart rate, blood pressure, and
  tolerability.

Broad genus ranges include vilazodone 10–40 mg/day and betaxolol
2.5–40 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses daytime vilazodone with
food and once- or twice-daily betaxolol depending on autonomic-
symptom load.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: clinically meaningful hepatic
impairment is exclusionary or requires careful monitoring where
vilazodone exposure may rise. Severe bradycardia, unstable
hypotension, decompensated heart failure, or clinically relevant
conduction abnormality are contraindications to betaxolol use in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: vilazodone dose adjustment may
be required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Betaxolol should be used
with caution alongside other beta blockers, calcium-channel
blockers, or agents that worsen bradycardia.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
PFAMC. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: clinician-confirmed psychological or behavioral factors
that measurably worsen the course, symptoms, or treatment
adherence of another medical condition; clinically meaningful
anxious distress or autonomic reactivity at baseline; and ongoing
standard care for the underlying medical illness. Exclusion
criteria include primary psychiatric disorders in which the
underlying medical condition is not being materially affected,
severe bradycardia, unstable cardiovascular disease, severe hepatic
or renal impairment, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

PFAMC is maintained by two interacting layers: an anxious-cognitive
layer in which symptom monitoring, threat interpretation, and
catastrophic appraisal worsen illness burden, and an autonomic
physiologic layer in which stress-linked adrenergic surges directly
amplify disease expression or reduce adherence.

Vilazodone addresses the anxious-cognitive layer through serotonin
reuptake inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Betaxolol addresses
the autonomic physiologic layer through beta1-selective reduction of
adrenergic output. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor
level and converge on the PFAMC impact phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in psychologically driven medical exacerbation
and better functional adherence than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; beta1-adrenoceptor binding and antagonism assays for
betaxolol; and combined stress-hormone / autonomic signaling
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include stress-potentiated autonomic tasks, anxiety-like behavior
paradigms, conditioned symptom-amplification paradigms, and disease-
relevant models where stress worsens medical outcomes. A supra-
additive effect of the combination on autonomic stabilization and
reduced threat amplification, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: betaxolol
10 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full
PK sampling, heart-rate and blood-pressure monitoring, and CNS
tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal
purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of clinically
meaningful PK or cardiovascular interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adults with PFAMC (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + betaxolol.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for betaxolol.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard-of-care
  interdisciplinary medical management.

All subjects continue standard care for the underlying medical
condition. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in PFAMC
impact score at week 12. Secondary endpoints include autonomic-
distress score, adherence / self-management score, responder rate,
healthcare-utilization burden, and CGI-I.

within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) betaxolol or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of PFAMC according
to a dosing regimen disclosed herein while continuing standard care
for the underlying medical condition.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with
a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from
10 mg/day. The invention contemplates betaxolol in a daily dose of
about 2.5 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once or twice
daily, with a preferred dose of 5 mg to 20 mg/day. The inventive
feature is the concurrent engagement of serotonergic threat-
modulation and beta1-selective autonomic stabilization in a subject
whose psychological factors worsen a medical condition.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with PFAMC, in psychological-factor-driven exacerbation of the
underlying medical condition, anxious distress, autonomic surges,
and adherence failure, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of vilazodone and betaxolol administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Betaxolol"* means the beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor
antagonist approved in oral and ophthalmic formulations. The term
includes betaxolol free base, hydrochloride or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
and prodrugs.

*"Psychological Factors Affecting Other Medical Conditions"* or
*"PFAMC"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 syndrome in which
psychological or behavioral factors adversely affect the course,
treatment, risk, or pathophysiology of another medical condition.

*"PFAMC impact score"* means a clinically meaningful composite of
psychological-factor-driven symptom amplification, nonadherence,
stress-linked exacerbation, or functional impairment associated
with the underlying medical condition.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the
treatment of PFAMC according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
PFAMC impact score, autonomic-distress score, adherence /
self-management score, healthcare-utilization burden, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious symptoms, intrusive threat-related cognition, and
anticipatory distress. In the present invention, the principal
mechanistic contribution of vilazodone is reduction of anxious-
interpretive amplification that worsens the course of another
medical condition.

#### 2.2 Betaxolol

Betaxolol is a beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist used in
the treatment of hypertension and glaucoma. In oral use, betaxolol
reduces heart rate, adrenergic drive, and somatic distress. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
betaxolol is reduction of stress-linked autonomic surges that
amplify medical symptoms, impair self-management, or trigger
avoidance behavior.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
betaxolol, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a vilazodone tablet and a
betaxolol tablet.

Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers. Excipients useful for the betaxolol
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and coating systems suitable for stable oral administration.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an immediate-release oral
tablet taken with food. Betaxolol is preferably formulated as an
immediate-release oral tablet.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of betaxolol tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to continuing care of the
underlying medical condition and monitoring of symptom triggers.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with PFAMC, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.
* Betaxolol 5 mg orally once daily on day 1, titratable to 10 mg
  or 20 mg/day according to heart rate, blood pressure, and
  tolerability.

Broad genus ranges include vilazodone 10–40 mg/day and betaxolol
2.5–40 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses daytime vilazodone with
food and once- or twice-daily betaxolol depending on autonomic-
symptom load.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: clinically meaningful hepatic
impairment is exclusionary or requires careful monitoring where
vilazodone exposure may rise. Severe bradycardia, unstable
hypotension, decompensated heart failure, or clinically relevant
conduction abnormality are contraindications to betaxolol use in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: vilazodone dose adjustment may
be required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Betaxolol should be used
with caution alongside other beta blockers, calcium-channel
blockers, or agents that worsen bradycardia.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
PFAMC. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: clinician-confirmed psychological or behavioral factors
that measurably worsen the course, symptoms, or treatment
adherence of another medical condition; clinically meaningful
anxious distress or autonomic reactivity at baseline; and ongoing
standard care for the underlying medical illness. Exclusion
criteria include primary psychiatric disorders in which the
underlying medical condition is not being materially affected,
severe bradycardia, unstable cardiovascular disease, severe hepatic
or renal impairment, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

PFAMC is maintained by two interacting layers: an anxious-cognitive
layer in which symptom monitoring, threat interpretation, and
catastrophic appraisal worsen illness burden, and an autonomic
physiologic layer in which stress-linked adrenergic surges directly
amplify disease expression or reduce adherence.

Vilazodone addresses the anxious-cognitive layer through serotonin
reuptake inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Betaxolol addresses
the autonomic physiologic layer through beta1-selective reduction of
adrenergic output. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor
level and converge on the PFAMC impact phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in psychologically driven medical exacerbation
and better functional adherence than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; beta1-adrenoceptor binding and antagonism assays for
betaxolol; and combined stress-hormone / autonomic signaling
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include stress-potentiated autonomic tasks, anxiety-like behavior
paradigms, conditioned symptom-amplification paradigms, and disease-
relevant models where stress worsens medical outcomes. A supra-
additive effect of the combination on autonomic stabilization and
reduced threat amplification, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: betaxolol
10 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full
PK sampling, heart-rate and blood-pressure monitoring, and CNS
tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal
purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of clinically
meaningful PK or cardiovascular interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adults with PFAMC (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + betaxolol.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for betaxolol.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard-of-care
  interdisciplinary medical management.

All subjects continue standard care for the underlying medical
condition. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in PFAMC
impact score at week 12. Secondary endpoints include autonomic-
distress score, adherence / self-management score, responder rate,
healthcare-utilization burden, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Psychological Factors Affecting Other Medical Conditions comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of
vilazodone and a therapeutically effective amount of the
beta1-selective antagonist betaxolol are disclosed. The two agents
act on non-overlapping anxious-interpretive and autonomic-surge
circuits implicated in psychologically driven worsening of medical
conditions and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in symptom amplification, adherence failure, and
autonomic distress than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose while continuing standard care for the underlying
medical condition.
