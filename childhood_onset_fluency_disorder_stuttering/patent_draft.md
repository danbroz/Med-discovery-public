## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Childhood-Onset Fluency Disorder (Stuttering)
(COFD) by co-administering (a) a histamine H3 receptor antagonist or
inverse agonist and (b) an oxytocin receptor agonist, in particular
the combination of pitolisant and oxytocin, in dosage forms and on
dosing schedules timed to scheduled fluency-shaping or
stuttering-modification intervention sessions.

## Background of the invention

Childhood-Onset Fluency Disorder (Stuttering) is a neurodevelopmental
condition defined in the *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental
Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision* (DSM-5-TR) and in the
International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11) at
code 6A01.1. The disorder is characterized by disturbances in the
normal fluency and time patterning of speech that are inappropriate
for the individual's age and language skills, persist over time, and
are characterized by frequent and marked occurrences of one or more
of: sound and syllable repetitions, sound prolongations of consonants
as well as vowels, broken words (e.g., pauses within a word), audible
or silent blocking (filled or unfilled pauses in speech),
circumlocutions (word substitutions to avoid problematic words),
words produced with an excess of physical tension, and monosyllabic
whole-word repetitions. The disturbance causes anxiety about
speaking, limitations in effective communication, social
participation, academic or occupational performance, individually or
in any combination. Onset is in the early developmental period; an
adult-onset (post-developmental) form is classified separately.

The estimated incidence of COFD is approximately 5–8 % of children
during the early developmental period (peak onset 2–5 years), with
spontaneous recovery in approximately 65–80 % of cases by school age.
Approximately 1 % of adults have persistent stuttering. The disorder
is associated with elevated rates of social anxiety disorder,
avoidance of speaking situations, reduced educational and
occupational attainment, and stuttering-related impact on
quality-of-life that is, in many adults with persistent COFD,
substantially greater than the impact of the audible stuttering
itself.

The current standard of care for COFD is non-pharmacological. In
preschool children, the Lidcombe Program (a parent-mediated operant
program) has the strongest evidence base. In school-age children and
adults, fluency-shaping (e.g., easy onset, light articulatory
contact) and stuttering-modification (e.g., cancellation, pull-out,
preparatory set) approaches, often combined with cognitive-behavioral
strategies for the affective and avoidance components, are the
mainstay. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States
Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of COFD as a
primary indication. Off-label use of dopamine D2 receptor antagonists
(haloperidol, risperidone, olanzapine, asenapine) and of selective
serotonin reuptake inhibitors is reported, but such off-label use is
limited by extrapyramidal, metabolic, sexual, and cardiometabolic
adverse effects; the published controlled evidence of fluency benefit
from these agents is limited and their risk-benefit posture in
pediatric populations is unfavorable.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, fluent speech production requires the on-line
maintenance of a fluency target (e.g., easy onset, continuous
phonation, light articulatory contact) in working memory across
phrase production, sustained attention to the subject's own
speech-motor output, and rapid motor-program updating based on
auditory feedback. Each of these subprocesses depends on
state-appropriate cortical arousal and on cholinergic and
dopaminergic signaling in left inferior frontal gyrus, basal
ganglia, supplementary motor area, and right-lateralized
homologues, all of which are implicated in stuttering by
neuroimaging studies. The histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic
auto- and heteroreceptor that, when constitutively active,
suppresses the release of histamine and of acetylcholine, dopamine,
and norepinephrine in cortex and striatum. Selective inverse
agonism at H3 by pitolisant (Wakix®, FDA-approved for excessive
daytime sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy) elevates extracellular
histamine and supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine and
dopamine, without the abuse liability of stimulant scheduling and
without the D2 antagonism that limits the use of antipsychotics in
COFD. The H3 axis therefore addresses the *vigilance,
working-memory, and motor-monitoring substrate* of fluency-shaping
practice, but does not address the social-evaluative anxiety and
avoidance substrate that constitutes a substantial portion of the
disability of persistent COFD.

Second, oxytocin receptor (OXTR) signaling in the prefrontal
cortex, amygdala, ventral tegmental area, and superior temporal
sulcus modulates social motivation, eye-gaze allocation, the
salience of socially contingent feedback, and (importantly for COFD)
the threat reactivity of the amygdala to social-evaluative cues.
Intranasal oxytocin reduces amygdala reactivity to socially
threatening cues in laboratory paradigms, supports trust and
approach behavior, and reduces social-anxiety symptoms in
investigational settings. No issued patent or published application
known to the present inventors discloses the use of an oxytocin
receptor agonist in combination with an H3 receptor antagonist or
inverse agonist for any indication, and none discloses such a
combination for COFD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance- and working-memory-
enabled fluency-shaping practice (an H3 antagonist axis) and
attenuation of social-evaluative anxiety with attendant
clinician-engagement (an OXTR agonist axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for fluency consolidation in COFD. The
combination is expected to produce a greater reduction in
stuttering severity, and a greater reduction in the
stuttering-related psychosocial impact, than either agent alone at
any individually achievable dose, by acting on two non-overlapping
CNS axes that converge on fronto-striatal speech-motor and
amygdala–prefrontal social-evaluative circuits.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) oxytocin or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, analogue, fragment, or
prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Childhood-Onset Fluency Disorder (Stuttering) in a subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant and a therapeutically
effective amount of oxytocin. Co-administration may be simultaneous,
in a unitary dosage form or in separate dosage forms, or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising (a)
pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) oxytocin or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form, and
(c) written instructions for the treatment of COFD in a pediatric
or adult subject according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
with the oxytocin actuation explicitly timed to within 30 minutes
of a scheduled fluency-shaping or stuttering-modification
intervention session, and (in adults with documented
social-evaluative speaking anxiety) optionally timed to anticipated
high-stakes speaking situations.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 8.9 to 17.8 mg/day and a preferred
pediatric dose weight-banded as set forth herein. The invention
contemplates oxytocin in a daily dose of about 8 IU to about 80 IU
administered intranasally in divided doses, with a preferred dose
of 24 IU twice daily timed to practice sessions. The mass ratio of
pitolisant to oxytocin (calculated on the free-base equivalents)
ranges broadly from about 1:200,000 to about 1:5,000, and is
preferably from about 1:50,000 to about 1:20,000; the
dose-equivalent molar relationship and the session-aligned timing
relationship are the inventive features, not the absolute mass
ratio.

The technical effect of the combination is (a) a reduction, in
subjects with COFD, in stuttering severity (Stuttering Severity
Instrument, 4th ed. (SSI-4) Total Score; percentage of stuttered
syllables (%SS) in a conversational speech sample) and (b) a
reduction in stuttering-related psychosocial impact (Overall
Assessment of the Speaker's Experience of Stuttering (OASES) total
impact rating), each greater than the sum of the individual effects
of pitolisant and oxytocin administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose. The combination is opioid-free,
stimulant-free, antipsychotic-free, and has no known dependence
liability.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the compound 1-{3-[3-(4-chlorophenyl)propoxy]
propyl}piperidine, also known as BF2.649, marketed in the United
States as Wakix® and in the European Union as Wakix®. Pitolisant
is a selective inverse agonist of the human histamine H3 receptor
with a binding affinity (Ki) of approximately 0.16 nM. The term as
used herein includes the free base, the hydrochloride salt and
other pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates,
polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically
equivalent analogues.

*"Oxytocin"* means the cyclic nonapeptide of sequence
H-Cys-Tyr-Ile-Gln-Asn-Cys-Pro-Leu-Gly-NH₂ with a disulfide bond
between Cys¹ and Cys⁶. The term as used herein includes the free
peptide and its pharmaceutically acceptable acetate or citrate
salts, glycosylated or PEGylated derivatives that retain agonism at
the human OXTR with EC₅₀ within ten-fold of native oxytocin,
peptidic agonists such as carbetocin, and small-molecule OXTR
agonists such as TC OT 39, WAY 267,464, LIT-001, or such other
agents that produce ≥ 50 % maximal calcium mobilization at the
human OXTR at concentrations ≤ 1 µM in a standard fluorometric
calcium-flux assay.

*"Childhood-Onset Fluency Disorder (Stuttering)"* or *"COFD"* means
the disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and in ICD-11 (6A01.1),
characterized by persistent disturbances in the normal fluency and
time patterning of speech, including sound and syllable
repetitions, sound prolongations, broken words, audible or silent
blocking, circumlocutions, words produced with an excess of
physical tension, and monosyllabic whole-word repetitions, with
deficits resulting in functional limitations in effective
communication, social participation, social relationships, academic
achievement, or occupational performance, with onset in the early
developmental period.

*"Stuttering severity outcome"* means a subject's measured score on
at least one of: SSI-4 Total Score; %SS in a 200-syllable
conversational speech sample; speech-naturalness rating on a 9-point
scale by a blinded listener; or a comparable validated stuttering
instrument.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a subject for the treatment of
COFD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: SSI-4 Total
Score, %SS, OASES total impact rating, speech-naturalness rating,
or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate dosage
forms, at the same time, or at different times within a window of
about 24 hours.

*"Session-aligned dosing"* means administration of intranasal
oxytocin within a window of about 30 minutes prior to or during a
scheduled fluency-shaping or stuttering-modification intervention
session, and (optionally, in adults) prior to anticipated
high-stakes speaking situations such as a public presentation or
job interview, with the purpose of aligning the central oxytocin
pharmacodynamic window with the period of socially scaffolded
fluency practice or social-evaluative speaking exposure.

### 2. The two active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a piperidine derivative and a high-affinity, selective
inverse agonist at the human histamine H3 receptor. The H3 receptor
is a presynaptic autoreceptor and heteroreceptor that, when
constitutively active, suppresses the release of histamine from
histaminergic neurons and the release of acetylcholine, dopamine,
and norepinephrine from cortical, striatal, and hippocampal neurons.
Inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant elevates extracellular histamine
and supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine
in left inferior frontal gyrus, basal ganglia, supplementary motor
area, and homologous right-lateralized regions — circuits
implicated in stuttering by structural and functional neuroimaging.
Pitolisant is approved by the FDA for the treatment of excessive
daytime sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy, and is approved in
the European Union for excessive daytime sleepiness and cataplexy
in adults with narcolepsy. Pitolisant is metabolized primarily by
CYP2D6 and CYP3A4. The medication is a substrate and weak inhibitor
of CYP3A4 and is not a significant inducer of common cytochrome
P450 enzymes. Pitolisant is *not* a D2 receptor antagonist and is
distinguished from off-label haloperidol, risperidone, olanzapine,
and asenapine in this regard; pitolisant is therefore expected to
avoid the extrapyramidal, metabolic, and prolactin-related
adverse-event burden that limits the use of those agents in COFD.
Pitolisant is under U.S. brand-patent protection through the late
2020s; clinical deployment of the present invention will require
coordination with the marketing authorization holder.

#### 2.2 Oxytocin

Oxytocin is the endogenous cyclic nonapeptide synthesized in the
paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei of the hypothalamus. Oxytocin
acts at the OXTR, a Gαq-coupled G protein-coupled receptor that
mobilizes intracellular calcium and modulates social cognition,
attachment, trust, eye-gaze allocation, recognition of socially
relevant affect, and amygdala threat reactivity to social-evaluative
cues. Synthetic oxytocin (Pitocin®, Syntocinon®) is approved in many
jurisdictions for the induction of labor and the prevention or
treatment of postpartum hemorrhage. Investigational use of intranasal
oxytocin in CNS indications, including social anxiety disorder, is
supported by a substantial peer-reviewed literature. Native oxytocin
is freely available from multiple suppliers and has no live U.S.
composition-of-matter patent restriction; the present invention does
not require any proprietary OXTR agonist. The intranasal route is
preferred to maximize central nervous system exposure relative to
peripheral OXTR engagement.

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
oxytocin, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a unitary
fixed-dose combination dosage form (for example, a single oral

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-
release oral tablet at strengths of 4.5 mg and 17.8 mg (free-base
equivalent), matching the marketed product. Modified-release oral
tablets at strengths up to 35.6 mg per unit are within the scope of
the invention.

Oxytocin is preferably formulated as a metered-dose intranasal
spray delivering 4 IU per actuation, with permeation enhancement to
maximize central nervous system bioavailability. Sublingual films,
buccal tablets, and depot subcutaneous formulations are within the
scope of the invention. Intravenous and intramuscular dosage forms
are appropriate for inpatient initiation but not for chronic
ambulatory use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-tablet bottle of
pitolisant immediate-release oral tablets at the appropriate
weight-band strength, (b) a 30-day supply of an intranasal oxytocin
spray device, and (c) a printed insert with administration timing
aligned to scheduled fluency-shaping or stuttering-modification
intervention sessions.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adults (subjects ≥ 18 years and ≥ 50 kg) with persistent COFD,
the preferred dosing regimen is pitolisant 8.9 mg once daily for
one week followed by 17.8 mg once daily, taken on awakening, plus
intranasal oxytocin 24 IU twice daily, with the first daily
actuation timed to within 30 minutes of scheduled fluency-shaping
practice or speech-language pathology sessions, and (optionally)
the second daily actuation timed to within 30 minutes of an
anticipated high-stakes speaking situation. Maximum recommended
pitolisant dose is 35.6 mg/day; maximum recommended oxytocin dose is
80 IU/day in three or four divided actuations.

For pediatric subjects ≥ 6 years and < 18 years, weight-banded
dosing is preferred:

| Body weight | Pitolisant (mg/day, oral) | Oxytocin (IU/day, intranasal) |
|---|---|---|
| 20–< 30 kg | 4.5 | 16 (8 IU twice daily) |
| 30–< 50 kg | 8.9 | 24 (12 IU twice daily) |
| ≥ 50 kg | 8.9 → 17.8 (titration) | 24–48 |

Hepatic adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in
subjects with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B) and is
not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C). No
oxytocin dose adjustment is required for hepatic impairment.

Renal adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²). No
oxytocin adjustment is required.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP2D6 inhibitors (paroxetine, fluoxetine, bupropion)
without halving the pitolisant dose. Avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inducers. No clinically significant
medication–medication interactions are expected between pitolisant
and intranasal oxytocin.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects who
meet DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 criteria for COFD. Inclusion criteria for
the preferred treatment population include: age ≥ 6 years and
≤ 65 years; SSI-4 Total Score in the moderate-to-severe range;
%SS ≥ 3 in a baseline conversational speech sample; intact
peripheral hearing (pure-tone average ≤ 25 dB HL bilaterally);
intelligence quotient ≥ 70; absence of a diagnosed neurologic
condition that better accounts for the disfluency (no acquired
neurogenic stuttering, no progressive neurological disease, no
psychogenic stuttering with documented psychogenic origin).
Comorbid social anxiety disorder, generalized anxiety disorder,
ADHD, language disorder, and Speech Sound Disorder are not
exclusionary; COFD frequently co-occurs with these conditions and
the present invention is expected to address fluency symptoms in
those subjects without exacerbating the co-occurring condition.
Exclusion criteria include known hypersensitivity to either active,
current treatment with a prohibited concomitant medication
(see § 5), pregnancy or breastfeeding, current treatment with an
antipsychotic for COFD (which must be tapered prior to enrollment),
and inability to comply with intranasal administration.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Stuttering is mediated by (a) intermittent breakdown in the
fronto-striatal speech-motor planning system that ordinarily
sequences phonation, articulation, and breath support across
phrase production; (b) impairment in the on-line motor monitoring
that ordinarily detects and corrects emerging disfluency before it
reaches audibility; and (c) a learned anxiety/avoidance response
to the social-evaluative consequences of audible disfluency, which
in adults often becomes the dominant contributor to disability.
Selective H3 inverse agonism by pitolisant addresses (a) and (b)
by elevating central histamine and supporting state-dependent
acetylcholine and dopamine release in left inferior frontal gyrus,
basal ganglia, and supplementary motor area, with documented
clinical effect on psychomotor vigilance in narcolepsy populations,
without the D2 antagonism that has historically been associated
with off-label haloperidol or risperidone use in COFD. OXTR
agonism by intranasal oxytocin addresses (c) by reducing amygdala
reactivity to social-evaluative cues and by enhancing
clinician-directed engagement during fluency-shaping practice. The
two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on
the fronto-striatal speech-motor and amygdala–prefrontal
social-evaluative circuits.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce
greater-than-additive improvement in (i) audible stuttering severity
and (ii) the affective/psychosocial impact of stuttering, neither of
which would be optimally addressed by an H3 antagonist alone or an
OXTR agonist alone.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding (³H-N-α-methylhistamine
displacement on rat cortical membranes; Ki for pitolisant
≈ 1.5 nM); H3 functional inverse agonism (cAMP accumulation in
CHO-K1 cells expressing human H3); OXTR binding (³H-oxytocin
displacement on human OXTR-CHO membranes; Kd for oxytocin
≈ 1 nM); OXTR functional agonism (calcium mobilization in OXTR-CHO
cells; EC₅₀ for oxytocin ≈ 5–20 nM).

Although stuttering is a uniquely human condition (no animal model
recapitulates linguistic disfluency), in-vivo behavioral paradigms
in rodent and non-human primate that probe the underlying substrates
are appropriate. These include: five-choice serial reaction time
task (5-CSRTT) for sustained attention; complex motor-sequence
learning in rodent forelimb reaching with feedback-mediated
correction; fear-potentiated startle and social-defeat paradigms
for amygdala-mediated social-evaluative threat; and three-chamber
social-novelty paradigms for clinician-analog social motivation. A
supra-additive effect of the combination on (i) 5-CSRTT
discrimination accuracy, (ii) motor-sequence completion fidelity
under feedback, and (iii) attenuation of fear-potentiated startle
in a social-defeat paradigm, relative to either monotherapy at
any individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof
point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years; equal sex
distribution). Period 1: pitolisant 17.8 mg orally once daily for
7 days; full PK sampling on days 1 and 7. Period 2 (after 7-day
washout): intranasal oxytocin 24 IU twice daily for 7 days; full
PK sampling on days 1 and 7. Period 3 (after 7-day washout):
combination at the same doses for 7 days; PK sampling for both
agents on days 1 and 7. The primary endpoint is the geometric
mean ratio for AUC₀–τ and Cmax of pitolisant in combination
versus monotherapy, with 90 % confidence intervals contained
within 0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult COFD (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm) with 80 %
power to detect a 6-point difference in SSI-4 Total Score change
from baseline between the combination arm and placebo at week 12,
assuming a standard deviation of 11 points and a two-sided α of
0.05.

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant 8.9 mg/day oral + intranasal
  oxytocin 24 IU twice daily.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pitolisant 8.9 mg/day oral + matching
  intranasal placebo.
* Arm C (placebo): matching oral placebo + matching intranasal
  placebo.

All subjects receive a standardized, manualized fluency-shaping or
stuttering-modification intervention of equal frequency, intensity,
and duration (twice-weekly 45-minute sessions over 12 weeks)
delivered by a certified speech-language pathologist and aligned
to the oxytocin actuation window. The primary endpoint is change
from baseline in SSI-4 Total Score at week 12. Secondary endpoints
include %SS in conversational speech, OASES total impact rating,
speech-naturalness rating by a blinded listener, responder rate
(≥ 30 % SSI-4 reduction), and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Childhood-Onset Fluency Disorder (Stuttering) comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
histamine H3 receptor inverse agonist pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of the oxytocin receptor agonist
oxytocin, in dosage forms and on dosing schedules timed to
scheduled fluency-shaping or stuttering-modification intervention
sessions and (in adults) anticipated high-stakes speaking
situations, are disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping
receptor classes that converge on the fronto-striatal speech-motor
circuit and on the amygdala–prefrontal social-evaluative circuit
implicated in Childhood-Onset Fluency Disorder, are devoid of D2
antagonism, and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in stuttering severity and stuttering-related
psychosocial impact than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.
