## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Cyclothymic Disorder by co-administering
(a) a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse agonist / antagonist, in
particular pimavanserin, and (b) a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist,
in particular perospirone. The invention is intended for subjects
with chronic fluctuating subthreshold hypomanic and depressive
symptoms and addresses affective lability, irritability, unstable
sleep-wake patterns, mood-reactive salience distortion, and
behavioral overactivation without asserting treatment of acute
mania, major depressive episodes, or full bipolar I / II disorder.

## Background of the invention

Cyclothymic Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
chronic mood disorder characterized by numerous periods of
hypomanic-like symptoms and numerous periods of depressive symptoms
that do not meet full episode criteria, persisting for at least two
years in adults or one year in youth. The disorder is associated
with unstable temperament, reactivity to stress, variable sleep,
irritability, impulsive behavior, and recurrent interpersonal
strain.

The prevalence of Cyclothymic Disorder is lower than that of major
mood disorders, but the clinical burden is substantial. Subjects
often experience chronic instability rather than discrete episodes,
with repeated periods of excessive activation, reduced sleep,
irritability, heightened social or goal-directed intensity,
followed by low-mood, fatigue, guilt, or withdrawal. Functional
impairment, substance-use comorbidity, anxiety, and later transition
to broader bipolar-spectrum illness may occur.

The current standard of care is supportive and extrapolated from
bipolar-spectrum practice. It consists primarily of psychotherapy,
sleep and routine stabilization, treatment of comorbid conditions,
and selected empirical use of mood stabilizers or antipsychotic
medications. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United
States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for the
treatment of Cyclothymic Disorder as a primary indication.
Borrowed treatments may over-sedate or incompletely address the
subthreshold but persistent salience and behavioral instability that
define cyclothymia.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, cyclothymia includes mood-reactive salience
distortion, irritability, and fluctuating interpretation of internal
and external stimuli that may be amplified by cortical 5-HT2A-
mediated processing. Pimavanserin (Nuplazid®) is a selective 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist approved by the FDA for hallucinations
and delusions associated with Parkinson disease psychosis.
Pimavanserin has no clinically meaningful D2 antagonism and
therefore can modify 5-HT2A-mediated salience physiology without
direct dopaminergic motor burden. The pimavanserin axis therefore
addresses the *irritability, salience amplification, and
interpretive instability* substrate of Cyclothymic Disorder.

Second, cyclothymia also includes recurrent periods of dopaminergic
overactivation with increased drive, impulsive action, reduced need
for sleep, and behavioral acceleration, even when full hypomanic
criteria are not met. Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic
approved in Japan for schizophrenia that functions as a D2 receptor
antagonist with additional serotonergic properties. In the present
invention, the perospirone axis addresses the *behavioral
overactivation, impulsive action-readiness, and subthreshold
hypomanic activation* substrate of Cyclothymic Disorder.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist /
antagonist in combination with a D2 antagonist for Cyclothymic
Disorder specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of 5-HT2A-
mediated salience amplification (a pimavanserin axis) and
attenuation of dopaminergic behavioral overactivation (a
perospirone D2 axis) are complementary and convergent substrates
for treatment of Cyclothymic Disorder. The combination is expected
to produce greater symptomatic improvement in mood lability,
irritability, sleep variability, and functional instability than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pimavanserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) perospirone or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Cyclothymic Disorder in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of pimavanserin and a therapeutically effective amount of
perospirone. Co-administration may be simultaneous or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) perospirone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Cyclothymic
Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in
conjunction with psychotherapy and sleep / routine stabilization.

The invention contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose of about
17 mg to about 34 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 34 mg/day. The invention contemplates
perospirone in a daily dose of about 4 mg to about 48 mg
administered orally in one or two divided doses, with a preferred
dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day. The inventive feature is the concurrent
engagement of 5-HT2A-linked salience modulation and D2-linked
behavioral-activation attenuation in a chronic mood-instability
syndrome.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with Cyclothymic Disorder, in affective-lability burden,
irritability, subthreshold hypomanic activation, sleep variability,
and functional impairment, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of pimavanserin and perospirone administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, tartrate or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically
equivalent 5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Cyclothymic Disorder"* means the chronic mood disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by numerous periods of hypomanic-like symptoms
and numerous periods of depressive symptoms that do not meet full
episode criteria.

*"Mood-lability composite"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
chronic fluctuation in activation, irritability, subthreshold
depressive symptoms, sleep variation, or functional instability,
quantified by structured clinician assessment or validated mood-
instability instruments.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of Cyclothymic Disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed
herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least
one of: mood-lability composite, irritability score, sleep-
variability index, activation burden, functional-stability score,
or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of pimavanserin is attenuation
of salience amplification and irritability-prone interpretive
instability across subthreshold mood states.

#### 2.2 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is attenuation of behavioral overactivation and
subthreshold hypomanic acceleration.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pimavanserin and
perospirone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a pimavanserin capsule or
tablet and a perospirone tablet.

Excipients useful for the pimavanserin composition include capsule
fill excipients, diluents, disintegrants, and stabilizers suitable
for marketed pimavanserin formulation profiles. Excipients useful
for the perospirone composition include diluents, binders,
disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable for oral
tablet manufacture.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or tablet
suitable for once-daily use. Perospirone is preferably formulated
as an oral tablet suitable for one or two daily doses.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pimavanserin capsules or tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to
psychotherapy, daily routine stabilization, and sleep regularity
tracking.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with Cyclothymic Disorder, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Pimavanserin 17 mg orally once daily, titratable to 34 mg once
  daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.
* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily, titratable to 8 mg twice
  daily and then up to 12 mg twice daily if clinically indicated
  and tolerated.

Broad genus ranges include pimavanserin 17-34 mg/day and
perospirone 4-48 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses once-daily
pimavanserin and one or two daily doses of perospirone.

Medication-medication interactions: corrected QT interval
monitoring should be considered when pimavanserin is combined with
other QT-prolonging agents. Perospirone requires monitoring for
orthostasis, sedation, and extrapyramidal symptoms. The regimen is
not intended to replace urgent treatment of full manic, mixed, or
major depressive episodes if they emerge.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
Cyclothymic Disorder. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: age >= 18 years; chronic subthreshold
oscillation between activation and depressive symptoms; clinically
meaningful distress or functional impairment; and willingness to
participate in psychotherapy and routine stabilization. Exclusion
criteria include current full manic, hypomanic, or major
depressive episode meeting higher-level bipolar or depressive
criteria, untreated primary psychotic disorder, severe hepatic
impairment, clinically significant baseline QT prolongation, and
known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Cyclothymia often includes two interacting layers: persistent
salience and interpretive instability that amplify irritability and
mood reactivity, and dopaminergic overactivation that drives
subthreshold behavioral acceleration.

Pimavanserin addresses the salience-instability layer through
selective 5-HT2A inverse agonism / antagonism. Perospirone
addresses the behavioral-overactivation layer through D2
antagonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on the same mood-lability phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in cyclothymic burden than either monotherapy

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A binding and inverse-agonism assays for
pimavanserin; D2 receptor binding and functional antagonism assays
for perospirone; and combined mood-reactivity network assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include reward-reactivity models, irritability paradigms, sleep-
variation and circadian-fragility models, and impulsive-activation
tasks. A supra-additive effect of the combination on reduction of
mood lability and overactivation, relative to either monotherapy at
any individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof
point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
pimavanserin 34 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: perospirone 8 mg/day
for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability assessments are obtained
in each period. The principal purpose is to establish tolerability
and absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult Cyclothymic Disorder (FIG. 4). Three arms,
1:1:1 randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pimavanserin + perospirone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): perospirone + placebo for
  pimavanserin.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with psychotherapy and routine
  stabilization.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in mood-lability
composite at week 16. Secondary endpoints include irritability
score, sleep-variability index, activation burden, functional-
stability score, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Cyclothymic Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of pimavanserin and a
therapeutically effective amount of perospirone are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping salience-instability and
behavioral-overactivation circuits implicated in chronic mood
lability and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
improvement in mood-lability burden, irritability, and sleep
variability than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.
