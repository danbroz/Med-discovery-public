## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Dissociative Amnesia by co-administering
(a) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor that is also a 5-HT1A receptor
partial agonist and (b) a corticotropin-releasing hormone receptor
type 1 (CRHR1) antagonist, in particular the combination of
vilazodone and crinecerfont. The invention is intended for
adolescent and adult subjects with Dissociative Amnesia who are
receiving evidence-based psychotherapy and addresses trauma-linked
retrieval blockade, dissociative distress, and stress-triggered
recurrent amnestic episodes. The invention is not intended to
create, implant, or distort autobiographical memory.

## Background of the invention

Dissociative Amnesia is a dissociative disorder defined in DSM-5-TR
and ICD-11 by an inability to recall important autobiographical
information, usually of a traumatic or stressful nature, that is
inconsistent with ordinary forgetting. The amnesia may be
localized, selective, generalized, continuous, or systematized, and
in some subjects may occur with dissociative fugue. The disorder is
associated with clinically meaningful distress, impairment, and
discontinuity of autobiographical narrative.

The prevalence of Dissociative Amnesia in the general population is
low and likely under-estimated because of diagnostic complexity and
overlap with trauma-spectrum conditions, depression, and neurologic
causes of memory complaint. Dissociative Amnesia is associated with
trauma history, high distress, identity confusion, depressive and
anxious symptoms, and crisis-driven service use. Comorbid PTSD,
major depressive disorder, and other dissociative symptoms are
common.

The current standard of care is psychotherapeutic. It consists
primarily of supportive therapy, trauma-focused treatment as
appropriate, stabilization, and careful work on autobiographical
narrative reconstruction. No pharmaceutical product is approved by
the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the
treatment of Dissociative Amnesia as a primary indication. Off-label
medication use generally targets comorbid depression, anxiety, or
insomnia rather than the core amnestic phenotype.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, Dissociative Amnesia is associated with anxious
avoidance, intrusive trauma-linked cognition, and state-dependent
retrieval blockade. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available,
FDA-approved antidepressant that combines selective serotonin
reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor.
This dual mechanism supports attenuation of anxious-compulsive
symptomatology, intrusive ideation, and avoidant stress responses.
The serotonergic axis therefore addresses the *anxious-avoidant and
intrusive-cognitive* substrate of Dissociative Amnesia, but does
not directly attenuate HPA-axis overactivation.

Second, retrieval of autobiographical memory is sensitive to stress
state. Excess CRF / CRHR1 signaling contributes to hyperarousal,
state-dependent retrieval failure, and physiologic escalation that
may interfere with access to autobiographical material during
therapy. Crinecerfont (CRENESSITY®) is a selective CRHR1
antagonist approved by the FDA in 2024 as adjunctive treatment in
classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, where it reduces CRF-driven
ACTH signaling. More broadly, CRHR1 antagonism has long been a
mechanistically attractive approach for stress-related psychiatric
conditions. In the present invention, crinecerfont addresses the
*stress-axis and retrieval-blockade* substrate of Dissociative
Amnesia.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a 5-HT1A-partial-agonist SSRI in
combination with a CRHR1 antagonist for Dissociative Amnesia
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that serotonergic attenuation of
anxious avoidance (a vilazodone HTR1A axis) and CRHR1-mediated
attenuation of stress-triggered retrieval blockade (a crinecerfont
axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of
Dissociative Amnesia. The combination is expected to produce a
greater reduction in amnestic episodes and dissociative distress,
and a more reliable recovery of access to existing autobiographical
memory under clinician-guided conditions, than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) crinecerfont or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Dissociative Amnesia in an adolescent or adult subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone and a
therapeutically effective amount of crinecerfont. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) crinecerfont or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Dissociative
Amnesia according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, including
coordination with evidence-based psychotherapy and safety planning.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally with food once daily, with
a preferred adult and adolescent dose of 20 to 40 mg/day after a
7-day titration from 10 mg/day. The invention contemplates
crinecerfont in a daily dose of about 100 mg to about 200 mg
administered orally in two divided doses with meals, with a
preferred adult dose of 100 mg twice daily. The inventive feature
is the concurrent engagement of the HTR1A / serotonergic and CRHR1
stress-axis pathways during psychotherapy-linked memory-retrieval
windows.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in
adolescent and adult subjects with Dissociative Amnesia, in
amnestic-episode frequency, dissociative distress, and trauma-
triggered retrieval blockade, with corresponding improvement in
clinician-guided access to existing autobiographical memory, that
is greater than the sum of the individual effects of vilazodone
and crinecerfont administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Crinecerfont"* means the selective corticotropin-releasing
hormone receptor type 1 (CRHR1) antagonist approved in the United
States as CRENESSITY®. The term includes crinecerfont free base,
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically
equivalent CRHR1 antagonists within the same activity genus.

*"Dissociative Amnesia"* means the dissociative disorder defined in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by an inability to recall important
autobiographical information, usually of a traumatic or stressful
nature, that is inconsistent with ordinary forgetting. The term
includes localized, selective, generalized, continuous, and
systematized forms, and may occur with dissociative fugue.

*"Amnestic episode"* means a clinically meaningful interval in
which the subject is unable to access important autobiographical
information inconsistent with ordinary forgetting.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adolescent or adult subject
for the treatment of Dissociative Amnesia according to a dosing
regimen disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful
improvement in at least one of: monthly amnestic-episode
frequency, Dissociative Experiences Scale amnesia subscale,
autobiographical-recall score, dissociative-distress score,
crisis intensity, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin
across multiple central serotonergic targets; by partially
agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, vilazodone supports attenuation of
anxious-compulsive symptomatology, intrusive ideation, and
avoidant stress responses. Vilazodone is FDA-approved for the
treatment of major depressive disorder in adults at oral doses of
10 mg/day (initial), titrated to 20 mg/day after one week and to
40 mg/day thereafter, taken once daily with food to support oral
bioavailability. Vilazodone is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4 and
is a substrate (and weak inhibitor) of CYP2C19 and CYP2D6.
Vilazodone is generic in the United States.

#### 2.2 Crinecerfont

Crinecerfont is a selective CRHR1 antagonist approved by the FDA
in 2024 as adjunctive treatment to glucocorticoid replacement in
classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia in adults and pediatric
patients 4 years of age and older. It suppresses CRF-driven ACTH
signaling and is available in capsule and oral-solution
formulations. Approved dosing is 100 mg twice daily with meals in
adults. The compound is under active U.S. patent protection into
the 2040s; clinical use of the present invention will require
coordination with the rights holder or a licenseable development
path.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
crinecerfont, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as a vilazodone
tablet and a crinecerfont capsule or oral solution.

The vilazodone component may be formulated as the hydrochloride
salt in immediate-release tablet form. The crinecerfont component
may be formulated as capsule or oral solution for administration
with meals. Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition
include diluents (lactose monohydrate, microcrystalline cellulose),
binders (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers. Excipients useful for
the crinecerfont composition include capsule fill excipients,
suspending agents, flavoring agents for oral solution, buffers, and
vehicles suitable for meal-associated dosing.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-
release oral tablet at strengths of 10 mg, 20 mg, and 40 mg.
Crinecerfont is preferably formulated as oral capsules and/or oral
solution suitable for twice-daily dosing with meals.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of crinecerfont capsules
or oral solution, and (c) written instructions aligned to meals,
psychotherapy, and safety planning.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with Dissociative Amnesia, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter.
* Crinecerfont 100 mg orally twice daily with meals in adults,
  with lower weight-based twice-daily dosing permissible in
  adolescent subjects according to the approved pediatric dose
  bands.

Broad genus ranges include vilazodone 10–40 mg/day and crinecerfont
100–200 mg/day in two divided doses. The preferred schedule aligns
morning crinecerfont with breakfast and the evening dose with the
evening meal, and maintains vilazodone at a consistent morning
administration time.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: severe hepatic or renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication. Standard meal-associated crinecerfont dosing is
maintained in mild-to-moderate impairment only if clinically
appropriate.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers until definitive DDI
characterization is available for the combination. Vilazodone dose
adjustment may be required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adolescent and
adult subjects with Dissociative Amnesia. Inclusion criteria for the
preferred treatment population include: age ≥ 12 years; current
Dissociative Amnesia diagnosis established by qualified clinician;
clinically meaningful recurrent amnestic episodes or persistent
autobiographical retrieval failure; participation in evidence-based
psychotherapy; and absence of neurologic or substance-related
conditions that better explain the memory disturbance. Exclusion
criteria include active psychosis, uncontrolled substance use
disorder, severe hepatic or renal impairment, pregnancy, and
inability to comply with structured psychotherapy and safety
planning.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Dissociative Amnesia is maintained by interaction between
trauma-linked anxious avoidance and stress-triggered retrieval
blockade. Trauma cues may provoke physiologic escalation and
avoidant dissociative responses that interfere with access to
autobiographical memory during therapy.

Vilazodone addresses anxious avoidance, intrusive trauma-linked
cognition, and affective destabilization. Crinecerfont addresses
the stress-axis component by attenuating CRHR1-mediated physiologic
escalation that may block retrieval. The two axes are non-
overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the same
retrieval-blockade loop.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in amnestic-episode frequency and dissociative
distress, and more reliable access to existing autobiographical
memory during psychotherapy, than either monotherapy alone. The
present invention is not intended to create, implant, or distort
autobiographical memory and should be used only with evidence-based
clinical safeguards.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; CRHR1 binding and ACTH-suppression cellular assays for
crinecerfont; and combined stress-hormone / memory-network assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include contextual fear-extinction models, stress-induced retrieval
interference paradigms, avoidance / conflict tasks, and HPA-axis
biomarker readouts such as ACTH and corticosterone suppression. No
animal model is asserted to reproduce Dissociative Amnesia itself;
rather, the preclinical program supports convergent mechanisms of
retrieval blockade under stress and anxious avoidance. A supra-
additive effect of the combination on reduction of stress-induced
retrieval interference, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 40 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2:
crinecerfont 100 mg twice daily with meals for 7 days; Period 3:
the combination for 7 days. Full PK sampling, ACTH / cortisol
biomarkers, and tolerability monitoring are obtained in each
period. The principal purpose is to establish tolerability and
absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult Dissociative Amnesia
(FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 90
(30 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + crinecerfont.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for
  crinecerfont.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard-of-care
  psychotherapy.

All subjects receive standardized psychotherapy with safeguards for
memory work. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in
monthly amnestic episodes at week 12. Secondary endpoints include
Dissociative Experiences Scale amnesia subscale,
autobiographical-recall score, dissociative-distress score, crisis
intensity, and CGI-I.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Dissociative Amnesia in adolescent and adult subjects comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
SSRI / 5-HT1A partial agonist vilazodone and a therapeutically
effective amount of the CRHR1 antagonist crinecerfont are
disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping receptor classes
that converge on trauma-hyperarousal and autobiographical-memory-
retrieval circuits implicated in Dissociative Amnesia, and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in amnestic-
episode frequency, dissociative distress, and clinician-guided
access to existing autobiographical memory than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose. The invention is not intended
to create, implant, or distort autobiographical memory.
