## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Bipolar I Disorder by co-administering
(a) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with 5-HT2C antagonist
activity and (b) a dopamine D2 / 5-HT2A antagonist atypical
antipsychotic, in particular the combination of agomelatine and
perospirone. The invention is directed to adult subjects with bipolar
I depression, mixed states, mania-associated circadian disruption, or
inter-episode instability, and is not intended to replace emergency
containment, hospitalization, or established mood-stabilizer care when
those are clinically required.

## Background of the invention

Bipolar I Disorder is a chronic mood disorder defined by at least one
manic episode and often accompanied by depressive episodes, mixed
features, psychotic symptoms, circadian destabilization, and major
functional impairment. Compared with bipolar II disorder, bipolar I
illness includes a greater burden of full mania, hospitalization, and
behavioral risk, yet long-term impairment is also driven by depressive
burden, unstable sleep, and incomplete inter-episode recovery.

Current treatment remains heterogeneous and frequently incomplete.
Typical regimens may include lithium, valproate, atypical
antipsychotics, psychotherapy, and sleep / routine stabilization.
Although many patients achieve partial benefit, persistent bipolar
depression, mixed irritability, recurrent sleep disruption, and
relapse-prone circadian instability remain common. There remains a
need for a mechanism-based combination that addresses both the
circadian-depressive substrate and the manic-salience / activation
substrate of bipolar I illness.

The mechanistic gap addressed by the present invention has two layers.
First, agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as an
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. This pharmacology supports circadian resynchronization,
sleep restoration, and reduction of depressive inertia. Second,
perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic with D2 antagonism, 5-HT2A
antagonism, and 5-HT1A partial agonist activity. In the present
invention, the perospirone axis provides stabilization of manic or
mixed activation, psychotic salience where present, and behavioral
overdrive.

The inventors recognized that these mechanisms are complementary in
bipolar I disorder. Agomelatine addresses the circadian and sleep-
linked recovery layer that is often destabilized before, during, and
after manic episodes and throughout bipolar depression. Perospirone
addresses the activation and salience layer that can drive mania,
mixed irritability, reduced need for sleep, and psychotic
destabilization. The combination is therefore expected to reduce
depressive burden, mixed activation, relapse-prone sleep disruption,
and mood cycling more effectively than either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose.

Prior-art searching identified *Use of agomelatine in obtaining
medicaments intended for the treatment of bipolar disorders* and other
bipolar references, including antimanic disclosures, endoxifen-based
bipolar I references, and general combination-therapy references.
Those references do not teach the specific combination of
agomelatine and perospirone for Bipolar I Disorder, nor the
circadian-stabilization plus manic-salience containment framework
disclosed herein.

The present invention is not intended to support unsupervised
antidepressant-style use in bipolar I disorder. Rather, it is intended
for bipolar-specific care under psychiatric supervision with explicit
switch monitoring and escalation to higher-acuity care when mania or
psychosis requires it.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof and
(b) perospirone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Bipolar I Disorder in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering a therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and
a therapeutically effective amount of perospirone. In preferred
embodiments, agomelatine is administered at bedtime and perospirone is
administered once or twice daily depending on manic or mixed symptom
burden and tolerability.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine in a first dosage form, (b) perospirone in a second
dosage form, and (c) written instructions for use in subjects with
Bipolar I Disorder, including guidance regarding sleep regularity,
mood monitoring, mania / psychosis escalation procedures, and
continued mood-stabilizer or psychotherapy support where appropriate.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg, preferably administered orally at bedtime. The
invention contemplates perospirone in a daily dose of about 4 mg to
about 48 mg, preferably about 8 mg to about 32 mg per day in one or
two divided doses.

The technical effect of the combination is expected to be greater than
additive improvement in one or more endpoints selected from the group
consisting of bipolar-depression severity, manic or mixed activation
burden, sleep continuity, episode recurrence frequency, role function,
and Clinical Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I), compared with
either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Bipolar I Disorder"* means bipolar I disorder as defined by DSM-5-TR,
ICD-11, or an equivalent diagnostic framework, including subjects with
a history of at least one manic episode and optional depressive,
mixed-feature, or psychotic episodes.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide and pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, co-crystals,
prodrugs, and metabolically equivalent forms that retain clinically
relevant MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan for
schizophrenia and includes the free base, pharmaceutically acceptable
salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, labeled forms, and
metabolically equivalent analogues that retain clinically relevant D2
and 5-HT2A antagonism.

*"Manic or mixed activation burden"* means a clinically meaningful
burden of elevated or irritable mood, reduced need for sleep, racing
thoughts, pressured behavior, psychomotor acceleration, psychotic
salience, or mixed-state agitation occurring in the context of bipolar
I illness.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount that produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in one or more bipolar I endpoints
when used according to a regimen disclosed herein.

### 2. Active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. In the present invention, its principal contribution is
restoration of circadian alignment, improvement in sleep continuity,
and reduction of depressive inertia.

#### 2.2 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic with D2 antagonism, 5-HT2A
antagonism, and 5-HT1A partial agonist activity. In the present
invention, its principal contribution is attenuation of manic or mixed
activation, psychotic salience, and behavioral overdrive.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
perospirone together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients. The actives may be provided as separate oral
tablets or capsules, as a co-packaged kit, or as a day-night blister
card that links bedtime agomelatine use with daytime or divided
perospirone use.

Suitable excipients include common diluents, binders, disintegrants,
lubricants, coating systems, and release-control excipients
appropriate for oral dosage forms. In preferred embodiments the
agomelatine component is administered at bedtime and the perospirone
component is titrated according to manic, mixed, or psychotic symptom
burden and tolerability.

### 4. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with Bipolar I Disorder, a preferred regimen
comprises:

* Agomelatine 25 mg to 50 mg orally at bedtime.
* Perospirone 4 mg to 48 mg per day, preferably 8 mg to 32 mg per day
  in one or two divided doses.

In certain embodiments perospirone is initiated first in subjects with
current mania, mixed activation, or psychotic destabilization and
agomelatine is layered in after initial containment and sleep
regularization. In other embodiments agomelatine is initiated during
bipolar depression or post-manic recovery while perospirone is
maintained or titrated to control activation risk.

In preferred embodiments the subject's background mood-stabilizer or
psychotherapy regimen is stable so that the symptomatic effect of the
present combination can be meaningfully assessed.

### 5. Subject populations

Preferred subjects are adults with clinician-confirmed Bipolar I
Disorder, especially subjects with bipolar depression, recurrent sleep
destabilization, mixed-feature agitation, post-manic recovery
instability, or residual manic-salience risk despite existing care.
Certain embodiments target subjects who have had incomplete response or
limited tolerability with prior mood stabilizers or antipsychotic-
based regimens.

Exclusion criteria for preferred embodiments include uncontrolled
psychiatric emergency requiring immediate inpatient management,
violent or severely disorganized mania requiring alternate acute
containment, severe hepatic impairment incompatible with agomelatine
use, intolerance to dopamine antagonists, or inability to participate
in close psychiatric monitoring.

### 6. Mechanistic rationale

Bipolar I illness often combines a circadian-depressive layer and a
manic-salience layer. The circadian-depressive layer includes
insomnia, rhythm destabilization, poor overnight recovery, and
depressive inertia. The manic-salience layer includes elevated or
irritable mood, reduced need for sleep, psychomotor acceleration, and
psychotic or overvalued salience amplification.

Agomelatine addresses the circadian-depressive layer through MT1 / MT2
agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism. Perospirone addresses the manic-
salience layer through D2 / 5-HT2A antagonism with additional 5-HT1A
partial agonist support. The combination is therefore expected to
produce larger improvements in bipolar-symptom burden and episode
stability than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

### 7. Administration and monitoring

Monitoring may include bipolar-depression rating scales, mania or
mixed-feature assessment, sleep diary, liver-function tests,
extrapyramidal symptom monitoring, prolactin-related tolerability,
psychosis burden, relapse tracking, and CGI-I.

The regimen is not intended to replace hospitalization, emergency
management of severe mania or psychosis, lithium / valproate or other
established mood-stabilizer care when indicated, or longitudinal
psychotherapy and psychosocial support.

### 8. Prophetic clinical study

A prophetic Phase 2 study enrolls adults with Bipolar I Disorder and
baseline depressive, mixed, or post-manic instability, provided acute
immediate-danger conditions are first stabilized. Subjects are
randomized to: (a) agomelatine plus perospirone; (b) perospirone plus
placebo; or (c) placebo plus stable standard of care, for 12 weeks
after titration.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in a bipolar-symptom
composite emphasizing depressive burden, activation control, and sleep
stability. Key secondary endpoints include manic or mixed activation
burden, sleep continuity, relapse frequency, role function, and CGI-I.
The combination arm is expected to show the largest improvement in
overall bipolar stability with acceptable tolerability. FIGS. 3-5 are
prophetic illustrations of those expected outcomes.

### 9. Prior-art distinction

The closest prior-art references identified in the prior-art
landscape include:

* *Use of agomelatine in obtaining medicaments intended for the
  treatment of bipolar disorders* - discloses agomelatine in bipolar
  illness generally, but does not teach perospirone co-administration.
* Bipolar I and antimanic references - disclose different agents or
  treatment frameworks, not the specific agomelatine + perospirone
  pairing disclosed herein.
* General psychiatric combination-therapy references - do not teach
  the circadian-stabilization plus manic-salience containment
  framework disclosed herein.

The present invention is distinct in the specific receptor classes
combined, the specific actives chosen, and the explicit bipolar I
framework coupling circadian stabilization with manic-salience
containment.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Bipolar I
Disorder comprising co-administration of a therapeutically effective
amount of the melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist and 5-HT2C antagonist
agomelatine and a therapeutically effective amount of the atypical
antipsychotic perospirone are disclosed. The two agents act on
non-overlapping circadian-depressive and manic-salience containment
pathways relevant to bipolar I illness and are expected to produce
greater-than-additive improvement in overall bipolar stability, sleep
continuity, and relapse control compared with either agent alone at
any individually achievable dose under psychiatric supervision.
