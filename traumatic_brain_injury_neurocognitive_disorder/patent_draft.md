## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Traumatic Brain Injury Neurocognitive Disorder
(TBI-NCD) by co-administering (a) a histamine H3 receptor
antagonist or inverse agonist and (b) a muscarinic M1/M4 receptor
agonist, in particular the combination of pitolisant and
xanomeline, optionally further with a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic such as trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic
adverse effects. The invention addresses attention, daytime
alertness, executive dysfunction, and cognitive inefficiency in
subjects with persistent neurocognitive symptoms after traumatic
brain injury; it does not assert treatment of acute head trauma or
disease-modification of structural brain injury itself.

## Background of the invention

Traumatic Brain Injury Neurocognitive Disorder is recognized in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a syndrome of cognitive decline attributable
to traumatic brain injury. The affected cognitive domains may
include complex attention, executive function, learning and memory,
processing speed, perceptual-motor function, and social cognition.
The syndrome may persist after mild, moderate, or severe TBI and
often coexists with sleep disturbance, fatigue, irritability,
headache, and reduced functional endurance.

The prevalence of persistent cognitive sequelae after TBI is
clinically meaningful across military, sports, accident, and
civilian trauma populations. The burden includes reduced work and
school performance, slowed information processing, daytime
sleepiness, impaired multitasking, caregiver burden, and reduced
quality of life. Unlike primary neurodegenerative conditions, the
clinical course is shaped by injury severity, recovery stage,
comorbid pain or sleep disturbance, mood symptoms, and systemic
physiologic stress.

The current standard of care is supportive and rehabilitative. It
consists primarily of cognitive rehabilitation, sleep optimization,
treatment of headache and mood symptoms, minimization of
anticholinergic or sedating polypharmacy, and selected empirical
use of stimulants or cognitive agents. No pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
specifically for TBI-NCD as a primary indication. Where clinicians
do use cognition-directed medication, they often borrow stimulants,
wake-promoting agents, or cholinergic therapies from other
indications, with inconsistent benefit and tolerability.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses is the
absence of a defensible, mechanism-based symptomatic regimen for
the mixed attention-plus-arousal phenotype of TBI-NCD. A single-
agent approach often leaves one half of the phenotype untreated: a
purely wake-promoting intervention may improve alertness without
restoring cortical cholinergic signal quality, while a purely
pro-cholinergic intervention may not adequately address fatigue and
vigilance instability.

The present invention recognizes that two non-overlapping CNS axes
can be combined to support cognition and daytime functioning in
TBI-NCD. First, the histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic auto-
and heteroreceptor that, when constitutively active, suppresses the
release of histamine and modulates acetylcholine, dopamine, and
norepinephrine transmission. Selective inverse agonism at H3 by
pitolisant elevates extracellular histamine and supports
state-dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine without the
abuse liability of conventional stimulants. The pitolisant axis
therefore addresses the *vigilance, attention, and daytime
activation* substrate of TBI-NCD.

Second, muscarinic M1 receptor signaling facilitates cortical and
hippocampal cognition, while M4 signaling contributes to behavioral
stabilization. Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist
developed in the 1990s and more recently formulated with the
peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium as the FDA-approved
xanomeline-trospium combination (Cobenfy®) for schizophrenia in
adults. The xanomeline axis therefore addresses the *cortical-
hippocampal cholinergic substrate* of TBI-NCD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an H3 antagonist or inverse agonist
in combination with a muscarinic M1/M4 agonist for TBI-NCD
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance- and arousal-
enabled cognitive support (a pitolisant H3 axis) and post-synaptic
muscarinic facilitation of cortical cognition (a xanomeline M1/M4
axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of
TBI-NCD. The combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic
cognitive and functional benefit than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose, without asserting neuroregeneration
or acute neuroprotection.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, (b) xanomeline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, and (c) optionally a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic agent such as trospium chloride in an amount
effective to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Traumatic Brain Injury Neurocognitive Disorder in a human subject in
need thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline, optionally further
with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic. Co-administration
may be simultaneous, in a unitary dosage form or in separate dosage
forms, or sequential, with each agent administered within a window
of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) xanomeline or a salt thereof, optionally co-formulated with
trospium, in a second unitary dosage form, and (c) written
instructions for the symptomatic treatment of TBI-NCD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily. The
invention contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about 50 mg to
about 250 mg administered orally as the free base or as the tartrate
salt in two divided doses; when xanomeline is co-administered with
trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is about 40 mg to about
60 mg administered orally in two divided doses.

The technical effect of the combination is (a) attenuation, in
subjects with TBI-NCD, of impairment on validated cognitive
instruments; (b) improvement in complex attention and executive
function; and (c) reduction in daytime fatigue and caregiver burden,
each greater than would be obtained with either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse
agonist marketed as Wakix®. The term includes pitolisant free base,
hydrochloride or other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent H3 inverse agonists.

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-
yl]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine, an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist. The term as used herein includes the free base,
the tartrate salt and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Trospium chloride"* or *"trospium"* means the quaternary ammonium
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist that does not
appreciably cross the blood-brain barrier and may be used to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline.

*"Traumatic Brain Injury Neurocognitive Disorder"* or *"TBI-NCD"*
means the neurocognitive syndrome attributable to traumatic brain
injury, including impairment in complex attention, executive
function, memory, processing speed, or related domains.

*"Attention / executive-function composite"* means a clinically
meaningful burden of impairment in attention, working memory,
processing speed, or executive function quantified by structured
neurocognitive assessment.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of TBI-NCD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces
a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: attention /
executive-function composite, processing speed, daytime sleepiness,
functional independence, caregiver burden, or investigator-rated
CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two or more active agents to a
subject so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted
on the subject during the period in which the pharmacological
effect of the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether
the two agents are administered in the same dosage form, in
separate dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse agonist
that increases histaminergic wake drive and supports state-
dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of pitolisant is
attenuation of fatigue, vigilance instability, and slowed
processing associated with TBI-NCD.

#### 2.2 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
xanomeline is support of cortical and hippocampal cognition and
executive function.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
xanomeline, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of a pitolisant tablet and a xanomeline or xanomeline-trospium
dosage form.

Excipients useful for the pitolisant composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the xanomeline
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and stabilizing excipients appropriate for muscarinic agonist oral
delivery.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
morning administration. Xanomeline is preferably formulated as an
oral immediate-release or modified-release dosage form administered
in divided doses. The preferred commercial-development embodiment
contemplates xanomeline co-administered with trospium to mitigate
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pitolisant tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of xanomeline or
xanomeline-trospium units, and (c) written instructions aligned to
cognitive rehabilitation, sleep optimization, and symptom
monitoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with TBI-NCD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pitolisant 4.5 mg orally once daily, titratable to 17.8 mg and
  then to 35.6 mg once daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.
* Xanomeline 50 mg orally twice daily, titratable to 100 mg or
  125 mg twice daily if clinically indicated and tolerated; when
  paired with trospium, trospium is administered at about 20 mg to
  30 mg twice daily.

The regimen is not intended to replace acute neurotrauma care,
management of intracranial complications, seizure treatment, or
primary rehabilitation. The preferred embodiment is symptomatic
use in persistent post-injury cognitive dysfunction.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
TBI-NCD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 18 years; prior traumatic brain injury with
persistent neurocognitive symptoms; clinically meaningful attention,
executive, or arousal deficits; and participation in or willingness
to engage in rehabilitation-based care. Exclusion criteria include
unstable acute intracranial injury, uncontrolled seizures requiring
alternate management, severe cholinergic intolerance, unsafe QT or
other cardiac-conduction abnormality, and known hypersensitivity to
either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

TBI-NCD often includes two interacting layers: impaired arousal and
vigilance support, and reduced cholinergic cortical signal quality.

Pitolisant addresses the vigilance-arousal layer through H3
antagonism / inverse agonism. Xanomeline addresses the cholinergic-
cognition layer through M1/M4 agonism. The two axes are non-
overlapping at the receptor level and converge on improved
attention and executive function.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in TBI-NCD burden than either monotherapy alone.
the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 28,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding and inverse-agonism assays
for pitolisant; M1 and M4 receptor binding and signaling assays for
xanomeline; and combined arousal / cognitive network assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include traumatic-injury cognitive models, attention-set shifting,
processing-speed tasks, wake-maintenance paradigms, and
goal-directed behavior assays. A supra-additive effect of the
combination on restoration of attention and daytime function,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
pitolisant 17.8 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: xanomeline or
xanomeline-trospium for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, orthostatic vital signs,
and tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The
principal purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of
clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult TBI-NCD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant + xanomeline.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pitolisant + placebo for xanomeline.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with rehabilitation-based
  standard care.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in attention /
executive-function composite at week 12. Secondary endpoints
include processing speed, daytime sleepiness, functional
independence, caregiver burden, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of an H3 antagonist or inverse
agonist with a muscarinic M1/M4 agonist for TBI-NCD specifically.
The closest hits are:

* Muscarinic-activation patents disclose disorders ameliorated by
  muscarinic receptor activation but do not teach the present
  attention-plus-arousal combination.
* Generic H3 antagonist patents disclose wake-promoting agents but
  not the present TBI-NCD indication.
* Broader CNS-disorder and neuropsychiatric patents do not disclose
  the present convergent-circuit rationale in persistent post-TBI
  cognitive dysfunction.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (H3 antagonism /
inverse agonism + M1/M4 agonism), (ii) the specific actives
(pitolisant + xanomeline), and (iii) the specific indication
(TBI-NCD). No prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-circuit
rationale on which the present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Traumatic Brain Injury Neurocognitive Disorder comprising co-
administration of a therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant
and a therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline are disclosed,
optionally with trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse
effects. The two agents act on non-overlapping arousal-support and
cortical-cholinergic circuits and are expected to produce greater-
than-additive improvement in attention, executive function, and
daytime functioning than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.
