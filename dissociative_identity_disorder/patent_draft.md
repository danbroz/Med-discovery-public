## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) by
co-administering (a) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor that is also a
5-HT1A receptor partial agonist and (b) a corticotropin-releasing
hormone receptor type 1 (CRHR1) antagonist, in particular the
combination of vilazodone and crinecerfont. The invention is
intended for adult subjects with DID who are concurrently receiving
phase-oriented psychotherapy and addresses dissociative switching,
trauma-triggered state shifts, dissociative distress, and
stress-reactive affective dysregulation.

## Background of the invention

Dissociative Identity Disorder is a dissociative disorder defined
in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by disruption of identity characterized by
two or more distinct personality states or an experience of
possession, accompanied by marked discontinuity in sense of self
and agency, with related alterations in affect, behavior,
consciousness, memory, perception, cognition, or sensory-motor
functioning. The disorder is also characterized by recurrent gaps
in recall of everyday events, important personal information, or
traumatic events that are inconsistent with ordinary forgetting.
The condition is strongly associated with severe developmental
trauma and chronic stress exposure.

The prevalence of DID in the general population is low but likely
under-estimated because of diagnostic complexity and overlap with
other trauma-spectrum disorders. DID is associated with marked
functional impairment, high rates of self-harm and suicidality,
post-traumatic stress symptoms, depressive symptoms, somatic
symptoms, sleep disturbance, and crisis-driven service utilization.

The current standard of care is psychotherapeutic. It consists
primarily of long-term phase-oriented trauma therapy focused on
stabilization, symptom reduction, trauma processing, and identity
integration or harmonization. No pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
for the treatment of DID as a primary indication. Off-label
medication use generally targets comorbid depression, anxiety,
insomnia, or post-traumatic stress symptoms rather than the core
dissociative phenotype. The pharmacologic literature in DID is
limited and largely indirect.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, DID is characterized by intrusive trauma-linked
cognition, anxious hyperarousal, affective dysregulation, and
state-dependent compulsive avoidance. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an
orally available, FDA-approved antidepressant that combines
selective serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the
5-HT1A receptor. This dual mechanism supports attenuation of
anxious-compulsive symptomatology, intrusive ideation, and
stress-sensitive affective lability. The serotonergic axis
therefore addresses the *intrusive-ideation and affective-
dysregulation* substrate of DID, but does not directly attenuate
stress-axis overactivation.

Second, DID is strongly linked to chronic trauma-related
hyperactivation of the HPA axis. Corticotropin-releasing factor
(CRF) acting at CRHR1 contributes to hyperarousal, exaggerated
stress responsivity, and context-triggered physiologic escalation
that may precede dissociative switching. Crinecerfont
(CRENESSITY®) is a selective CRHR1 antagonist approved by the FDA
in 2024 as adjunctive treatment to glucocorticoid replacement in
classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, where it reduces CRF-driven
ACTH signaling. More broadly, CRHR1 antagonism has long been a
mechanistically attractive approach for trauma-spectrum and stress-
related conditions. In the present invention, crinecerfont
addresses the *stress-axis and switch-triggering hyperarousal*
substrate of DID.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a 5-HT1A-partial-agonist SSRI in
combination with a CRHR1 antagonist for DID specifically.

The present invention recognizes that serotonergic attenuation of
intrusive-ideation and affective dysregulation (a vilazodone
HTR1A axis) and CRHR1-mediated attenuation of trauma-triggered
hyperarousal (a crinecerfont axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for treatment of DID. The combination is
expected to produce a greater reduction in dissociative switching,
dissociative distress, and crisis intensity than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) crinecerfont or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Dissociative Identity Disorder in an adult subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective
amount of crinecerfont. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) crinecerfont or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of DID according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein, including coordination with
phase-oriented psychotherapy and safety planning.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally with food once daily, with
a preferred adult dose of 20 to 40 mg/day after a 7-day titration
from 10 mg/day. The invention contemplates crinecerfont in a daily
dose of about 100 mg to about 200 mg administered orally in two
divided doses with meals, with a preferred dose of 100 mg twice
daily. The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of the
HTR1A / serotonergic and CRHR1 stress-axis pathways during trauma-
triggered destabilization and psychotherapy-linked stabilization
windows.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in adult
subjects with DID, in dissociative state-switch frequency,
dissociative distress, trauma-triggered crisis intensity, and
overall dissociation severity, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of vilazodone and crinecerfont administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Crinecerfont"* means the selective corticotropin-releasing
hormone receptor type 1 (CRHR1) antagonist approved in the United
States as CRENESSITY®. The term includes crinecerfont free base,
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically
equivalent CRHR1 antagonists within the same activity genus.

*"Dissociative Identity Disorder"* or *"DID"* means the dissociative
disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by disruption of identity
characterized by two or more distinct personality states or an
experience of possession, accompanied by marked discontinuity in
sense of self and agency and recurrent gaps in recall of everyday
events, important personal information, or traumatic events.

*"Dissociative switch"* means a clinically meaningful transition
between identity states or marked discontinuity in sense of self /
agency as documented by subject report, therapist report, or
structured diary.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult subject for the
treatment of DID according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
monthly dissociative-switch frequency, Dissociative Experiences
Scale-II total, dissociative-distress score, crisis intensity
score, self-harm event count, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin
across multiple central serotonergic targets; by partially
agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, vilazodone supports attenuation of
anxious-compulsive symptomatology, intrusive ideation, and
affective lability. Vilazodone is FDA-approved for the treatment
of major depressive disorder in adults at oral doses of 10 mg/day
(initial), titrated to 20 mg/day after one week and to 40 mg/day
thereafter, taken once daily with food to support oral
bioavailability. Vilazodone is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4 and
is a substrate (and weak inhibitor) of CYP2C19 and CYP2D6.
Vilazodone is generic in the United States.

#### 2.2 Crinecerfont

Crinecerfont is a selective CRHR1 antagonist approved by the FDA
in 2024 as adjunctive treatment to glucocorticoid replacement in
classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia in adults and pediatric
patients 4 years of age and older. It suppresses CRF-driven ACTH
signaling and is available in capsule and oral-solution
formulations. Approved dosing is 100 mg twice daily with meals in
adults. The compound is under active U.S. patent protection into
the 2040s; clinical use of the present invention will require
coordination with the rights holder or a licenseable development
path.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
crinecerfont, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as a vilazodone
tablet and a crinecerfont capsule or oral solution.

The vilazodone component may be formulated as the hydrochloride
salt in immediate-release tablet form. The crinecerfont component
may be formulated as capsule or oral solution for administration
with meals. Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition
include diluents (lactose monohydrate, microcrystalline cellulose),
binders (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers. Excipients useful for
the crinecerfont composition include capsule fill excipients,
suspending agents, flavoring agents for oral solution, buffers, and
vehicles suitable for meal-associated dosing.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-
release oral tablet at strengths of 10 mg, 20 mg, and 40 mg.
Crinecerfont is preferably formulated as oral capsules and/or oral
solution suitable for twice-daily dosing with meals.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of crinecerfont capsules
or oral solution, and (c) written instructions aligned to meals,
phase-oriented psychotherapy, and safety planning.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with DID, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter.
* Crinecerfont 100 mg orally twice daily with meals.

Broad genus ranges include vilazodone 10–40 mg/day and crinecerfont
100–200 mg/day in two divided doses. The preferred schedule aligns
morning crinecerfont with breakfast and the evening dose with the
evening meal, and maintains vilazodone at a consistent morning
administration time.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: severe hepatic or renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication. Standard meal-associated crinecerfont dosing is
maintained in mild-to-moderate impairment only if clinically
appropriate.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers until definitive DDI
characterization is available for the combination. Vilazodone dose
adjustment may be required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with DID. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age ≥ 18 years; current DID diagnosis established by
qualified clinician using structured diagnostic interview where
available; baseline clinically meaningful dissociation severity;
participation in phase-oriented psychotherapy; and a documented
safety plan. Exclusion criteria include active psychosis better
explaining the identity disturbance, uncontrolled substance use
disorder, severe hepatic or renal impairment, pregnancy, and
inability to comply with structured psychotherapy and safety
planning.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

DID is maintained by interaction between trauma-triggered
hyperarousal and state-dependent dissociative coping. Trauma cues
produce physiologic escalation, intrusive internal experience, and
in some subjects state transitions that reduce moment-to-moment
integration of memory, agency, and affect.

Vilazodone addresses intrusive ideation, anxious-compulsive
amplification, and affective lability. Crinecerfont addresses the
stress-axis component by attenuating CRHR1-mediated escalation of
trauma-triggered physiologic arousal. The two axes are non-
overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the same
dissociative-switch maintenance loop.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in dissociative-switch frequency, crisis
intensity, and dissociative distress than either monotherapy alone.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; CRHR1 binding and ACTH-suppression cellular assays for
crinecerfont; and combined stress-hormone / salience-network assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include stress-potentiated startle, contextual fear-extinction
models, avoidance / conflict paradigms, and HPA-axis biomarker
readouts such as ACTH and corticosterone suppression. No animal
model is asserted to reproduce DID itself; rather, the preclinical
program supports the convergent mechanisms of intrusive stress
amplification and physiologic hyperarousal. A supra-additive effect
of the combination on stress-triggered behavioral destabilization,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 40 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2:
crinecerfont 100 mg twice daily with meals for 7 days; Period 3:
the combination for 7 days. Full PK sampling, ACTH / cortisol
biomarkers, and tolerability monitoring are obtained in each
period. The principal purpose is to establish tolerability and
absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult DID (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 90 (30 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + crinecerfont.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for
  crinecerfont.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard-of-care
  phase-oriented psychotherapy.

All subjects receive standardized psychotherapy emphasizing
stabilization and safety. The primary endpoint is change from
baseline in monthly dissociative-switch frequency at week 12.
Secondary endpoints include Dissociative Experiences Scale-II
total, crisis intensity score, dissociative-distress score,
self-harm events, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Dissociative Identity Disorder in adult subjects comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
SSRI / 5-HT1A partial agonist vilazodone and a therapeutically
effective amount of the CRHR1 antagonist crinecerfont are
disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping receptor classes
that converge on trauma-hyperarousal and dissociative-switching
circuits implicated in DID, and are expected to produce greater-
than-additive improvement in dissociative-switch frequency,
dissociative distress, and crisis intensity than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose when used in conjunction with
phase-oriented psychotherapy.
