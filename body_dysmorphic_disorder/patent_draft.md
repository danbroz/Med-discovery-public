## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Body Dysmorphic Disorder (BDD) by
co-administering (a) a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse agonist /
antagonist, in particular pimavanserin, and (b) a serotonergic
antidepressant having serotonin reuptake inhibition and 5-HT1A
partial agonist activity, in particular vilazodone. The invention
is intended for subjects with persistent preoccupation with
perceived defects or flaws in physical appearance and addresses
appearance-related salience distortion, mirror checking,
camouflaging, social avoidance, and intrusive dysmorphic
preoccupation while continuing behavioral treatment and not
promoting unnecessary cosmetic intervention.

## Background of the invention

Body Dysmorphic Disorder is an obsessive-compulsive and related
disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by preoccupation with one
or more perceived defects or flaws in physical appearance that are
not observable or appear slight to others, together with repetitive
behaviors or mental acts such as mirror checking, excessive
grooming, reassurance seeking, comparing appearance with others, or
camouflaging. The disorder may involve poor insight or delusional-
intensity beliefs concerning the perceived defect.

The prevalence of BDD is clinically meaningful and the burden is
substantial. The disorder is associated with marked shame, social
withdrawal, depression, suicidality, occupational dysfunction,
excessive dermatologic or cosmetic procedures, and high comorbidity
with anxiety disorders, obsessive-compulsive symptoms, eating-
related concerns, and substance use. Many subjects become trapped
in repetitive appearance-focused checking and avoidance loops that
do not respond adequately to reassurance or cosmetic change.

The current standard of care is primarily psychotherapeutic and
serotonergic. It consists of specialized cognitive-behavioral
therapy with exposure and response prevention, trial use of SSRIs
or related antidepressants, and management of comorbid conditions.
No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for the treatment of BDD as
a primary indication. Borrowed serotonergic approaches are helpful
for some subjects but often leave persistent appearance-defect
salience and referential misinterpretation insufficiently treated.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, BDD includes intense anxious-distressed
preoccupation, intrusive defect-focused rumination, and compulsive
checking / avoidance loops that are strongly linked to serotonergic
tone and 5-HT1A-regulated anxious-compulsive cognition.
Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available antidepressant that
combines selective serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial
agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual mechanism supports
attenuation of anxious-compulsive symptomatology and intrusive
appearance-related ideation. The vilazodone axis therefore
addresses the *dysmorphic distress and anxious-rumination*
substrate of BDD.

Second, BDD also includes maladaptive salience tagging of perceived
appearance flaws, rigid attentional capture by mirrors or reflective
surfaces, and referential misinterpretation of others' gaze or
social cues. Pimavanserin (Nuplazid®) is a selective 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist approved by the FDA for hallucinations
and delusions associated with Parkinson disease psychosis.
Pimavanserin has no clinically meaningful D2 antagonism and
therefore can modify 5-HT2A-mediated salience processing without
adding direct dopaminergic motor burden. The pimavanserin axis
therefore addresses the *appearance-defect salience and
referential-interpretation rigidity* substrate of BDD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist /
antagonist in combination with a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist for BDD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of maladaptive
appearance-defect salience (a pimavanserin axis) and attenuation of
dysmorphic distress and anxious-rumination (a vilazodone axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of BDD. The
combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement
in dysmorphic preoccupation, checking, avoidance, and insight than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pimavanserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) vilazodone or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating BDD
in a human subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to
the subject a therapeutically effective amount of pimavanserin and
a therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of BDD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with specialized
behavioral treatment and without promoting unnecessary cosmetic
procedures.

The invention contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose of about
17 mg to about 34 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 34 mg/day. The invention contemplates
vilazodone in a daily dose of about 10 mg to about 40 mg
administered orally once daily with food, with a preferred dose of
20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from 10 mg/day. The inventive
feature is the concurrent engagement of 5-HT2A-linked salience
modulation and serotonergic / 5-HT1A modulation in a dysmorphic
preoccupation syndrome.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with BDD, in dysmorphic preoccupation, appearance-checking burden,
referential misinterpretation, and social avoidance, that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of pimavanserin and
vilazodone administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically equivalent
5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Body Dysmorphic Disorder"* or *"BDD"* means the obsessive-
compulsive and related disorder defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by
preoccupation with one or more perceived defects or flaws in
appearance that are not observable or appear slight to others,
together with repetitive behaviors or mental acts in response to
appearance concerns.

*"BDD severity total"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
preoccupation, distress, checking, camouflaging, avoidance, or poor
insight quantified by structured clinician assessment or validated
BDD instruments such as the BDD-YBOCS or comparable scales.

*"Referential-interpretation score"* means a clinically meaningful
burden of belief that others are noticing, judging, or reacting to
the perceived appearance defect in a distorted or rigid manner.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of BDD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: BDD severity
total, checking burden, referential-interpretation score,
social-avoidance score, insight score, or investigator-rated
CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. It has no clinically meaningful
affinity for dopamine D2 receptors. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of pimavanserin is attenuation
of maladaptive appearance-defect salience and referential
misinterpretation.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious-compulsive distress and intrusive dysmorphic rumination. In
the present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
vilazodone is reduction of dysmorphic distress and appearance-
focused anxious preoccupation.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pimavanserin and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a pimavanserin capsule or
tablet and a vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the pimavanserin composition include capsule
fill excipients, diluents, disintegrants, and stabilizers suitable
for marketed pimavanserin formulation profiles. Excipients useful
for the vilazodone composition include diluents (microcrystalline
cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders (hydroxypropyl
methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants (crospovidone, sodium
starch glycolate), lubricants (magnesium stearate), glidants
(colloidal silicon dioxide), and film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or tablet
suitable for once-daily use. Vilazodone is preferably formulated as
an oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pimavanserin capsules or tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to
specialized behavioral treatment, social-exposure work, and careful
review of requests for unnecessary cosmetic procedures.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with BDD, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Pimavanserin 17 mg orally once daily, titratable to 34 mg once
  daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.

Broad genus ranges include pimavanserin 17-34 mg/day and vilazodone
10-40 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses once-daily pimavanserin
and once-daily vilazodone with food.

Medication-medication interactions: vilazodone dose adjustment may
be required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Corrected QT interval
monitoring should be considered when pimavanserin is combined with
other QT-prolonging agents. The regimen is not intended to replace
clinically necessary treatment of actual dermatologic, ophthalmic,
or surgical conditions, nor to promote elective cosmetic procedures
as a primary psychiatric intervention.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
BDD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 12 years; persistent preoccupation with perceived
appearance flaws; repetitive appearance-related behaviors or mental
acts; clinically meaningful distress or impairment; and willingness
to participate in specialized behavioral treatment. Exclusion
criteria include untreated primary psychotic disorder unrelated to
BDD, severe hepatic impairment, clinically significant baseline QT
prolongation, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

BDD often includes two interacting layers: intense dysmorphic
distress and intrusive rumination, and maladaptive salience
assigned to perceived defects together with rigid interpretation of
others' gaze or reactions.

Vilazodone addresses the dysmorphic-distress layer through SERT
inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Pimavanserin addresses the
appearance-salience layer through selective 5-HT2A inverse agonism /
antagonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on the same BDD phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in BDD burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 34,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A binding and inverse-agonism assays for
pimavanserin; SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; and combined salience / obsessive-rumination network
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include compulsive checking models, salience-allocation paradigms,
social-evaluation threat paradigms, and repetitive attention-
capture tasks. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
reduction of dysmorphic salience and intrusive checking-like
behavior, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
pimavanserin 34 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone titrated to
20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability assessments are obtained
in each period. The principal purpose is to establish tolerability
and absence of clinically meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult BDD (FIG. 4). Three arms,
1:1:1 randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pimavanserin + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for
  pimavanserin.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with specialized behavioral
  treatment.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in BDD severity total
at week 16. Secondary endpoints include checking burden,
referential-interpretation score, social-avoidance score, insight
score, and CGI-I.

* Pimavanserin psychosis and impulse-control filings disclose the
  active but not the present pair or the present dysmorphic

* Generic psychiatric or antipsychotic-combination patents do not
  disclose the present appearance-salience rationale.
* Dermatologic or dystrophy-related patents surfaced by semantic

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (selective 5-HT2A
inverse agonism / antagonism + SERT inhibition + 5-HT1A partial
agonism), (ii) the specific actives (pimavanserin + vilazodone),
and (iii) the specific indication (BDD). No prior-art disclosure
suggests the convergent-circuit rationale on which the present
invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Body
Dysmorphic Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of pimavanserin and a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone are disclosed. The
two agents act on non-overlapping appearance-defect salience and
dysmorphic-distress circuits implicated in persistent appearance-
focused preoccupation and are expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in BDD severity, checking burden, and social
avoidance than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose.
