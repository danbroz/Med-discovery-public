## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorder Due to
Another Medical Condition (OCRD-AMC) by co-administering
(a) a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse agonist / antagonist and
(b) a serotonergic antidepressant with serotonin reuptake
inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism, in particular the
combination of pimavanserin and vilazodone. The invention is
intended for subjects whose obsessional, compulsive, or related
symptoms are attributable to a defined medical condition and who
continue receiving standard care for that underlying condition; it
does not assert disease-modification of the underlying medical
condition itself.

## Background of the invention

Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorder Due to Another Medical
Condition is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a syndrome in
which obsessional thoughts, compulsive rituals, preoccupations, or
repetitive behaviors arise as a direct physiologic consequence of a
medical condition. The underlying medical condition may include
neurologic injury, autoimmune or inflammatory disease, basal
ganglia disorders, temporal-lobe epilepsy, post-infectious
syndromes, endocrine disease, or other medically recognized causes
of obsessive-compulsive symptomatology. In such cases, the
phenotype may resemble primary OCD, but the etiologic driver is
medical rather than idiopathic.

The prevalence of OCRD-AMC is difficult to estimate because the
disorder is typically coded by the underlying medical condition
rather than by the symptom syndrome itself. Nonetheless, the
clinical burden is substantial. Subjects may experience intrusive
thoughts, checking rituals, contamination-like fears, symmetry /
ordering behaviors, or repetitive mental acts that interfere with
adherence, rehabilitation, sleep, and caregiver functioning.
Unlike idiopathic OCD, the course is often shaped by the stability
of the underlying medical condition and by concurrent neurologic or
medical therapy.

The current standard of care is supportive and etiology-directed. It
consists primarily of treating the underlying medical condition to
the extent possible, reducing precipitating medications where
feasible, and using psychotherapy or empiric serotonergic treatment
when clinically appropriate. No pharmaceutical product is approved
by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
specifically for OCRD-AMC as a primary indication. When medication
is used, clinicians often borrow standard OCD regimens, but those
approaches are inconsistently effective and may not adequately
address the altered salience-network physiology associated with a
medical-cause obsessional syndrome.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, obsessional distress and compulsive drive are
strongly linked to serotonergic tone and the regulation of
anxious-compulsive cognition. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally
available antidepressant that combines selective serotonin
reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor.
This dual mechanism supports attenuation of anxious-compulsive
symptomatology and intrusive ideation. The vilazodone axis
therefore addresses the *obsessional distress and anxious-
compulsive amplification* substrate of OCRD-AMC.

Second, medically driven obsessive-compulsive syndromes often
include pathologic salience tagging, rigid internal cue weighting,
and perseverative attentional capture. Pimavanserin (Nuplazid®) is
a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist approved by the FDA
for hallucinations and delusions associated with Parkinson disease
psychosis. Pimavanserin has no clinically meaningful D2 antagonism
and therefore can modify 5-HT2A-mediated salience processing
without adding dopaminergic motor burden. The pimavanserin axis
therefore addresses the *maladaptive salience and perseverative
attention* substrate of OCRD-AMC.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist /
antagonist in combination with a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist for OCRD-AMC specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of obsessional
distress (a vilazodone HTR1A / SERT axis) and attenuation of
maladaptive salience tagging (a pimavanserin 5-HT2A axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of OCRD-AMC.
The combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic
improvement in obsessive-compulsive severity than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose, without asserting
disease-modification of the underlying medical condition.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pimavanserin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) vilazodone or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorder Due to Another Medical
Condition in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of pimavanserin and a therapeutically effective amount of
vilazodone. Co-administration may be simultaneous or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of OCRD-AMC
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein while continuing
standard care for the underlying medical condition.

The invention contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose of about
17 mg to about 34 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred adult dose of 34 mg/day. The invention contemplates
vilazodone in a daily dose of about 10 mg to about 40 mg
administered orally once daily with food, with a preferred dose of
20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from 10 mg/day. The inventive
feature is the concurrent engagement of 5-HT2A salience regulation
and 5-HT1A / serotonin-transporter-mediated reduction of obsessional
distress.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in
subjects with OCRD-AMC, in obsessional severity, compulsive
frequency, intrusive-thought burden, and caregiver burden, that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of pimavanserin and
vilazodone administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically equivalent
5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorder Due to Another Medical
Condition"* or *"OCRD-AMC"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 syndrome in
which obsessional thoughts, compulsive acts, or related repetitive
behaviors arise as a direct physiologic consequence of a defined
medical condition rather than as primary idiopathic OCD.

*"Obsessional severity"* means a clinically meaningful burden of
intrusive thoughts, urges, or repetitive behaviors as quantified by
a validated instrument such as the Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive
Scale (Y-BOCS) or by a comparable disorder-specific scale.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the
treatment of OCRD-AMC according to a dosing regimen disclosed
herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least
one of: Y-BOCS total, obsession subscale, compulsion subscale,
caregiver burden, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. It has no clinically meaningful
affinity for dopamine D2 receptors, which differentiates it from
many atypical antipsychotics and avoids direct dopaminergic motor
burden. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of pimavanserin is modulation of maladaptive salience
assignment and rigid attentional capture by obsessional stimuli.
The labeled adult dose is 34 mg orally once daily, with no dose
titration required.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin;
by partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation
of anxious-compulsive symptomatology and intrusive ideation.
Vilazodone is approved for major depressive disorder in adults at
oral doses titrated from 10 mg to 20 mg and then to 40 mg/day,
taken with food to support bioavailability. Vilazodone is generic
in the United States.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pimavanserin and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a pimavanserin capsule and a
vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the pimavanserin composition include capsule
fill excipients, diluents, disintegrants, and stabilizers suitable
for the marketed pimavanserin formulation profile. Excipients useful
for the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or tablet
suitable for once-daily dosing. Vilazodone is preferably formulated
as an immediate-release oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pimavanserin capsules or tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to
continuation of standard care for the underlying medical
condition.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with OCRD-AMC, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pimavanserin 34 mg orally once daily.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter.

Broad genus ranges include pimavanserin 17–34 mg/day and
vilazodone 10–40 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses consistent
once-daily administration for both agents, with vilazodone taken
with food.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: severe hepatic or renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers without review of pimavanserin
exposure. Vilazodone dose adjustment may be required with strong
CYP3A4 inhibitors. Caution is warranted with other QT-prolonging
agents.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
OCRD-AMC. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: clinician-confirmed obsessive-compulsive and related
symptoms attributable to a defined medical condition; stable
management of the underlying medical condition for at least
4 weeks where clinically possible; clinically meaningful
obsessional severity at baseline; and capacity to participate in
psychotherapy or supportive behavioral management where indicated.
Exclusion criteria include primary idiopathic OCD without a
medical-condition etiology, uncontrolled psychosis, unstable
medical disease requiring acute intervention, severe hepatic or
renal impairment, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

OCRD-AMC is characterized by two interacting symptom layers:
obsessional distress / anxiety and maladaptive salience tagging of
intrusive thoughts or repetitive acts to the cognitive systems
already perturbed by the underlying medical condition.

Vilazodone addresses the anxious-compulsive amplification layer by
reducing intrusive ideation and obsessional distress through
serotonergic mechanisms. Pimavanserin addresses the maladaptive
salience-tagging layer by attenuating 5-HT2A-mediated cortical
salience dysregulation without introducing D2 receptor antagonism.
The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and
converge on the obsessional-salience circuit.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in obsessional severity and compulsive
frequency than either monotherapy alone. The taxonomy-aware pair
score for this combination, derived from the master receptor-
efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: 5-HT2A binding and inverse-agonism assays for
pimavanserin; SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; and combined cortical salience-network assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include marble-burying, reversal learning, uncertainty-driven
checking paradigms, and compulsive grooming or perseveration models
superimposed on disease-relevant neurologic or inflammatory models
where appropriate. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
obsessional / compulsive behavior and inflexible salience
assignment, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
pimavanserin 34 mg once daily for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone
titrated to 40 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult OCRD-AMC (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pimavanserin + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for
  pimavanserin.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard-of-care medical
  management and psychotherapy where indicated.

All subjects continue standard care for the underlying medical
condition. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in Y-BOCS
total at week 12. Secondary endpoints include obsession subscale,
compulsion subscale, responder rate, caregiver burden, and CGI-I.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorder Due to Another Medical
Condition comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of the selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist /
antagonist pimavanserin and a therapeutically effective amount of
the serotonin reuptake inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial agonist
vilazodone are disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping
salience-regulation and obsessional-distress circuits implicated in
medically caused obsessive-compulsive symptomatology and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive symptomatic improvement
than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose while
continuing standard care for the underlying medical condition.
