## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Non-REM Sleep Arousal Disorders by
co-administering (a) an orexin receptor antagonist, in particular
suvorexant, and (b) a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist with
5-HT2C antagonist activity, in particular agomelatine. The
invention is intended for subjects with recurrent confusional
arousals, sleepwalking, or sleep terrors and addresses unstable
arousal thresholds, sleep-fragmentation, and circadian
misalignment.

## Background of the invention

Non-REM Sleep Arousal Disorders are parasomnias characterized by
incomplete arousals from deep non-rapid-eye-movement sleep, with
impaired awareness and variable autonomic or behavioral activation.
The major phenotypes include confusional arousals, sleepwalking,
and sleep terrors. Episodes often occur in the first third of the
night, are associated with relative amnesia, and may be
precipitated or worsened by sleep deprivation, stress, fever,
circadian misalignment, or co-existing sleep disorders.

The prevalence of occasional non-REM parasomnia behaviors is
meaningful in the population, while clinically impairing recurrent
forms are less common but often highly disruptive. The clinical
burden includes injury risk, sleep disruption to bed partners or
family, daytime fatigue, anxiety about sleep, and reduced quality
of life. In adults, persistent or recurrent non-REM parasomnias are
especially problematic because of injury risk, comorbidity with
sleep-disordered breathing, and the occupational / social burden of
nocturnal episodes.

The current standard of care is largely behavioral and trigger-
directed. It consists of sleep hygiene, stress reduction, treatment
of co-occurring sleep disorders, environmental safety measures, and
selected off-label pharmacologic measures such as benzodiazepines
or melatonin in specific patients. No pharmaceutical product is
approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
specifically for the treatment of non-REM sleep arousal disorders
as a primary indication.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, non-REM parasomnias reflect instability at the
boundary between deep sleep and wakefulness, with partial arousal
intrusions. Orexin signaling contributes to wake maintenance and
arousal threshold control. Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor
antagonist approved for insomnia that facilitates sleep onset and
maintenance by reducing orexin-driven wakefulness. In the present
invention, the suvorexant axis addresses the *maladaptive nocturnal
arousal instability* substrate of non-REM parasomnias.

Second, non-REM parasomnias are often worsened by circadian
misalignment, poor sleep scheduling, and fragmented sleep
architecture. Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant that
acts as a potent agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and a neutral
antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors. This pharmacologic profile supports
circadian resynchronization and subjective sleep-quality
improvement. The agomelatine axis therefore addresses the
*circadian-disorganization and sleep-architecture instability*
substrate of non-REM parasomnias.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an orexin receptor antagonist in
combination with a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist with 5-HT2C
antagonism for non-REM sleep arousal disorders specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of orexin-driven
arousal instability (a suvorexant axis) and restoration of stable
circadian entrainment (an agomelatine axis) are complementary and
convergent substrates for treatment of non-REM sleep arousal
disorders. The combination is expected to produce greater reduction
in episode frequency and sleep-fragmentation than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) suvorexant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) agomelatine or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, co-crystal,
or prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically
acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating a
non-REM sleep arousal disorder in a human subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of suvorexant and a therapeutically effective
amount of agomelatine. Co-administration may be simultaneous or
sequential, with each agent administered within a window of about
24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) suvorexant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of a non-REM sleep
arousal disorder according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in
conjunction with sleep-hygiene and safety measures.

The invention contemplates suvorexant in a daily dose of about
5 mg to about 20 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a
preferred adult dose of 10 mg to 20 mg nightly. The invention
contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about 25 mg to about
50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a preferred dose of
25 mg nightly and optional escalation to 50 mg nightly after about
2 weeks if clinically indicated.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with non-REM sleep arousal disorders, in episode frequency,
episode intensity, sleep fragmentation, and injury risk, that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of suvorexant and
agomelatine administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Suvorexant"* means the dual orexin receptor antagonist approved
for insomnia. The term includes suvorexant free base, salts,
polymorphs, solvates, hydrates, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent orexin receptor antagonists
with a clinically comparable sleep-promoting profile.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Non-REM sleep arousal disorder"* means a parasomnia characterized
by incomplete arousals from non-REM sleep, including confusional
arousals, sleepwalking, and sleep terrors.

*"Parasomnia-episode frequency"* means the number of clinically
meaningful non-REM parasomnia episodes over a defined interval,
including events documented by patient, bed partner, caregiver, or
validated sleep diary.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of a non-REM sleep arousal disorder according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in
at least one of: parasomnia-episode frequency, parasomnia-
intensity score, sleep-fragmentation index, injury / near-injury
rate, quality of life, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Suvorexant

Suvorexant is a dual orexin receptor antagonist approved for
insomnia. By reducing orexin-mediated wake drive, it facilitates
sleep onset and maintenance. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of suvorexant is reduction of
abnormal wake intrusions that destabilize deep non-REM sleep.

#### 2.2 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant acting as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and a neutral antagonist at
5-HT2C receptors. This profile supports circadian resynchronization
and subjective sleep-quality improvement. In the present invention,
the principal mechanistic contribution of agomelatine is
stabilization of sleep timing and nocturnal architecture.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise suvorexant and
agomelatine, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a suvorexant tablet and an
agomelatine tablet.

Excipients useful for the suvorexant composition include tablet
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and film-coating polymers
appropriate for low-dose bedtime administration. Excipients useful
for the agomelatine composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Suvorexant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
bedtime dosing. Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral
tablet suitable for bedtime administration.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
suvorexant tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of agomelatine tablets,
(c) written instructions aligned to sleep scheduling and safety
planning, and (d) optional sleep diary materials.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with non-REM sleep arousal
disorders, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Suvorexant 5 mg orally at bedtime for about 3 nights, titratable
  to 10 mg and then to 20 mg nightly if clinically indicated and
  tolerated.
* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for about 14 days,
  titratable to 50 mg nightly if clinically indicated.

Broad genus ranges include suvorexant 5–20 mg/day and agomelatine
25–50 mg/day. The preferred schedule administers both agents at
bedtime on a consistent nightly schedule.

Hepatic adjustment: clinically meaningful hepatic impairment is
exclusionary or requires careful monitoring because agomelatine is
associated with hepatotoxicity risk in susceptible patients.

Medication–medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors. Suvorexant should be used with
caution alongside other CNS depressants. The regimen is intended to
complement, not replace, behavioral and environmental safety
management.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
non-REM sleep arousal disorders. Inclusion criteria for the
preferred treatment population include: age ≥ 12 years; recurrent
confusional arousals, sleepwalking, or sleep terrors causing
distress, injury risk, or significant household disruption; and
ability to participate in sleep-hygiene and safety measures.
Exclusion criteria include untreated severe sleep-disordered
breathing, active nocturnal seizures not yet controlled, severe
hepatic disease, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Non-REM parasomnias are maintained by instability at the boundary
between deep sleep and wakefulness. This instability is worsened by
circadian drift, fragmented sleep, and low arousal-threshold
control.

Suvorexant addresses the abnormal wake intrusion layer through
orexin antagonism. Agomelatine addresses the circadian-
destabilization layer through MT1 / MT2 agonism and 5-HT2C
antagonism. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on the same parasomnia-episode phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in non-REM parasomnia burden than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: orexin receptor binding and antagonism assays
for suvorexant; MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays for
agomelatine; and combined sleep-state transition assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include sleep-fragmentation models, circadian-shift paradigms,
state-transition instability paradigms, and parasomnia-relevant
arousal-threshold models. A supra-additive effect of the
combination on reduction of abnormal wake intrusions and sleep
fragmentation, relative to either monotherapy at any individually
achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point that
motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
suvorexant 10 mg nightly for 3 days; Period 2: agomelatine 25 mg
nightly for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, liver-function monitoring, and next-day sedation /
tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal
purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of clinically
meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in non-REM sleep arousal disorders (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): suvorexant + agomelatine.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): suvorexant + placebo for
  agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard safety and
  sleep-hygiene management.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in parasomnia-episode
frequency at week 8. Secondary endpoints include parasomnia-
intensity score, sleep-fragmentation index, injury / near-injury
rate, quality of life, and CGI-I.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in

invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
non-REM sleep arousal disorders comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the orexin antagonist
suvorexant and a therapeutically effective amount of the
melatonergic antidepressant agomelatine are disclosed. The two
agents act on non-overlapping arousal-threshold and circadian-
stability circuits implicated in recurrent confusional arousals,
sleepwalking, and sleep terrors, and are expected to produce
greater-than-additive symptomatic improvement in parasomnia-episode
frequency and sleep continuity than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.
