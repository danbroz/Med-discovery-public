## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Major Neurocognitive Disorder by co-administering
(a) a histamine H3 receptor antagonist or inverse agonist and
(b) a muscarinic receptor agonist, in particular the combination of
pitolisant and xanomeline, optionally together with trospium or
another peripherally restricted anticholinergic to limit peripheral
cholinergic adverse effects. The invention is directed to symptomatic
improvement in cognition, daytime alertness, and functional
participation in adults with established major neurocognitive
impairment from Alzheimer-type, vascular, Lewy body, Parkinsonian,
mixed, or otherwise clinically recognized neurodegenerative etiologies.

## Background of the invention

Major Neurocognitive Disorder (major NCD) is a syndrome of acquired
substantial cognitive decline sufficient to interfere with
independence in everyday activities. The affected domains may include
complex attention, executive function, learning and memory, language,
visuospatial function, perceptual-motor ability, and social cognition.
Major NCD may arise from Alzheimer-type pathology, vascular injury,
Lewy body disease, Parkinson's disease, frontotemporal degeneration,
mixed etiologies, or other medically recognized neurodegenerative
states.

The clinical burden of major NCD is very high. Patients commonly
experience impaired memory, attentional fluctuation, slowed
information processing, loss of instrumental daily function, caregiver
dependence, reduced daytime engagement, and increased institutional
care risk. Many subjects also experience apathy, somnolence,
fragmented attention, or variable behavioral availability that limit
the benefit of cognitive rehabilitation, daily routine support, or
caregiver prompting.

Current pharmacologic care remains limited and incomplete.
Cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine may provide modest symptomatic
benefit in certain subtypes, and disease-specific background therapies
may be used in selected etiologies, but persistent attention,
alertness, and functional-cognition deficits remain common. A purely
wake-promoting approach often fails to restore cholinergic signal
quality, while a purely cholinergic approach may not sufficiently
improve behavioral availability, fluctuating alertness, or daytime
engagement.

The mechanistic gap addressed by the present invention has two layers.
First, pitolisant increases histaminergic tone through H3 inverse
agonism / antagonism and supports state-dependent release of
acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine, thereby improving
wakefulness, attention, and the ability to sustain purposeful daytime
engagement. Second, xanomeline is a centrally active muscarinic
agonist with M1/M4-preferring clinical activity that strengthens
cortical cholinergic signaling relevant to encoding, executive
processing, and cognitive organization.

The inventors recognized that these two axes are complementary and
non-redundant. Pitolisant improves the waking state in which cognition
occurs. Xanomeline improves the quality of cortical cholinergic
processing within that state. Together, the two mechanisms are
expected to produce greater symptomatic cognitive and functional
benefit than either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose.

Prior-art searching identified muscarinic-receptor activation
references such as *Methods and compositions for treatment of
disorders ameliorated by muscarinic receptor activation*, cognitive-
disorder references such as *Compositions and methods for treating
cognitive disorders*, and various histamine H3 antagonist references.
Those disclosures do not teach the specific combination of pitolisant
and xanomeline for Major Neurocognitive Disorder broadly, nor the
combined vigilance-support plus cholinergic-processing strategy
disclosed herein.

The present invention is symptom-directed and supportive. It does not
claim cure, reversal of underlying neurodegeneration, or guaranteed
prevention of further decline. Rather, it provides a mechanism-based
combination intended to improve cognition, behavioral availability,
and day-to-day functioning within the context of comprehensive
dementia care.

dosage form, and (c) written instructions for use in a subject with

background dementia care, blood-pressure monitoring, gastrointestinal

4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered once daily. The invention
contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about 50 mg to about
250 mg, preferably in divided doses. In certain embodiments trospium
chloride is administered in a total daily dose of about 20 mg to about
60 mg.

additive improvement in one or more endpoints selected from the group
consisting of cognitive composite score, attention / alertness score,
caregiver-rated daily-function efficiency, Clinical Global
Impression-Improvement (CGI-I), apathy or engagement burden, and
caregiver burden, compared with either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Major Neurocognitive Disorder"* or *"major NCD"* means a syndrome of
substantial acquired cognitive decline that interferes with
independence in everyday activities, regardless of whether the
underlying etiology is Alzheimer-type, vascular, Lewy body,
Parkinsonian, frontotemporal, mixed, or otherwise clinically
recognized and consistent with a major neurocognitive diagnosis.

*"Pitolisant"* means the H3 inverse agonist / antagonist and its
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues that
retain clinically relevant H3 activity.

*"Xanomeline"* means xanomeline and pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, and metabolically equivalent
forms that retain clinically relevant muscarinic receptor agonist
activity.

*"Peripheral anticholinergic mitigator"* means trospium chloride or
another antimuscarinic agent with limited central nervous system
penetration used to reduce peripheral cholinergic adverse effects
associated with xanomeline administration.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount that produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in one or more major NCD endpoints
when used according to a regimen disclosed herein.

### 2. Active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant increases histaminergic tone and supports wakefulness,
attention, and state-dependent cortical neurotransmitter release. In
the present invention, its principal contribution is improvement in
vigilance, daytime alertness, and behavioral availability for
cognitive activity.

#### 2.2 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is a centrally active muscarinic receptor agonist with
M1/M4-preferring clinical activity. In the present invention, its
principal contribution is enhancement of cortical cholinergic signal
quality relevant to attention, memory, and executive processing.

#### 2.3 Optional trospium chloride

Trospium chloride may optionally be administered with xanomeline to
reduce peripheral cholinergic adverse effects such as nausea,
diarrhea, abdominal cramping, sweating, or urinary urgency, while
preserving central cholinergic efficacy because of limited
blood-brain-barrier penetration.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
xanomeline, optionally together with trospium chloride, plus one or
more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients. The actives
may be administered as separate oral dosage forms, as a co-packaged
kit, or as a synchronized blister-card system aligned to morning and
evening dosing.

Suitable excipients include common diluents, binders, disintegrants,
lubricants, capsule-fill agents, coating systems, and release-control
polymers appropriate for oral solid dosage forms. In preferred
embodiments pitolisant is given in the morning and xanomeline in
divided doses to balance central efficacy and tolerability.

### 4. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with Major Neurocognitive Disorder, a preferred
regimen comprises:

* Pitolisant 4.5 mg to 35.6 mg orally once daily, preferably in the
  morning.
* Xanomeline 50 mg to 250 mg daily, preferably in divided doses.
* Optional trospium chloride 20 mg to 60 mg daily in divided doses.

In certain embodiments pitolisant is titrated first to establish
tolerability and alertness benefit, after which xanomeline is added at
a low dose and increased gradually. In certain embodiments xanomeline
and trospium are co-initiated.

In preferred embodiments, any disease-specific background regimen such
as cholinesterase inhibitors, memantine, Alzheimer-directed therapy,
vascular risk-factor management, or Parkinson's disease therapy is
stable at baseline so that change can be attributed to the present
invention rather than major concurrent medication shifts.

### 5. Subject populations

Preferred subjects are adults with clinician-confirmed major NCD and
persistent deficits in attention, memory retrieval, executive
function, functional cognition, daytime alertness, or caregiver-
dependent task completion. In certain embodiments the subject has
mixed etiologies or an uncertain dementia subtype but a stable major
NCD diagnosis.

Preferred embodiments include subjects with residual cognitive
impairment despite optimized background care or with marked daytime
fluctuation that interferes with participation in routines,
rehabilitation, or caregiver-directed prompting.

Exclusion criteria for preferred embodiments include acute delirium,
uncontrolled severe psychosis requiring alternate management, unstable
orthostatic hypotension, severe gastrointestinal intolerance to
cholinergic therapy, uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma, or inability
to participate in monitoring.

### 6. Mechanistic rationale

Major NCD often combines a cortical cholinergic deficit with impaired
daytime arousal and inconsistent behavioral availability. A subject
who is insufficiently alert may perform poorly even when cholinergic
tone is partially restored, while a subject with improved arousal but
no cholinergic support may remain limited by poor encoding, attention,
and cognitive organization.

Pitolisant addresses the arousal layer through H3 antagonism / inverse
agonism. Xanomeline addresses the cholinergic-processing layer through
central muscarinic agonism. The combination is therefore expected to
produce larger improvements in cognition, task engagement, and
caregiver-observable daily function than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.

### 7. Administration and monitoring

Monitoring may include cognitive composite testing, bedside attention
or alertness measures, caregiver diary, daily-function scales,
orthostatic vital signs, bowel tolerance, urinary symptoms, and CGI-I.
In certain embodiments motor status or hallucination burden is also
tracked depending on the underlying subtype.

The regimen is not intended to replace comprehensive dementia care,
disease-specific background treatment, management of acute delirium,
or environmental / caregiver interventions that remain essential in
major NCD.

### 8. Prophetic clinical study

A prophetic Phase 2 study enrolls adults with established Major
Neurocognitive Disorder, baseline impairment in cognition and daily
function, and stable background care. Subjects are randomized to:
(a) pitolisant plus xanomeline, optionally with trospium;
(b) pitolisant plus placebo; or (c) placebo plus standard care, for
12 weeks after a titration phase.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in a cognitive composite
score emphasizing attention, executive function, and functional
memory. Key secondary endpoints include caregiver-rated daily-function
efficiency, daytime alertness, CGI-I, engagement burden, and caregiver
burden. The combination arm is expected to show the largest
improvement in cognition and behavioral availability with acceptable
tolerability. FIGS. 3-5 are prophetic illustrations of those expected
outcomes.

### 9. Prior-art distinction

The closest prior-art references identified in the prior-art
landscape include:

* *Methods and compositions for treatment of disorders ameliorated by
  muscarinic receptor activation* - disclose muscarinic activation but
  do not teach co-administration with pitolisant for major NCD.
* *Compositions and methods for treating cognitive disorders* and
  other broad cognitive-support references - do not teach the specific
  H3 plus muscarinic combination disclosed herein.
* Histamine H3 receptor antagonist references - disclose H3-axis
  chemistry without pairing it with xanomeline for major NCD.

The present invention is distinct in the specific receptor classes
combined, the specific actives chosen, and the symptom-layered
strategy of coupling vigilance support with cortical cholinergic
enhancement in established Major Neurocognitive Disorder.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Major
Neurocognitive Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the histamine H3 receptor inverse
agonist / antagonist pitolisant and a therapeutically effective
amount of the muscarinic receptor agonist xanomeline are disclosed,
optionally together with trospium chloride or another peripherally
restricted anticholinergic to reduce peripheral cholinergic adverse
effects. The two agents act on non-overlapping vigilance and
cholinergic-processing pathways relevant to major NCD and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in cognition and
daytime function compared with either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose within the context of stable background dementia care.
