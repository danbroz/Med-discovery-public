## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Personality Change Due to Another Medical
Condition (PC-AMC) by co-administering (a) a dopamine D2 receptor
antagonist and (b) a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor inverse agonist /
antagonist, in particular the combination of perospirone and
pimavanserin. The invention is intended for subjects whose
persistent behavioral, affective, or interpersonal dyscontrol is
attributable to a defined medical condition and who continue
receiving standard care for that underlying condition; it does not
assert disease-modification of the underlying medical condition
itself.

## Background of the invention

Personality Change Due to Another Medical Condition is recognized in
DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a syndrome in which a persistent disturbance
in personality or characteristic behavior pattern is judged to be
the direct pathophysiologic consequence of a medical condition. The
clinical presentation may include affective lability, disinhibition,
aggression, apathy, suspiciousness, impulsivity, or mixed
personality / behavioral change depending on the locus and nature
of the underlying medical condition. Common etiologies include
traumatic brain injury, epilepsy, stroke, frontal-lobe lesions,
neuroinflammatory disease, endocrine disease, and other neurologic
or systemic medical causes.

The prevalence of PC-AMC is difficult to estimate because the
disorder is usually coded under the underlying medical condition or
broader neuropsychiatric syndrome rather than as a stand-alone
diagnosis. Nonetheless, the clinical burden is substantial. The
syndrome is associated with caregiver exhaustion, loss of
independence, impaired judgment, aggression, social and family
conflict, occupational loss, and reduced adherence to the medical
regimen for the underlying illness.

The current standard of care is supportive and etiology-directed. It
consists primarily of treating the underlying medical condition to
the extent possible, minimizing provoking medications, and using
behavioral supports or empiric neuropsychiatric medications when
clinically necessary. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for
PC-AMC as a primary indication. Clinicians often borrow treatments
from psychosis, agitation, OCD-spectrum, or impulse-control
practice, but those approaches are inconsistently effective and may
carry substantial motor, sedative, or metabolic burden.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, many PC-AMC presentations include dopaminergic
disinhibition, reward-salience misassignment, irritability, or
impulsive behavioral output. Perospirone is an atypical
antipsychotic approved in Japan for schizophrenia that functions as
a D2 receptor antagonist with additional serotonergic properties.
In the present invention, the D2-antagonist axis addresses the
*disinhibition, impulsivity, and reinforcement-misalignment*
substrate of PC-AMC.

Second, medically driven personality change often includes
maladaptive salience tagging, suspiciousness, rigid attentional
capture, or affective lability linked to cortical serotonin 5-HT2A
signaling. Pimavanserin (Nuplazid®) is a selective 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist approved by the FDA for hallucinations and
delusions associated with Parkinson disease psychosis. Pimavanserin
has no clinically meaningful D2 antagonism and therefore can modify
5-HT2A-mediated salience physiology without adding direct
dopaminergic motor burden. The selective 5-HT2A axis therefore
addresses the *maladaptive salience, suspiciousness, and rigid
attentional capture* substrate of PC-AMC.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a D2 antagonist in combination with
a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist for PC-AMC
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that attenuation of dopaminergic
disinhibition (a perospirone D2 axis) and attenuation of 5-HT2A-
mediated maladaptive salience and suspiciousness (a pimavanserin
axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of
PC-AMC. The combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic
improvement in behavioral stability, interpersonal function, and
caregiver burden than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose, without asserting disease-modification of the
underlying medical condition.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) perospirone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) pimavanserin or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Personality Change Due to Another Medical Condition in a human
subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject
a therapeutically effective amount of perospirone and a
therapeutically effective amount of pimavanserin. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) perospirone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) pimavanserin or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of PC-AMC according
to a dosing regimen disclosed herein while continuing standard care
for the underlying medical condition.

The invention contemplates perospirone in a daily dose of about
4 mg to about 48 mg administered orally in one or two divided doses,
with a preferred adult dose of 8 mg to 24 mg/day. The invention
contemplates pimavanserin in a daily dose of about 17 mg to about
34 mg administered orally once daily, with a preferred adult dose
of 34 mg/day. The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement of
the D2 and 5-HT2A axes in a subject whose maladaptive behavioral
pattern is the direct physiologic consequence of a medical
condition.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with PC-AMC, in behavioral dyscontrol, suspiciousness, disinhibition,
and caregiver burden, that is greater than the sum of the
individual effects of perospirone and pimavanserin administered as
monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Perospirone"* means the atypical antipsychotic marketed in Japan
for schizophrenia that acts as an antagonist at dopamine D2
receptors, an inverse agonist / antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and
a partial agonist at 5-HT1A receptors. The term includes
perospirone free base, salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Pimavanserin"* means the selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse
agonist / antagonist marketed in the United States as Nuplazid®.
The term includes pimavanserin free base, tartrate or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically
equivalent 5-HT2A inverse agonists.

*"Personality Change Due to Another Medical Condition"* or
*"PC-AMC"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 syndrome in which a
persistent disturbance in personality or characteristic behavior
pattern is attributable to the direct physiologic effect of a
defined medical condition, and may include affective lability,
disinhibition, aggression, apathy, or suspiciousness subtypes.

*"Behavioral dyscontrol"* means a clinically meaningful pattern of
impulsivity, aggression, disinhibition, rigid interpersonal
maladaptation, suspiciousness, or other behavioral instability that
can be rated on a standardized instrument or structured clinician
assessment.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the
treatment of PC-AMC according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein,
produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
behavioral dyscontrol score, suspiciousness / salience score,
disinhibition score, caregiver burden, or investigator-rated
CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Perospirone

Perospirone is an atypical antipsychotic developed and approved in
Japan for schizophrenia. It acts as a D2 antagonist, 5-HT2A
inverse agonist / antagonist, and 5-HT1A partial agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
perospirone is D2 antagonism directed toward reduction of
disinhibition, impulsive behavioral output, and maladaptive reward-
salience reinforcement. Perospirone is generally used in adult
schizophrenia at oral doses in the range of about 8 mg to
48 mg/day. The compound is not FDA-approved in the United States.

#### 2.2 Pimavanserin

Pimavanserin is a selective 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist
approved by the FDA for hallucinations and delusions associated
with Parkinson disease psychosis. It has no clinically meaningful
affinity for dopamine D2 receptors. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of pimavanserin is modification
of maladaptive salience assignment, suspiciousness, and rigid
attentional capture without adding direct dopaminergic motor
burden. The labeled adult dose is 34 mg orally once daily, with no
dose titration required.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise perospirone and
pimavanserin, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms, such as a perospirone
tablet and a pimavanserin capsule or tablet.

Excipients useful for the perospirone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone), lubricants (magnesium stearate), and coating
polymers. Excipients useful for the pimavanserin composition
include capsule fill excipients, diluents, disintegrants, and
stabilizers suitable for the marketed pimavanserin formulation
profile.

### 4. Dosage forms

Perospirone is preferably formulated as an immediate-release oral
tablet. Pimavanserin is preferably formulated as an oral capsule or
tablet suitable for once-daily dosing.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
perospirone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of pimavanserin capsules
or tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to continuation of
the standard medical regimen for the underlying condition.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with PC-AMC, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Perospirone 4 mg orally twice daily for 7 days, then 8 mg twice
  daily thereafter, with titration up to 12 mg twice daily if
  clinically needed.
* Pimavanserin 34 mg orally once daily.

Broad genus ranges include perospirone 4–48 mg/day and
pimavanserin 17–34 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses a morning
and evening perospirone regimen and a once-daily pimavanserin
regimen at a consistent clock time.

Hepatic and renal adjustment: severe hepatic or renal impairment is
exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding data are generated in
this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers without review of pimavanserin
exposure. Corrected QT interval monitoring should be considered
when other QT-prolonging medications are used.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with PC-AMC. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment
population include: age ≥ 18 years; clinician-confirmed persistent
personality / behavioral change attributable to a defined medical
condition; stable management of the underlying medical condition
for at least 4 weeks where clinically possible; and clinically
meaningful behavioral dyscontrol, suspiciousness, or disinhibition
at baseline. Exclusion criteria include primary personality
disorders without a medical-condition etiology, active psychosis
unrelated to the medical condition, uncontrolled substance use
disorder, severe hepatic or renal impairment, and known
hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

PC-AMC is characterized by two interacting layers: medically driven
salience dysregulation / suspiciousness and dopaminergic
disinhibition / behavioral impulsivity. These two layers are often
not optimally addressed by a single generic sedative or atypical
antipsychotic alone.

Perospirone addresses the disinhibition / impulsive-output layer by
reducing dopaminergic reward-salience and behavioral drive through
D2 antagonism. Pimavanserin addresses the maladaptive salience and
suspiciousness layer through 5-HT2A inverse agonism without adding
D2 burden. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on the behavioral-dyscontrol phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in behavioral instability, suspiciousness,
caregiver burden, and interpersonal disruption than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: D2 receptor binding and functional antagonism
assays for perospirone; 5-HT2A binding and inverse-agonism assays
for pimavanserin; and combined salience-network assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include aggression and disinhibition paradigms, reversal learning,
salience misassignment models, and disease-relevant neurologic
models where personality / behavioral change is induced by a known
medical process. A supra-additive effect of the combination on
behavioral stabilization and reduction of maladaptive salience,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
perospirone titrated to 16 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2:
pimavanserin 34 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult PC-AMC (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): perospirone + pimavanserin.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pimavanserin + placebo for
  perospirone.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard-of-care medical
  management and supportive psychotherapy where indicated.

All subjects continue standard care for the underlying medical
condition. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in a
behavioral-dyscontrol composite at week 12. Secondary endpoints
include suspiciousness / salience score, disinhibition score,
responder rate, caregiver burden, and CGI-I.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in

(personality change due to another medical condition). No prior-art

present invention is based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Personality Change Due to Another Medical Condition comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the D2
antagonist perospirone and a therapeutically effective amount of
the 5-HT2A inverse agonist / antagonist pimavanserin are disclosed.
The two agents act on non-overlapping disinhibition and maladaptive
salience circuits implicated in medically caused personality /
behavioral change and are expected to produce greater-than-additive
symptomatic improvement in behavioral stability and caregiver
burden than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose
while continuing standard care for the underlying medical
condition.
