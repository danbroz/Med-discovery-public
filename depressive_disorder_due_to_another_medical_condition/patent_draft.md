## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Depressive Disorder Due to Another Medical
Condition (DD-AMC) by co-administering (a) a melatonergic
MT1 / MT2 receptor agonist having 5-HT2C antagonist activity, in
particular agomelatine, and (b) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with
5-HT1A partial agonist activity, in particular vilazodone. The
invention is intended for subjects whose depressive syndrome is the
direct physiologic consequence of a defined medical condition and
who continue receiving standard care for that underlying condition;
it does not assert disease-modification of the underlying medical
condition itself.

## Background of the invention

Depressive Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition is recognized
in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a mood disorder in which a prominent and
persistent period of depressed mood, markedly diminished interest
or pleasure, or both is judged to be the direct physiologic
consequence of a medical condition. The underlying medical
condition may include endocrine disease, neurologic disease,
autoimmune or inflammatory disease, cardiovascular disease,
infectious disease, metabolic dysregulation, cancer, chronic pain,
or other medically recognized etiologies of secondary depression.

The prevalence of DD-AMC is difficult to estimate because the
depressive syndrome is often subsumed under the primary medical
diagnosis, yet the clinical burden is substantial. Subjects may
experience depressed mood, anhedonia, early-morning awakening or
circadian disruption, anxiety, psychomotor slowing, reduced
adherence to medical treatment, and increased caregiver burden.
Unlike primary major depressive disorder, the course of DD-AMC is
often shaped by the activity of the underlying medical condition,
inflammatory state, sleep disruption, and medication burden.

The current standard of care is supportive and etiology-directed.
It consists primarily of treating the underlying medical condition,
optimizing sleep and pain control, reducing provocation by
medications where feasible, psychotherapy when clinically
appropriate, and empiric antidepressant treatment. No
pharmaceutical product is approved by the United States Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for DD-AMC as a primary
indication. Conventional antidepressant borrowing from primary MDD
practice is often only partially effective, especially in subjects
whose depressive syndrome has a strong circadian / sleep and
somatic-hyperarousal component.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, medically caused depressive states frequently
involve circadian disorganization, sleep fragmentation, reduced
daytime entrainment, and blunted motivational drive. Agomelatine is
a melatonergic antidepressant that acts as a potent agonist at
melatonin MT1 and MT2 receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2C
receptors. This combination supports resynchronization of circadian
timing, improvement in sleep architecture, and disinhibition of
dopaminergic and noradrenergic transmission in frontal cortex. The
agomelatine axis therefore addresses the *circadian, sleep-linked,
and motivational* substrate of DD-AMC.

Second, medically caused depressive syndromes often include anxious
distress, intrusive negative cognition, and reduced serotonergic
resilience. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available
antidepressant that combines selective serotonin reuptake
inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual
mechanism supports attenuation of anxious-depressive symptoms and
may improve tolerability relative to some conventional SSRIs. The
vilazodone axis therefore addresses the *anxious-serotonergic and
intrusive-cognitive* substrate of DD-AMC.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a melatonergic MT1 / MT2 agonist
with 5-HT2C antagonism in combination with a serotonin reuptake
inhibitor / 5-HT1A partial agonist for DD-AMC specifically.

The present invention recognizes that circadian / sleep restoration
and frontal dopaminergic disinhibition (an agomelatine axis) and
anxious-serotonergic stabilization (a vilazodone axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of DD-AMC.
The combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic
improvement in mood, sleep, and functional adherence than either
agent alone at any individually achievable dose, without asserting
disease-modification of the underlying medical condition.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) agomelatine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, co-crystal, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Depressive Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition in a human
subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject
a therapeutically effective amount of agomelatine and a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) agomelatine or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of DD-AMC according
to a dosing regimen disclosed herein while continuing standard care
for the underlying medical condition.

The invention contemplates agomelatine in a daily dose of about
25 mg to about 50 mg administered orally at bedtime, with a
preferred initiation dose of 25 mg nightly and a preferred
escalation to 50 mg nightly after about 2 weeks if clinically
indicated. The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of
about 10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with
food, with a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration
from 10 mg/day. The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement
of circadian-melatonergic regulation and serotonergic / 5-HT1A
stabilization in a medically caused depressive syndrome.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with DD-AMC, in depressive-symptom severity, sleep disruption,
anxious distress, and functional impairment, that is greater than
the sum of the individual effects of agomelatine and vilazodone
administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Agomelatine"* means the melatonergic antidepressant
N-[2-(7-methoxy-1-naphthyl)ethyl]acetamide or a pharmaceutically
acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph, co-crystal, or
prodrug thereof. Agomelatine acts as an agonist at MT1 and MT2
receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2C receptors.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Depressive Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition"* or
*"DD-AMC"* means the DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 syndrome in which a prominent
and persistent depressed mood and / or marked anhedonia is the
direct physiologic consequence of a defined medical condition.

*"Depressive-severity score"* means a clinically meaningful measure
of depressive symptoms using an instrument such as the Hamilton
Depression Rating Scale (HDRS), Montgomery–Åsberg Depression Rating
Scale (MADRS), Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9), or a
validated medical-illness depression instrument.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of DD-AMC according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces
a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of:
depressive-severity score, sleep-quality score, anxious-distress
score, functional / adherence score, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Agomelatine

Agomelatine is a melatonergic antidepressant acting as a potent
agonist at MT1 and MT2 receptors and a neutral antagonist at
5-HT2C receptors. This profile supports circadian resynchronization,
improvement in sleep quality, and disinhibition of cortical
dopaminergic / noradrenergic activity. Agomelatine is commonly used
at 25 mg nightly with escalation to 50 mg nightly if clinically
indicated. Although not approved in the United States, agomelatine
is marketed in multiple other jurisdictions and has well-described
formulation and dosing reality.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious-depressive symptoms and intrusive negative cognition.
Vilazodone is FDA-approved for major depressive disorder in adults
at oral doses titrated from 10 mg to 20 mg and then to 40 mg/day,
taken with food to support bioavailability.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise agomelatine and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as an agomelatine tablet and a
vilazodone tablet.

Excipients useful for the agomelatine composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), and coating polymers. Excipients useful for
the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Agomelatine is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
nightly administration. Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an
immediate-release oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
agomelatine tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of vilazodone tablets,
and (c) written instructions aligned to bedtime agomelatine dosing
and daytime vilazodone dosing with food.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with DD-AMC, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Agomelatine 25 mg orally at bedtime for 14 days, titratable to
  50 mg at bedtime if clinically indicated.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.

Broad genus ranges include agomelatine 25–50 mg/day and
vilazodone 10–40 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses nightly
agomelatine and daytime vilazodone with food.

Hepatic adjustment: clinically meaningful hepatic impairment is
exclusionary or requires careful monitoring because agomelatine is
associated with hepatotoxicity risk in susceptible patients.
Severe renal impairment is exclusionary until dedicated dose-finding
data are generated in this indication.

Medication–medication interactions: agomelatine should not be used
with strong CYP1A2 inhibitors. Vilazodone dose adjustment may be
required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. The regimen is intended to
be layered onto stable treatment of the underlying medical
condition rather than to replace it.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
DD-AMC. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: clinician-confirmed depressive syndrome attributable to a
defined medical condition; clinically meaningful depressive
severity at baseline; stable management of the underlying medical
condition for at least 4 weeks where clinically possible; and
capacity to participate in psychotherapy or supportive behavioral
management where indicated. Exclusion criteria include primary
idiopathic major depressive disorder without a medical-condition
etiology, bipolar mania, severe hepatic disease, severe renal
impairment, and known hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

DD-AMC is often characterized by two interacting symptom layers:
circadian / sleep disruption with reduced daytime activation, and
anxious-depressive serotonergic distress with intrusive negative
cognition.

Agomelatine addresses the first layer through MT1 / MT2 agonism and
5-HT2C antagonism, thereby resynchronizing circadian timing,
improving sleep architecture, and supporting daytime motivational
drive. Vilazodone addresses the second layer through serotonin
reuptake inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism, thereby reducing
anxious-depressive symptom amplification. The two axes are
non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on the
depressive / functional phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in depressive severity, sleep quality, and
functional adherence than either monotherapy alone. The taxonomy-
receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: MT1 / MT2 receptor binding and signaling assays
for agomelatine; 5-HT2C antagonism assays for agomelatine; SERT
binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for vilazodone; and combined
circadian / monoaminergic-network assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include circadian-disruption models, chronic mild stress paradigms,
sleep-fragmentation paradigms, and medically induced
depression-like models with validated affective and motivational
endpoints. A supra-additive effect of the combination on sleep
normalization, anhedonia reduction, and stress-resilience proxies,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
agomelatine 25 mg nightly for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone
titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, liver-function monitoring, and
tolerability monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal
purpose is to establish tolerability and absence of clinically
meaningful PK interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult DD-AMC (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): agomelatine + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for
  agomelatine.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with standard-of-care medical
  management and psychotherapy where indicated.

All subjects continue standard care for the underlying medical
condition. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in a
depressive-severity score at week 12. Secondary endpoints include
sleep-quality score, anxious-distress score, responder rate,
functional / adherence score, and CGI-I.

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (MT1 / MT2 agonism +
5-HT2C antagonism + SERT inhibition + 5-HT1A partial agonism),
(ii) the specific actives (agomelatine + vilazodone), and
(iii) the specific indication (Depressive Disorder Due to Another
Medical Condition). No prior-art disclosure suggests the
convergent-circuit rationale on which the present invention is
based.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Depressive Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition comprising
co-administration of a therapeutically effective amount of the
melatonergic antidepressant agomelatine and a therapeutically
effective amount of the serotonin reuptake inhibitor / 5-HT1A
partial agonist vilazodone are disclosed. The two agents act on
non-overlapping circadian-melatonergic and serotonergic-anxious
circuits implicated in medically caused depressive syndromes and
are expected to produce greater-than-additive symptomatic
improvement in mood, sleep, and functional adherence than either
agent alone at any individually achievable dose while continuing
standard care for the underlying medical condition.
