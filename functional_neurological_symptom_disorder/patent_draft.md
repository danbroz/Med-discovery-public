## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder
(FNSD) by co-administering (a) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with
5-HT1A partial agonist activity, in particular vilazodone, and
(b) a beta-adrenoceptor antagonist, in particular betaxolol. The
invention is intended for subjects with genuine, non-volitional
functional motor, sensory, speech, or attack-like neurologic
symptoms, and addresses anxious threat processing, hypervigilance,
autonomic amplification, and symptom-triggered functional
impairment while continuing standard neurologic and rehabilitation
care.

## Background of the invention

Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder, also referred to as
functional neurologic disorder in modern clinical practice, is
recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a condition in which altered
voluntary motor or sensory function is accompanied by clinical
features demonstrating internal inconsistency or incongruity with
recognized neurologic disease patterns. Typical presentations
include functional weakness, tremor, gait disorder, speech
disturbance, sensory loss, and dissociative / psychogenic
nonepileptic attacks. The symptoms are real and disabling; they are
not intentionally produced or feigned.

The prevalence and clinical burden of FNSD are substantial. The
disorder is a common reason for emergency, neurology, and
rehabilitation referral. Subjects often experience recurrent
disability, high healthcare utilization, repeated diagnostic
testing, occupational loss, and persistent distress. Co-occurring
anxiety, depression, trauma-spectrum symptoms, and autonomic
hyperarousal are common and often track with symptom severity or
episode recurrence.

The current standard of care is multidisciplinary and incompletely
effective. It consists primarily of clear diagnostic explanation,
physiotherapy or occupational rehabilitation, psychotherapy,
treatment of co-occurring sleep or psychiatric symptoms, and
avoidance of unnecessary reinvestigation once a stable diagnosis is
established. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the United
States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for FNSD as
a primary indication. A clinically meaningful gap remains for a
regimen that reduces anxious-hypervigilant symptom amplification
and the autonomic surges that precipitate or maintain functional
episodes.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, FNSD commonly includes anxious expectancy,
intrusive monitoring of bodily state, intolerance of uncertainty,
and rigid threat predictions regarding movement, sensation, or
physiologic cues. Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available
antidepressant that combines selective serotonin reuptake
inhibition with partial agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual
mechanism supports attenuation of anxious distress, intrusive
threat-related cognition, and hypervigilant symptom monitoring. The
vilazodone axis therefore addresses the *anxious-serotonergic and
threat-amplification substrate* of FNSD.

Second, functional symptoms and attacks are frequently accompanied
or preceded by tachycardia, tremor, chest tightness, sweating,
adrenergic surge, or escalating physiologic arousal that reinforces
the perceived imminence of a neurologic event. Betaxolol is a
beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist approved for
hypertension and glaucoma that reduces heart rate and adrenergic
surge. Relative to nonselective beta blockers, the beta1-selective
profile may offer a more targeted autonomic intervention where
bronchospasm risk or broad nonselective blockade is undesirable.
The betaxolol axis therefore addresses the *autonomic-amplification
and symptom-escalation substrate* of FNSD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a serotonin reuptake inhibitor /
5-HT1A partial agonist in combination with a beta1-selective
antagonist for FNSD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that reduction of anxious threat
amplification (a vilazodone axis) and reduction of adrenergic
escalation (a betaxolol axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of FNSD. The combination is expected to
produce greater symptomatic improvement in episode burden,
functional disability, and autonomic distress than either agent
alone at any individually achievable dose, while continuing
standard neurologic and rehabilitation care and without asserting
correction of structural neurologic disease.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) betaxolol or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, or prodrug thereof,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder in a human subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone and a
therapeutically effective amount of betaxolol. Co-administration
may be simultaneous or sequential, with each agent administered
within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) betaxolol or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of FNSD according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein while continuing standard
neurologic assessment and rehabilitation / psychotherapy support.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with food, with
a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration from
10 mg/day. The invention contemplates betaxolol in a daily dose of
about 2.5 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once or twice
daily, with a preferred dose of 5 mg to 20 mg/day. The inventive
feature is the concurrent engagement of serotonergic threat-
modulation and beta1-selective autonomic stabilization in a subject
with FNSD.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with FNSD, in functional symptom burden, episode frequency,
autonomic-distress intensity, and healthcare utilization, and an
increase in functional participation, that is greater than the sum
of the individual effects of vilazodone and betaxolol administered
as monotherapies at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Betaxolol"* means the beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor
antagonist approved in oral and ophthalmic formulations. The term
includes betaxolol free base, hydrochloride or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
and prodrugs.

*"Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder"* or *"FNSD"* means the
DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 condition in which altered motor or sensory
function occurs with internal inconsistency or incongruity with
recognized neurologic disease, including functional weakness,
tremor, gait disorder, speech disturbance, sensory symptoms, and
dissociative / psychogenic nonepileptic attacks.

*"Genuine, non-volitional functional symptom"* means a symptom not
intentionally produced or feigned, even though it lacks the
pathophysiologic signature of a recognized structural neurologic
disorder.

*"FNSD symptom-burden composite"* means a clinically meaningful
composite of symptom frequency, symptom intensity, functional
impairment, attack burden, or rehabilitation participation in FNSD.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of FNSD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: FNSD symptom-
burden composite, attack frequency, motor-function score,
autonomic-distress score, healthcare-utilization burden, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
anxious distress and hypervigilant symptom monitoring. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
vilazodone is reduction of anxious threat expectancy and rigid
symptom-focused attention in FNSD.

#### 2.2 Betaxolol

Betaxolol is a beta1-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist with
oral formulations suitable for autonomic stabilization. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
betaxolol is damping of adrenergic escalation that reinforces
functional symptom severity or attack propagation.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
betaxolol, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of two separate dosage forms, such as a vilazodone tablet and a
betaxolol tablet.

Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate), binders
(hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers. Excipients useful for the betaxolol
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and coating polymers suitable for oral tablet manufacture.

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as an oral tablet taken with
food. Betaxolol is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable
for once-daily or twice-daily use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
vilazodone tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of betaxolol tablets, and
(c) written instructions aligned to rehabilitation participation,
attack tracking, and continued neurologic follow-up.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with FNSD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.
* Betaxolol 5 mg orally once daily, titratable to 10 mg and then to
  20 mg daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.

Broad genus ranges include vilazodone 10–40 mg/day and betaxolol
2.5–40 mg/day. The preferred schedule uses once-daily vilazodone
with food and once-daily betaxolol.

Medication–medication interactions: vilazodone dose adjustment may
be required with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. Betaxolol should be used
with caution in subjects with clinically meaningful bradycardia,
atrioventricular conduction disturbance, decompensated heart
failure, or bronchospasm risk. The regimen is not intended to
replace urgent evaluation for acute neurologic change.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
FNSD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age ≥ 18 years; clinician-confirmed FNSD with stable
diagnostic formulation; clinically meaningful motor, sensory,
speech, or attack-like symptom burden; and participation in or
willingness to engage in multidisciplinary care. Exclusion criteria
include unexplained acute neurologic deterioration requiring new
emergency evaluation, severe bradyarrhythmia or other unsafe beta-
blocker contraindication, severe hepatic impairment, and known
hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

FNSD often includes two interacting layers: anxious hypervigilant
attention to bodily cues and adrenergic escalation that converts
warning signals into recurrent functional symptom episodes or
disability.

Vilazodone addresses the anxious-expectancy layer through SERT
inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. Betaxolol addresses the
autonomic-escalation layer through beta1-selective antagonism. The
two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level and converge on
the functional symptom phenotype.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in FNSD burden than either monotherapy alone.
the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 30,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for
vilazodone; beta1-adrenoceptor binding and functional antagonism
assays for betaxolol; and combined autonomic-threat modulation
assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include stress-reactivity paradigms, startle / threat-expectancy
models, autonomic-trigger models, and sensorimotor-gating or
motor-control paradigms sensitive to arousal escalation. A supra-
additive effect of the combination on reduction of autonomic and
threat-reactive symptom proxies, relative to either monotherapy at
any individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof
point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: betaxolol
10 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for 7 days. Full PK
sampling, ECG monitoring, orthostatic vital signs, and tolerability
monitoring are obtained in each period. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult FNSD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone + betaxolol.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo for betaxolol.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with multidisciplinary FNSD
  care.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in an FNSD symptom-
burden composite at week 12. Secondary endpoints include functional
attack frequency, motor-function score, autonomic-distress score,
healthcare-utilization burden, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder comprising co-
administration of a therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone
and a therapeutically effective amount of betaxolol are disclosed.
The two agents act on non-overlapping threat-amplification and
autonomic-escalation circuits implicated in genuine, non-volitional
functional neurologic symptoms and are expected to produce greater-
than-additive improvement in functional symptom burden, attack
frequency, and autonomic distress than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose.
