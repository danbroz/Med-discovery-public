## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Binge Eating Disorder (BED) by co-administering
(a) a selective melanocortin-4 receptor (MC4R) agonist, in
particular setmelanotide, and (b) a serotonin reuptake inhibitor
with 5-HT1A partial agonist activity, in particular vilazodone. The
invention is intended for subjects with recurrent binge-eating
episodes and loss of control, and addresses impaired satiation,
stress-linked binge urge, loss-of-control eating, and post-binge
distress. The invention is not intended as a general weight-loss
product, cosmetic appetite suppression, or a substitute for eating-
disorder psychotherapy and nutritional care.

## Background of the invention

Binge Eating Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as an
eating disorder characterized by recurrent episodes of binge eating
accompanied by loss of control and associated distress, without the
regular compensatory behaviors typical of bulimia nervosa. Typical
features include eating much more rapidly than normal, eating until
uncomfortably full, eating large amounts when not physically
hungry, eating alone due to embarrassment, and feeling disgusted,
depressed, or very guilty afterward.

The prevalence of BED is clinically meaningful and the burden is
substantial. The disorder is associated with obesity in many but
not all subjects, shame, mood and anxiety comorbidity, reduced work
and social functioning, and repeated failure of self-directed
dieting efforts. The clinical problem is not simply high weight;
it is recurrent loss-of-control eating accompanied by significant
distress and reinforcement of maladaptive eating cycles.

The current standard of care is multidisciplinary. It consists
primarily of psychotherapy, nutritional work, and in some settings
lisdexamfetamine or other off-label medications. No pharmaceutical
combination product is approved by the United States Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) specifically to address both satiation failure
and anxious / compulsive binge urge in BED. A clinically meaningful
gap remains for a mechanism-based regimen that targets both the
satiety axis and the intrusive urge / distress axis.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, BED frequently includes deficient satiation and
continued eating beyond physiologic repletion. Setmelanotide
(Imcivree®) is a selective MC4R agonist approved by the FDA for
rare genetic obesity syndromes. Setmelanotide is administered as a
once-daily subcutaneous injection, typically initiated at 2 mg once
daily and titrated to 3 mg once daily in adults and older
adolescents, with lower doses used for tolerability or special
populations. The setmelanotide axis therefore addresses the
*satiation failure and impaired appetite regulation* substrate of
BED.

Second, BED also includes loss-of-control urge states, stress-linked
binge drive, anxious rumination, and post-binge dysphoria.
Vilazodone (Viibryd®) is an orally available antidepressant that
combines selective serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial
agonism at the 5-HT1A receptor. This dual mechanism supports
attenuation of anxious distress and intrusive urge-related cognition.
The vilazodone axis therefore addresses the *loss-of-control binge
urge and anxious-serotonergic distress* substrate of BED.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an MC4R agonist in combination with
a 5-HT1A-partial-agonist SSRI for BED specifically.

The present invention recognizes that restoration of satiation
signaling (a setmelanotide MC4R axis) and attenuation of binge urge
and loss-of-control distress (a vilazodone axis) are complementary
and convergent substrates for treatment of BED. The combination is
expected to produce greater symptomatic improvement in binge-
eating frequency, loss of control, post-binge distress, and
functional impairment than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) setmelanotide or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, peptide variant, or prodrug thereof, and
(b) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating BED
in a human subject in need thereof, comprising co-administering to
the subject a therapeutically effective amount of setmelanotide and
a therapeutically effective amount of vilazodone.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) setmelanotide or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) vilazodone or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of BED according to
a dosing regimen disclosed herein in conjunction with nutritional
and psychotherapeutic care.

The invention contemplates setmelanotide in a daily dose of about
1 mg to about 3 mg administered by subcutaneous injection once
daily, with a preferred initiation dose of 2 mg once daily and a
preferred maintenance dose of 3 mg once daily in adult embodiments,
with downward adjustment to 1 mg or 2 mg daily if tolerability
requires. The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of
about 10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally once daily with
food, with a preferred dose of 20 mg to 40 mg/day after titration
from 10 mg/day. The inventive feature is the concurrent engagement
of MC4R-mediated satiation support and serotonergic urge /
distress attenuation in BED.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with BED, in binge-eating episode frequency, loss-of-control
severity, post-binge distress, and compulsive eating drive, that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of setmelanotide and
vilazodone administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Setmelanotide"* means the selective melanocortin-4 receptor
agonist marketed as Imcivree®. The term includes setmelanotide free
base, pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates,
polymorphs, peptide variants retaining MC4R agonist activity,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and pharmacologically
equivalent MC4R agonists within the same activity genus.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Binge Eating Disorder"* or *"BED"* means the eating disorder
defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 by recurrent binge-eating episodes
with loss of control and associated distress in the absence of
regular inappropriate compensatory behavior.

*"Weekly binge-eating episodes"* means the number of discrete binge-
eating episodes meeting diagnostic criteria within a one-week
interval.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of BED according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a
clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: weekly binge-
eating episodes, loss-of-control score, post-binge distress,
appetite / satiation score, role-function score, or investigator-
rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Setmelanotide

Setmelanotide is a selective MC4R agonist approved by the FDA for
specific genetic obesity syndromes. In the present invention, the
principal mechanistic contribution of setmelanotide is restoration
of satiation signaling and reduction of continued eating beyond
physiologic satiety.

#### 2.2 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally available selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin; by
partially agonizing the 5-HT1A receptor, it supports attenuation of
binge urge and loss-of-control distress. In the present invention,
the principal mechanistic contribution of vilazodone is reduction
of anxious-compulsive binge drive.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise setmelanotide and
vilazodone, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of a once-daily subcutaneous setmelanotide delivery system and an
oral vilazodone dosage form.

Excipients useful for the setmelanotide component include aqueous
injection vehicles, buffers, stabilizers, and prefilled syringe or
multi-dose vial systems suitable for once-daily subcutaneous
administration. Excipients useful for the vilazodone composition
include diluents (microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate),
binders (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, povidone), disintegrants
(crospovidone, sodium starch glycolate), lubricants
(magnesium stearate), glidants (colloidal silicon dioxide), and
film-coating polymers.

### 4. Dosage forms

Setmelanotide is preferably formulated as a once-daily
subcutaneous injectable product, including a prefilled syringe or
other controlled injection device. Vilazodone is preferably
formulated as an oral tablet taken with food.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
setmelanotide injectable units, (b) a 30-day supply of vilazodone
tablets, and (c) written instructions aligned to binge tracking,
nutritional care, and psychotherapy.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adolescent and adult subjects with BED, the preferred dosing
regimen is:

* Setmelanotide 2 mg by subcutaneous injection once daily for about
  2 weeks, titratable to 3 mg once daily if tolerated; if 2 mg is
  not tolerated, reduce to 1 mg once daily and re-titrate as
  clinically appropriate.
* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1-7, then
  20 mg once daily on days 8-14, then 40 mg once daily thereafter
  if clinically indicated.

The regimen is not intended to replace psychotherapy, nutritional
care, or evaluation of obesity-related or metabolic comorbidity.
It is also not intended as a generic anti-obesity intervention in
subjects who do not have BED.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
BED. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: recurrent binge-eating episodes with loss of control;
clinically meaningful distress or functional impairment; and
willingness to engage in psychotherapeutic and nutritional care.
Exclusion criteria include active psychotic disorder, severe eating-
disorder medical instability requiring inpatient acute management,
severe hepatic impairment, and known hypersensitivity to either
active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

BED often includes two interacting layers: impaired satiation that
fails to terminate intake appropriately, and loss-of-control binge
urge with anxious or compulsive reinforcement.

Setmelanotide addresses the satiation layer through MC4R agonism.
Vilazodone addresses the urge / distress layer through SERT
inhibition and 5-HT1A partial agonism. The two axes are non-
overlapping at the receptor level and converge on reduction of
binge frequency and distress.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in BED burden than either monotherapy alone. The
master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 31,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: MC4R agonism assays for setmelanotide; SERT
binding and 5-HT1A functional assays for vilazodone; and combined
satiation / urge-regulation network assays where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include binge-eating models, satiation-signaling paradigms, stress-
triggered overconsumption tasks, and loss-of-control feeding
paradigms. A supra-additive effect of the combination on reduction
of binge episodes, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
setmelanotide 2 mg once daily for 7 days; Period 2: vilazodone
titrated to 20 mg/day for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, injection-site tolerability monitoring,
and adverse-event assessments are obtained.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adolescent and adult BED (FIG. 4). Three arms,
1:1:1 randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): setmelanotide + vilazodone.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone + placebo injection.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo injection + placebo tablets, with
  standard-of-care psychotherapy.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in weekly binge-
eating episodes at week 12. Secondary endpoints include loss-of-
control score, post-binge distress, appetite / satiation score,
role-function score, and CGI-I.

### 10. Differentiation from the prior art

A semantic search of the local FAISS-indexed corpus of U.S. and
EP patents from 1999 onward, executed across ten distinct query
angles (see `prior_art.md` in the same draft folder), returns no
hit disclosing the combination of an MC4R agonist with a 5-HT1A-
partial-agonist SSRI for BED specifically. The closest hits are
food-intake control and generic MC4R patents, none of which teach
the present dual satiation-plus-urge rationale in BED.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Binge
Eating Disorder comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of setmelanotide and a therapeutically effective
amount of vilazodone are disclosed. The two agents act on non-
overlapping satiation and urge-distress circuits and are expected
to produce greater-than-additive improvement in weekly binge-
eating episodes and loss-of-control eating than either agent alone
at any individually achievable dose.
