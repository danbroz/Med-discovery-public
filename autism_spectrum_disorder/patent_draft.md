## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) by co-administering
(a) an oxytocin receptor agonist and (b) a histamine H3 receptor
antagonist or inverse agonist, in particular the combination of
oxytocin and pitolisant, in dosage forms and on schedules timed to
structured therapy, caregiver-mediated practice, or social-communication
training sessions.

## Background of the invention

Autism Spectrum Disorder is a neurodevelopmental condition defined by
persistent differences in social communication and social interaction,
together with restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests,
or sensory processing, where those features result in clinically
meaningful impairment or support needs. ASD is heterogenous in
etiology, severity, developmental course, and co-occurring
conditions. Many subjects experience impairing difficulties with joint
attention, social reciprocity, pragmatic language, caregiver-directed
engagement, emotional regulation, and learning participation in school
or home environments.

Current pharmacotherapy does not adequately address the core
social-communication and engagement phenotype of ASD. Approved
medications largely target associated irritability rather than the
underlying social-learning or arousal-regulation substrate. Behavioral
therapy, speech-language therapy, occupational therapy, caregiver
coaching, and educational supports remain first-line and essential,
but their effectiveness depends heavily on the subject's ability to
remain alert, socially engaged, and available for repeated learning
trials.

The mechanistic gap addressed by the present invention has two
components. First, oxytocin receptor signaling in amygdala,
hypothalamus, ventral striatum, superior temporal sulcus, and medial
prefrontal cortex modulates social salience, affiliative motivation,
eye gaze, and caregiver-directed trust / engagement. Intranasal
oxytocin and related agonists have shown mechanism-consistent signals
in autism-pathway studies, particularly on social attention and
caregiver-linked responsiveness. Second, pitolisant increases cortical
histamine and supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine,
dopamine, and norepinephrine, thereby improving wakefulness, task
engagement, and learning readiness without the controlled-substance
burden of classic stimulants.

The inventors recognized that these axes are complementary. Oxytocin
improves the salience and emotional meaningfulness of social and
caregiver signals. Pitolisant improves the vigilance and sustained
task engagement required to benefit from those signals. Together, the
two act on non-overlapping receptors that converge on a single
functional outcome: greater capacity to participate in structured
social-communication learning.

Prior-art searching identified autism-focused oxytocin disclosures,
including *Treatment of autism and similar disorders* and *Intranasal
carbetocin formulations and methods for the treatment of autism*, as
well as *Oxytocin and opioid antagonists for treatment of social
dysfunction disorder* and *Methods of improving behavioral therapies*.
Those references disclose oxytocin-pathway approaches but do not teach
pitolisant, H3 inverse agonism, or the specific combination and timing
relationship disclosed herein.

The present invention is directed to clinically impairing ASD-related
social-communication and engagement difficulties. It is not intended
to erase identity, suppress harmless self-regulatory behaviors, or
pathologize neurodiversity in the absence of meaningful functional
impairment. Rather, it provides an adjunctive pharmacologic platform
for subjects and families who seek improved participation in therapy,
caregiver-directed learning, or daily adaptive functioning.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) oxytocin or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
analogue, fragment, prodrug, or formulation thereof and
(b) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate,
polymorph, or prodrug thereof, together with one or more
pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating Autism
Spectrum Disorder in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering a therapeutically effective amount of oxytocin and a
therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant. In preferred
embodiments, the oxytocin is delivered within about 15 to 60 minutes
before a structured social-communication, caregiver-mediated, or
behavioral-learning session.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising (a)
oxytocin in a first dosage form, (b) pitolisant in a second dosage
form, and (c) written instructions for administration in conjunction
with therapy or caregiver-directed practice, including guidance for
timing the intranasal oxytocin actuation relative to scheduled
learning activities.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily, with a
preferred pediatric or adolescent range of about 4.5 mg to 17.8 mg
and a preferred adult range of about 8.9 mg to 35.6 mg. The invention
contemplates oxytocin in a daily dose of about 8 IU to about 80 IU,
preferably delivered intranasally in divided doses of 4 IU to 24 IU
per administration.

The technical effect of the combination is expected to be a
greater-than-additive improvement in one or more endpoints selected
from the group consisting of Social Responsiveness Scale (SRS-2),
Vineland-3 Socialization domain score, caregiver-rated joint
attention, structured-session task engagement, Aberrant Behavior
Checklist irritability subscale, Clinical Global Impression-
Improvement (CGI-I), and caregiver burden, compared with either
monotherapy at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Autism Spectrum Disorder"* means ASD as defined by DSM-5-TR, ICD-11,
or an equivalent clinical framework, where the subject has
social-communication impairment or support needs that are clinically
meaningful and appropriate for targeted treatment.

*"Oxytocin"* means the endogenous cyclic nonapeptide hormone and
neuropeptide, together with pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
analogs, fragments, pegylated forms, prodrugs, and formulations that
retain therapeutically relevant OXTR agonist activity.

*"Pitolisant"* means the H3 inverse agonist / antagonist described
herein and its pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates,
polymorphs, prodrugs, and metabolically equivalent forms.

*"Caregiver-mediated session"* means a structured teaching, play,
social-practice, speech-language, occupational-therapy, or daily-life
training session in which a caregiver, therapist, teacher, or other
trained adult provides repeated prompts, reinforcement, or modeling.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount that improves one
or more ASD-related target symptoms or functional endpoints according
to a regimen disclosed herein.

### 2. Active agents

#### 2.1 Oxytocin

Oxytocin is an OXTR agonist that modulates social salience,
caregiver-directed attention, affiliative motivation, and the
reward value of socially contingent interaction. In the present
invention, the principal contribution of the oxytocin axis is to make
relevant social cues more accessible and more reinforcing during
therapy or practice.

#### 2.2 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a selective H3 inverse agonist / antagonist that
increases histaminergic tone and supports cortical release of
acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine. In the present
invention, the principal contribution of the pitolisant axis is to
improve learning readiness, vigilance, and sustained attention during
the interval in which social-communication work occurs.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise oxytocin and
pitolisant together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients. The composition may be provided as a
co-packaged kit with separate dosage forms or, in some embodiments, as
an integrated package in which oxytocin spray and pitolisant tablets
are linked by date-coded or session-coded labeling.

Suitable oxytocin formulations include metered-dose intranasal sprays,
mucoadhesive nasal gels, dry-powder intranasal systems, sublingual
films, or buccal dosage forms, with intranasal delivery preferred.
Suitable pitolisant formulations include immediate-release or
modified-release oral tablets and capsules.

### 4. Dosing regimens

For pediatric, adolescent, or adult subjects with ASD, a preferred
regimen comprises:

* Pitolisant 4.5 mg to 35.6 mg orally once daily, typically in the
  morning.
* Oxytocin 8 IU to 80 IU per day, preferably delivered intranasally in
  1 to 4 divided administrations.

In certain embodiments, oxytocin is administered 15 to 60 minutes
before a scheduled therapy or caregiver-mediated session. In certain
embodiments, pitolisant is administered in the morning to establish a
background arousal and task-engagement state across the day, while one
or more oxytocin administrations are linked to specific social or
learning sessions.

Weight-based or age-based titration may be used in pediatric
embodiments. A representative adolescent regimen comprises pitolisant
4.5 mg to 8.9 mg once daily and intranasal oxytocin 8 IU to 24 IU
before one or two targeted therapy sessions.

### 5. Subject populations

Preferred subjects include children, adolescents, or adults with ASD
who have clinically meaningful impairment in social reciprocity,
joint attention, pragmatic communication, or engagement in structured
learning sessions. Certain embodiments focus on subjects with
co-occurring sleepiness, low arousal, distractibility, or inconsistent
participation in therapy.

Preferred subjects are on a stable educational, behavioral, or
therapeutic plan for at least 2 to 4 weeks prior to treatment
initiation so that the effect of the pharmacologic combination on
therapy participation can be meaningfully observed.

Exclusion criteria for preferred embodiments include uncontrolled
seizures, active psychosis or mania, unstable cardiovascular disease,
intolerance to either active, and situations in which intranasal
oxytocin would be medically inappropriate.

### 6. Mechanistic rationale

The social-learning burden of ASD often involves both a salience
problem and a state-regulation problem. Social cues may not reliably
acquire sufficient motivational value, and the subject may not remain
consistently alert, engaged, or behaviorally available long enough to
benefit from repeated therapy trials.

Oxytocin addresses the salience and affiliation layer by increasing
responsiveness to caregiver and therapist cues. Pitolisant addresses
the state-regulation layer by improving attention, vigilance, and
learning readiness. The combination is therefore expected to produce
larger gains in structured social-learning outcomes than either agent
alone, especially when one or both are timed to therapy sessions.

### 7. Ethical and clinical guardrails

The present invention is intended as an adjunct to therapy,
communication supports, educational services, caregiver coaching, and
other standard ASD supports. It is not intended to replace those
supports.

The invention is further not intended to normalize identity, eliminate
non-impairing autistic behaviors, or impose treatment on individuals
who do not seek improvement in clinically meaningful areas of
functioning. Therapeutic goals are defined collaboratively around
participation, communication, adaptive functioning, distress, or
safety, rather than around cosmetic social conformity.

### 8. Prophetic clinical study

A prophetic Phase 2 study enrolls children and adolescents with
clinician-confirmed ASD and baseline impairment in SRS-2 and
caregiver-rated task engagement. Subjects are randomized to:
(a) oxytocin plus pitolisant plus standard therapy; (b) pitolisant
plus placebo spray plus standard therapy; or (c) placebo plus standard
therapy, for 12 weeks.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in SRS-2 total score.
Secondary endpoints include Vineland-3 Socialization, caregiver-rated
joint attention, structured-session engagement, CGI-I, and caregiver
burden. The combination arm is expected to show the largest
improvement on socialization and session-engagement endpoints,
especially when oxytocin administration is synchronized to scheduled
therapy. FIGS. 3-5 are prophetic illustrations of such results.

### 9. Prior-art distinction

The closest prior-art references identified in the prior-art
landscape include:

* *Treatment of autism and similar disorders* and *Intranasal
  carbetocin formulations and methods for the treatment of autism* -
  disclose oxytocin-pathway or related peptide approaches in autism,
  but do not teach pitolisant or H3 inverse agonism.
* *Oxytocin and opioid antagonists for treatment of social dysfunction
  disorder* - discloses a different partner drug and does not teach
  an H3 axis or the therapy-timed regimen disclosed herein.
* *Methods of improving behavioral therapies* - suggests oxytocin as
  an adjunct to behaviorally mediated treatment, but does not teach
  the combination of oxytocin with pitolisant.

The present invention is distinct in the specific receptor classes
combined, the specific actives used, and the session-timed dosing
relationship that links oxytocin-mediated social salience to
pitolisant-enabled engagement and learning readiness.

### Example 3 - Prophetic Phase 2 outcome

After 12 weeks of treatment, the combination arm is expected to show a
greater improvement in SRS-2 total score and Vineland-3 Socialization
than either comparator arm, with a particularly notable improvement in
caregiver-rated joint attention and structured-session participation.
PROPHETIC.

### Example 4 - Worked patient case

A 13-year-old adolescent with ASD, high support needs in social
communication, inconsistent engagement in speech therapy, and no
history of mania or seizure instability begins pitolisant 4.5 mg each
morning and intranasal oxytocin 12 IU 20 minutes before speech therapy
and caregiver-led social-practice sessions. After 10 weeks the subject
shows improved session participation, increased joint attention, and
better caregiver-rated social reciprocity, with CGI-I rated 2 ("much
improved"). PROPHETIC.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Autism
Spectrum Disorder comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of the oxytocin receptor agonist oxytocin and a
therapeutically effective amount of the histamine H3 receptor inverse
agonist / antagonist pitolisant are disclosed. The two agents act on
non-overlapping receptor classes that converge on social salience,
attention, and learning-participation circuits relevant to ASD and are
expected to produce greater-than-additive improvement in social-
communication and therapy-engagement endpoints compared with either
agent alone at any individually achievable dose. Preferred embodiments
time oxytocin administration to structured therapy or caregiver-led
practice sessions.
