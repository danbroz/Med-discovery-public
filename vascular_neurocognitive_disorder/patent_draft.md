## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Vascular Neurocognitive Disorder (V-NCD) by
co-administering (a) a histamine H3 receptor antagonist or inverse
agonist and (b) a muscarinic M1/M4 receptor agonist, in particular
the combination of pitolisant and xanomeline, optionally further
with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic such as trospium to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects. The invention
addresses attention, executive dysfunction, processing-speed
reduction, and daytime alertness in subjects whose cognitive
impairment is attributable to cerebrovascular injury; it does not
assert disease-modification of the vascular lesions themselves or
replacement of vascular risk-factor control.

## Background of the invention

Vascular Neurocognitive Disorder is recognized in DSM-5-TR and
ICD-11 as a syndrome of cognitive decline attributable to
cerebrovascular disease. The phenotype may arise from strategic
infarcts, subcortical ischemic disease, lacunar burden, diffuse
white-matter injury, hemorrhagic injury, mixed ischemic-hypoxic
states, or combinations thereof. Commonly affected domains include
complex attention, executive function, processing speed,
visuospatial ability, and functional planning.

The prevalence of V-NCD is clinically meaningful and its burden is
substantial because vascular cognitive syndromes are common after
stroke and in small-vessel disease populations. Subjects frequently
experience slowed thinking, attentional fluctuation, reduced
multitasking, gait-linked functional inefficiency, fatigue, and
high caregiver burden. Compared with Alzheimer-type amnestic
presentations, the vascular phenotype is often especially marked by
executive dysfunction and slowed information processing.

The current standard of care is supportive and vascular-risk-
directed. It consists primarily of control of blood pressure,
glucose, lipids, and other vascular contributors, prevention of
further stroke or ischemic injury, sleep and mood optimization, and
rehabilitation-based support. No pharmaceutical product is approved
by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically
for the treatment of V-NCD as a primary indication. A clinically
meaningful gap remains for a symptomatic regimen that addresses both
vigilance support and impaired cortical cholinergic signal quality.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses is the
absence of a defensible, mechanism-based symptomatic regimen for
the mixed attention-plus-executive phenotype of V-NCD. A single-
agent approach often leaves one half of the phenotype untreated:
a wake-promoting intervention may improve alertness without
restoring cortical signal quality, while a pro-cholinergic
intervention may not adequately address fatigue and fluctuating
attention.

The present invention recognizes that two non-overlapping CNS axes
can be combined to support cognition and daytime functioning in
V-NCD. First, the histamine H3 receptor is a presynaptic auto- and
heteroreceptor that suppresses histamine release and modulates
acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine transmission. Selective
inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant elevates extracellular
histamine and supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine and
dopamine without stimulant scheduling. The pitolisant axis
therefore addresses the *vigilance, processing-speed, and daytime
activation* substrate of V-NCD.

Second, the muscarinic M1 receptor facilitates cortical and
hippocampal cognition, while M4 signaling contributes to behavioral
stabilization. Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist
developed in the 1990s and more recently formulated with the
peripherally restricted antimuscarinic trospium as the FDA-approved
xanomeline-trospium combination (Cobenfy®) for schizophrenia in
adults. The xanomeline axis therefore addresses the *cortical
cholinergic and executive-control substrate* of V-NCD.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of an H3 antagonist or inverse agonist
in combination with a muscarinic M1/M4 agonist for V-NCD
specifically.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance- and arousal-
enabled cognitive support (a pitolisant H3 axis) and post-synaptic
muscarinic facilitation of cortical cognition (a xanomeline M1/M4
axis) are complementary and convergent substrates for treatment of
V-NCD. The combination is expected to produce greater symptomatic
cognitive and functional benefit than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose, without asserting prevention of
future vascular events or reversal of established structural
injury.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, (b) xanomeline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, and (c) optionally a peripherally restricted
antimuscarinic agent such as trospium chloride in an amount
effective to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects,
together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or
excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Vascular Neurocognitive Disorder in a human subject in need
thereof, comprising co-administering to the subject a
therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline, optionally further
with a peripherally restricted antimuscarinic.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) xanomeline or a salt thereof, optionally co-formulated with
trospium, in a second unitary dosage form, and (c) written
instructions for the symptomatic treatment of V-NCD according to a
dosing regimen disclosed herein while continuing vascular medical
management.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about
4.5 mg to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily. The
invention contemplates xanomeline in a daily dose of about 50 mg to
about 250 mg administered orally as the free base or as the tartrate
salt in two divided doses; when xanomeline is co-administered with
trospium chloride, the trospium daily dose is about 40 mg to about
60 mg administered orally in two divided doses.

The technical effect of the combination is improvement, in subjects
with V-NCD, in attention, executive function, processing speed, and
daytime functioning, greater than would be obtained with either
agent alone at any individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse
agonist marketed as Wakix®. The term includes pitolisant free base,
hydrochloride or other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and pharmacologically equivalent H3 inverse agonists.

*"Xanomeline"* means the compound 3-[4-hexyloxy-1,2,5-thiadiazol-3-
yl]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine, an M1/M4-preferring
muscarinic agonist. The term as used herein includes the free base,
the tartrate salt and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts,
solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs, isotopically labeled
forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Trospium chloride"* or *"trospium"* means the quaternary ammonium
peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist that does not
appreciably cross the blood-brain barrier and may be used to
mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse effects of xanomeline.

*"Vascular Neurocognitive Disorder"* or *"V-NCD"* means a
neurocognitive syndrome attributable to cerebrovascular disease or
vascular brain injury, including strategic infarcts, lacunar burden,
small-vessel disease, or related ischemic injury.

*"Attention / executive-function composite"* means a clinically
meaningful burden of impairment in attention, processing speed,
working memory, or executive control quantified by structured
neurocognitive assessment.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of V-NCD according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces
a clinically meaningful improvement in at least one of: attention /
executive-function composite, processing speed, functional
independence, daytime fatigue, caregiver burden, or investigator-
rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a histamine H3 receptor antagonist / inverse agonist
that increases histaminergic wake drive and supports state-
dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of pitolisant is
attenuation of fatigue, vigilance instability, and slowed
processing.

#### 2.2 Xanomeline

Xanomeline is an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist. In the
present invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of
xanomeline is support of executive control and cortical cholinergic
signal quality.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
xanomeline, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a co-packaged kit
of a pitolisant tablet and a xanomeline or xanomeline-trospium
dosage form.

Excipients useful for the pitolisant composition include diluents,
binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and coating polymers suitable
for oral tablet manufacture. Excipients useful for the xanomeline
composition include diluents, binders, disintegrants, lubricants,
and stabilizing excipients appropriate for muscarinic agonist oral
delivery.

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as an oral tablet suitable for
morning administration. Xanomeline is preferably formulated as an
oral immediate-release or modified-release dosage form administered
in divided doses. The preferred commercial-development embodiment
contemplates xanomeline co-administered with trospium to mitigate
peripheral cholinergic adverse effects.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-day supply of
pitolisant tablets, (b) a 30-day supply of xanomeline or
xanomeline-trospium units, and (c) written instructions aligned to
cognitive rehabilitation, vascular risk-factor management, and
caregiver support.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with V-NCD, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Pitolisant 4.5 mg orally once daily, titratable to 17.8 mg and
  then to 35.6 mg once daily if clinically indicated and tolerated.
* Xanomeline 50 mg orally twice daily, titratable to 100 mg or
  125 mg twice daily if clinically indicated and tolerated; when
  paired with trospium, trospium is administered at about 20 mg to
  30 mg twice daily.

The regimen is not intended to replace control of blood pressure,
glucose, lipids, antithrombotic care where indicated, or stroke
prevention strategies. The preferred embodiment is symptomatic use
in persistent vascular cognitive impairment.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects with
V-NCD. Inclusion criteria for the preferred treatment population
include: age >= 18 years; cerebrovascularly mediated cognitive
impairment; clinically meaningful attention, executive, or
processing-speed difficulty; and participation in supportive or
rehabilitation-based care. Exclusion criteria include unstable
acute stroke, severe cholinergic intolerance, unsafe cardiac
conduction abnormality, and known hypersensitivity to either
active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

V-NCD often includes two interacting layers: reduced vigilance /
daytime arousal support and impaired cortical cholinergic signal
quality affecting executive and attentional performance.

Pitolisant addresses the vigilance-arousal layer through H3
antagonism / inverse agonism. Xanomeline addresses the cholinergic-
cognition layer through M1/M4 agonism. The two axes are non-
overlapping at the receptor level and converge on improved
attention and executive function.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in V-NCD burden than either monotherapy alone.
the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 28,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding and inverse-agonism assays
for pitolisant; M1 and M4 receptor binding and signaling assays for
xanomeline; and combined executive / arousal network assays where
available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include vascular cognitive-impairment models, attention-set
shifting, processing-speed tasks, wake-maintenance paradigms, and
goal-directed behavior assays. A supra-additive effect of the
combination on restoration of attention and daytime function,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose,
is the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2
design in Section 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult volunteers (age 18-55 years). Period 1:
pitolisant 17.8 mg/day for 7 days; Period 2: xanomeline or
xanomeline-trospium for 7 days; Period 3: the combination for
7 days. Full PK sampling, ECG monitoring, orthostatic vital signs,
and tolerability assessments are obtained.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult V-NCD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 150 (50 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant + xanomeline.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pitolisant + placebo for xanomeline.
* Arm C (placebo): placebo for both, with supportive standard care.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in attention /
executive-function composite at week 12. Secondary endpoints
include processing speed, functional independence, daytime fatigue,
caregiver burden, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Vascular Neurocognitive Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of pitolisant and a
therapeutically effective amount of xanomeline are disclosed,
optionally with trospium to mitigate peripheral cholinergic adverse
effects. The two agents act on non-overlapping arousal-support and
executive-cognitive circuits and are expected to produce greater-
than-additive improvement in attention, processing speed, and
daytime function than either agent alone at any individually
achievable dose.
