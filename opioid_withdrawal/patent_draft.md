## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Opioid Withdrawal by co-administering
(a) a mu-opioid receptor agonist, in particular sufentanil, in a
tightly controlled specialist-supervised bridge or induction-support
regimen, and (b) an alpha2A-adrenergic agonist, in particular
oxymetazoline, preferably in a rapid-onset intranasal or other
fast-delivery dosage form. The invention addresses dysphoria,
autonomic arousal, craving-linked physiologic distress, and acute
withdrawal escalation in medically supervised opioid-withdrawal
management. The invention is not intended to replace standard
evidence-based long-term medication treatment for opioid use
disorder when such treatment is available and clinically indicated.

## Background of the invention

Opioid Withdrawal is recognized in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 as a
clinically significant syndrome that occurs after cessation or
reduction of prolonged opioid use or after administration of an
opioid antagonist. Typical manifestations include dysphoria,
nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, myalgia, lacrimation or rhinorrhea,
pupillary dilation, piloerection, yawning, fever, insomnia,
tachycardia, tremor, and intense subjective distress.

The prevalence of opioid withdrawal is substantial because of the
ongoing burden of opioid use disorder, chronic pain opioid exposure,
and transitions through emergency care, detoxification, hospital
admission, incarceration, or outpatient induction. The burden is
high because untreated withdrawal drives relapse, treatment dropout,
leaving against medical advice, and poor engagement with recovery
services.

The current standard of care is medication-based when feasible and
primarily consists of buprenorphine or methadone, with alpha2-
adrenergic agonists such as clonidine or lofexidine used in some
settings for symptom relief. No single approach fits every early
induction or bridge situation. There remains a clinically
meaningful gap in short-duration, medically supervised stabilization
when standard induction is delayed, complicated, or being actively
initiated and the patient has severe physiologic withdrawal
distress.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, opioid withdrawal includes profound loss of
mu-opioid receptor tone, dysphoria, pain amplification, and reward
deficit. Sufentanil is a potent mu-opioid receptor agonist approved
for analgesic use in tightly supervised medical settings and
available in parenteral and sublingual / transmucosal formulations.
The present invention is based on the recognition that *very low-
dose, tightly time-limited, medically supervised* mu-opioid agonism
can be used as a short bridge or induction-support strategy to
stabilize severe withdrawal physiology during a defined transition
window. The sufentanil axis therefore addresses the *acute opioid-
deficit and dysphoria* substrate of opioid withdrawal.

Second, opioid withdrawal also includes intense sympathetic
overdrive manifested as tachycardia, diaphoresis, tremor, and
internal agitation. Oxymetazoline is an imidazoline alpha-
adrenergic agonist with recognized alpha2A activity and established
intranasal formulation pathways. In the present invention, the
oxymetazoline axis is used as a restricted rapid-onset adjunct to
attenuate autonomic escalation and buy time for stabilization or
transition to standard maintenance therapy. The oxymetazoline axis
therefore addresses the *adrenergic-autonomic escalation* substrate
of opioid withdrawal.

No issued patent or published application known to the present
inventors discloses the use of a short-duration mu-opioid agonist
bridge strategy in combination with an alpha2A agonist for Opioid
Withdrawal specifically.

The present invention recognizes that tightly limited mu-opioid
bridge stabilization (a sufentanil axis) and rapid attenuation of
adrenergic surge (an oxymetazoline alpha2A axis) are complementary
and convergent substrates for treatment of opioid withdrawal during
the acute medically supervised stabilization window. The combination
is expected to produce greater reduction in composite withdrawal
severity than either agent alone at any individually achievable
dose, while remaining explicitly short-duration and transition-
oriented rather than a substitute for long-term MOUD.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) sufentanil or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) oxymetazoline or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Opioid Withdrawal in a human subject in need thereof, comprising
co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount
of sufentanil and a therapeutically effective amount of
oxymetazoline, wherein the sufentanil component is used as a
tightly time-limited medically supervised bridge or induction-
support intervention and is not used as chronic outpatient opioid
maintenance therapy.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising
(a) sufentanil or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) oxymetazoline or a salt thereof in a second rapid-onset dosage
form, and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Opioid
Withdrawal according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein in a
medically supervised setting, together with warnings on respiratory
risk, cardiovascular risk, and restricted duration of use.

The invention contemplates sufentanil in a daily dose of about
2.5 mcg to about 120 mcg administered by sublingual, transmucosal,
or other specialist-controlled route in divided doses over a
short-duration bridge window, with a preferred total use period of
about 24 hours to about 7 days. The invention contemplates
oxymetazoline in a rescue dose of about 25 mcg to about 200 mcg
intranasally or by another fast-delivery route administered during
autonomic escalation, with specialist-controlled frequency limits.
The inventive feature is the combination of a short-duration
mu-opioid bridge with rapid alpha2A-mediated autonomic
stabilization.

The technical effect of the combination is a reduction, in subjects
with Opioid Withdrawal, in composite withdrawal severity,
autonomic-arousal burden, dysphoric distress, and treatment dropout
risk, that is greater than the sum of the individual effects of
sufentanil and oxymetazoline administered as monotherapies at any
individually achievable dose.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Sufentanil"* means the potent mu-opioid receptor agonist
N-[4-(methoxymethyl)-1-[2-(2-thienyl)ethyl]-4-piperidyl]-N-
phenylpropanamide or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt thereof.
The term includes parenteral, sublingual, transmucosal, and other
clinically equivalent low-dose formulations used in tightly
supervised settings.

*"Oxymetazoline"* means the imidazoline alpha-adrenergic agonist
commonly formulated for intranasal or topical use. The term as used
herein includes oxymetazoline free base, hydrochloride or other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and rapid-onset delivery
formats suitable for restricted rescue use.

*"Opioid Withdrawal"* means the clinically significant withdrawal
syndrome defined in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 that occurs after opioid
cessation, reduction, or antagonist-precipitated receptor blockade.

*"Composite withdrawal severity"* means a clinician-rated or
subject-rated multi-domain score capturing autonomic arousal,
dysphoria, gastrointestinal distress, myalgia, sleep disturbance,
and craving-related distress, whether by a validated scale or a
study-specific composite.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a human subject for the treatment
of Opioid Withdrawal according to a dosing regimen disclosed
herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in at least
one of: composite withdrawal severity, autonomic-distress score,
craving distress, sleep score, successful transition to maintenance
therapy, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

### 2. The active agents

#### 2.1 Sufentanil

Sufentanil is a potent mu-opioid receptor agonist approved for
analgesic use in tightly supervised settings. In the present
invention, the principal mechanistic contribution of sufentanil is
short-duration replacement of opioid tone during an acute bridge or
induction-support window.

#### 2.2 Oxymetazoline

Oxymetazoline is an imidazoline alpha-adrenergic agonist with
recognized alpha2A activity and rapid intranasal formulation
pathways. In the present invention, the principal mechanistic
contribution of oxymetazoline is rapid attenuation of autonomic
escalation during acute withdrawal.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise sufentanil and
oxymetazoline, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of a
co-packaged kit of a low-dose specialist-supervised sufentanil
delivery form and a rapid-onset oxymetazoline rescue form.

Excipients useful for the sufentanil component include
mucoadhesive carriers, stabilizers, and low-dose unit-delivery
matrices appropriate for sublingual or transmucosal use. Excipients
useful for the oxymetazoline component include buffered aqueous
carriers, isotonic agents, stabilizers, and metered-delivery
devices suitable for rescue use.

### 4. Dosage forms

Sufentanil is preferably formulated as a low-dose sublingual,
transmucosal, or other specialist-controlled unit dose suitable for
short-duration inpatient or closely supervised use. Oxymetazoline is
preferably formulated as a metered intranasal spray or other fast-
delivery rescue unit.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a limited short-duration
supply of sufentanil bridge doses, (b) a limited supply of
oxymetazoline rescue units, and (c) written instructions aligned to
medical monitoring, transition planning, and withdrawal scoring.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adult subjects with Opioid Withdrawal in specialist care, the
preferred dosing regimen is:

* Sufentanil about 2.5 mcg to about 30 mcg per unit dose by
  sublingual or transmucosal route, repeated in specialist-
  supervised divided doses to a total daily exposure of about
  2.5 mcg to about 120 mcg over a bridge window of about 24 hours
  to about 7 days.
* Oxymetazoline about 25 mcg to about 200 mcg intranasally during
  autonomic-escalation periods, with specialist-controlled dosing
  limits.

The preferred embodiment is explicitly transition-oriented: use
during severe withdrawal while initiating or moving toward standard
maintenance treatment such as buprenorphine or methadone when
clinically indicated. The regimen is not intended as a chronic take-
home opioid program.

Medication-medication interactions: concurrent benzodiazepines,
alcohol, or other respiratory depressants require great caution or
exclusion depending on setting. Oxymetazoline should be used with
caution in subjects with uncontrolled hypertension, arrhythmia, or
other unsafe adrenergic sensitivity. The regimen is not intended to
replace standard evidence-based MOUD when available and appropriate.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects
with Opioid Withdrawal. Inclusion criteria for the preferred
treatment population include: age >= 18 years; clinically
significant opioid withdrawal; medically supervised setting; and a
plan for transition to maintenance therapy or monitored taper.
Exclusion criteria include unstable respiratory compromise,
uncontrolled sedative-hypnotic co-withdrawal requiring alternate
priority management, severe cardiovascular instability, and known
hypersensitivity to either active.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Opioid Withdrawal often includes two interacting layers: acute
mu-opioid deficit with dysphoria and somatic distress, and intense
adrenergic escalation that amplifies suffering and dropout risk.

Sufentanil addresses the opioid-deficit layer through tightly
limited mu-opioid receptor agonism. Oxymetazoline addresses the
adrenergic-escalation layer through alpha2A agonist rescue
signaling. The two axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level
and converge on acute withdrawal stabilization.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive reduction in opioid-withdrawal burden than either
combination, derived from the master receptor-efficacy summary
(`all_disorders_taxonomy_aware_master_summary.csv`), is 29,
indicating mechanistic compatibility above the reporting threshold
for the indication.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: mu-opioid receptor binding and agonism assays
for sufentanil; alpha2A receptor binding and agonism assays for
oxymetazoline; and combined withdrawal-distress modulation assays
where available.

In-vivo paradigms appropriate for probing the relevant substrates
include opioid-withdrawal models, autonomic-escalation tasks,
conditioned withdrawal-distress paradigms, and transition-to-
maintenance behavior models. A supra-additive effect of the
combination on reduction of withdrawal severity and improved
retention in supervised care, relative to either monotherapy at any
individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof point
that motivates the Phase 2 design in Section 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication-medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, supervised study in 24 healthy adult
volunteers or stable opioid-tolerant volunteers, focusing on low-
dose sufentanil and oxymetazoline coadministration. Full PK
sampling, respiratory monitoring, cardiovascular monitoring, and
tolerability assessments are obtained. The principal purpose is to
establish tolerability and absence of clinically meaningful PK
interaction.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm study

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in medically supervised adult Opioid Withdrawal
(FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120
(40 per arm).

* Arm A (combination): sufentanil bridge + oxymetazoline rescue.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): oxymetazoline rescue + placebo opioid
  bridge.
* Arm C (standard supportive care): placebo bridge + placebo rescue,
  with standard supportive care / maintenance transition.

The primary endpoint is change from baseline in composite
withdrawal severity over 5 days. Secondary endpoints include
autonomic-distress score, craving distress, sleep score, successful
transition to maintenance therapy, and CGI-I.

* Oxymetazoline formulation patents disclose nasal formulations and

The present invention is distinct from each of the foregoing in
(i) the specific receptor classes combined (mu-opioid agonism +
alpha2A agonism), (ii) the specific actives (sufentanil +
oxymetazoline), and (iii) the specific indication (Opioid
Withdrawal). No prior-art disclosure suggests the convergent-

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Opioid Withdrawal comprising co-administration of a therapeutically
effective amount of sufentanil and a therapeutically effective
amount of oxymetazoline are disclosed, wherein sufentanil provides
a short-duration medically supervised mu-opioid bridge and
oxymetazoline provides rapid attenuation of autonomic withdrawal
surge. The two agents act on non-overlapping opioid-deficit and
autonomic-escalation circuits and are expected to produce greater-
than-additive improvement in acute withdrawal stabilization than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
