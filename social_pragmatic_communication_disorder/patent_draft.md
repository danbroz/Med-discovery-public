## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Social (Pragmatic) Communication Disorder (SPCD) by
co-administering (a) a histamine H3 receptor antagonist or inverse
agonist and (b) an oxytocin receptor agonist, in particular the
combination of pitolisant and oxytocin, in dosage forms and on dosing
schedules timed to scheduled speech-language-pragmatics intervention
sessions.

## Background of the invention

Social (Pragmatic) Communication Disorder is a neurodevelopmental
condition defined in the *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental
Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision* (DSM-5-TR) at code 315.39 and
in the International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11)
at code 6A01.22. The disorder is characterized by persistent
difficulties in the social use of verbal and nonverbal communication,
including difficulties in (i) using communication for social purposes
in a manner appropriate to the social context, (ii) changing
communication to match the context or needs of the listener,
(iii) following the rules of conversation and narrative, and
(iv) understanding what is not explicitly stated and nonliteral or
ambiguous meanings of language. The deficits are not better explained
by low ability in the domains of word structure or grammar, by Autism
Spectrum Disorder, by intellectual developmental disorder, or by global
developmental delay. Onset is in the early developmental period, and
deficits result in functional limitations in effective communication,
social participation, social relationships, academic achievement, or
occupational performance.

The estimated population prevalence of SPCD is between 0.4 % and 7.5 %
of school-age children, depending on the diagnostic instrument used and
the inclusion of mild presentations. Approximately 50–70 % of
diagnosed children continue to meet criteria into adolescence and
adulthood. SPCD frequently co-occurs with Attention-Deficit /
Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), specific learning disorder,
developmental coordination disorder, and language disorder. The
disorder produces meaningful and persistent impairment in peer
relationships, in academic discourse, in employment, and in mental
health, and is associated with elevated rates of anxiety, depression,
and social isolation in adolescence and adulthood.

The current standard of care for SPCD is non-pharmacological. It
consists primarily of speech-language pathology directed to pragmatic
language, narrative skills, and conversational discourse; social skills
groups; and parent- and teacher-mediated interventions intended to
generalize pragmatic skills across settings. No pharmaceutical product
is approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
for the treatment of SPCD as a primary indication. Off-label use of
stimulants (methylphenidate, mixed amphetamine salts) and selective
serotonin reuptake inhibitors is reported in subjects with comorbid
ADHD or anxiety, but these agents do not target the
attention-to-discourse or social-motivation substrates that mediate
pragmatic skill acquisition, and the published controlled evidence of
pragmatic-language benefit from these agents is limited and largely
inferential.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, pragmatic-language acquisition requires sustained
attention to a conversational partner, on-line monitoring of the
partner's verbal and nonverbal cues, and rapid switching between
listening and producing. Each of these subprocesses depends on
state-appropriate cortical arousal and on cholinergic and dopaminergic
signaling in the prefrontal cortex and dorsal striatum. The histamine
H3 receptor is a presynaptic auto- and heteroreceptor that, when
constitutively active, suppresses the release of histamine and of
acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine in cortex and striatum.
Selective inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant (Wakix®, FDA-approved
for excessive daytime sleepiness in adults with narcolepsy) elevates
extracellular histamine and supports state-dependent release of
acetylcholine and dopamine without the abuse liability of stimulant
scheduling. The H3 axis therefore addresses the *vigilance and
discourse-attention substrate* of pragmatic learning, but does not
address the social-motivational substrate that determines whether a
child practices pragmatic exchange and attends to corrective feedback
from a communication partner.

Second, oxytocin receptor (OXTR) signaling in the prefrontal cortex,
amygdala, ventral tegmental area, and superior temporal sulcus
modulates social motivation, eye-gaze allocation, recognition of
affective prosody, theory of mind, and the salience of socially
contingent feedback. Intranasal oxytocin has been investigated in
autism spectrum disorder, Prader–Willi syndrome, schizophrenia, and
social anxiety, with mechanism-consistent signals on social motivation,
eye-contact behavior, and recognition of social affect. No issued
patent or published application known to the present inventors
discloses the use of an oxytocin receptor agonist in combination with
an H3 receptor antagonist or inverse agonist for any indication, and
none discloses such a combination for SPCD specifically.

The present invention recognizes that vigilance-enabled
discourse-attention (an H3 antagonist axis) and socially scaffolded
practice with a communication partner (an OXTR agonist axis) are
complementary and convergent substrates for pragmatic-language
consolidation in SPCD. The combination is expected to produce a
greater pragmatic-language outcome than either agent alone at any
individually achievable dose, by acting on two non-overlapping CNS axes
that converge on the same task-relevant cortical–limbic circuit.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) pitolisant or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) oxytocin or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, analogue, fragment, or
prodrug thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating Social
(Pragmatic) Communication Disorder in a subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically effective
amount of pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount of
oxytocin. Co-administration may be simultaneous, in a unitary dosage
form or in separate dosage forms, or sequential, with each agent
administered within a window of about 24 hours of the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising (a)
pitolisant or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) oxytocin or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form, and
(c) written instructions for the treatment of SPCD in a pediatric or
adult subject according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, with the
oxytocin actuation explicitly timed to within 30 minutes of a
scheduled pragmatic-language session.

The invention contemplates pitolisant in a daily dose of about 4.5 mg
to about 35.6 mg administered orally once daily, with a preferred
adult dose of 8.9 to 17.8 mg/day and a preferred pediatric dose
weight-banded as set forth herein. The invention contemplates oxytocin
in a daily dose of about 8 IU to about 80 IU administered intranasally
in divided doses, with a preferred dose of 24 IU twice daily timed to
practice sessions. The mass ratio of pitolisant to oxytocin
(calculated on the free-base equivalents) ranges broadly from about
1:200,000 to about 1:5,000, and is preferably from about 1:50,000 to
about 1:20,000; the dose-equivalent molar relationship and the
session-aligned timing relationship are the inventive features, not
the absolute mass ratio.

The technical effect of the combination is an increase, in subjects
with SPCD, in pragmatic-language skill on a validated pragmatic
instrument (for example, the Children's Communication Checklist,
2nd ed. (CCC-2) Pragmatic Composite, or the Pragmatic Language Skills
Inventory (PLSI)) and in social-participation outcomes, that is
greater than the sum of the individual effects of pitolisant and
oxytocin administered as monotherapies at any individually achievable
dose. The combination is opioid-free, stimulant-free, and has no known
dependence liability.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have the
meanings set forth below.

*"Pitolisant"* means the compound 1-{3-[3-(4-chlorophenyl)propoxy]
propyl}piperidine, also known as BF2.649, marketed in the United
States as Wakix® and in the European Union as Wakix®. Pitolisant is a
selective inverse agonist of the human histamine H3 receptor with a
binding affinity (Ki) of approximately 0.16 nM. The term as used
herein includes the free base, the hydrochloride salt and other
pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs,
prodrugs, isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent
analogues.

*"Oxytocin"* means the cyclic nonapeptide of sequence
H-Cys-Tyr-Ile-Gln-Asn-Cys-Pro-Leu-Gly-NH₂ with a disulfide bond between
Cys¹ and Cys⁶. The term as used herein includes the free peptide and
its pharmaceutically acceptable acetate or citrate salts, glycosylated
or PEGylated derivatives that retain agonism at the human OXTR with
EC₅₀ within ten-fold of native oxytocin, peptidic agonists such as
carbetocin, and small-molecule OXTR agonists such as TC OT 39,
WAY 267,464, LIT-001, or such other agents that produce ≥ 50 % maximal
calcium mobilization at the human OXTR at concentrations ≤ 1 µM in a
standard fluorometric calcium-flux assay.

*"Social (Pragmatic) Communication Disorder"* or *"SPCD"* means the
disorder defined in DSM-5-TR § 315.39 and ICD-11 (6A01.22),
characterized by persistent difficulties in the social use of verbal
and nonverbal communication, including difficulties in social
purposes of communication, contextual modulation of communication,
conversational and narrative rules, and inferential language, with
deficits resulting in functional limitations in effective
communication, social participation, social relationships, academic
achievement, or occupational performance.

*"Pragmatic-language skill"* means a subject's measured ability on at
least one of the Children's Communication Checklist, 2nd ed. (CCC-2)
Pragmatic Composite, the Pragmatic Language Skills Inventory (PLSI)
total, the Test of Pragmatic Language, 2nd ed. (TOPL-2) Pragmatic
Language Usage Index, or the Yale in vivo Pragmatic Protocol score.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to a subject for the treatment of SPCD
according to a dosing regimen disclosed herein, produces a clinically
meaningful improvement in at least one of: CCC-2 Pragmatic Composite,
PLSI total, TOPL-2 Pragmatic Language Usage Index, or
investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject so
that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the subject
during the period in which the pharmacological effect of the other
agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two agents are
administered in the same dosage form, in separate dosage forms, at the
same time, or at different times within a window of about 24 hours.

*"Session-aligned dosing"* means administration of intranasal
oxytocin within a window of about 30 minutes prior to or during a
scheduled speech-language pragmatics intervention session, with the
purpose of aligning the central oxytocin pharmacodynamic window with
the period of socially scaffolded pragmatic practice.

### 2. The two active agents

#### 2.1 Pitolisant

Pitolisant is a piperidine derivative and a high-affinity, selective
inverse agonist at the human histamine H3 receptor. The H3 receptor is
a presynaptic autoreceptor and heteroreceptor that, when
constitutively active, suppresses the release of histamine from
histaminergic neurons and the release of acetylcholine, dopamine, and
norepinephrine from cortical, striatal, and hippocampal neurons.
Inverse agonism at H3 by pitolisant elevates extracellular histamine
and supports state-dependent release of acetylcholine and dopamine in
prefrontal cortex and dorsal striatum. Pitolisant is approved by the
FDA for the treatment of excessive daytime sleepiness in adults with
narcolepsy, and is approved in the European Union for excessive
daytime sleepiness and cataplexy in adults with narcolepsy.
Pitolisant is metabolized primarily by CYP2D6 and CYP3A4. The
medication is a substrate and weak inhibitor of CYP3A4 and is not a
significant inducer of common cytochrome P450 enzymes. Pitolisant is
under U.S. brand-patent protection through the late 2020s; clinical
deployment of the present invention will require coordination with the
marketing authorization holder. The present invention does not require
any specific salt, polymorph, or excipient combination of pitolisant
and is not asserted to be a composition-of-matter invention as to
pitolisant.

#### 2.2 Oxytocin

Oxytocin is the endogenous cyclic nonapeptide synthesized in the
paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei of the hypothalamus. Oxytocin
acts at the OXTR, a Gαq-coupled G protein-coupled receptor that
mobilizes intracellular calcium and modulates social cognition,
attachment, trust, eye-gaze allocation, and the recognition of
socially relevant affect. Synthetic oxytocin (Pitocin®, Syntocinon®)
is approved in many jurisdictions for the induction of labor and the
prevention or treatment of postpartum hemorrhage. Investigational use
of intranasal oxytocin in CNS indications is supported by a
substantial peer-reviewed literature. Native oxytocin is freely
available from multiple suppliers and has no live U.S.
composition-of-matter patent restriction; the present invention does
not require any proprietary OXTR agonist. The intranasal route is
preferred to maximize central nervous system exposure relative to
peripheral OXTR engagement.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise pitolisant and
oxytocin, together with at least one pharmaceutically acceptable
excipient. The compositions may take the form of a unitary fixed-dose
combination dosage form (for example, a single oral tablet plus a
co-packaged intranasal device) or a co-packaged kit of two separate
dosage forms.

The mass ratio of pitolisant (calculated as the free base) to oxytocin
(calculated as the unmodified peptide free base) is in the range of
about 1:200,000 to about 1:5,000, preferably about 1:100,000 to about
1:10,000, and most preferably about 1:50,000 to about 1:20,000,
reflecting that the molar potency of oxytocin at OXTR is several
orders of magnitude greater than the per-milligram potency of
pitolisant at H3.

Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of pitolisant include the
hydrochloride, fumarate, succinate, citrate, and tartrate salts.
Pharmaceutically acceptable polymorphic forms include all crystalline
and amorphous forms whose pharmacokinetic profile in humans is
bioequivalent to the marketed pitolisant hydrochloride. Solvates and
hydrates are included. Pharmaceutically acceptable forms of oxytocin
include the free peptide acetate or citrate salt, the carbetocin
analogue, glycosylated and PEGylated derivatives that retain agonism
at OXTR within ten-fold of native, and small-molecule OXTR agonists as
defined in § 1.

Excipients useful in the compositions include diluents
(microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate, mannitol), binders
(povidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose), disintegrants (crospovidone,
sodium starch glycolate, croscarmellose sodium), lubricants (magnesium
stearate, sodium stearyl fumarate), glidants (colloidal silicon
dioxide), film-coating polymers (hypromellose, polyvinyl alcohol),

sodium taurocholate, surfactin), preservatives (benzyl alcohol,
chlorobutanol), tonicity agents (sodium chloride, mannitol), and
buffers (citrate, phosphate, acetate).

### 4. Dosage forms

Pitolisant is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-release
oral tablet at strengths of 4.5 mg and 17.8 mg (free-base
equivalent), matching the marketed product. Modified-release oral
tablets at strengths up to 35.6 mg per unit are within the scope of
the invention.

Oxytocin is preferably formulated as a metered-dose intranasal spray
delivering 4 IU per actuation, with permeation enhancement to maximize
central nervous system bioavailability. Sublingual films, buccal
tablets, and depot subcutaneous formulations are within the scope of
the invention. Intravenous and intramuscular dosage forms are
appropriate for inpatient initiation but not for chronic ambulatory
use.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-tablet bottle of
pitolisant immediate-release oral tablets at the appropriate
weight-band strength, (b) a 30-day supply of an intranasal oxytocin
spray device, and (c) a printed insert with administration timing
aligned to scheduled speech-language pragmatics intervention sessions.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adults (subjects ≥ 18 years and ≥ 50 kg), the preferred dosing
regimen is pitolisant 8.9 mg once daily for one week followed by
17.8 mg once daily, taken on awakening, plus intranasal oxytocin
24 IU twice daily, timed to within 30 minutes of scheduled pragmatic
practice or speech-language pathology sessions. Maximum recommended
pitolisant dose is 35.6 mg/day; maximum recommended oxytocin dose is
80 IU/day in three or four divided actuations.

For pediatric subjects ≥ 6 years and < 18 years, weight-banded dosing
is preferred:

| Body weight | Pitolisant (mg/day, oral) | Oxytocin (IU/day, intranasal) |
|---|---|---|
| 20–< 30 kg | 4.5 | 16 (8 IU twice daily) |
| 30–< 50 kg | 8.9 | 24 (12 IU twice daily) |
| ≥ 50 kg | 8.9 → 17.8 (titration) | 24–48 |

Hepatic adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B) and is not recommended
in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C). No oxytocin dose
adjustment is required for hepatic impairment.

Renal adjustment: pitolisant should be reduced by 50 % in subjects
with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²). No oxytocin
adjustment is required.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with strong
CYP2D6 inhibitors (paroxetine, fluoxetine, bupropion) without halving
the pitolisant dose. Avoid co-administration with strong CYP3A4
inducers. No clinically significant medication–medication interactions
are expected between pitolisant and intranasal oxytocin.

### 6. Subject populations

The methods of the present invention are directed to subjects who meet
DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 criteria for SPCD. Inclusion criteria for the
preferred treatment population include: age ≥ 6 years and ≤ 65 years;
CCC-2 Pragmatic Composite score ≤ 7th percentile for age; absence of
a current diagnosis of Autism Spectrum Disorder (because DSM-5-TR
§ 315.39 explicitly excludes SPCD when criteria for ASD are met);
intelligence quotient ≥ 70; structural and core-language functioning
sufficient to engage in conversational discourse (e.g., Clinical
Evaluation of Language Fundamentals, 5th ed. (CELF-5) Core Language
Index ≥ 75). Comorbid ADHD, language disorder, specific learning
disorder, and developmental coordination disorder are not exclusionary;
SPCD frequently co-occurs with these conditions and the present
invention is expected to address pragmatic symptoms in those subjects
without exacerbating the co-occurring condition. Exclusion criteria
include known hypersensitivity to either active, current treatment
with a prohibited concomitant medication (see § 5), pregnancy or
breastfeeding, and inability to comply with intranasal administration.

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Pragmatic-language acquisition in SPCD is impaired at the level of
(a) the maintenance of attention to a conversational partner across
turns, (b) the on-line monitoring of partner cues (eye gaze,
prosody, gesture) and the contingent updating of the subject's own
output, and (c) the affective and motivational scaffolding by which a
child engages with a communication partner and attends to corrective
feedback. Selective H3 inverse agonism by pitolisant addresses (a) and
in part (b) by elevating central histamine and supporting
state-dependent acetylcholine and dopamine release in prefrontal
cortex and dorsal striatum, with documented clinical effect on
psychomotor vigilance and discourse-relevant attention in narcolepsy
populations. OXTR agonism by intranasal oxytocin addresses (b) and
(c) by enhancing eye-gaze allocation, recognition of affective
prosody, and the salience of socially contingent feedback, all of
which mediate the quality of pragmatic practice during pediatric
speech-language intervention. The two axes are non-overlapping at the
receptor level and converge on the prefrontal–temporal–limbic circuit
that supports pragmatic discourse.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in pragmatic-language outcomes that depend on
both vigilance-enabled discourse-attention and socially scaffolded
discourse practice.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: H3 receptor binding (³H-N-α-methylhistamine
displacement on rat cortical membranes; Ki for pitolisant ≈ 1.5 nM);
H3 functional inverse agonism (cAMP accumulation in CHO-K1 cells
expressing human H3); OXTR binding (³H-oxytocin displacement on human
OXTR-CHO membranes; Kd for oxytocin ≈ 1 nM); OXTR functional agonism
(calcium mobilization in OXTR-CHO cells; EC₅₀ for oxytocin
≈ 5–20 nM).

In-vivo behavioral paradigms in rodent and non-human primate
appropriate for confirming the combination's effect on the
discourse-attention and social-motivation substrates include:
five-choice serial reaction time task (5-CSRTT) for sustained
attention; ultrasonic-vocalization (USV) emission and partner-USV
contingency in juvenile rat dyads as a proxy for socially contingent
communicative output; three-chamber social novelty paradigm for
social motivation; and operant social-reward paradigms in which the
reinforcer is the opportunity to interact with a familiar conspecific.
A supra-additive effect of the combination on (i) 5-CSRTT
discrimination accuracy and (ii) socially contingent USV emission,
relative to either monotherapy at any individually achievable dose, is
the principal in-vivo proof point that motivates the Phase 2 design in
§ 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in 24
healthy adult volunteers (age 18–55 years; equal sex distribution).
Period 1: pitolisant 17.8 mg orally once daily for 7 days; full PK
sampling on days 1 and 7. Period 2 (after 7-day washout): intranasal
oxytocin 24 IU twice daily for 7 days; full PK sampling on days 1 and
7 (oxytocin plasma concentrations and a salivary or central biomarker
if validated). Period 3 (after 7-day washout): combination at the
same doses for 7 days; PK sampling for both agents on days 1 and 7.
The primary endpoint is the geometric mean ratio for AUC₀–τ and Cmax
of pitolisant in combination versus monotherapy, with 90 % confidence
intervals contained within 0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of the
combination in pediatric SPCD (FIG. 4). Three arms, 1:1:1
randomization, target N = 180 (60 per arm) with 80 % power to detect
a 0.45-standard-deviation difference in CCC-2 Pragmatic Composite
between the combination arm and placebo at week 12, assuming a
standard deviation of 12 standard-score points and a two-sided α of
0.05.

* Arm A (combination): pitolisant 8.9 mg/day oral + intranasal
  oxytocin 24 IU twice daily.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): pitolisant 8.9 mg/day oral + matching
  intranasal placebo.
* Arm C (placebo): matching oral placebo + matching intranasal
  placebo.

All subjects receive a standardized, manualized speech-language
pragmatics intervention of equal frequency, intensity, and duration
(twice-weekly 45-minute sessions over 12 weeks) delivered by a
certified speech-language pathologist and aligned to the oxytocin
actuation window. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in
CCC-2 Pragmatic Composite at week 12. Secondary endpoints include
PLSI total, TOPL-2 Pragmatic Language Usage Index, responder rate
(≥ 1 standard-deviation CCC-2 Pragmatic Composite improvement), the
SCQ Lifetime social-communication sub-score, parent-reported social
participation, and CGI-I.

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of Social
(Pragmatic) Communication Disorder comprising co-administration of a
therapeutically effective amount of the histamine H3 receptor inverse
agonist pitolisant and a therapeutically effective amount of the
oxytocin receptor agonist oxytocin, in dosage forms and on dosing
schedules timed to scheduled speech-language pragmatics intervention
sessions, are disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping
receptor classes that converge on the prefrontal–temporal–limbic
discourse-attention and social-motivation circuit implicated in
Social (Pragmatic) Communication Disorder, and are expected to
produce greater-than-additive improvement in pragmatic-language
outcomes than either agent alone at any individually achievable dose.
