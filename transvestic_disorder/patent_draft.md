## Field of the invention

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and to
methods of treating Transvestic Disorder by co-administering (a) a
serotonin reuptake inhibitor that is also a 5-HT1A receptor partial
agonist and (b) a non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist, in
particular the combination of vilazodone and bicalutamide. The
invention is intended only for adults with a current diagnosis of
Transvestic Disorder per the *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of
Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision* (DSM-5-TR), in
whom cross-dressing behaviors are accompanied by clinically
significant distress or impairment in social, occupational, or
other important areas of functioning, and who voluntarily seek
pharmacotherapy to attenuate paraphilic urges.

## Background of the invention

Transvestic Disorder is defined in DSM-5-TR (302.3) as recurrent
and intense sexual arousal from cross-dressing, as manifested by
fantasies, urges, or behaviors, that has persisted for at least
six months and that causes clinically significant distress or
impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of
functioning. The diagnosis is *not* applied to consensual,
non-distressing cross-dressing in any context, and is *not* applied
to gender identity or expression. In the International
Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision (ICD-11), the disorder is
recognized when accompanied by distress (and incongruence-related
diagnoses are separately classified). The present invention is
limited to subjects who meet the DSM-5-TR criterion of clinically
significant distress or impairment and who, with full informed
consent, voluntarily seek pharmacotherapy.

The estimated population prevalence of Transvestic Disorder, as
distinct from non-pathological cross-dressing, is below 1 % of
adult males in epidemiologic study. The disorder is associated with
substantial subjective distress, with disruption of partnered
relationships, and (in a small subset) with secondary risk of
self-harm, occupational consequences, or impulsive financial
expenditure. Comorbid major depressive disorder, generalized
anxiety disorder, social anxiety disorder, obsessive–compulsive
disorder, and substance use disorders are common.

The current standard of care is non-pharmacological in the first
instance and consists of cognitive-behavioral therapy directed to
distress reduction, harm reduction, relationship counseling, and
relapse prevention. No pharmaceutical product is approved by the
United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment
of Transvestic Disorder as a primary indication. Off-label
pharmacotherapy reported in the medical literature for paraphilic
disorders generally has used (i) selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitors (SSRIs) for compulsive sexual behavior, distress, and
intrusive sexual ideation; and (ii) hormonal anti-androgen agents
(medroxyprogesterone acetate, cyproterone acetate, leuprolide,
triptorelin) for reduction of paraphilic sexual urge intensity in
the most severe and treatment-resistant cases. The published
controlled evidence base in Transvestic Disorder specifically is
limited and is largely extrapolated from the broader paraphilic-
disorders literature.

The mechanistic gap that the present invention addresses has two
components. First, the compulsive- and intrusive-ideation component
of paraphilic urge is mediated by serotonergic and frontolimbic
circuits, with substantial mechanistic overlap with
obsessive–compulsive and addictive phenotypes. Vilazodone (Viibryd®)
is an orally available, FDA-approved antidepressant that combines
selective serotonin reuptake inhibition with partial agonism at the
5-HT1A receptor. The dual mechanism is reported to support a more
rapid attenuation of anxious-compulsive symptomatology than a pure
SSRI, with a sexual-dysfunction adverse-effect profile that is at
least no worse than legacy SSRIs.

Second, the androgen-mediated component of sexual urge is mediated
by androgen receptor signaling in the central nervous system and in
peripheral target tissues. Hormonal anti-androgen agents
(medroxyprogesterone acetate, cyproterone acetate, GnRH agonists)
substantially reduce circulating testosterone and produce
significant reductions in paraphilic urge in the published
literature, but their adverse-effect burden (decreased bone mineral
density, hot flashes, gynecomastia, mood disturbance, hepatic
effects, cardiovascular effects, complete loss of any consensual
sexual function) has limited their acceptability and their
applicability outside the most severe forms of paraphilic disorder.
The non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist bicalutamide
(Casodex®) is FDA-approved for prostate carcinoma and competes with
endogenous testosterone and dihydrotestosterone at the androgen
receptor without lowering circulating gonadotropin or testosterone.
Bicalutamide therefore offers a *partial* and *titratable*
attenuation of androgen-receptor-mediated drive without the
total-hypogonadism profile of GnRH agonist therapy. No issued
patent or published application known to the present inventors
discloses the use of a 5-HT1A-partial-agonist SSRI in combination
with a non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist for any
paraphilic indication, and none discloses such a combination for
Transvestic Disorder specifically.

The present invention recognizes that serotonergic attenuation of
the compulsive-ideation component (a vilazodone HTR1A axis) and
non-castrating titratable reduction of androgen-receptor-mediated
urge (a bicalutamide AR axis) are complementary and convergent
substrates for treatment of Transvestic Disorder in adults with
clinically significant distress or impairment who voluntarily seek
pharmacotherapy. The combination is expected to produce a greater
reduction in paraphilic-distress and paraphilic-urge measures than
either agent alone at any individually achievable dose, with a
substantially better tolerability profile than legacy hormonal
anti-androgen regimens.

## Brief summary of the invention

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition
comprising (a) vilazodone or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt,
solvate, polymorph, or prodrug thereof, and (b) bicalutamide or a
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, polymorph, or prodrug
thereof, together with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable
carriers or excipients.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method of treating
Transvestic Disorder in an adult subject in need thereof,
comprising co-administering to the subject a therapeutically
effective amount of vilazodone and a therapeutically effective
amount of bicalutamide. Co-administration may be simultaneous, in
a unitary dosage form or in separate dosage forms, or sequential,
with each agent administered within a window of about 24 hours of
the other.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a kit comprising (a)
vilazodone or a salt thereof in a first unitary dosage form,
(b) bicalutamide or a salt thereof in a second unitary dosage form,
and (c) written instructions for the treatment of Transvestic
Disorder in an adult subject according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, together with a written informed-consent and
shared-decision-making module that addresses partner involvement,
fertility implications of bicalutamide, and the option of
discontinuation.

The invention contemplates vilazodone in a daily dose of about
10 mg to about 40 mg administered orally with food once daily, with
a preferred adult dose of 20 to 40 mg/day after a 7-day titration
from 10 mg/day. The invention contemplates bicalutamide in a daily
dose of about 25 mg to about 150 mg administered orally once daily,
with a preferred adult dose of 50 mg/day. The mass ratio of
vilazodone to bicalutamide (calculated on the free-base
equivalents) ranges broadly from about 1:15 to about 4:1, and is
preferably from about 1:5 to about 1:1. The dose-equivalent molar
relationship and the partner- and clinician-engaged dosing
schedule are the inventive features, not the absolute mass ratio.

The technical effect of the combination is (a) a reduction, in
adults with Transvestic Disorder, in the frequency and intensity of
paraphilic urge, as measured by the Multiphasic Sex Inventory-II
(MSI-II) Paraphilic Sexual Outlet sub-scale and the Sexual Outlet
Inventory; (b) a reduction in disorder-specific distress as
measured by the Sexual Distress Scale; and (c) preservation of
consensual sexual function (relative to GnRH-agonist therapy), each
greater than the sum of the individual effects of vilazodone and
bicalutamide administered as monotherapies at any individually
achievable dose, and with substantially better tolerability than
hormonal anti-androgen monotherapy.

## Detailed description of the invention

### 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms have
the meanings set forth below.

*"Vilazodone"* means the compound 5-{4-[4-(5-cyano-1H-indol-3-yl)
butyl]piperazin-1-yl}benzofuran-2-carboxamide, marketed in the
United States as Viibryd®. Vilazodone is a selective serotonin
reuptake inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist with high
affinity for both targets. The term as used herein includes the
free base, the hydrochloride salt and other pharmaceutically
acceptable salts, solvates, hydrates, polymorphs, prodrugs,
isotopically labeled forms, and metabolically equivalent analogues.

*"Bicalutamide"* means the compound (R,S)-N-[4-cyano-3-
(trifluoromethyl)phenyl]-3-[(4-fluorophenyl)sulfonyl]-2-hydroxy-2-
methylpropanamide, marketed in the United States as Casodex®.
Bicalutamide is a non-steroidal competitive androgen receptor
antagonist with no agonist activity. The term as used herein
includes the racemate, the active R-enantiomer, and any
pharmaceutically acceptable salt, solvate, hydrate, polymorph,
prodrug, isotopically labeled form, or metabolically equivalent
analogue.

*"Transvestic Disorder"* means the disorder defined in DSM-5-TR
(302.3) and the corresponding ICD-11 construct: recurrent and
intense sexual arousal from cross-dressing, as manifested by
fantasies, urges, or behaviors, that has persisted for at least
six months and that causes *clinically significant distress or
impairment* in social, occupational, or other important areas of
functioning. The term *expressly excludes* consensual, non-
distressing cross-dressing in any context, and *expressly excludes*
gender identity and gender expression as such; the present
invention is *not* directed to and shall not be administered for
the purpose of altering gender identity or expression.

*"Paraphilic urge"* means an experienced internal pressure to
engage in cross-dressing that is associated, in the subject, with
the recurrent and intense sexual arousal characteristic of
Transvestic Disorder.

*"Therapeutically effective amount"* means an amount of an active
agent which, when administered to an adult subject for the
treatment of Transvestic Disorder according to a dosing regimen
disclosed herein, produces a clinically meaningful improvement in
at least one of: MSI-II Paraphilic Sexual Outlet sub-scale, Sexual
Outlet Inventory, Sexual Distress Scale, partner-reported
relationship-functioning, or investigator-rated CGI-I.

*"Co-administer"* means administer two active agents to a subject
so that the pharmacological effect of one agent is exerted on the
subject during the period in which the pharmacological effect of
the other agent is also exerted, regardless of whether the two
agents are administered in the same dosage form, in separate
dosage forms, at the same time, or at different times within a
window of about 24 hours.

*"Adult subject"* means a human subject of age ≥ 18 years at the
time of consent.

### 2. The two active agents

#### 2.1 Vilazodone

Vilazodone is an orally bioavailable selective serotonin reuptake
inhibitor and 5-HT1A receptor partial agonist. By inhibiting the
serotonin transporter, vilazodone elevates synaptic serotonin
across multiple central serotonergic targets; by partially agonizing
the 5-HT1A receptor, vilazodone is reported to support a more rapid
attenuation of anxious-compulsive symptomatology than a pure SSRI
and may attenuate the SSRI-typical "switch" agitation in the first
weeks of treatment. Vilazodone is FDA-approved for the treatment
of major depressive disorder in adults at oral doses of 10 mg/day
(initial), titrated to 20 mg/day after one week and to 40 mg/day
thereafter, taken once daily with food to support oral
bioavailability. Vilazodone is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4 and
is a substrate (and weak inhibitor) of CYP2C19 and CYP2D6.
Vilazodone is generic in the United States.

#### 2.2 Bicalutamide

Bicalutamide is a non-steroidal competitive antagonist at the
androgen receptor with no agonist activity. By competitively
displacing testosterone and dihydrotestosterone from the AR ligand-
binding domain, bicalutamide attenuates AR-mediated transcriptional
activity in central and peripheral target tissues *without
suppressing endogenous gonadotropin or testosterone secretion*.
Bicalutamide is FDA-approved for the treatment of advanced
prostate carcinoma at oral doses of 50 mg/day (in combination with
a luteinizing-hormone-releasing-hormone analog) and 150 mg/day
(monotherapy in selected indications outside the United States).
Bicalutamide is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4. Bicalutamide is
generic in the United States. The present invention adopts the
50 mg/day dosing as the starting dose for adult subjects with
Transvestic Disorder, with optional titration up to 100 mg/day in
non-responders, recognizing that the dose-response relationship in
the paraphilic-disorders setting is not fully characterized.

### 3. Compositions

The compositions of the present invention comprise vilazodone and
bicalutamide, together with at least one pharmaceutically
acceptable excipient. The compositions may take the form of (a) a
co-packaged kit of two separate dosage forms (vilazodone
hydrochloride tablet + bicalutamide tablet); or (b) a unitary

The mass ratio of vilazodone (calculated as the free base) to
bicalutamide (calculated as the free base) is in the range of about
1:15 to about 4:1, preferably about 1:5 to about 1:1, reflecting
that the typical vilazodone daily dose (20–40 mg) is approximately
0.4 to 1.6 times the typical bicalutamide daily dose (25–100 mg)

Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of vilazodone include the
hydrochloride and other pharmaceutically acceptable salts of

Excipients useful in the compositions include diluents

### 4. Dosage forms

Vilazodone is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-
release oral tablet at strengths of 10 mg, 20 mg, and 40 mg
(free-base equivalent), matching the marketed product. The tablet
should be taken with food to support oral bioavailability.

Bicalutamide is preferably formulated as a once-daily immediate-
release oral tablet at strengths of 25 mg, 50 mg, and 100 mg.

A kit form of the invention comprises (a) a 30-tablet bottle of
vilazodone tablets at the appropriate dose-titration strength,
(b) a 30-tablet bottle of bicalutamide 50 mg tablets, and (c) a
printed insert with administration timing, titration schedule, an
informed-consent and shared-decision-making module, and a list of
warning signs (rash, hepatic dysfunction, mood change) requiring
clinical re-evaluation.

### 5. Dosing regimens

For adults (subjects ≥ 18 years and ≥ 50 kg) with Transvestic
Disorder, the preferred dosing regimen is:

* Vilazodone 10 mg orally once daily with food on days 1–7, then
  20 mg once daily with food on days 8–14, then 40 mg once daily
  with food thereafter, matching the FDA-approved titration for
  major depressive disorder.
* Bicalutamide 50 mg orally once daily, with no titration. In
  non-responders at 12 weeks, the dose may be increased to
  100 mg/day with monitoring for hepatic adverse effects.

There is no defined pediatric population; the present invention is
limited to adults.

Hepatic adjustment: vilazodone should be reduced or avoided in
moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment per the prescribing
information. Bicalutamide should be used with caution in moderate
hepatic impairment and avoided in severe hepatic impairment;
serum aminotransferases should be measured at baseline, monthly for
the first 4 months, and periodically thereafter.

Renal adjustment: no specific adjustment is required for either
agent in mild-to-moderate renal impairment; in severe renal
impairment, dose review is recommended.

Medication–medication interactions: avoid co-administration with
strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (clarithromycin, ketoconazole, ritonavir,
itraconazole) without halving the vilazodone dose. Avoid
co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inducers. Co-administration
of bicalutamide with warfarin requires INR monitoring.

The methods of the present invention are directed to adult subjects

### 7. Mechanistic rationale

Paraphilic urge in Transvestic Disorder is mediated by (a) a
compulsive-ideation component supported by serotonergic and
frontolimbic circuits, with mechanistic overlap with
obsessive–compulsive and addictive phenotypes; (b) an
androgen-receptor-mediated central and peripheral drive component,
which in published literature responds substantially to anti-androgen
therapy; and (c) a learned and habit-mediated behavioral component
addressed by cognitive-behavioral therapy.

Vilazodone, by combining selective serotonin reuptake inhibition
with 5-HT1A partial agonism, addresses (a) by reducing the intensity
and intrusiveness of paraphilic ideation and the associated
distress, with the empirically validated antidepressant mechanism of
vilazodone in major depressive disorder providing the
written-description support for serotonergic central effect.
Bicalutamide, by competitively antagonizing the androgen receptor
*without castrating* the subject, addresses (b) in a titratable
manner that preserves a degree of consensual sexual function and
avoids the metabolic, bone-density, cardiovascular, and
gonadotropin-suppression burden of GnRH-agonist therapy. The two
axes are non-overlapping at the receptor level (serotonergic +
5-HT1A vs. androgen receptor) and converge on the
prefrontal–striatal–hypothalamic paraphilic-urge and
compulsive-ideation circuit.

Co-administration is therefore expected to produce
greater-than-additive improvement in paraphilic-distress and
paraphilic-urge measures, with a tolerability profile substantially
better than legacy hormonal anti-androgen monotherapy.

### 8. Pre-clinical support

In-vitro assays useful for confirming the mechanistic basis of the
invention include: SERT binding (³H-citalopram displacement on rat
brain membranes; Ki for vilazodone ≈ 0.5 nM); 5-HT1A binding
(³H-8-OH-DPAT displacement; Ki for vilazodone ≈ 0.2 nM); 5-HT1A
functional partial agonism (cAMP / GTPγS in 5-HT1A-CHO cells);
androgen receptor binding (³H-mibolerone or ³H-R1881 displacement
on LNCaP or AR-CHO cells; Ki for bicalutamide ≈ 100 nM); androgen
receptor functional antagonism (transactivation in MMTV-luciferase
LNCaP reporter line).

Pre-clinical paraphilia is not directly modelable in animals, but
in-vivo paradigms in rodent that probe the underlying substrates
include: marble-burying for compulsive behavior; novel-object
recognition for cognitive-domain reversibility; testosterone-
administration paradigms for AR-mediated copulatory behavior; and
defeminization / masculinization of preference paradigms in
neonatally-androgenized rodents. A supra-additive effect of the
combination on (i) marble-burying suppression and (ii) AR-mediated
copulatory paradigm intensity, relative to either monotherapy at
any individually achievable dose, is the principal in-vivo proof
point that motivates the Phase 2 design in § 9.

### 9. Clinical study designs

#### 9.1 Phase 1 — pharmacokinetics and medication–medication interaction

A single-center, open-label, three-period, fixed-sequence study in
24 healthy adult male volunteers (age 18–45 years). Period 1:
vilazodone titration to 40 mg/day for 7 days; full PK sampling on
days 1 and 7 with steady-state. Period 2 (after 7-day washout):
bicalutamide 50 mg/day for 28 days (to reach steady state given
the long bicalutamide half-life); full PK sampling on days 1, 14,
and 28. Period 3 (after appropriate washout): combination at the
same doses; PK sampling for both agents on days 1 and 14. The
primary endpoint is the geometric mean ratio for AUC₀–τ and Cmax
of vilazodone and of bicalutamide in combination versus
monotherapy, with 90 % confidence intervals contained within
0.80–1.25.

#### 9.2 Phase 2 — randomized, double-blind, three-arm

A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study of
the combination in adult Transvestic Disorder (FIG. 4). Three
arms, 1:1:1 randomization, target N = 120 (40 per arm) with 80 %
power to detect a clinically meaningful difference in MSI-II
Paraphilic Sexual Outlet sub-scale change between the combination
arm and placebo at week 16, assuming the standard deviations
reported in published paraphilic-disorders studies and a two-sided
α of 0.05.

* Arm A (combination): vilazodone titrated to 40 mg/day +
  bicalutamide 50 mg/day.
* Arm B (active monotherapy): vilazodone titrated to 40 mg/day +
  matching placebo for bicalutamide.
* Arm C (placebo): matching placebo for both, with cognitive-
  behavioral therapy as the standard-of-care comparator.

All subjects receive a standardized, manualized cognitive-
behavioral therapy program of equal frequency, intensity, and
duration (weekly 60-minute sessions over 16 weeks). The primary
endpoint is change from baseline in MSI-II Paraphilic Sexual
Outlet sub-scale at week 16. Secondary endpoints include Sexual
Outlet Inventory, Sexual Distress Scale, partner-reported
relationship-functioning, responder rate, and CGI-I. Safety
endpoints include hepatic enzyme monitoring, gynecomastia
assessment, sexual function inventory, and bone-mineral-density
DEXA at baseline and week 24 (a single 24-week DEXA suffices for
the duration of this study).

### Example 3 — Prophetic Phase 2 outcome

A Phase 2 study conducted in accordance with § 9.2 is expected to
demonstrate, at week 16, a least-squares mean reduction in MSI-II
Paraphilic Sexual Outlet sub-scale of approximately 35 % from
baseline in the combination arm, 18 % in the vilazodone monotherapy
arm, and 6 % in the placebo arm, with combination-versus-placebo
p < 0.001. Sexual Distress Scale reductions of approximately 40 %,
22 %, and 8 % are expected in the three arms. Hepatic-enzyme
elevations > 3 × upper limit of normal occur in approximately 2 %
of bicalutamide-exposed subjects with appropriate monitoring; no
clinically significant DEXA changes are expected over 24 weeks at
the 50 mg/day bicalutamide dose. The forest plot of prophetic
effect estimates across all six pre-specified endpoints is shown
in FIG. 5. PROPHETIC.

### Example 4 — Worked patient case

A 38-year-old male with a 12-year history of recurrent and intense
sexual arousal from cross-dressing, with associated marked distress
in the relational domain (partner-reported and self-reported), no
desire to alter gender identity or expression, no history of
hepatic disease, normal baseline ALT/AST, and a stable weekly CBT
regimen begins vilazodone 10 mg orally with food for 7 days,
titrated to 20 mg for 7 days and to 40 mg thereafter, plus
bicalutamide 50 mg orally once daily, with informed-consent
counseling addressing fertility implications and the option of

## Abstract

A pharmaceutical composition and method for the treatment of
Transvestic Disorder in adult subjects with clinically significant
distress or impairment per DSM-5-TR comprising co-administration of
a therapeutically effective amount of the SSRI / 5-HT1A partial
agonist vilazodone and a therapeutically effective amount of the
non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist bicalutamide are
disclosed. The two agents act on non-overlapping receptor classes
that converge on the prefrontal–striatal–hypothalamic
paraphilic-urge and compulsive-ideation circuits implicated in
Transvestic Disorder, and are expected to produce greater-than-
additive improvement in paraphilic-distress and paraphilic-urge
measures with substantially better tolerability than legacy
hormonal anti-androgen monotherapy. The invention is *not*
indicated for and shall not be administered for the purpose of
altering gender identity or expression.
